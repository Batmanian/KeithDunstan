Topic: William Kelly

4 items include the topic of William Kelly.

William Kelly was a nineteenth-century Irish-Australian journalist and goldfields chronicler whose reports on the colonial period Keith Dunstan drew on in 'Knockers', finding in them an early domestic voice joining the chorus of external critics sizing up Melbourne's character.

“Has Anyone Here Seen Kelly?”

Published 18 Feb 1967

Melbourne's Lord Mayor launches a $10,000 fly-control quiz competition, prompting Keith Dunstan to revisit the city's long war against blowflies through the writings of William Kelly, whose 1853 account of Melbourne's plague of flies remains the most vivid on record.

“The Evil Desecration of the Sabbath”

Published 1968

Charts the century-long Sabbatarian campaign to keep Melbourne silent on Sundays, from the riotous 1883 row over opening the National Gallery, and its nude scandal painting Chloe, to the 1968 bill that finally ended it.

“The Australian Stage”

Published 1972

Traces the knocking of the Australian stage from Lola Montez's 1855 Spider Dance controversy and Harry Seekamp's Ballarat horsewhipping through The Triad's savage music and theatre criticism, Nellie Melba's battles with John Norton, Frank Thring's career as a drama critic, the hostile reception given to Australian plays including Summer of the Seventeenth Doll and The One Day of the Year, the Katherine Brisbane libel case, and David Williamson's The Removalists, ending with a collection of ABC-era music criticism.
