“The art of being nostalgic”

Published 01 Feb 1975

Keith Dunstan finds nostalgia in a Thomas Records shop selling old 78rpm discs, in Tarzan comic books illustrated by Burne Hogarth, and in a 1923 Foy and Gibson catalogue, and argues that in an anxious present the past has never seemed more attractive.

“The serials we loved”

Published 17 May 1975

Keith Dunstan recalls the golden age of radio serials — Tarzan on 3UZ, the Lone Ranger, the Green Hornet — and the rituals of childhood listening, arguing that the old radio serial produced a quality of imagination that television can never replicate.

“The best time”

Published 27 Dec 1975

Keith Dunstan recalls his best times — the girls' school dance where he danced every dance with Priscilla, only to fail to recognise her at a ball twenty-five years later — alongside a Christmas Day 1950 flight with Prime Minister Robert Menzies from Singapore to Calcutta, where the BOAC steward announced that time had become irrelevant and proposed a Courvoisier before dinner.

“My house is not, repeat not, a home”

Published 20 Aug 1977

Moving out of a family home of 20 years in a single day — a chain reaction of Melbourne removalists all shifting at once — becomes an excavation through pogo sticks, hula hoops, cigarette card collections of Woodfull and Cazaly, the Guest House at Lorne photograph trick of sprinting to appear in the panoramic portrait twice, and the children's heartbreak over discarded school essays.

“A lament for the All Australian Car”

Published 11 Sep 1984

Keith Dunstan proposes a bronze statue of an FJ Holden at Fisherman's Bend to mourn the passing of the genuine Australian car — recalling the 1948 launch under Ben Chifley, the awe it inspired in Melbourne streets, and the humiliation of discovering Danish "Commodores" that turned out to be German Opels; now GM-H is replacing its six-cylinder engine with Japanese Nissan units, Ford is in bed with Mazda, and the world car will be owned by Japan.

“The great leap forward into history”

Published 13 Nov 1984

Melbourne is in the grip of a nostalgia boom — the Old Melbourne Hotel (bluestone from a demolished Fitzroy Methodist chapel, authentic Victorian squeaks), the Windsor after an $11 million Oberoi restoration, and the crown jewel Menzies at Rialto ($50 million, two 1890s buildings with an atrium and a Royal Suite at $600 a day featuring a $30,000 Steinway, gold taps and His-and-Hers bathrooms). The Historic Buildings Council fought to preserve the 1890 corrugated iron urinal shells behind the galleries, but the finest fin de siècle porcelain has quietly vanished.
