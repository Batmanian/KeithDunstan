“Business deals that are hard to swallow”

Published 23 Oct 1984

The businessmen's breakfast is spreading, the American Chamber of Commerce in Australia celebrated its 500th breakfast briefing last month, and Campbell Soups president Gordon McGovern invited press to 7.30am Hilton sessions after rising at 4am. Keith Dunstan resists: breakfast is a time for meditation, the mind is at its most savage, and nothing civilised should ever be decided at that hour. “How to survive until December 2”

Published 30 Oct 1984

The eight-week federal election campaign has Keith Dunstan suffering from "Panfophobiabesmdesondeb", an inability to read large headlines. This is Australia's 38th election since federation, its ninth legislature, the ALP is spending $5 million on ads, and 663 hours of broadcast time were sold in 1975. His survival kit: throw alien conversation-stoppers at people, tune into ABC FM for Bach cantatas after 7pm, and paste the kit on the refrigerator for all emergencies.

“The case of the sulking cricket pitch”

Published 04 Dec 1984

Cricket pitches are as mysterious and unfathomable as old wines, the WACA wicket prompted 500,000 words after the first test. Keith Dunstan proposes a Costigan royal commission to investigate the personalities of pitches: the Gabba uses black soil from Goodna (adjacent to the local mental asylum), the SCG soil from Bulli powders and takes spin, the Adelaide Oval is ultra-right-wing and unyielding. The MCG sold 2000 pewter urns of original 1853–1983 wicket soil at $390 each. The current MCG pitch is sulking, it doesn't like pyjama cricket and is on strike.
