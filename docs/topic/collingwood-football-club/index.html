“The Queue — A Kind of Cherished Tradition”

Published 23 Aug 1969

Melbourne psychologist Leon Mann, now lecturing at Harvard, publishes his research on Melbourne's football-final ticket queues, finding that 25,000 people once waited a full week in mud for 12,500 tickets, that veterans regard queuing as a cherished ritual earning Agincourt-level kudos, and that organised scalpers flew Tasmanian students to Melbourne at $1 an hour to stand in line before reselling tickets at $35 apiece.

“When the Queuing Has to Stop”

Published 13 Sep 1969

This year's MCG grand-final ticket queue disintegrates at 2 a.m. in a fist-fight that sends eight people to hospital and leaves two days of bottles and rubbish to clean up, reviving the annual question of whether tickets should be sold by mail, handed to clubs for distribution among worthy members, or — Keith Dunstan's modest proposal — by broadcasting all finals from a secret location in Alice Springs on coaxial cable.
