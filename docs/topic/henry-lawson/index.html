Topic: Henry Lawson

4 items include the topic of Henry Lawson.

Henry Lawson was Australia's foremost bush balladist and short story writer, whose 1892 verse mocking the nation's readiness to 'make a hero of a clod' for sport Keith Dunstan quoted to show the sporting super-religion was already entrenched a century before he wrote about it.

“Our Sporting Obsession”

Published 1973

Opens 'Sports' by arguing that sport is Australia's true national religion, tracing how politicians, Governors-General and even Prime Ministers have hitched themselves to football clubs and cricket teams to win public esteem, and how a colonial inferiority complex turned nineteenth-century sporting victories into proof the new nation could produce men and women equal to Britain.

“How to Enjoy Hot Christmases”

Published 01 Dec 1974

A defence of the Australian habit of serving roast turkey and plum pudding in midsummer heat, tracing Keith Dunstan's own Melbourne Christmases from the 1880s boom-era house of his childhood to a flying doctor's Christmas at Mt Isa, an Antarctic station's six-bottle dinner, and a Barrier Reef yacht.

“Demonic people”

Published 1994

Closes the book with portraits of the liquor trade's larger-than-life characters, led by Carlton & United's autocratic boss "Foge" Fogarty, who froze Keith out entirely after one imagined slight.

“Joy of sport lost in a country that takes it far too seriously”

Published 29 Dec 2010

Reflects on Australia's outsized reaction to the 2010-11 Ashes loss under Ricky Ponting, arguing the country has forgotten that sport, unlike genuine disasters such as Cyclone Tracy, is meant to be enjoyed regardless of the result.
