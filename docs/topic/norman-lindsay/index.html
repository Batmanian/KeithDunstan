Topic: Norman Lindsay

8 items include the topic of Norman Lindsay.

Norman Lindsay was the prolific Australian artist and writer whose last defence of Ned Kelly, written aged eighty-eight, argued the Kelly boys' era had shaped his own generation's boyhood games and that claims of Kelly's homosexuality were absurd, given the plain shortage of women in the bush.

“Anyone Here from Ballarat?”

A tour of Ballarat, the Victorian goldfields city that once held itself to be the grandest inland city in the British Empire, examining its extraordinary statues, threatened iron-lace verandahs, decaying gold-rush hotels, and modest hopes for a future as a tourist and cultural centre.

“The Evil of the Printed Word”

Published 1968

Traces Australia's long, erratic history of literary censorship, from Annie Besant's 1888 birth-control obscenity trial and Professor Marshall Hall's scandalous 1900 dismissal from Melbourne University, through the gaoling of novelist Robert Close in 1948, to the secret Customs blacklist and the eventual 1960s release of Lady Chatterley's Lover and Lolita.

“Australian Myths”

Published 1972

Examines the knocking of Australia's most sacred national myths, from the vicious attacks on Ned Kelly in Melbourne Punch and the homosexuality allegation that proved the one knock too far, through the sustained persecution of Les Darcy as a wartime shirker, the failed attempts to deflate Don Bradman and Kingsford Smith, and the long campaign against the Sydney Harbour Bridge and the Melbourne Shrine of Remembrance, ending with the Anzac Day controversies of the 1960s.
