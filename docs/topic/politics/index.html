“Toward a more viable tax scene”

Published 13 Oct 1973

Treasurer Crean's inflation problem could be solved by a cliche tax ranging from $50 for the return of "churching the old whore" to 25 cents for each "in fact," since Keith Dunstan's monitoring of Parliament and TV current affairs reveals an epidemic of "viable," "cogent," "in this area," "gut feeling" and the coming word "elan," with Senator Gair's "fleas on an old dog" worth another $50.

“How not to go bonkers at election time”

Published 03 Dec 1977

Australia's nine legislatures have produced a federal election every second year since Federation, and with both parties spending $2 million on advertising for the current campaign, Keith Dunstan offers the Bulletin Guide to Mental Purity: avoid TV between 6 and 8.30 pm, listen to FM radio (Wagner's Siegfried is safe), throw away your letterbox junk, read newspapers from the back page, and start conversations about whether the Pope would make a good Presbyterian.

“How to survive until December 2”

Published 30 Oct 1984

The eight-week federal election campaign has Keith Dunstan suffering from "Panfophobiabesmdesondeb", an inability to read large headlines. This is Australia's 38th election since federation, its ninth legislature, the ALP is spending $5 million on ads, and 663 hours of broadcast time were sold in 1975. His survival kit: throw alien conversation-stoppers at people, tune into ABC FM for Bach cantatas after 7pm, and paste the kit on the refrigerator for all emergencies.
