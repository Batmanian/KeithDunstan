“Best Hotels in the World”

Published 01 Oct 1951

Reports on the two most modern hotels in the world, the El Panama in Panama City, designed by Ed Stone with no windows, louvred "breeze-ways" and no lobby, and the Caribe Hilton in San Juan, Puerto Rico, with 300 air-conditioned rooms, a private beach and 14-acre gardens, arguing that such American investment could transform Australian tourism.

“The problem of the Woo Factor”

Published 21 Aug 1984

The Woo Factor, the unnecessary gunk added to products to beguile the buyer, is worst in hotels: bathroom telephones by the dunny, automatic refrigerators that register instantly on your bill, mirrors on the ceiling, 2113 slot machines and four-poster beds as big as a squash court at Caesar's Palace. Keith Dunstan proposes a hotel that offers only a comfortable bed, adequate light, a working shower, and no refrigerator.

“The great leap forward into history”

Published 13 Nov 1984

Melbourne is in the grip of a nostalgia boom, the Old Melbourne Hotel (bluestone from a demolished Fitzroy Methodist chapel, authentic Victorian squeaks), the Windsor after an $11 million Oberoi restoration, and the crown jewel Menzies at Rialto ($50 million, two 1890s buildings with an atrium and a Royal Suite at $600 a day featuring a $30,000 Steinway, gold taps and His-and-Hers bathrooms). The Historic Buildings Council fought to preserve the 1890 corrugated iron urinal shells behind the galleries, but the finest fin de siècle porcelain has quietly vanished.
