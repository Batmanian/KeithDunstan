“The bagnio capital of the world”

Published 17 Apr 1984

Victoria's Minister for Planning announces the decriminalisation of prostitution in massage parlours, prompting Keith Dunstan to survey Melbourne's long history with the "social evil" — from 597 fallen women in 1885, to the Duke of Edinburgh's visit to Sarah Fraser's bagnio in Stephen Street in 1867, to the 190 parlours now serving 60,000 to 70,000 clients a week.

“Cinders – it's back to the ball”

Published 08 May 1984

Keith Dunstan travels by K-class steam locomotive to celebrate the centenary of T94 at the North Williamstown Railway Museum — 21 steam locomotives, a magnificent 1890 Norman car used by Victorian governors and royalty, and a coming Steam Age Australia luxury excursion service modelled on the Orient Express.

“The art of spending pennies”

Published 12 Jun 1984

The Rialto, William Pitt's magnificent 1891 Victorian Gothic building at the western end of Collins Street, is being recycled into the grand Menzies at the Rialto hotel — but its refurbishment stalls when the Historic Buildings Council insists on preserving the corrugated iron lavatory cubicles at the end of its galleries, arguing they tell the not-so-glorious story of "Smelbourne" in the 1880s, when night-soil men collected waste and the urinals drained into the Yarra.

“The action at the Paris end”

Published 04 Sep 1984

Victoria legalised brothels in June 1984, giving them 12 months to relocate or obtain council permits — triggering a battle over King's Place ("Cain Lane"), a Collins Street establishment on the 15th floor charging up to $150 for "fantasy," and vivid history of Madame Brussells' garden labyrinth, Annie Wilson's Boccaccio House, and the parliamentary mace that vanished on the night of 8 October 1891.

“Cricket as the game is written”

Published 13 Nov 1984

A review of Bat and Pad, compiled by Pat Mullins and Philip Derriman (Oxford University Press, $25) — a collection of writings on Australian cricket from 1804 to 1984 with a foreword by Bob Hawke. Neville Cardus described Bradman reeling off runs while "Woodfull was as much taken for granted as Kreisler's accompanist," and a 1838 Sydney match produced the complaint about "low slang and insulting remarks." A Bob Menzies courtroom anecdote on the cricketophile judge is the book's high point.

“A burning desire to find another pub”

Published 11 Dec 1984

The Eureka flag has been so captured by Norm Gallagher and the Builders' Laborers' Federation that Ballarat citizens despair of reclaiming it. For the 130th anniversary, the Eureka Re-enactment Committee burnt down a real Bentley's Eureka Hotel — bark roof, timber uprights, full Eureka Hotel sign — built in a fortnight from two wrecked houses at Sovereign Hill. More than 3000 people watched it burn magnificently for over an hour; afterwards the march to Bakery Hill re-enacted the monster meeting of 10,000 miners, though the original Bakery Hill is now occupied by a McDonald's restaurant.
