“When Disney comes to town”

Published 30 Nov 1974

Australia's first Walt Disney production — Ride a Wild Pony, budget $1 million, filming around Chiltern near Wodonga — sends 30 vehicles of crew including cinematographer Jack Cardiff to cover the bitumen roads with earth, repaint all the shops in gold and black, install antique Plume and Atlantic petrol pumps and create a Crystal Palace Theatre with Pola Negri posters, transforming a nearly-demolished country town into a 1928 period piece that Disney plans to leave intact as a tourist attraction.

“Quintaphonic and other sounds”

Published 26 Apr 1975

The film Tommy is being shown in quintaphonic sound at the Esquire cinema, using a system invented by Melbourne's John Mosely, while Keith Dunstan reflects on the generation gap in music — from Deep Purple at Sunbury to Tommy to the Rolling Stones — and wonders what has become of melody.

Published 03 May 1975

The production team for Godfather II, including producer Gray Frederickson, casting director Fred Roos and production designer Dean Tavoularis, arrives in Melbourne to scout locations and discuss the film's spectacular killing sequences, bringing Hollywood's most prestigious franchise to Australia.

“Women on the warpath”

Published 05 Jul 1975

In International Women's Year, Keith Dunstan examines the aggressive new female roles in films such as Shampoo, Alvin Purple and Petersen, finding that the liberated woman in Australian cinema is a more predatory figure than her 1950s predecessor, and wondering what Warren Beatty makes of it all.

Published 20 Sep 1975

The Victorian budget debate produces fanciful proposals to tax pornography and massage parlours as a revenue source, with Keith Dunstan examining the R-certificate film business, the film Histoire d'O and the difficulty of drawing lines between acceptable and unacceptable material in a permissive age.
