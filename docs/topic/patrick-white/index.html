Topic: Patrick White

5 items include the topic of Patrick White.

Patrick White was the Nobel Prize-winning Australian novelist whose dense, visionary works including 'Voss', 'The Tree of Man' and 'The Vivisector' set him apart as the country's greatest literary voice, yet whose fame Keith Dunstan repeatedly cited in 'Knockers' as evidence of the national paradox, a writer of world stature almost unknown to a dozen random Australians on the street.

“Knockers”

Published 1972

Opens 'Knockers' by tracing the Australian compulsion to cut down its own achievers, from a Melbourne newspaper importing a London editor to soothe its colonial cringe, through visiting critics from James Cameron to Clement Freud, to the vicious reception of the Sydney Opera House and Australian writers at home.

“Australian Writers”

Published 1972

Traces the long contempt shown to Australian writers, from the colonial press's dismissal of Adam Lindsay Gordon and the Victorian Parliament's refusal to grant a pension to Marcus Clarke's destitute widow, through Henry Lawson's fury at being ignored, the decades of neglect suffered by Joseph Furphy and Henry Handel Richardson, D. H. Lawrence's savage reception, the Ern Malley hoax and the prosecution of Max Harris, the critical savaging of Patrick White, and the knocking of Germaine Greer and Barry Humphries on their return from overseas triumph.

Grace's disputed 1874 letter savaging colonial cricket fans to Malcolm Muggeridge's lotus-eating portraits and Arthur Koestler's Qantas plastic hook epiphany, tracing also the long shame and eventual pride in the Australian accent.
