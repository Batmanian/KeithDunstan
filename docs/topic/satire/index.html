“The satirist at work”

Published 12 Jul 1975

Amid the constitutional crisis of 1975, Keith Dunstan imagines a satirical Australian state in which journalists and politicians are jailed and caves are named after Ozakitsch Company products, drawing on the confrontation between Gough Whitlam and Malcolm Fraser to explore the limits of political satire.

“Stagflation is NOT blowing up deer”

Published 23 Jul 1977

On the eve of another budget, with 38 percent of economists optimistic, 28 percent predicting ruin and 34 percent waffling, Keith Dunstan offers a seminar in economic vocabulary — spiralling inflation, stagflation, buoyant or rocky outlooks, haemorrhaging reserves, and the vital concept of "viable" — so readers can hold their own at dinner parties.

“Cold comfort”

Published 17 Sep 1977

A letter from Trans Time Inc. of Canberra prompts a conversation with Australian agent Dr Thomas Donaldson, a mathematics lecturer at the ANU, about cryonic suspension — $50,000 for the full body, $15,000 for the head only, 28 Americans frozen so far with 14 still in thermos-bottle storage at minus 169 degrees — leading Keith Dunstan to doubt whether waking in the year 3000 would be worth the trouble.

“How not to go bonkers at election time”

Published 03 Dec 1977

Australia's nine legislatures have produced a federal election every second year since Federation — and with both parties spending $2 million on advertising for the current campaign — Keith Dunstan offers the Bulletin Guide to Mental Purity: avoid TV between 6 and 8.30 pm, listen to FM radio (Wagner's Siegfried is safe), throw away your letterbox junk, read newspapers from the back page, and start conversations about whether the Pope would make a good Presbyterian.

“The colorful cloying of company reports”

Published 10 Dec 1977

Annual reports in 1977 are 22.8 percent bigger than 1976, with color up 29.7 percent — EMI's features a National Geographic seascape and a gentleman wielding an electron into a cathode ray tube, Carlton and United Breweries shows a million empty pottles, BHP's board poses like a Marylebone cricket team of the 1880s, and Peter Kurts Properties labels their executives "DYNAMIC," "A LARGE YOUNG LION," and "NOT EASILY RUFFLED" in the chairman's own handwriting.

“Please keep on the grass”

Published 17 Dec 1977

The muesli-and-fitness revolution has overtaken Keith Dunstan's household — 5BX exercises, isometrics, Japanese cookers, vitamin E for immortality, lunchtime joggers fleeing their coronaries through the Treasury Gardens — but after mixing muesli in the bath and graduating to a plastic rubbish bin, the most remarkable result is a stomach like a five-gallon fermenting tank, and the next trend is grasses, herbs and weeds, with nasturtiums recommended to arouse the sluggish man from his torpidity.

“Come to Sydney and get bitten”

Published 24 Dec 1977

Melbourne's new Tourism Authority and Sydney's new Visitors Bureau launch simultaneously — Melbourne sends Lord Mayor Kennedy, Cr Rockman, league footballers and local beauties to Hyde Park to give away 2200 carnations and kick footballs into the crowd while playing "Why Don't You Come And See Melbourne" over loudspeakers — prompting Keith Dunstan to propose Sydney reciprocate with mock funnel-web spiders, Manly sharks in a tank, and Madame Justice Evatt as compere.
