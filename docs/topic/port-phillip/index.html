Topic: Port Phillip

5 items include the topic of Port Phillip.

Port Phillip is the great bay on which Melbourne stands, whose heads at Queenscliff were where newspapermen once raced to meet incoming ships for the latest overseas news.

“Wireless Telegraphy will Bring us the News”

Published 1988

The first direct wireless messages from England reach Australia in September 1918, sent by Prime Minister Billy Hughes and Navy Minister Joseph Cook from Marconi's Welsh station to Ernest Fisk's experimental receiver at Wahroonga, promising news for a shilling a word instead of three.

Published 1988

News that the British Parliament has passed the Separation Bill reaches Port Phillip in November 1850, and Melbourne celebrates its freedom from New South Wales with beacons, a 21-gun salute, illuminated houses and shop transparencies mocking the 'Sydney robbers'.

“Illegal Fisticuffs”

Published 1988

Irishman Jim Kelly beats Jonathan Smith in a bare-knuckle prize fight at Fiery Creek near Daylesford in December 1854, the longest fight the colonies had seen at six and a quarter hours, in a sport the polite papers refused to mention and the sporting press refused to give up.

Published 1988

Giant convict bricklayer William Buckley escapes over Christmas 1803 from David Collins's short-lived settlement at Sullivan Bay on Port Phillip, and while his companions perish or come back starving, he lives with the Wathaurong people for 32 years.
