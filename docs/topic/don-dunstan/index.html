Topic: Don Dunstan

6 items include the topic of Don Dunstan.

Don Dunstan was the progressive Labor premier of South Australia from 1967 to 1979, notable for progressive social reforms and an iconoclastic personal style; Keith Dunstan noted his all-white suit, dark blue shirt and white tie at the opening of the Sydney Opera House.

“All Boys in the Ban, But for a Naughty Neighbor”

Published 12 Sep 1970

Victoria's struggle to maintain public decency is undermined at every turn by South Australia, where Don Dunstan allows 'Portnoy's Complaint' to be sold, wears shorts to Parliament and presides over the longest drinking hours in Australia, prompting Keith Dunstan to quote at length a Victorian preacher's Victorian-era pamphlet on the perils of self-pollution.

“Fashion gets it in the neck”

Published 03 Nov 1973

Attending the Sydney Opera House opening in an outdated suit, Keith Dunstan consults Melbourne fashion designer Mike Treloar, who reports that ties are dying except for enormous Oscar Wilde bow ties, open-necked shirts are the new look and chest hair is the status symbol, while Italian tie-maker Ottavio Ermini arrives to argue that the tie is the key of a man's personality and the Japanese will pay anything for a Gallieni pure silk spinnaker.

Al Grassby's push to attract 250,000 expatriate Australians home inspires Keith Dunstan to propose a network of Australian Nostalgia Centres in London, Paris and New York, stocked with Fosters, Four 'n' Twenty pies, footy replays and Violet Crumble Bars.

“My secret dossier”

Published 07 Feb 1978

"Batman" confesses his ASIO dossier to prove that history shows Australia's real enemies have always come from the right, not the left, by listing his own impeccably conservative credentials including Melbourne Grammar, the Melbourne Club, and devotion to Henry Bolte candles.
