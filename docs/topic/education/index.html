“Ear-bashers' annual orgy”

Published 15 Dec 1973

Melbourne's school speech nights, unchanged in pattern since Keith Dunstan first attended 45 years ago, follow an invariable formula of Gilbert and Sullivan, the headmaster's report on school finances, a visiting dignitary who has been booked as guest speaker at every school at the same time, and speeches so excruciating that one is left wondering why there have been campaigns for the abolition of examinations but none for the abolition of school speech nights.

“Room to learn at the top”

Published 18 May 1974

A Jones, Lang and Wootton review reveals 11 per cent of Melbourne's office space stands empty — 1.7 million square feet by year's end — prompting Keith Dunstan to propose turning the city's prestige towers, including the 50-storey Nauru House and the twin 40-storey Collins Place towers, into schools with panoramic views and wall-to-wall carpet.

“The merging of the schools”

Published 10 May 1975

The merger of Clyde School at Woodend with Geelong Grammar, and the Hermitage's merger with another school, mark an economically driven movement toward co-education in Australian private schools, with Keith Dunstan examining what is gained and lost when single-sex boarding schools become co-educational.

“The "Old dark blue" goes pink”

Published 16 Jul 1977

Melbourne Grammar announces 25 girls will start at Grimwade House next year — a momentous break for Australia's oldest Anglican public school, which has produced three Prime Ministers — while cold economics drives the co-ed trend across Victoria's private schools, with over 200 girls already at Geelong Grammar and female crews heading for the Head of the River.
