Topic: Windsor Hotel

6 items include the topic of Windsor Hotel.

The Windsor Hotel is Melbourne's grandest surviving nineteenth-century hotel, on Spring Street opposite Parliament House, whose Cricketers Bar, open fireplaces and high ceilings Keith Dunstan declared compulsory for any visitor to the city.

“You Need More Than a 'D' in Toorak”

Published 26 Dec 1964

The opening of 'Nareeb', Toorak's last great private estate, for public auction draws 15,000 visitors to the 53-acre Kooyong Road property unchanged since 1888, where the contents, from a 1920 Rolls-Royce to aspidistras, sell for over £18,000.

“The Phantom Rolls-Royce”

Published 16 Apr 1966

The Melbourne City Council orders a Phantom V Rolls-Royce for the Lord Mayor at $23,800, prompting a survey of the Council's peculiar anachronisms, from the Civic Group's gentlemanly rotation of the mayoralty to the ongoing failure to build a civic square or make sensible use of a city block opposite Myer.

“Another Encore for the Square Dance”

Published 02 Jul 1966

Melbourne's 110-year obsession with building a civic square is traced through a parade of proposals, from an 1856 newspaper leader and a Jubilee scheme tied to Parliament House, through post-war plans to demolish the Windsor Hotel and Princess Theatre, to the current stand-off with the English Hammerson development group over the Swanston and Collins Streets corner site.

The Victoria Coffee Palace's decision to seek a liquor licence marks the end of Melbourne's last great temperance hotel, prompting a survey of the coffee palace movement's rise and fall from James Munro's 1880 crusade to stripping clubs, executive bus trips to Sydney, and the creeping normalisation of alcohol in the city.
