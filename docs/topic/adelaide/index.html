Topic: Adelaide

10 items include the topic of Adelaide.

Adelaide is South Australia's capital, a city Keith Dunstan alternately admired and teased in his Bulletin writing, whether comparing its cultural ambitions with Melbourne's or tracing Australia's earliest artificial ice-skating rinks.

“Goodbye to the Glaci”

Published 27 Apr 1963

Farewells Melbourne's Glaciarium after fire destroyed the pioneering ice rink, recalling its genteel opening in 1906, orchestras, speed skaters and eventual defeat by St Moritz and changing fashions.

“A Mural for 1000 Years”

Published 29 May 1965

A profile of Melbourne artist Stanislaus Ostoja-Kotkowski, who fuses electronics, laser science and industrial pigments into mural works for BP buildings in Melbourne and Adelaide, and who trained at the National Gallery under William Dargie while cutting sandwiches through the night to stay fed.

Read more...

Related topics: Adelaide, Melbourne, National Gallery of Victoria, South Yarra, William Dargie

“The Last Swill”

Published 18 Dec 1965

Victoria's 49-year-old six o'clock closing finally ends, ushering in 10 p.m. trading and a rush of late-night pub entertainment, restaurants with beer licences, and a new .05 blood-alcohol limit for drivers.

Related topics: 6 Oclock swill, Adelaide, Alexander Peacock, Australian Hotels Association, Beer, Melbourne, Victoria

“Which City is in Front...?”

Published 12 Nov 1966

A survey of the Melbourne versus Sydney rivalry, examining competing newspaper claims about population growth, migration, new industries, building completions and personal income, prompted by the CIA-level precision with which LBJ's visit was split equally between the two cities.

“Getting into Troubled Waters”

Published 26 Nov 1966

The long history of the Victoria-South Australia border dispute, from the 1839 survey through successive High Court and Privy Council cases to the 1966 standoff over offshore oil rights, with Sir Henry Bolte threatening a blockade of Corio whisky and Melbourne Cup horses.

Related topics: Adelaide, Melbourne, Sir Henry Bolte, South Australia, Victoria

Published 10 Jun 1967

The centenary of Prince Alfred's 1867 Australian tour passes unremarked, prompting a retelling of the extraordinary colonial welcome he received in Melbourne, culminating in the disastrous Citizens' Free Banquet in Richmond Park where 60,000 descended on provisions for 10,000 and the wine flowed literally into the street.
