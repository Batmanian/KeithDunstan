Timeline

Every transcribed article in the archive, alongside Keith Dunstan's published books, in the order they appeared — from his earliest Walkabout pieces through to his latest columns in The Age.
Select a linked entry to read it in full.

1962 The paddock that grew Book

Keith's first book, about the origins and history of the Melbourne Cricket Ground, tracing its transformation from a swampy paddock beside the Yarra into Australia's premier sporting arena.

01 Feb 1962 Bird Watching Walkabout Magazine

Profiles Melbourne's Bird Observers Club, from a postman-secretary who charms Dutch sea captains to the Land Rover expedition that rediscovered the Eyrean Grasswren, unseen since 1874.

01 Feb 1962 Rough Riding for Five Minutes a Year Walkabout Magazine

Rides the outback rodeo circuit with Australia's professional rough-riders, from the Myrtleford Rodeo's untamed high-plains bulls to a wild-horse race that leaves one rider knocked cold and refusing all help.

03 Mar 1962 The New Image The Bulletin

On Melbourne's sudden reputation for permissiveness, from a swimsuit-censorship row at the Australian Swimming Championships to the Moomba Committee's controversial beer garden in the Alexandra Gardens.

17 Mar 1962 Football and TV The Bulletin

Covers Melbourne's fierce row over the VFL's ban on televising the last quarter of League football, then Victoria's new tallest building, the 24-storey Consolidated Zinc, opening almost unnoticed.

24 Mar 1962 No Camels for Swanston Street The Bulletin

Mourns the decline of Melbourne's once-riotous Eight Hour Day march into a corporate-sponsored Moomba procession, its circus camels banned, then reports on Sandown Park's star-studded car-racing debut.

31 Mar 1962 The New Breed The Bulletin

Previews Pan-American's new Miami-style Southern Cross Hotel rising on the old Eastern Market site, then the Lord Mayor's £770 replacement robes after the old ones, handed down for decades, finally fell apart.

07 Apr 1962 The Lash The Bulletin

A spate of unprovoked gang bashings sparks public calls for the return of the lash, running into Melbourne's ongoing water restrictions and a stake-money spat between the Caulfield and Melbourne Cups.

14 Apr 1962 Forbidden City The Bulletin

An undercover survey of Melbourne's new strip-tease clubs, comparing the silent, appraising Melbourne audience to Sydney's, before turning to the Richmond Brewery's absorption into Carlton and United on Friday the 13th.

21 Apr 1962 To the Spencer Street Station The Bulletin

Compares the muted arrival of the new Southern Aurora express at Spencer Street with the wild 1883 celebrations when Melbourne and Sydney's rail lines first met at Albury, then covers Victoria's new ban on tattooing minors.

28 Apr 1962 Football and M.F.L. The Bulletin

Covers the Collingwood Football Club's ceremonial guernsey-presentation dinner for its new season, then the box-office phenomenon of "My Fair Lady"'s second Melbourne season at Her Majesty's Theatre.

05 May 1962 Rembrandt and Phar Lap The Bulletin

A reluctant Melburnian tourist takes a sightseeing coach tour of his own city, from Ned Kelly's gaol to the National Gallery, and finds Phar Lap draws more attention than the Rembrandt.

12 May 1962 The Release of John Bryan Kerr The Bulletin

On the eve of his release after 12 years, revisits Melbourne's most sensational murder case, the conviction of the charming, well-connected John Bryan Kerr for the 1949 strangling of Elizabeth Maureen Williams, and the newspaper campaign that fought to reopen it.

26 May 1962 A Viscount, a Viscount, a Viscount . . . The Bulletin

Profiles bandleader Tommy Davidson, Melbourne's leading writer of singing television commercials, from his award-winning Viscount cigarette jingle to a £5500 TV shoot with a 46-piece orchestra.

02 Jun 1962 Not Only Serious – Solemn! The Bulletin

A blow-by-blow account of Melbourne's raging Age letters-page feud between realist and modernist painters, pitting William Dargie against National Gallery director Eric Westbrook.

09 Jun 1962 The Great Bank Robbery The Bulletin

Investigates Melbourne's record £40,000 bank robbery and the mystery of the thieves' 21 keys, then the Puffing Billy Preservation Society's fight to reopen the narrow-gauge line through the Dandenongs.

16 Jun 1962 The Stampede and the Fall-Out Shelter The Bulletin

Follows the new south-eastern freeway's traffic overflow into once-exclusive Toorak, then tours a fully-furnished nuclear fall-out shelter on display at the Building Industry Fair.

23 Jun 1962 Wild Life in Suburbia The Bulletin

A Brotherhood of St Laurence survey of a Melbourne housing estate puts hard numbers on Barry Humphries' Mrs Everage caricature, finding suburban Melburnians were overwhelmingly stay-at-home, TV-watching and incurious.

30 Jun 1962 Now They Call Him "Satchmo" Bolte The Bulletin

Traces the Exhibition Building's rise from would-be demolition target to beloved 1963 Trade Fair venue via its riotous 1880s exhibition history, then catches Henry Bolte earning the nickname "Satchmo" over a misquoted Anthony Trollope line about Victorian boasting.

01 Jul 1962 Paddy's Market to Luxury Hotel Walkabout Magazine

Traces the site of the new £5 million Southern Cross Hotel back through the raucous history of the old Eastern Market, phrenologists, dancing ducks, a fortune-teller called Madame Zinga Lee, and an 1898 murder.

14 Jul 1962 Inside Toorak The Bulletin

An affectionate dissection of Toorak's century of unrivalled Melbourne snob value, from its jealously small postcode SE2 to its chauffeur-driven Rolls Royces and Tudor-revival Village shops.

28 Jul 1962 A Little Red Rented Rowboat The Bulletin

A tour of Melbourne's National Boat Show, where the speedboat has replaced the car as the new status symbol, headlined by Bob Dyer's near-£4000 shark-fishing cruiser Tennessee IV.

04 Aug 1962 Return of Norm and Edna Everage The Bulletin

Welcomes Barry Humphries home from London for a nine-night Assembly Hall season, recounting his elaborate public pranks, including the "Ashburton Line" breakfast-in-a-carriage stunt and slices of ham hidden in a David Jones bargain sale.

11 Aug 1962 Not Just a Beer-Up Club! The Bulletin

Mourns the demolition of the historic Scotts Hotel as Melbourne's city pubs vanish one by one, then visits the newly formed Beer Appreciation Society of Australia conducting solemn blind tastings at the Kilkenny Inn.

18 Aug 1962 The Coal Exchange Rotunda The Bulletin

Architect Roy Grounds proposes shipping London's condemned Victorian Coal Exchange rotunda 12,000 miles to Melbourne's new Cultural Centre, igniting a row over whether it's a masterpiece or, as one letter-writer put it, "ostentatious Victorian junk."

25 Aug 1962 £500 Is a Poodle The Bulletin

The three-day, city-wide hunt for Heloise, a runaway pedigree poodle worth £500 sent from Adelaide for breeding, complete with a police search, a "poodle posse," and a televised on-air appeal.

01 Sep 1962 A Bowling Alley and a Bidet The Bulletin

A tour through the newly-opened Southern Cross Hotel, Melbourne's first brand-new hotel in decades, from Australia's first hotel bidet to its bowling alley built over a bank.

08 Sep 1962 St Kilda Rd — or Disneyland Modern The Bulletin

Laments the once-grand St Kilda Road's decline into brash "Disneyland Modern" office architecture, then reports on the inaugural meeting of Melbourne's new Noise Abatement Society, modelled on "America's quietest city," Memphis.

15 Sep 1962 The Fortress Affair — or SP in the Raw The Bulletin

Mines the Post Office Royal Commission's evidence on Victoria's illegal SP bookmakers, from a St Kilda penthouse called "The Fortress" that police had to storm by ledge, to a bookie's electronic betting bunker under a kitchen floor.

22 Sep 1962 £7000 for "Divinely Dressed Ladies" The Bulletin

The Victoria Racing Club, alarmed by falling attendances, unveils a £7000 best-dressed-racegoer competition for that year's Melbourne Cup carnival, turning the horses into something close to an afterthought.

29 Sep 1962 Kindly Smile from an Aberdeen Angus The Bulletin

A dutiful trudge round the ever-identical Royal Show turns up an unexpected revival, a tug-o'-war contest recalling Melbourne's forgotten 1892 world championship pull and its two legendary twenty-stone anchor men.

06 Oct 1962 Among My Souvenirs The Bulletin

A wistful post-Grand-Final survey of Melbourne's VFL football obsession, from poodle-sized club sweaters to fans who queued 58 hours outside the MCG, plus a wary eye on soccer's fast-growing crowds at Olympic Park.

13 Oct 1962 Liquor and the Silk Trade The Bulletin

Traces Victoria's absurd six o'clock closing laws back to a wartime "state of emergency" that never officially ended, then joins 1200 delegates from the Associated Country Women of the World at their Melbourne conference.

20 Oct 1962 Heavens! Changes at Georges? The Bulletin

The staid ladies'-only department store Georges opens a men's-wear department for the first time in 82 years, complete with a private-club atmosphere and £250 vicuna coats.

27 Oct 1962 Mr Bolte and the Students The Bulletin

Profiles David Hirt, the bearded theological student leading Melbourne University's campaign against the hanging of Robert Tait, and the students' months-long standoff with a dismissive Henry Bolte government.

03 Nov 1962 "La Ville de Beauté" (as the French Say) The Bulletin

Melbourne wins bragging rights as Australia's "Conference City," hosting the World Power Conference's 1000-plus delegates through a whirlwind of interpreters, transistor earphones, and two 1600-guest banquets in the Exhibition Building.

10 Nov 1962 "A Wonderful Lot of Drinkin' Men" The Bulletin

Marks the centenary of the first All-England cricket tour of 1861-62 by revisiting its Melbourne Cricket Ground opening, balloon ascent, and lopsided country matches against 22-man local sides.

17 Nov 1962 The Cup - Some Bowlers Were Low and Rakish The Bulletin

Reports back from Melbourne Cup day on the results of the VRC's inaugural "fashions, flowers and favorites" festival, including the fashion judges' undercover hunt for the best-dressed racegoer under £30 or £50.

24 Nov 1962 NSW–Vic: Is Peaceful Co-Existence Possible? The Bulletin

A tongue-in-cheek peace plan for the Sydney-Melbourne rivalry, sparked by Melbourne's jetport frustrations and a Senate row over an anti-Melbourne pamphlet, complete with a proposed Bolte-Heffron summit in a railway carriage at Albury.

01 Dec 1962 Rediscovering the Yarra The Bulletin

Previews the Queen's 1963 royal barge voyage up the Yarra to Henley Landing, and the belated riverbank clean-up it has prompted after years of floating trash and half-sunken barges.

01 Dec 1962 Epicurean Melbourne Walkabout Magazine

A tour of Melbourne's booming and increasingly cosmopolitan restaurant scene, from a Scottish Week haggis ceremony at The Capers and shoes-off sukiyaki at Sukiyaki House to Jimmy Watson's Carlton wine bar and the menu-free nine-course extravagance of the Walnut Tree.

15 Dec 1962 An Albert Tucker for 2s. a Mile The Bulletin

Tours the newly opened John Batman motor inn, Robin Boyd's design showcase hung with original Nolans and Tuckers, from its £500,000 build cost down to its black-carpeted penthouse suite.

22 Dec 1962 Mr Fogarty's Silent Spokesman The Bulletin

A pre-Christmas beer bottle shortage forces Melbourne to drink imported Toohey's and West End, unmasking Carlton and United's Reg Fogarty as the ruthless "beer czar" who controls the price and supply of beer across Victoria.

29 Dec 1962 A Rug of Abyssinian Monkey Skins The Bulletin

Visits used-car king Emmanuel Margolin, once Australia's self-styled biggest car dealer, as the credit squeeze forces him to auction his Toorak mansion's zebra hides, matador suit, and 500-monkey-pelt rug.

01 Jan 1963 Freedom of Beach Walkabout Magazine

A survey of Australia's unusually open beaches, contrasting the free run of the Australian coastline with the private, fenced-off beaches of the Italian Riviera, Hawaii and California, and tracing the colonial land law and the Bondi bikini scandals that shaped the public's right to walk the sand.

05 Jan 1963 Gather Ye Rosebuds While Ye May The Bulletin

A satirical guide to how Victorians spend their summer by the water, from Portsea's status-symbol boat sheds and Harold Holt's earnest spearfishing hobby to Lake Eildon's flotilla of cabin cruisers and Rosebud's vast camping ground.

12 Jan 1963 Henry Bolte's Brilliant Duck The Bulletin

Premier Henry Bolte stands in as a replacement batsman in a Centenary of International Cricket replay match at the MCG, kitted out in a garish spotted shirt and a learner's L-plate pinned to his back.

19 Jan 1963 Senator Kennelly and the Monster The Bulletin

Albert Park Lake's decade-long infestation of Canadian waterweed defeats every remedy the Albert Park Trust's chairman, Senator Pat Kennelly, throws at it, from an imported weed-cutting boat to a fleet of speedboats dumping tons of copper sulphate.

26 Jan 1963 Under the King Street Bridge The Bulletin

Profiles Whelan the Wrecker, the Melbourne demolition firm founded in 1891 by a bowler-hatted Irish immigrant, now engaged to dismantle three cracked spans of King's Bridge and busily expanding into wrecking Sydney as well.

09 Feb 1963 Mayhem, Rapine and Sin The Bulletin

A gleeful account of radio station 3UZ's Sunday-morning "Newsbeat" bulletin, which thrusts a microphone in front of car-crash victims and brawl survivors moments after the police and ambulance arrive, delivering Melbourne its weekly dose of actionable gossip that the once-lurid Truth newspaper no longer supplies.

23 Feb 1963 About Cigarettes and Gondolas The Bulletin

Surveys Melbourne's booming cigarette trade and Nobel laureate Macfarlane Burnet's lonely campaign against smoking, then reports the mishap befalling Moomba's imported 16th-century Venetian gondola, which shrank so badly out of water that it now refuses to float.

09 Mar 1963 All the Queen's Men The Bulletin

On the exhausted vocabulary of Royal Tour reporting nine years after "obviously" and "radiant" were banned in 1954, and on Melbourne's noticeably cooler, less crowd-mobbed reception for the Queen's 1963 visit compared with her first.

16 Mar 1963 In the Depths of SE 2 The Bulletin

Follows up on used-car dealer Emmanuel Margolin's spectacular financial collapse with a report from the auction of his Toorak household effects, from leopard-skin rugs and a matador suit tailored in Madrid to the Ford Thunderbird he had already bought twice over.

30 Mar 1963 Bunnies in Black Silk Stockings The Bulletin

A dispatch from Melbourne's new Playboy Restaurant, the local answer to Hugh Hefner's American bunny clubs, where five waitresses in bunny tails and 18-inch stockings serve drinks only, chosen from a hundred applicants and forbidden from dating the customers.

06 Apr 1963 Those Lucky OYO Men The Bulletin

A tour of Domain Park, Robin Boyd's new 20-storey "own-your-own-flat" tower opposite the Botanic Gardens, from its Georges furniture and £37,500 penthouses to the view over Government House, the MCG and St Patrick's from its balcony.

13 Apr 1963 Getting Publicity for Peanuts The Bulletin

Wangaratta throws everything at the Australian Wool Bureau's national publicity contest, from a 500-guest "Wild Colonial" banquet with Sir Robert Menzies as guest speaker to a week-long bullock-wagon re-enactment of Major Mitchell's 1836 expedition down the Hume Highway.

01 Jul 1963 From Prickly Pear to Petroleum Walkabout Magazine

A visit to the Queensland oil boomtowns of Tara and Moonie, tracing the district's turn from a prickly-pear-choked backwater once known as 'The Lost Province' into the birthplace of Australia's oil industry, and meeting the selection farmers whose land the oil was found under.

27 Jul 1963 When Hoyts was Hoyts The Bulletin

Marks Hoyts' 50th anniversary, from dentist-turned-showman Arthur Russell's 1913 start to the neck-and-neck 1929 opening race between the Regent and the State, alongside the wreckers' demolition of the old Savoy (once the temperance-movement Temperance Hall).

01 Dec 1963 Christmas across a continent Walkabout Magazine

A survey of how Australians spend Christmas Day across wildly different settings, from turkey conservatives at Rosebud and water-skiing Santas at Surfers Paradise to Warramunga camps, Antarctic research bases, and mail runs to Cocos Island and Tasman Island lighthouse.

25 Jan 1964 The Capitol and ‘The World's Mightiest Spectacle.’ The Bulletin

On the closure of Walter Burley Griffin's Capitol Theatre, recalling its 1924 opening with "The Ten Commandments" and its Egyptian-god stage prelude, £15,000 Wurlitzer organ, and famous prismatic ceiling.

14 Mar 1964 When Flappers Fluttered Through the Flames’ The Bulletin

On the belated Melbourne opening of "Cleopatra," at the old Lyceum cinema renamed for the occasion, digging up its history back to 1915 as the Paramount and its era of silent-film sirens like Theda Bara.

01 May 1964 Melbourne's Evergreen Exhibition Walkabout Magazine

A history of Melbourne's Exhibition Building, from the lavish international exhibitions of 1880 and 1888 through Sidney Myer's Depression-era Christmas dinner for 11,000 unemployed people to its 1963 revival as a trade fair venue.

01 Dec 1964 Adventures of a Would-be Wine Snob Walkabout Magazine

A home bottler's tour of Victoria's wine industry in 1964, from siphoning hogsheads into gallon flasks at kitchen tables to a wineabout through Rutherglen's Morris and All Saints vineyards and Nagambie's Chateau Tahbilk.

01 May 1965 Battle of the Flower Walkabout Magazine

A tour of Australia's competitive gardening culture and its flower festivals, from Toowoomba's Carnival of Flowers and Ballarat's begonias to the Sydney Morning Herald's statewide garden contest and Western Australia's wildflower season.

1966 Supporting a column Book

Reflects on the trials of taking on a daily column in the Sun News-Pictorial, a behind-the-scenes account of Australian newspaper life in the 1950s and 60s.

09 Apr 1966 The Mighty Wurlitzer Rose in a Pool of Light. The Bulletin

A visit to the Regent Theatre's Mighty Wurlitzer organ, one of only two left working in Australia, and a morning practice session with virtuoso organist Tony Fenelon.

01 Oct 1966 Melbourne Cup Winners: Horses or Hats? Walkabout Magazine

A survey of the Melbourne Cup as spectacle as much as sport, from Jean Shrimpton's mini-skirted 1965 Derby appearance and the ritual of Cup Day toppers and fashion parades to the race's history, Carbine's 1890 record run, and Archer's 550-mile walk to Flemington in 1861.

01 Dec 1966 1966 and All That Walkabout Magazine

A tongue-in-cheek review of 1966, the year of decimal currency, Robert Menzies' retirement and Harold Holt's spear-fishing bikini photographs, the Pentridge escape of Ronald Ryan, conscription protests, the mini-skirt, and Lyndon Johnson's visit to Australia.

18 Mar 1967 This is a fine state to be in The Bulletin

A survey of the deteriorating relations between Victoria and New South Wales, arguing Melbourne's case for its rightful place in the international air network via the new Tullamarine airport.

15 Apr 1967 Alas, poor Tivoli, I knew it well. The Bulletin

A eulogy for Melbourne's fire-gutted Tivoli Theatre, tracing its history back to the 1850s diggers' Varieties Music Hall through Harry Rickard's opulent 1901 rebuild and its parade of stars from Marie Lloyd to Harry Houdini.

30 Dec 1967 The Ghost of the Princess The Bulletin

Tells the story of Federici, the Princess Theatre's baritone ghost who collapsed and died on stage playing Mephistopheles in 1888, and the book launch that tried, and failed, to lure his spirit out with themed cocktails.

01 Jan 1968 The Bikini, What Next? Walkabout Magazine

A history of Australia's long, losing battle to regulate what beachgoers wear and when they may swim, from the segregated sea baths and bathing machines of the 1890s and William Gocher's 1902 defiance of Manly's bathing laws to the 1968 furore over the bikini.

1968 Wowsers Book

Documents the prudery of an early Australia aiming to shed its image as a place of convicts and rough behaviour.

01 Apr 1968 The Year of Anti-Football Protest Walkabout Magazine

Keith Dunstan's account of founding the Anti-Football League in 1967 with Douglas Wilkie, from the badge sold for the Berry Street Babies Home to the posthumous Wilkie Medal awarded to Prime Minister Harold Holt for never once being seen at a match.

01 May 1968 How to cheat at wine tastings The Australian Gourmet

A tongue-in-cheek guide to bluffing your way through a masked-bottle wine tasting, from reading cork brands and capsule colours to timing your "hint of crushed violets" line for maximum effect.

01 Oct 1968 Beef Boom in Tombstone Territory Walkabout Magazine

A tour of American-financed cattle stations on Cape York Peninsula, where Sir William Gunn's Gunn Rural Management and the King Ranch operation at Tully were fencing scrubmaster herds, breeding Brahmans and Santa Gertrudis, and bulldozing rainforest for legume pasture.

01 May 1969 Walkabout Profile: Jean Battersby Walkabout Magazine

A profile of Dr Jean Battersby, newly appointed Executive Director of the Australian Council for the Arts, on subsidising drama, ballet, opera and film, her Sorbonne years on a French government scholarship, and her rejection of the "bricks and concrete" test for judging cultural investment.

01 Dec 1969 Summer Madness Walkabout Magazine

A wry survey of Australia's overloaded summer beach culture, surfboards, caravans, and competitive Rosebud campsites, from Portsea and Palm Beach's Toorak-set glamour to Surfers Paradise's Miami-style tower blocks.

1971 The Australian Uppercrust Book Book

A collection of essays dissecting snobbery across Australian society; Keith contributed the chapter on 'Snobbery in Business'.

1972 Knockers Book

Examines the Australian habit of denigrating success and cutting down tall poppies.

1973 Sports Book

Looks at the Australian obsession with sport and sporting heroes, from suburban football fields to the country's biggest sporting institutions.

1979 The store on the hill Book

The story of Georges, the luxury Collins Street department store famed for its hushed, exclusive service.

1980 Ratbags Book

Profiles Australia's great eccentrics, from Bea Miles to Percy Grainger.

1980 Saint Ned Book

A light-hearted look at the life of bushranger Ned Kelly and the growth of the Kelly legend into an industry of its own.

1989 A Day in the Life of Australia Book

An historical account of Australia for each day of the year, commissioned by The Age for the 1988 bicentennial.

1990 No brains at all Book

Keith's first book of memoirs, covering his career in writing, reflections on Melbourne, working abroad and his family life.

1991 No brains on Tuesday Book

A compilation of the best of Keith Dunstan's newspaper columns, gathering decades of his wit and observations on Australian life.

1994 My life with the demon Book

Recounts Keith's experiences with, around and under the influence of the Demon Drink, from boozy newsroom culture to Melbourne's six o'clock swill.

13 Oct 1997 This street was made for walking The Age

A defence of the pedestrianised Swanston Walk against councillors pushing to reopen it to traffic, citing survey figures showing it as the busiest pedestrian corner in Melbourne and the return of retailers like Levi Strauss and Nike.

27 Oct 1997 The happy thwack of cash on willow The Age

A meditation on cricket's transformation from a game of beautifully organised boredom, best enjoyed dozing at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, into a business fought over by players threatening strike action for a share of ballooning sponsorship money.

29 Dec 1997 Harbor no grudge, it's better here; The bay and me The Age

A Boxing Day case for Port Phillip Bay over its more celebrated rival, touring favourite spots from Sea Winds and Point Nepean to the Sorrento ferry and a flathead-fishing trip with mate Doug Crittenden.

05 Mar 1998 Moombas and shakers The Age

A profile of the Wittingslow family, the fairground dynasty who have run Moomba's rides and sideshows since patriarch Tom Wittingslow started out guessing weights at Penny Land in 1932, and who now stage the festival itself.

08 Aug 1998 A new clutch of ugly ducklings blots the cityscape The Age

A nomination round for Melbourne's ugliest sights, from the Foxtel and Optus advertising signs looming over the historic Young and Jackson's Hotel to the kitsch interior of the new Imax Theatre and the warring columns of 101 Collins Street.

31 Oct 1998 On the right track at last The Age

The Lilydale-Warburton Rail Trail opens along a disused line closed by Henry Bolte in 1964, part of a growing Victorian movement converting old railway corridors into 36 kilometres of bush trail free of motor vehicles.

14 Nov 1998 Once a vision, now a site for sore eyes The Age

The gutted, empty Southern Cross Hotel four years after the Nauru Government's redevelopment plans collapsed, and a look back at the lively Eastern Market it replaced in the 1960s, the market where C.J. Dennis's Sentimental Bloke first met Doreen.

05 Dec 1998 After 500 kilometres, still the kings (and queens) of the road The Age

The 15th Great Victorian Bike Ride brings 2100 cyclists 500 kilometres from Wahgunyah to Melbourne, among them 83-year-old veteran Alan "Curly" Hartup and the ride's youngest finisher, nine-year-old Anna Clements.

1999 The confessions of a bicycle nut Book

Recounts a lifelong passion for cycling, from founding the Bicycle Institute of Victoria to touring by bicycle across the United States and through China.

12 May 1999 Obituary: Richard Cudlipp The Age

An obituary for advertising executive Dick Cudlipp, who rose to managing director of George Patterson Bates in 1965, drove Carlton and United Breweries' campaign against the Courage Brewery, and later became a Western District cattle and vineyard grazier.

2004 Batman in the Bulletin: The Melbourne I Remember Book

A collection of Keith's columns written under the pseudonym 'Batman' for the Bulletin magazine, a wry record of Melbourne life and characters.

2005 Collins: the story of Australia's premier street Book

A richly illustrated history of Collins Street, Melbourne's premier boulevard, with contributions from Keith Dunstan.

2005 Moonee Ponds to Broadway Book

A study of Keith's friend and fellow Melburnian Barry Humphries, tracing his journey from suburban Melbourne to international stardom as Dame Edna Everage.

29 Dec 2010 Joy of sport lost in a country that takes it far too seriously The Age

Reflects on Australia's outsized reaction to the 2010-11 Ashes loss under Ricky Ponting, arguing the country has forgotten that sport, unlike genuine disasters such as Cyclone Tracy, is meant to be enjoyed regardless of the result.

04 Jan 2011 Plagues be upon us, let's hope for no divine retribution The Age

A mock-biblical catalogue of Melbourne's afflictions during a wet 2010, led by a possum population boom under the Wildlife Act, and running through cars, mobile phones, football and noisy restaurants via the ten plagues of Exodus.

27 Jan 2011 It's crunch time for Melbourne: we need a congestion tax The Age

Argues Melbourne should follow London and Stockholm in adopting a congestion tax to break the city's addiction to the motor car, citing Bicycle Victoria figures on the rising share of peak-hour commuter cyclists.

06 Jun 2011 Swear fines a nonsense when invective is so ineffective The Age

Marks new Victorian on-the-spot fines for swearing by recalling editor Michael Davie's landmark 1979 decision to print an obscenity in The Age, and proposes Paul Keating chair an inquiry into supplying Australians with fresh invective.

17 Jul 2011 All I know about ethics, I learned at sword-point The Age

Recalls a career of manufactured tabloid outrage, from a fake letters-page furore over Myer's window mannequins to a night spent hiding in a wardrobe from a sword-wielding husband, prompted by reflecting on the collapse of the News of the World.

22 Aug 2011 Once, footy dominated our lives. Now, things are much worse The Age

Surveys the exponential growth of Australian Football League saturation since Keith Dunstan and Douglas Wilkie founded the Anti-Football League in 1967, from a single Saturday afternoon competition to year-round coverage, gambling and coaching sackings.

11 Feb 2012 Obituary: Walter Joseph Hauser The Age

An obituary for Walter Hauser, one of the touring Vienna Mozart Boys' Choir singers stranded in Melbourne by the outbreak of war in 1939, who was taken in by Archbishop Daniel Mannix and went on to a career as an industrial chemist and Keith Dunstan's regular bridge partner.

21 Jun 2012 Oh, for the daily thud on my lawn The Age

A tribute to the ritual of reading a printed daily newspaper in bed each morning, tracing Keith Dunstan's own newspaper family history against the decades-long death toll of Melbourne mastheads and Rupert Murdoch's prediction that print itself would be gone by 2020.

2017 Kiwi: The Australian brand that brought a shine to the world Book

Traces the story of the Ramsay family, who founded the Kiwi shoe polish brand in Melbourne in 1906 and built it into a global business.
