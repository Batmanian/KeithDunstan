Books

As well as writing a daily column, magazines and scripts for television and radio, Keith Dunstan wrote over 35 books.

Some of these texts are available for reading here, select the title to view more information.

Keith's first book, about the origins and history of the Melbourne Cricket Ground. It follows the ground's transformation from a patch of swampy paddock beside the Yarra into Australia's premier sporting arena.

Revised and republished by MCG trustees as 'The People's Ground'

Published by Cassell Australia
Keith's second book, published in 1966, reflects on the trials of taking on a daily column in the Sun News Pictorial and his efforts in keeping it upright. It offers a behind-the-scenes account of Australian newspaper life in the 1950s and 60s.

Keith's third book, documents the prudery of an early Australia aiming to shed its image as a place of convicts and rough behaviour.

A collection of essays dissecting snobbery across Australian society, from business and food to wine and the arts. Keith's chapter on 'Snobbery in Business' can be read here.

Other contributors to this book on Snobbery in society, sex, wine, food and the arts, include contributions by Ian Moffitt, Max Lake, Sandra Hall, Daphne Guinness and Philip Adams.

Knockers examines the Australian habit of denigrating success and cutting down 'tall poppies', part of Dunstan's quartet of books on national character alongside Wowsers, Sports and Ratbags. Drawing on historical anecdote and press cuttings, he traces the knocker from colonial times to the present.

Read available chapters here.

Sports looks at the Australian obsession with sport and sporting heroes, from suburban football fields to the country's biggest sporting institutions. It is part of Dunstan's quartet of books examining Australian character, alongside Wowsers, Knockers and Ratbags.

Read the acknowledgments and available chapters here.

An anthology edited by John Ferguson in which seven Australian writers each profile one of the country's major cities. Keith contributed the Melbourne chapter, 'Melbourne: A Perfect Nineteenth-Century City', tracing its gold rush confidence, the crash of 1892, Toorak's snobberies and the city's finest buildings.

Read Keith's chapter here.

The story of 'Georges', the luxury department store in Collins Street, Melbourne, famed for its hushed, exclusive service and its status as the shopping destination of the city's high society. Dunstan traces its rise from a small drapery to Melbourne's most prestigious retailer.

A light-hearted look at the life of the bushranger Ned Kelly and the growth of the Kelly legend into an industry of its own, from folk hero to marketing icon. Dunstan uses Kelly's near-canonisation to explore Australia's enduring fascination with underdogs and outlaws.

From the first edition: "Ratbags is Keith Dunstan's fourth book which looks at Australians and the offbeat way they behave. The other titles which complete the quartet are Wowsers, Knockers and Sports.

It has been suggested that Keith Dunstan himself is highly qualified as a ratbag, having spent a long and industrious career practising his eccentricities. He is the founder of The Anti Football League, The Pro Mini Club and The National Distrust.

He has ridden a bicycle across America, has become a member of the anti-automobile lobby and has been a daily columnist for twenty-four years. But perhaps he has best earned the title of Ratbag by surviving as Batman of the Bulletin for 17 years. He is an achiever. "

Kiwi traces the story of the Ramsay family, who founded the iconic shoe polish brand in Melbourne in 1906 and built it into a global business that supplied Anzac troops and eventually ran two dozen factories worldwide. Dunstan follows the family's fortunes, rivalries and famous advertising campaigns across more than a century.
