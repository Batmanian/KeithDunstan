Foreword

3 min read.

First published 1980

Barry Humphries' foreword to 'Ratbags', a comic definition of the Australian ratbag that ends with his own confession -- 'I am that Ratbag'.

What is a Ratbag? A Ratbag is a man who saves all his dead matches for that tea tray he knows he will never make.

A Ratbag writes letters to Raquel Welch enclosing his photograph and a stamped addressed envelope.

A Ratbag spends five minutes every morning transferring virginian cigarettes into an old menthol packet to save an expensive round.

A Ratbag takes correspondence lessons in ballroom dancing, chews every mouthful of food thirty-seven times without failing and never leaves home without checking his hip pocket for toilet paper.

A Ratbag likes indoor golf, train-spotting, scotch and tonic, Australian postage stamps and Bruckner's Romantic Symphony.

He loves phoning up on talk back programs, writing letters to editors, participating in Walkathons, amateur hypnosis, folk music and bee-keeping.

He hates pillows, central heating, champagne, James Bond movies, other Ratbags and jelly beans.

His wife makes her own bread, rolls her own cigarettes, gets photographed at student demonstrations and attends a hypnotherapist to give up smoking.

Ratbags believe in the Divine Right of Kings, the Flat Earth Society, flying saucers and the Book of Revelations.

They have their ancestries traced, their faces lifted and eat Chinese food with chopsticks when they're at home when no-one is watching.

When the garbage man says Merry Christmas and holds out his hand, a Ratbag shakes it.

If you're a vegetarian, a Jehovah's Witness birdwatcher with five-colored Biros in your breast pocket and you belong to a sky diving club, you are a potential Ratbag.

If you are a hippy over nineteen who thinks Shakespeare was a woman, Hitler was Jewish and if Beethoven were alive today he would be writing TV jingles, you are a transitional Ratbag.

But if you read The Great Gatsby once a year, if you can whistle the Brahms Hungarian Dances, don't have a television set, grow your own marijuana and have just ghost-written the autobiography of a surf rider, you are a terminal or raving Ratbag.

There are Ratbags in all walks of life, but especially amongst potters, inventors, politicians, war-relic enthusiasts, lone yachtsmen, underground movie makers and sperm donors.

A Ratbag is a curious blend of Rasputin, Lady Godiva, Doctor Timothy Leary, the Emperor Nero, Albert Schweitzer and Yoko Ono.

He's a fascinating composition with the arms of Venus de Milo, the head of John the Baptist, thoughts of Mao Tse Tung, the heart of Dr Christian Barnard and the ear of Vincent Van Gogh.

He's always lending you books you don't read, and giving advice you don't need. But when you are tired and fed up after a tough day of household chores, when life seems drab and grey and meaningless and suddenly you hear the sound of his vintage car in the drive, the sound of that voice you know so well coming from the lounge-room, and the familiar echo of that laughter you have grown to love — all your little worries disappear.

You know's he's there in the lounge reciting his favorite monologue and playing his favorite record on the stereo phonograph...

And friends this story is true... I AM THAT RATBAG!

Barry Humphries.
