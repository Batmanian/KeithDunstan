Dawn Fraser Expelled

3 min read.

First published 1988

Dawn Fraser, Australia's greatest swimmer, is expelled for ten years by the Australian Swimming Union following the Tokyo Olympics, along with three other girls given bans of three to four years, in sentences the entire swimming world regards as savage and without precedent for Olympic athletes, prompting both Dawn Fraser and Linda McGill to engage QCs and take legal action.

Dawn Fraser, 27, has been expelled for ten years by the Australian Amateur Swimming Union. This means that the greatest Australian swimmer, and other girls who swam for Australia at the Tokyo Olympics, also have been expelled for varying periods: Marlene Dayman, four years; another girl, three years; and Nan Duncan, 17, three years.

The entire swimming world is stunned at the severity of the sentences. No Olympian before has ever been expelled. The ASU did not give the charges against the girls and there is no appeal.

Both Dawn Fraser and Linda McGill intend to take legal action. Dawn Fraser's solicitor, Mr Edward France, has briefed Mr Clive Evatt QC. Linda McGill's solicitor, Mr Carl Melvey, has briefed Mr A. Goran QC. Mr Melvey said he had read the charges against Linda McGill set out in a letter to her by Mr W. Berge Phillips, ASU secretary-treasurer. 'They are too trivial for words' Mr Melvey said. 'We consider that the action of the Australian Swimming Union is in complete contravention of both Common Law and Equity'.

Last night Dawn Fraser said:

I'm not going to take it lying down. I have the right as an individual to stand in front of the Amateur Swimming Union committee and face it and fight it. My name has been slandered right across the world. I am not going to let it be slandered without an explanation. All I did wrong in Tokyo was march in the opening ceremony and wear a wrong swimsuit in my races. The official swimsuit was too tight and uncomfortable.

Linda McGill says from Rome: 'Please tell all you can to fight this slur. As Australians we did everything we could for Australia at the Olympic Games in Tokyo and what has happened to us now seems a very poor reward. We did all I can assure all Australians we need be ashamed of.'

This morning the papers are filled with angry letters. 'Disgusted' (Footscray) writes: 'What a narrow minded lot of officials we must have in the Amateur Swimming Union, condemning these swimming champions without a trial. Swimming will be a thing of the past for our girls before long.'

'Mother' (Dromana) says 'If the chaperone appointed by the ASU could not control a 15-year-old girl, then it is she, and not Marlene Dayman who should be banned'.

And 'Be Fair' Carnegie insists 'The treatment meted out to our girl swimmers will quench the enthusiasm of future swimmers. The sentences are more in the nature of those for murder and should, in all fairness, be reduced'.

So what will happen now. Even Henry Bolte, Premier of Victoria, is angry. No sufficient explanation has been given for the decisions. He would give careful consideration before the State again gave money for swimming funds. The Victorian Government gave a substantial sum to the appeal for the Tokyo Games.
