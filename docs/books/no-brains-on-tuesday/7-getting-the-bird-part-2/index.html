Getting the Bird (Part 2)

5 min read.

First published 1991

Advice pours in from around Australia on deterring vineyard birds, from fermenting-gruel hangovers to electrified bait traps and revolving mirror units, and the Easter vintage goes ahead with yellow plastic netting, panty hose over individual bunches, and granddaughters Hannah and Isabel recruited to foot-tramp the pinot noir.

The vintage, the grape picking and the battle of the birds is now at its height on the Peninsular.

You will remember a month back in the midst of our agonies we formed the Ghould League of Birds. The Ghould League was the protection society for grape growers who were feeding the seeming millions of starlings, blackbirds, parrots and silver eyes.

There were a number of remarkable calls and letters. From Gingin Western Australia we heard from 'Invent Australia' which has sold many thousands of its Bird Deterrent Unit at $74 each. This is a revolving weather vane of reflecting mirrors designed to dazzle the birds with flashes of sun. What happens when you don't have any sun?

We have sent for three of those, but they will not arrive until after the big feed is all over.

A gentleman from Tascott NSW told us that the answer is to grow sunflowers between the rows. When the birds see the huge yellow flowers always they will eat the flower seed before the grapes.

From Balranald a farmer told us: 'Give them bowls of fermenting gruel and keep them all drunk. After a few hangovers they will never come back.' My experience is that hangovers have never been a deterrent to humans.

Southport Queensland was a real darling. He gave us a design for a sheet of chicken wire connected to positive and negative electricity leads. In the centre was a tin plate, an enamel bowl of sweet bran, and when the birds landed, they made perfect contact...ZAP!

If I were a nervous type and wanted to soothe the greenies, he suggested that I could reduce the charge to 110 volts.

In the end we went for no fancy solutions. We netted the really productive rows with a brand of yellow plastic netting. As reported earlier, we covered individual bunches of pinot noir with panty hose. Indeed we went round the district pleading for used panty hose and came up with some very attractive specimens, green, yellow, blue and even sexy black. You get three bunches to a leg.

The panty hose was a fantastic success, but with the yellow netting we lost 80 to 90 per cent of our grapes. Alas the quality was not good enough and it stretched too easily. We put the nets all over the vines and clipped them underneath. The tiny silvereyes were unbelievable. You could watch them wriggling inside, first one leg through the web, then the other and — bewdee — inside. They just make a needle-thin gap.

The starlings and blackbirds can't get through the net but they push it against the grapes, then do the job. They are enthusiastic early eaters. I would get up at dawn and run up and down and shriek: GEEDAWAY YOU LITTLE BUGGERS.

The man who delivered the papers would blush as he heard this daily fuss.

Sometimes the larger birds would get tangled in the vines. Last Wednesday we found a blackbird, limp. Was it dead? I freed it from the net and it gave a faint shudder.

My wife said: 'We can't leave the poor thing like this, the dogs will get it.' I won't say we gave it mouth to mouth resuscitation but we did everything else. We put it in an intensive care unit at the back of the house. We gave it a light spray with water, put it in cool shade and would you believe the ghastly grape filled object made a complete recovery.

When foolishly I reported the tale to our neighbouring vigneron he was shocked. 'This is an outrage,' said he. 'I will report you to the committee and have you expelled from the Ghould Birds. I bet that bird went straight over to eat at my place.'

O.K. we had our vintage at Easter, such grapes that were left. We had to go over every bunch and pick off all those that had silver eye puncture marks. Yes, once a silver eye has popped in his hypodermic, terrible bacteria can result.

We put the grapes in a large plastic rubbish tin and, of course, the classic method from ancient times is to have the fruit foot-tramped by virgins. Maidens doing the job ensures that the wine has absolute purity.

We do not have a great many virgins available on the Peninsular, but we managed to arrange some granddaughters, Hannah, 7, and Isabel, 4.

They took off their dresses, shoes, socks, wearing only their under things and hopped into the plastic bin and started squelching. Actually it took rather a long time. My recommendation would be to look for heavier virgins, 80 or 90 kilo maidens would be wonderful.

However, these girls did a splendid job and Hannah found it such fun, she didn't want to get out. There was no worry about the red juice around her knees, the strawberry squash between the toes. She thought it was lovely.

A little sulphur, some warm yeast all these things went in. The laundry is the temporary winery and some fine bubbling and very noble aromas are coming from that part of the house. There are suggestions that we should make some pinot blanc, some white wine, and in that case we will need to take over the refrigerator and put a bin there for controlled fermentation. Clothes washing and eating might have to be delayed for four or five days.

And again if the pinot noir fermentation doesn't reach the right temperatures I will have to take electric blankets off the bed to give the brew an extra boost. Wives need to be very understanding at vintage time.

The next step will be to determine a name for the wine. Do you think Pinot Panty Hose would be rather fetching?

This story is rather like Neighbours, Home and Away, or even East End. You might even hear some more gaudy episodes...
