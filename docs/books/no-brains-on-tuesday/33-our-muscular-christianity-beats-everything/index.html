Our Muscular Christianity Beats Everything

4 min read.

First published 1991

A proposed Fourth Law of Motion applied to Australian sport coverage, arguing that the greater the victory the greater the hometown press, that Australia being thrashed in Pakistan barely rated a headline, and that losing gracefully abroad might actually generate more publicity than winning.

As we enter a week of agonising sporting euphoria, could one put forward a theory.

In homage to Isaac Newton we could call it the Fourth Law of Motion. If I remember my schoolboy physics, Newton had three laws of motion. The Fourth Law is this:

The Greater the Victory the Greater the Hometown Coverage.

The Greater the Loss the Smaller the Hometown Coverage.

I have taken out my ruler and I think the ratio for a grand victory is ten to one. The important thing is to stress the pluck and courage in coming in. It is not really important to discover who actually won.

The usual practice is to report only the triumphs and sufferings of your own beloved Australians. Others don't really exist.

Yesterday we had some fine headlines concerning the 100km team time-trial cycling. Did the Germans actually win? It doesn't matter really.

We have been playing a Test match in Pakistan. The truth is we have been mercilessly thrashed and the Fourth Law has been well applied.

Even though I am a passionate cricket lover I find it more satisfying to read of an Australian victory against Korea at hockey, a sport which in my school days was considered on the feminine side.

Ah, but had it been an Australian cricket victory with Tony Dodemaide taking nine wickets and 300 runs on the board, the Fourth Law would have been operating at full force. Channel Nine and big columns on page one. It is difficult to discern.

Back in the 19th century there were many fine theories about sport. One of the most interesting was that of the muscular Christian. If one was good in muscle then one was also good in mind; sport was for the soul. Sport spread decency, honour, comradeship and fellowship, and this was why Britain and its Empire were great.

Well, muscular Christianity has suffered some famous setbacks since going global. Relations between England and Pakistan are similar to those between Britain and Germany after the Treaty of Versailles. There have been accusations of cheating and umpiring. Indeed even during the current Test we have made formal complaints against Pakistani umpires and the state of the wicket.

In football, international relations are so bad that the Americas Cup brought out quite aggressive Australian feelings toward the United States. New Zealand, having departed from the ANZUS alliance, is almost in a state of war with the United States. Simple examples of muscular heroics can be found in most codes. We have just witnessed the annual execution of coaches who have failed to bring their teams to glory.

In sport, Victoria is not talking to South Australia. There are rumblings of a secession movement in West Australia.

Every affluent country on earth is spending a fortune on sport. We have built in Canberra the Institute of Sport. East Germany and Russia spend even more. The belief is that through sport you gain international prestige. But your stories are only slightly reported in the foreign press.

What if other countries got bitterly angry at seeing all your fellows on television? Take every country — Russia, those who get themselves slightly defeated at tennis, football, athletics, hockey and basketball.

After every defeat they would get ten times as much publicity in the foreign press as a victory would earn. And if they went down clearly, proudly and magnanimously, I bet you they would be loved instead of feared.

Keep notes during the next fortnight, apply the Fourth Law of Motion and I assure you, the result will be fascinating.
