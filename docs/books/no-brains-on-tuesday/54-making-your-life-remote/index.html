Making Your Life Remote

5 min read.

First published 1991

A survey of the household chaos caused by an ever multiplying pile of remote controls, from mixing up the video and the CD player in the dark to friends who need four separate remotes for their garage doors, hi-fi and front gate, and a mail-order catalogue's promise of a single universal remote to replace them all.

We learn something every day. Now we know how to murder by video.

Did you watch 'Mother Love' on TV? Diana Rigg, the darling old Emma Peel days used to beat people up with karate and judo. Now as Helena, the poisoning mother, she is a sort of Lucretia Borgia, poisoning everyone who gets in her path.

One of the heart-warming scenes, showed her with video monitor in her hand. She had taped a television interview with the hated second wife Lady Vesey. Smiling sweet, villainous smiles, she played back this TV show, then going through it frame by frame, she studied how to impersonate the Lady Vesey voice, she noted how the lady had a dark room in an old air raid shelter.

She could see that it was possible to close the air vents, lock her in. Then, by pretending to be Lady Vesey's voice on the telephone, she got an innocent friend to drive over to the Vesey house. As he locked the door and closed the air raid shelter air vents he did not realise the woman was inside. For 20 minutes the ABC showed the nice entertainment of Lady Vesey slowly suffocating to death.

All done by push-button TV monitor, an absolute first for TV thrillers. Technology triumphs again.

Actually, at our place, we have three remote control monitors. There's the remote that controls the straight television. There's the remote that works the video, and incredibly, we have a remote for the compact disc player.

They all look alike, long thin black boxes covered in buttons. I suspect that the television invention in the entire 20th century, even ahead of the electric blanket, is the button that turns the whole thing off. There are two buttons, which gives you instant quiet and button that turns the whole thing off. There's no quibble, no beg your pardons. Instant obeyance. I just love the omnipotent power. Goodbye Daryl Somers. Be quiet Graham Kennedy. You're talking nonsense Derryn Hinch. ZAP!

Our problem is this. The buttons are so small and inevitably we are operating these things in the dark. Not only that, two times out of three we pick up the wrong monitor. We go to squash Derryn Hinch and we turn on the compact disc player. We try to kill poor Paul Cronin in the Mitre 10 ad and we put the video into fast forward.

We are watching our carefully prepared video of the Wimbledon final, Edberg has just won his serve, we try to squash a Toyota ad, press the wrong button and dammit we're back on regular TV with Mr Burke teaching us how to prune hydrangeas.

Our friends Hilton and Olivia are in an even worse situation. They have an extra remote for their radio and hi-fi, which makes four remotes altogether. Fred down the road has a remote for his garage doors and Lindsay even has one for the front gates.

Heavens knows how they get on. I wouldn't be surprised if they too have terrible trouble. Very likely, when Lindsay tries to kill Derryn, he opens the front gates, or when hopping from Brian Naylor to Jennifer Kyte he closes the garage doors.

Then there is another problem. In times of sheer desperation, the much required remote has slipped down under the sofa cushions. Sometimes it can take four days to find a lost remote.

There must be a simpler way. Yesterday in the mail we received one of these extraordinary fancy colour catalogues, offering every Universal Remote Control gadget you need. But one caught my eye, 'The Genius'. 'This will replace all the remote controls in your home or office,' it said. 'It will work an infra-red controlled audio visual device, television, video, cable TV, digital tapes, sound amplifiers, in fact up to eight separate components.'

This little black box costs $145, and complex? I noted that it had 68 buttons. You would be pushing the button to get Joan Sutherland on to a bit of the Mad Scene from Lucretia and lo, the microwave would start cooking breakfast.

No, I'm not going to buy. I think we will wait until they improve the technology. I would like a remote with a sound zapper which I could use in all directions. Screeching motor bikes. ZAP. The character that never stops talking at dinner parties. ZAP. ZAP. The rock'n'roll party next door. ZAP.

It could be used for even subtler purposes. Instead of long kicks under the dinner table, the re-activate button could go into action. It could be used to awaken one at dinner parties. ZAP. A remote would be good for letting the dog in and out the back door and useful for changing baby nappies at 2 a.m.

It would be wonderful to make use of the fast forward or fast reverse buttons. It would be nice, for example, to throw into fast reverse and go back to that beautiful time when you could take it for granted you could get a bank loan for around four per cent. I would like to return to the time when you were going to win Wimbledon and no doubt there are misguided creatures who would like to fastback to when an Australian won a Grand Final.

Yes, we could fastback to watch exactly how the young Don Bradman handled Larwood in full cry when bowling to those Bodyline series, or observe again the grace of Ray Lindwall in 1932-33. Or perhaps one could re-do Len Hutton. Or perhaps one could fastback when the sun was warm, when the surf never seemed to stop shining and one heard 'In the Mood' for the first time.

On the other hand I'd just love to push the fast forward button and see how those 1990 vintages are going to taste in 1992.
