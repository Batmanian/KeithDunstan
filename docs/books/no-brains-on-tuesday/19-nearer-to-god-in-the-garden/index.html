Nearer to God in the Garden?

4 min read.

First published 1991

A pot obsession sparked by Tom Paterson's anonymous call to Kevin Heinze's ABC gardening program blaming him for a household of 6000 pots, matched by Keith Dunstan's own horror stories of orchid rotation plans, lavender drying and seaweed expeditions to the beach.

Columns on Domestic Horticulture

At our place we are addicted to the ABC gardening program on Saturday mornings.

Last week I was listening to 'Tom' from a call-in program. He was blaming Kevin Heinze for his matrimonial difficulties. It went something like this.

'Years ago I married this charming, lovely girl. She was lovely but then she turned out to be a gardener. Now, as a result of your program I am married to a garden. We have 4000 pots outside. We have 2000 pots inside.

'When I go into the bathroom, there are pots in the bath. When I walk out the door I get cracked on the head by hanging baskets. When I open the door of the house. Pots! If I go down the side of the house, Pots! There isn't even room for a wheelbarrow. Pots! Aaaar, there isn't even room for a wheelbarrow. Pots!'

'We can't go away, can't leave the place for more than a day, because we have 6000 pots to feed and I blame it all on you.'

Mr Heinze was most tactful in dealing with Tom's matrimonial problem. He said Tom's wife could keep up her enthusiasm but she could give some of her pots away to charity.

'She does. She does,' replied Tom. 'But every time she gives away a pot she replaces it with another three. You've got to help me. I really need a helicopter now to get away from the place.'

Oh, I know exactly how Tom feels, but he didn't tell the half. Hanging baskets! We have a whole line of them on the veranda. Each hanging basket is worth another child, for they have to be fed and watered every day. As for banging your head, we even have one in the bathroom. Each time you get into the bath you bang your head.

We are devoted to orchids. They get moved all around the garden in a quest for the right degree of sunshine or shade. They are on a rotation plan. As soon as they look a bit tired inside, they get carted outside. Plants are like T.S. Eliot's cats — they want to be inside when they are outside and when they are outside they want to be inside.

There are the African violets. They want hardly any water, but we are inclined to drown them individually. Maiden hair ferns like to be almost hated and you have to keep them fresh and, Ye Gods! there are other plants that get their leaves polished. We polish more plants than the furniture. But we are not as bad as some people we know. We have some friends who are mad for the indoor garden. They went to do the watering and got into trouble for flooding on to the carpet.

The dying of lavender is 'in'. Pot pourri is 'in'. You dry off all the rose petals and make little scented bowls.

We are not allowed to throw away any kitchen scraps. Everything has to be put into the compost, and not a single leaf or twig may go to waste. Everything has to be recycled. In the old days you would have a nice big compost heap in the corner. All they had to do was throw it all in. But that's not good enough now. We have to make expeditions to the beach for seaweed.

And I sympathise with Tom over the watering. It never stops.

Yet there is always that touch of anxiety. We were at a dinner party where I met a man who set his wrist alarm for 9 p.m. Just as soon as it went off: 'Gawd, got to water the garden.' 'I couldn't make certain it was all working,' said he.

Oh, I tell you, it is better to live in a flat.

However, there is a sequel to the tale about the man on talk-back to the ABC. His voice was Scottish and definitely familiar. Yes, it was Tom Paterson, former chief of the Road Safety Council.

Someone tried to find out the result. 'Well,' he said, 'when it was on Nancy had a tremendous laugh. Some friends asked did we hear the gardening program. "Did you listen to it?" I said. "If only I'd got on to the end of it," she replied. "But there was some bloke belly aching. He sounded just like you."'
