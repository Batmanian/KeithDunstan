Please Can We Continue to Have Sex?

3 min read.

First published 1991

A defence of sex in advertising and art against the Commonwealth Style Manual's drive to eliminate gendered language and a minister's campaign against female exploitation, citing Michelangelo, Norman Lindsay and Rubens, and arguing that the joy of ice cream ads and soft drink commercials is all we have left.

You can see it all — there is a move at a high Government level to wipe out sex.

I present, as justification from the moving evidence, two news stories this past week.

1 The Commonwealth produced A Style Manual for Authors, Editors and Printers, which reworked the entire English language. We no longer have fishermen, we have fisher people; the ombudsman is an ombudsperson; Ye Gods, even manholes are out. Now they are access holes.

2 There is a formal complaint that females are being exploited as sex objects and there is to be a Minister on the Status of Women. Advertising has been found to exploit sex prominently in television commercials.

Against all these campaigns to keep sex out of advertising I am prepared to fight to the death.

Rubens would have had no time for joggers, weight watchers, or Pritikin. He would not have gone for those anorexic models who are so popular today. I have a Noel Counihan painting on my living room wall of a lovely picnic. Now that beats the hell out of mere abstract objects.

But look here, there are persons in Government who want to eradicate sex from advertising. If there is one creature I find appalling in my worst nightmares, it is advertising without sex.

I have always adored sex-mad artists and sculptors. One thinks of artists from Michelangelo through to Norman Lindsay who liked to paint and sculpt humans with no clothes on. I am passionate about Botticelli and Rubens females, with an irresistible lovely pink texture to human flesh.

I left school with the ability to measure a female with a slide rule, the ability to exchange with her a few phrases in Latin, and the ability to paint for her to sit on — but that was, to the best of my knowledge, the lot. I knew nothing of her function.

I smuggled a book on the subject. In my early journalism the subjects one could mention were: child bearing, parliamentary vilification, car accidents, murder, armed assault, assassination. But sex was a subject much avoided. The clubs I joined were male only. Women were kept out of hotel bars. I wondered wishfully what females were like.

Now, glory be, at last there is a possibility of finding out. Women are now being allowed into clubs and pubs, being allowed to use words like 'bastard'. Elysium seems to be at hand. And then they want to eradicate sex.

Thank God for the soft drink and liquor ads. I remember one ad: a young fellow in his apartment. He swings a wall which reveals, backlit, all those fascinating bottles of soft drink. You could see, immediately, she was his.

Hair ads are redolent with sex. There is something seductive almost every night. This poor girl has dandruff. Then she has this marvellous, enticing head of hair and is suddenly showing it around all over the place.

Ice cream ads also make you perspire. Have you noticed this girl who just moves her tongue in and out of her ice cream. Good Heavens!

That's the interesting thing about sex objects. No matter how long you spend on this earth you never lose your interest.
