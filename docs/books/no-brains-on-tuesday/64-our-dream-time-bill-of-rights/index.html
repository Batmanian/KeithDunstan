Our Dream Time Bill of Rights

5 min read.

First published 1991

A mock Bill of Rights for the truly important freedoms Canberra's actual debate keeps overlooking, from a cap on cricket replays and telephone hold music to an illuminated supermarket directory board and a curfew on sons borrowing the family car, capping off with a plea that politicians simply sound half civilised.

You will have noticed that among other sweet discussions going on in Canberra they have been talking about a Bill of Rights.

Indeed it has been going on for years; Labor, Liberal and Democrats can't agree on what our rights should be.

Sir Robert Menzies used to say our rights were designated under common law and there was no need to go further. Yet to my mind the present discussions have been absurd; they don't even come close. It is all very well to talk about freedom of speech, religion and that sort of thing. They should get down to even deeper, fundamental rights, protection from things that finally send us all out of our minds.

I would like to see a Bill of Rights which touches on domestic issues.

For example we would start with television. We would make it the right of human beings to have a look at no more than two repeats a day; not the current 26. We would make our right that we have to look at commercials only for the first eight or 10 minutes in the hour. We would make it the law in a number of countries so that you can make a nice clean break so that you can make a cup of coffee. It should also be a fundamental right that there be at least one watchable program on Friday and Saturday nights, to date. It has not happened. Why should they preserve the ultimate turgidity for the week-end?

What's more, we could include a rule that would return cricket telecasts to days of greater refinement. We would have cameras take pictures of events as they happen, not this constant turning back of the reel, giving us six replays every time a cricketer makes a decent shot.

And while we are thinking of advertising it should be the right of human beings that we don't have to look at advertising wherever we go. We will order there be no outside hoardings, no disfiguring ads on the exterior of every public building, no shrieking messages on every corner of sporting grounds.

Our Bill of Rights will give us freedom of our letter boxes. No longer will we be besieged by every store, hardware merchant, insurance salesman, or strange Gold Coast lotteries. This will leave space again for the Myer, David Jones and Bankcard bills.

Our Bill of Rights will place severe restrictions on the two stroke motor. Our ear drums will have sacred rights against persecution and torture via chain saws, motor cycles, and rock noises from car radios.

Our Bill of Rights will help suffering humans in supermarkets. No longer will unfortunate husbands and even wives have to wander for two hours trying to find the mustard, the Earl Grey Tea or the self-raising flour. Supermarkets like to keep you confused so that you will indulge in impulse buying and spend more. My Bill of Rights will insist that every supermarket have a large illuminated directory board showing precisely where you can find every item.

And have you noticed the spreading curse of providing sweet music on the telephone when they put you on hold? You ring Board of Works, Telecom, or one of these big companies. A recorded message tells you that you are going into a queue. Then they play musical gunk. The longer you wait the more maddening it becomes. If we have to wait alone with our thoughts, is that so awful? Oh yes, silence should be part of the Bill of Rights.

Then there would be rights for people eating in restaurants. You know how the French have an appellation controllee, where they have to state the origin of the wine. Under our bill, if the restaurant has had their fish or their chicken in an ice box for six months, and just whacked it into the microwave when they saw you coming through the door, then they will have to say so.

If they are not giving you real coffee but infamous powder made out of gunk then surely it is the right of all humans to know about it. This fact must be stated on the menu. And would it not be right that all wines on the list have a mark up of no more than 25 per cent on retail prices?

There should be a few human rights for parents. Daughters should vacate the bathroom after 15 minutes, hair washing should be restricted to 10. Sons should be required to do three hours a week gardening and when they borrow the family car it should be back by midnight.

The rights of minorities should be protected, particularly those not obsessed by football. No newspaper should contain more than 10 per cent football in any given day and football replays should be restricted to one hour on any one channel. A less kindly liberationist would ban them altogether.

Finally, do you agree it should be a right that politicians sound at least half civilised as if they were conducting an eternal bar-room brawl and act as if they were working for the good of the nation?
