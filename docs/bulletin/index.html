The Bulletin

Keith Dunstan wrote in The Bulletin from 1962 until 1984, initially under the psuedonym, 'Batman'.

"The Bulletin" magazine shaped how Australian identity, literature and public debate were understood and represented. Through early writers such as Henry Lawson and Banjo Paterson, it gave prominence to a distinctive national literary voice and helped popularise the bush myth: an influential but limited story of independence, mateship and life beyond the city.

Prior to its last edition in 2008, The Bulletin was also important as a record of the cultural values and social attitudes of its time, including the exclusions and assumptions that shaped public conversation. Its development from a politically charged publication into a modern news magazine showed how Australian media changed over time.
