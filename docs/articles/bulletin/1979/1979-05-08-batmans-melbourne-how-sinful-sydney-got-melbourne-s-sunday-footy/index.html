How sinful Sydney got Melbourne's Sunday footy

5 min read.

First published 08 May 1979

The VFL finds a creative way around the Victorian Government's Sunday football ban by staging free "Commodore Cup" reserves matches telecast on Channel Seven, while the VFA retaliates and the VFL books the Sydney Cricket Ground for two proper Sunday premiership matches.

AS WE told you last year, if Dick Hamer has done nothing else at least he has spared us the horror and violence of VFL football on Sundays. He has a slogan: "Hamer makes it happen." This is absolutely the finest of his non-happenings.

Hamer, like the rest of us, is proud of the superb, sepulchral calm of the Melburnian Sunday, but there is more to it than that. There are two football codes for Australian Rules in Melbourne. The big fellow, shall we say the Ford LTD, is the Victorian Football League, the tiny outfit, the Ford Escort or the Mini Minor, is the Victorian Football Association.

The VFA plays on Sundays and as soon as the VFL defiles the Sabbath also, then the VFA is finished. The battle this year is intense. We have a very lively magazine here called The Footballer, which is actually published by the VFL Players Association.

Just to show you how trendy this magazine is last month, the editor, Rod Galt, star ruckman for Carlton (VFL), decided to run a series of nude centrefolds of footballers, no less. The footballer is known as "The Mate of the Month." The first Mate was the Carlton half-forward, Mark McClure and he was shown nicely naked except that he was holding a football in a place where it is a good idea to hold a football.

Galt commented: "We decided to use the nude photographs because we have had many letters from girls wanting to see their favorite player without his football jumper."

There was a stir about this. Many critics thought the nude centrefold was bad for the young people who buy the magazine, so for the current magazine, we have a centrefold of the Collingwood half-forward, Rene Kink. He is an immensely muscular gentleman and we see him in the gym with his weight-lifting devices, but now our mate of the month is wearing football shorts.

But that's not all. If we turn to page 16 in the same magazine there is a cartoon. In a pen there is a revolting looking pig which is labelled "VFL." Outside the pen there is a pumpkin which is labelled VFA and the big fat VFL pig is thinking "Now that we've almost emptied our feed bin that pumpkin looks appetising!"

The cartoon is signed "Spook," Rod Galt's nickname. It has caused some rumblings, particularly in the halls of the VFL house, but as Mr Galt says, many of the VFL players do not want to play on Sunday, that's the one day they like to spend with their families.

The Victorian Government time after time has refused the VFL permission to play on Sunday. Last June the VFL tried once again. Could they play a Sunday match and give the entire proceeds to the appeal for the Commonwealth Games? The answer was "No."

This year the VFL tried another idea. It decided to play a reserves match every Sunday. It would be beautifully pure with no booze and nobody could stop it because admission would be free. You can see the merit of this, of course.

The VFL was crafty. It wouldn't be called a reserves match, it would be the Commodore Cup, in honor of the Commodore motor car, with sponsorship by General Motors for $75,000.

But here's the rub. The VFA has always had a nice outlet with one of their matches being telecast live on Sunday through Channel 0. So the VFL arranged for their Commodore Cup match to be telecast live on Channel Seven.

It would be an understatement to say that the VFA was angry. The VFA president, Alec Gillon, told the Herald: "It's nothing more than a lust for power and a greed for money ... It is disgusting the lengths the VFL will go to get into Sunday football. This is not even a clever or sneaky way of doing it. They are just making a blatant intrusion into our Sunday competition and we intend to safeguard our area in any way we can."

The first match was on Sunday April 7 and the angry VFA put forward their program two weeks so that they could meet the VFL head on. While the VFL Carlton and Geelong reserves were on Seven the VFA played a top first division match, Preston and Brunswick, on Channel 0.

The VFL billed their game as "the family fun spectacular." There was precision driving plus sky divers who sailed out high over the ground. Admission was free and attendance was not disclosed.

Meanwhile the VFA had their Sunday match at Preston. However, there were no precision cars, no sky divers, but they did have the Preston Municipal Band and an official attendance of 6200.

The Commodore Cup matches will continue on Sunday until the end of the season, but you may well ask what is the next step in this extraordinary Sabbatarian contest.

The VFL has booked the Sydney Cricket Ground for two Sunday matches this season. The matches will be North Melbourne-Hawthorn on June 10 and probably Fitzroy-Richmond on July 15. These will be fair dinkum premiership matches.

What's more they will be telecast back here live for Melbourne viewers. You can see the merit of this, of course. Our Sabbatarian sensibilities will not be upset. Our sense of sin will be nicely transferred to Sydney and all the dropping of litter, noise, car parking congestion and such will take place in Paddington.

And so the dead-quiet perfection of our Sunday will be as beautiful as ever with the bonus that if anyone wants the violence of VFL football it is there on the screen.

I think this idea has huge merit. It is the perfect solution for the Sunday squabble. If it doesn't work, well then, why not shift the VFL football even further, say, to Bangkok or Omsk.
