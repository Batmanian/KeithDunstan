A serious case of maladjustment

5 min read.

First published 13 Mar 1979

Dunstan offers himself as an image consultant to Prime Minister Malcolm Fraser, prescribing contact lenses, a moustache, gay shirts, a cook book, C. J. Dennis poetry readings at Moomba, and a daily bicycle ride to Parliament House.

YOU MUST have noticed that there is concern over the image of our Prime Minister. He is showing up as insufficiently jolly, friendly and lovable. Earlier Prime Ministers such as Stanley Bruce and R. G. Menzies rather enjoyed being detested, but apparently this outlook is no longer acceptable in the electronic 70s.

The ABC's Four Corners has even had a program on the subject. "What is wrong with the Prime Minister?" they asked.

The psychologist Ronald Conway, author of The Great Australian Stupor, said Fraser's image was "very stiff, shy, reserved and perhaps even a little priggish."

Michael Baume, Liberal MHR, said Fraser appeared on television as very severe and dour, which gave the false impression of a lack of concern.

Kevin Luscombe, a Melbourne marketing consultant, said: "You couldn't say the Prime Minister was a colorful person." And he suggested that the colors of Fraser's personality ranged from pale fawn to mid grey with splashes of railway brown.

And John Temple said, no indeed, he did not win any critics' award as one of the boys.

I do not know who actually is handling the Prime Minister's image at this minute, whether it is John Singleton, Eric White, Laurie Kerr or even Phillip Adams, but if further assistance is needed naturally I am available.

First I would study the behaviour of some of our more popular leaders such as Sir Henry Bolte, Neville Wran, and of course, the recently retired Don Dunstan.

For a start let us take that formidable chairman-of-the-board, headmaster-on-speech-day appearance, that Fraser always gives on TV. He must get rid of those ponderous glasses. Contact lenses are the thing for a PM of the 80s.

His hair would look conservative even in the police force. It should be medium-long over the ears. Take that fashion plate John Halfpenny, he knows precisely the length which is appropriately trendy.

Then a moustache is really compulsory. I would grow it to the length as prescribed by Messrs Newcombe, Yallop and Max Walker.

Fraser has to be more avant garde in his dress, with gay shirts and even shorts in the House on hot days. If he keeps his eye on what is doing in South Yarra he would note that the smart young men are wearing open-necked, pale linen shirts with matching trousers.

Now what about his personality? Don Dunstan did very well with his cook book. I don't think a Malcolm Fraser Cook Book would do any harm — those favourite little dishes he bakes for Tammie on Friday nights.

Nor would there be any objection to poetry readings. The ideal would be a little C. J. Dennis narrated at the Myer Music Bowl during Moomba. I would suggest for a start The Play from The Sentimental Bloke: ... "Doreen an' me, we bin to see a show — The swell two-dollar touch. Bong tong, yeh know ... the drarmer's writ be Shakespeare, years ago, about a barmy goat called 'Romeo.'"

The sport, Life. Be In It thing is very good. First up as PM Harold Holt had himself photographed in a sensational picture with his three near-naked daughters-in-law.

John Gorton had himself photographed, playing tennis, swimming and indulging in derring-do with a surf life-boat crew. Bob Hawke gets himself into a cricket picture on every conceivable occasion.

Chairman Mao improved his image by swimming three miles down the Yangtse. I would like to see Malcolm doing all these things and a three-mile swim down the Yarra would do no harm.

Lord Mayor Irvin Rockman likes to get himself caught in jogging gear and track suits. A shot of the PM panting down Alexandra Avenue with sweating security men bringing up the rear would be a great idea.

The concept of the Laird of Nareen, the Western District squire, is no help at all. Henry Bolte also had his property, but always he managed to give the impression of a struggling selector. If any shots were taken at his place, there he was sweating at the wheel of a tractor, indulging in crutching or something infinitely humble.

Malcolm, though, likes to show off his motor bikes and his passion for cars both vintage and continental. We have the impression that he owns a fleet of Lancias.

Recently we had a photograph of the Squire beside his superb vintage sports Lancia, which we were told had been expensively restored even to the tune of 22 coats of paint. Neville Wran was smarter — he had himself pictured on a bicycle. I would recommend that the PM pedal daily from the Lodge to Parliament House. This would be a vote-winner and there is an excellent precedent for it.

Back in Edwardian days when Melbourne was the national capital, Prime Minister Alfred Deakin used to bicycle from his house in South Yarra to Parliament House.

Fraser has to prove that he has a sense of humor. Glory be, even Prince Charles is aware of that. According to The Australian, Charles was so amused by the Pickering calendar featuring the Royal piece that he asked for the original drawing.

Well, Malcolm Fraser in the Pickering calendar was even more remarkable. As honorary consultant I would advise the PM to ask for that original drawing, have it framed, and place it in some suitable spot in the Lodge.

It might take a year, but I'm sure I could push up his popularity rating at least 20 percent.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: A serious case of maladjustment, The Bulletin, 13 March 1979. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
