Don't move the grand final to Swan Lake

5 min read.

First published 04 Oct 1983

Keith Dunstan argues passionately against the VFL's plan to shift the grand final from the Melbourne Cricket Ground to VFL Park at Waverley, warning that the MCG is woven into Melbourne's soul and that the league is on the verge of a self-immolating decision.

MELBOURNE is hard to bear during grand final week. Like somebody with a psychotic illness, it becomes totally introverted without a care or a whimper for anyone outside its borders.

The newspapers are unconcerned for death in the Lebanon, mayhem in Montilla or agony in Chad. The Age, The Herald and The Sun are equally demented. The Sun, after the choosing of the Brownlow Medal winner, devoted all page one, all the back page and part of page two to the selection of the fairest and best VFL player.

The Herald on Monday, when the whole damn thing was over, ran a full colour supplement recording all the matches, the entire year's statistics and interviews with all the coaches.

Channel Seven, which throbs eternally to the sound of bouncing balls and hoopla, had a final eve footy marathon. Honestly, they had a program which began at 12.05am and the devoted fan, no doubt with club scarf around his neck and tinnie in his hand, could watch the game for eight solid hours until it was time to march to the arches at the MCG.

Is there any escape from all this? On grand final afternoon the Anti Football League made a special offer. Those who could stand it no longer could take refuge at a special matinee performance of West Side Story at the Princess Theatre.

It wasn't a complete success. Even members of the Anti Football League couldn't stand the strain. I noticed one man who sneaked out to the lavatory to get the progress of the game on his trannie.

Having established a case that I am not concerned vitally about the outcome of the grand final, could I pass comment that the Victorian Football League is on the verge of making a sad and almost certainly self-immolating decision? Last Saturday, we were told, was the last grand final at the Melbourne Cricket Ground.

The 1984 grand final will be at VFL Park, Waverley. What sort of craziness is this? It's like Nero announcing that no longer will he have his Christians eaten at the Colosseum in Rome. He will have everyone get into their chariots and go to some lesser, swamp-laden arena six leagues out of town.

There are good historic reasons for all this. Cricket has dominated the sporting arena. There is a fair bit of jealousy, there has been arrogance on both sides but there is one thing the VFL has forgotten or doesn't understand. The soul of Melbourne is tied up in a neat triangle with the Carlton Brewery at one end of Swanston Street, the Shrine at the other and, down the axis along Flinders Street and Wellington Parade, is the MCG.

I don't think any major stadium in the world is so close to a city's heart with such superb public transport — tram, bus and train. It has been there since 1853 and even in 1861, when H. H. Stephenson's touring XI arrived, it was the best cricket ground in the world.

But the VFL should be reminded that it is not just the home of cricket, it is everything in Melbourne. Dammit, the Melbourne Cricket Club invented their game of Australian Rules football. This is where it all began.

There is something unique, something special about the noise that rises from the MCG. When the crowd becomes excited the cheers, even the oohs and aahs, rise and fall like some enormous pipe organ. Or how about the awesome sound when 121,696 show their appreciation of some act of perfidy such as the grand final of 1970, when Carlton beat Collingwood.

VFL Park has a capacity of 76,000. The crowds there during the finals were desperately disappointing. For North Melbourne versus Hawthorn, the crowd of 41,063 was the smallest for a semi-final since 1944. Meanwhile 81,090 turned up at the MCG for the clash between Essendon and Fitzroy.

There were 63,785 at the preliminary final between Essendon and North Melbourne. But had the game been at the MCG there would have been 100,000.

All this season VFL Park was a swamp. There even has been a suggestion that the Sydney Swans should be given VFL Park as their permanent home, with the name Swan Lake.

The VFL claims that by increasing the capacity of their ground to 110,000 they will make an extra $5 million from VFL Park. However it could also be argued that the entire league has been impoverished by the establishment costs of their Swan Lake.

The Herald took a poll and they claim four out of five players prefer the MCG to VFL Park. I reckon in a poll of the citizens of Melbourne the score would be more like 99 out of 100.

Gentlemen leaders of the VFL, think now. Don't do it.
