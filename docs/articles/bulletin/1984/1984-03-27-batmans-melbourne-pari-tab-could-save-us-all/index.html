Pari Tab could save us all

6 min read.

First published 27 Mar 1984

Frank Hardy laments Australia's guilt-ridden TAB with no lavatories or cafe comforts, while Victoria prepares to introduce Pub TAB and Footy TAB — inspiring Keith Dunstan to propose Parl TAB (bet on ministerial resignations and royal commissions), Crick TAB, and Train TAB to get John Cain's Government out of the red.

FRANK HARDY WAS musing last week over the miserable Presbyterian qualities of our TAB. He said we had never managed to sink our feelings of guilt over gambling.

He said the civilised Parisians not only had a marvellous racing paper which was published every day of the year but also never let a day go past without a race meeting. They had races even on Good Friday and Christmas Day.

The equivalent of the TAB in Paris was the PMU, Hardy said. The PMU was a cosy, friendly place. You could get coffee or a meal. It was a proper cafe in which you could linger, chat about form and enjoy life.

But our TAB was designed on the basis that gambling was a sin. TAB agencies were as clinical as operating theatres — no seats, no creature comforts. "You can't even go to the lavatory at the TAB," said Hardy. "It is designed this way because you are not supposed to loiter."

Of course, Hardy is an old Carringbush man. He lived over the road from John Wren's famous Collingwood Tote, which ostensibly was a tea shop at 136 Johnston St. But before World War I it was one of the marvels of our time. Wren owned not only No 136 but also adjoining houses and the tote had an ingenious system of secret passages and escape routes. At its prime more than 1500 people an hour went through.

Well, it is fascinating to report that at last we are going to overcome our sense of sin. Our Minister for Youth, Sport and Recreation, Neil Trezise, has announced that discussions are in full cry for Pub TAB in Victoria. The plan is to follow the lead of NSW, South Australia and Tasmania. If all goes well we could have legal betting in pubs by the spring racing season.

Trezise is hoping that the government will get a share of the immense SP betting market which, through a deeply-felt folk tradition, always has operated tax free at the corner pub.

But that's not all. Clearly we don't have to stick to horses. We will be able to bet on humans as well. Again Trezise — who has this curious portfolio of Youth, Sport and Recreation; surely Minister for Gambling and Quick Revenue would be a better title — has announced that, come the imminent footy season, we will have Footy TAB.

We did have a system called Footy Trifecta. However it was a failure. Footy ball followers did not bet with sufficient devotion and turnover sank to as slow as $15,000 a week. With Footy Trifecta the football enthusiast had to pick the three highest scores.

In the new Footy TAB the punter will be able to select teams to win each of the six games by a certain number of points. What's more, the punter will be able to treat footballers almost like horses because there will be a footy quadrella. Punters will be asked to pick four of the six weekly winners.

For years the racing people have been trying to gain a beautiful communion with football. Last year the Moonee Valley Racing Club had a Grand Final Day meeting on September 24. They provided TV screens so that patrons could watch the big game and it was their dream to have the bookies providing a special Grand Final service. They wanted to give the odds before the match started and have them fluctuating as it proceeded.

Alas, it wasn't to be. It would have required a special amendment to the Lotteries, Gaming and Betting Act.

Naturally this season we will expect both the racing and footy writers to give us their Footy Tab Guide on Form. For example, we will want to know the state of the track at Victoria Park and the MCG, the times for the early morning gallops at Essendon, whether the Fitzroy players are cantering well and who is carrying top weight at Geelong.

I expect, too, that racing writers will want to see closer attention paid to the breeding of footballers. They will want to see bloodlines published and which great sires from various clubs are standing at stud. They will expect also to have the equivalent of yearling sales. I don't know what would be the best age for auctioning off potential good young footballing stock but I imagine 14 or 15 years would be ideal.

The Victorian Government has great hopes of gaining splendid profits both from Pub TAB and Footy TAB. The operators of the TAB are aware that their costly computerised service is working under capacity. We are simply not betting hard enough.

If Footy TAB does go well there is no reason why the government should not open up an entire new field of exciting and interesting bets as the Brits do.

We could bet on parliamentary tapes. Imagine how fascinating it would be to be able to wager on who is likely to be named in the House on any given day. All of us would want to bet on the outcome of the NSW elections; how richly rewarding it would be to have Parl TAB.

We could bet, say, on the number of inquiries and royal commissions likely to take place during 1984 coupled with the charming double, the number of ministers who would be forced to resign. We could have bets on censure motions and, if you particularly like betting on humans, how about bets on the Australian Democrats?

Betting on Senator Don Chipp alone would be an intriguing exercise. How could you predict which way he would turn on any given issue?

Then in Parl TAB imagine the beaut odds you could get picking the date that Bob Hawke is going to set for the next Federal election.

Crick TAB would be a natural and we could be having it now on the series in the West Indies. We could be betting on the number of ducks likely to be made by Kim Hughes, the number of catches dropped or the first Australians likely to make Test centuries.

We could have Train TAB, betting on the number of trains which would arrive on time at Flinders St on any given day. This would not only serve to focus interest on public transport but also would be a fine revenue producer.

The ways of exploiting the facilities of the TAB are endless. If only the agencies were made more charming with lavatories, cafe comforts, Sid Nolan and John Brack prints on the walls, I am sure we could get John Cain's Government out of the red.
