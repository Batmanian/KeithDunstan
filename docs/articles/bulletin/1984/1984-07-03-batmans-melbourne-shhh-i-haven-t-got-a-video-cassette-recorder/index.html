Shhh! I haven't got a video cassette recorder

6 min read.

First published 03 Jul 1984

Keith Dunstan confesses to being possibly the only person in his street without a video cassette recorder — his wife forbids it, he could not work one anyway, and movie reviewers who send him preview tapes are incredulous. Meanwhile the local petrol station, chemist and Seven-Eleven deli all hire out videos, Victoria has just legalised X-rated tapes, and a friend with the full setup dims the living room after dinner for his private blue-movie screenings.

IT IS BECOMING a matter of deep humiliation that we don't have a video cassette recorder.

I think we are the only people in our street who do not own a video. We have already been advised in the newspapers that the video is the greatest boom consumer product since the wireless and soon 50 percent of families in Victoria will own one.

There are at least 12 videos between here and the corner and four of my friends have had their videos stolen. One friend has had his video stolen three times. Can you imagine how left out a man feels at parties when he can't even mention that he has a video to be thieved. "Was yours a Sanyo?" "No mine was a Hitachi."

The stealing of videos is never out of the news. Three weeks ago we had the heartbreaking tale of the young couple who had just returned from their honeymoon in Bali. Denis slipped out to buy a video cleaning head when a thief moved in and stole his National VHS plus the tape of their wedding. Now they are appealing to the compassion of the thief to give back their wedding tape.

One of the motion picture houses called up last week and invited me to a preview of the newest piece of horror from Los Angeles.

"Sorry. No, I can't get to your theatrette at 5pm. I'll still be working."

"That's all right. We'll send you the video cassette. We're doing that now for all the movie reviewers."

"No point in doing that for me. I don't own a video."

"You're kidding?"

"No, honestly, I don't."

And that's the way it is. It is not entirely the economics of the thing. Videos are coming down in price at a furious pace. Brashs have been running this ad: "Brashs have smashed the video price barrier. For the first time a video recorder under $400. A brand new, fully guaranteed Toshiba. Buy now, pay nothing until after the Olympics."

It wasn't much under $400 — $399 to be exact — but it's just a matter of time before they give away videos with subscriptions to Readers Digest or the chemist will give you one when you get a film developed.

My wife has laid down the law. "No, we're not getting a video. We watch too much damn TV already. If we got a video we'll want to tape episodes of Carson's Law, Jewel in the Crown, tennis matches that we've missed then, even worse, we'll bring home awful tapes from the video library."

Actually I'm not sure that I would work it. My old mate the artist succumbed to a video the week before last. His son and girlfriend were desperate to see Giant, a vintage 1956 movie. You must appreciate there is a curious mystic James Dean-was-the-greatest-ever cult.

I ask you to believe this awful James Dean, Rock Hudson, Rod Taylor, Carroll Baker movie ran for three hours 50 minutes on Channel 10.

Well, son and girlfriend were going to an unmissable Saturday night party. The artist said generously, "I'll tape it for you." He reported it was hell, the movie couldn't have been worse. He started taping at 8.30pm and didn't finish until 12.30am. The poor coot sat there with his monitor editing out at the underarm de-odorant ads plus the Tattslotto results.

The kids came home joyously at 12.30 and he announced triumphantly: "I'll give you Giant right now."

Do you know what happened? Channel numbers are different on the video. Instead of Giant on Channel 10, he had mucked things and taped Daryl Somers and Ossie Ostrich with Hey, Hey It's Saturday Night, Channel 9.

How much longer can we hold out? I tell you it's like the Battle of the Bulge; we are practically encircled. The local petrol station is now selling bundles of firewood, briquettes and he's hiring out video tapes. The local chemist is selling toys, shirts, digital watches, cameras and hiring video tapes.

Then there's the Seven-Eleven deli. He doesn't close at 11, he goes 24 hours, seven days a week and he's into video tapes. Hells bells, apparently you can hip out for a quick fix of Jaws or a Chainsaw Massacre at four in the morning.

Of course, blue movies are the latest thing. On May 2 it became legal in Victoria to sell or rent X-rated video tapes. Under an X-rating, bestiality, excessive violence, child pornography and acts of terrorism are banned but practically everything else that moves or wobbles is OK.

It's a bit tricky though at the garage or the Seven-Eleven. Those in the X-file business cannot put their wares on public show; they must be under the counter or in a separate room. I called into a video shop in Lonsdale St. The window display was all horror. The Tide of Terror That Shook America said one poster and they had a collection of dummy spiders hanging here and there. Down the end of the shop was The Red Room. It had a handsome closed red door and behind this they kept the super steam. Anyone over 18 was invited to enter.

The Red Room was decorated with posters of beaut breasted bare young beauties and all the video tapes were waiting for hire. There were titles such as My First Time, Sexual Madness, Intimate Illusions, Portrait of Seduction and Liquid Lips.

We have one friend who now has the full bit. He has his hi-fi all laid out like the control room at the State Electricity Commission, he has his video and he has a wonderful big screen TV all recessed into the wall. He has his own personal library of blue movies. As soon as dinner is over the living room is slowly dimmed just like your Hoyts suburban theatre and on comes the blue movies.

Damned disturbing. In the old days it was easy to go to sleep when your friends showed you the slides they took at Noosa or the Greek islands. But a blue movie keeps me surprisingly awake.

When I first started in journalism we had an old leader writer. Very elegant he was. He wore stiff collars and always carried a cane. He swore he'd never buy a damned wireless. Time wasting piece of nonsense which stopped sensible people from reading books. He was the last of the non wireless buyers.

I am trying valiantly to do the same with the video.
