Getting our government on the ball

6 min read.

First published 28 Feb 1984

Australia is turning into a sportocracy, VFL champion Kevin Bartlett is crowned King of Moomba, footballers seek parliamentary pre-selection, Robert de Castella wins Australian of the Year, and Bob Hawke rides his sporting connections to a record 73 percent approval rating. Keith Dunstan proposes a fully sporting cabinet led by Alan Bond.

THERE IS irrefutable evidence at hand that Australia is poised close to becoming a sportocracy.

Champion Richmond footballer named Kevin Bartlett is Melbourne's choice to be King of Moomba. It is not the first time we have had a footballer as king. Lou Richards, former Collingwood champion, did the job some years back.

The crowning of Mr Bartlett was a deeply emotive occasion. He has always been known as "Hungry" because of a deep, unsatisfied hunger for footballs and he became King on the grounds that he is the only man to have played 400 VFL games.

He started making royal decrees immediately.

Cliff Young would become chairman of Dunlop and all Victorians would wear gumboots for the full span of Moomba. The Prime Minister, Bob Hawke, would be banned from attending any sports function for a year. What's more, Bartlett ordered that Premier John Cain and the VFL president, Dr Allen Aylett, should swap jobs.

Almost everywhere that sporting people are on the move, it is recognised.

The Sunday papers recently had the front page news that members of the Liberal Party had asked Leigh Matthews to stand for the State seat of Hawthorn. Matthews, a champion rover, captain of Hawthorn, unquestionably is the most important man in the electorate. He is known everywhere as "Lethal Leigh," a title which will give him a commendable start when he becomes a politician.

Ah, but he is not the only one. Percy Jones, the former Carlton star, has announced that he is seeking pre-selection for the seat of Melbourne on behalf of the Liberal Party. Labor romped in there last time with a very large majority but Mr Jones' background should cause a splendid Liberal breakback. After all, he did play in four premiership sides for Carlton.

Political moves on behalf of sporting people are taking place everywhere.

Just possibly, you might have noticed that Greg Chappell has retired from international cricket. The significance of the event was not unnoticed by the Queensland Premier, Joh Bjelke-Petersen. At once, he stated that the Nationals would be keen to have Chappell. A choice appeared to be open. Immediately, he could become a National MP or perhaps he would prefer to run for Mayor of Brisbane.

One would advise any mother, if she wishes her child to succeed in this country, not to worry about absurd academic qualifications — get him into sport. This has been recognised officially.

Who was the Australian of the Year? Not a politician, not a scientist, not the clever manufacturer who found the most jobs for the unemployed during 1983, not a brilliant inventor who found ways of increasing our exports but Robert de Castella, the champion marathon runner. Perhaps it was a toss-up between him and another fellow who runs a very long way, Cliff Young.

The orgy goes further. John Bertrand and his crew of Australia II received honors on Australia Day and Mr Bertrand has become a symbol, like Lorenzo de Medici, whipping us on to a new cultural renaissance. Whenever there is a crisis, Bertrand is invoked to inspire us in a patriotic fervor to victory.

It was Bertrand who was called in during the Davis Cup tennis Challenge Round against Sweden. He spoke to the players and the memory of the triumphant Australia II spurred them to battle.

In moments of peril at the Melbourne Cricket Ground when all seemed lost against the West Indies, cricket captain Kim Hughes reminded his men of Bertrand and Australia II and we managed a tie against the seemingly invincible men from the Caribbean.

Nor has the significance of all this escaped our Prime Minister. Hardly a sporting event has he missed.

He went to Newport, he was with Bertrand on his birthday, went to the Davis Cup Challenge Round and — cleverest of all — revived the Prime Minister's XI, not seen since the days of R. G. Menzies, and staged a match in Canberra against the West Indies. Australians appreciated Mr Hawke's political perspicacity in doing this and immediately he hit a record 73 percent popularity rating on The Bulletin's Morgan Gallup Poll. One trusts that he will not let up for the rest of the year and one may be sure that, despite King Kevin's edict, the Prime Minister will have a heavy schedule taking in football matches — both VFL and rugby league — and it will be politically vital that he be with our team at the Olympic Games in Los Angeles. It is only when he gets on to difficult topics such as automobile production and nuclear energy that his fortunes seem to sag.

The time is perfect for some mastermind to form a political party and shape the true sportocracy.

I think I would form the party around Alan Bond and he would become the first sporting prime minister, his candidates sweeping the field in every electorate.

As Federal Treasurer, I would suggest Greg Norman. He has shown extraordinary skills at making vast sums of money. He has a close association with the Americans, the fountain from which all culture and economics appear to emanate.

Minister for Trade? The choice is obvious: Greg Chappell, with his devotion to Benson & Hedges, his constant appearances in TV commercials and his keen appreciation of real estate, would be the ideal man.

Minister for Defence: Rodney Marsh. His popularity is unquestioned. He has just retired in one of the greatest mass loving orgies the game of cricket has seen. And he is the greatest defender the world has known.

Minister for Health: ex-footballer Dr Aylett. Actually, he is a dentist. But it is important to have a doctor in the role.

The minister for Foreign Affairs is a tricky one but I am inclined to go for John Newcombe. He looks the part with his large moustache and his international experience is remarkable. He has performed in almost every part of the world except Outer Mongolia.

To have a lady in cabinet is compulsory so I am going for Evonne Goolagong as minister for Immigration and Ethnic Affairs.

The Attorney-General would have to be the Australian Cricket Board chairman, Fred Bennett. Nobody could equal the legal experience he has gained over the past few weeks.

The minister for the Navy will be John Bertrand. Cliff Young will be wonderful as minister for Communications and I have a noble choice as minister for Employment and Industrial Relations, Dennis Lillee. He has been enormously popular and would handle the portfolio with brilliance!

Kevin Bartlett is the oldest footballer. I would like him to be the minister for Veterans' Affairs and we could put Alan Jones, the racing driver, at Transport.

I haven't filled all the portfolios but I would like to keep some for good candidates likely to prove their worth at the Olympics.

Other worthy people might have wanted me to look for an artocracy. But that's very difficult. Our renaissance despises artists. You will have noticed that the Throsby committee found that 30,000 of our artists struggled to make an average of $10,000 a year.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Getting our government on the ball, The Bulletin, 28 February 1984. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
