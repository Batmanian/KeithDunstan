As torpid as Gomorrah on a rainy Sunday

6 min read.

First published 13 Mar 1984

Germaine Greer attacks Melbourne as dull and boring, but Keith Dunstan mourns that the city's magnificent old torpor is being steadily destroyed by Sunday trading, hotel bars, one-day cricket, Collins Street towers, and a Truth newspaper full of explicit sex advertisements that would have shocked Gomorrah itself.

OUR LEADING publicist, Miss Germaine Greer, has been on the job again. She says nothing ever happens in Melbourne. A wonderful torpor pervades us all. What's more she accuses Melbourne of being dull and boring.

Of course we are dull and boring. It's our gift. We are torpor specialists. Any town can be electric, exciting, but we have always been the masters of the dull. Tokyo, West Berlin and particularly Beirut are loaded with excitement.

Melbourne for many years was so sepulchral on Sundays it became a tourist attraction. Feature writers from the New York Times, Le Figaro, Time, Newsweek and London's Daily Mirror came to look at the wonder of our torpor.

But I'm deathly worried. I think it is all beginning to break down. Take the Melbourne Sunday: remember when Brian Fitzpatrick described it as a work of art, so perfect you could compare it only with The Winged Victory of Samothrace in the Louvre.

And it was beautiful. No trams, no trains, no sport, no movies. There was a law against Sunday newspapers because our wise legislators feared newsboys would call out horrid headlines within earshot of churches. The only place that actually opened was the zoo.

Now it is appalling. The Winged Victory has crumbled to tiny fragments. All kinds of sport and vulgar commerce take place in the suburbs and we have had even the Myer Emporium opening its doors.

But worse things have happened. Since January, Melbourne's hotels have been allowed to open on Sundays and you'll be worried to hear that Young & Jackson's right opposite St Paul's Cathedral is indulging in bar trade. Fortunately, our legislators acknowledge still that drinking is a sin and they make the whole operation as difficult as possible. Hotels can open from noon to 2pm and from 4pm to 6pm.

So the hotel keeper who wants to bring on staff has to call them aboard for two hours at a time. Meanwhile you can feed the swans in the botanic gardens or go to the zoo until 4pm.

We also have a World Trade Centre which has two restaurants that open on Sundays. This again has ruined things because now some days movie-makers used to look to us for their wonderful end-of-the-world scenes. It was possible to get shots of Swanston Street on a Sunday that looked more desolate than it is on Friday.

The latest Sunday pastime is for boat owners to run down the Yarra and park their boats at the World Trade Centre and have booze and caviar at the two restaurants. One is called the Rubbity and the other is Squizzy's.

In Melbourne we always like to immortalise our most famous robbers-killers. We have named everything in sight in honor of Ned Kelly and now we are showing our affection for Squizzy Taylor, the standover gunman of the 1920s.

Perhaps future generations will be more up to date and we will see restaurants named after the heroes of the computer age, those who have been involved in bottom-of-the-harbor schemes with $19 million.

The nicest and best quality torpor always was found at the cricket. Our cricket was an event where you almost could guarantee that nothing would happen. Where else could you have a sporting event which lasted for five days and usually still was unresolved at the end of the fifth?

Now we have one-day cricket played in comic opera clothes, so exciting that many of the patrons even forget to get drunk. In the finals against the West Indies not only did we have a crowd of 42,000 but there was a tie, producing sport so tense it was almost too hard to endure.

All this sort of thing is terrible for our reputation. Miss Greer was gracious enough to say that if the people of Melbourne had any real energy they would have rebuilt their city four times over by now. I know what she's thinking of. There was a lovely period between 1890 and 1950 when not a building went up in the City of Melbourne. Bruce Beresford used to say it was the most gorgeous 19th century city on Earth. He could move his cameras in, make vintage movies and not have to change a thing.

Collins Street had such a gracious air of torpor we used to refer to it as the Paris end. Good God! Four times? We have rebuilt it six times. Now it looks more like the Chicago or Dallas end. We have all those towers with the lovely reflecting windows that look like President Marcos's sunglasses. We preserve a front here and there like the Rialto. But there's nothing at all behind. Those fronts are just like a J. C. Williamson set for Maid of the Mountains.

And remember the deeply satisfying dullness of St Kilda Road? It used to be all residential houses — little Greek villas, fussy bits of Victoriana with cute towers and gazebos so that you could see the bay. It's awful now. Not a domestic building left ... all offices and "luxury prestige" apartments. Some look like aircraft carriers and some vast, white and glassy, a sort of imitation of The Love Boat.

Our sex also was a work of art. We didn't tolerate naked people. Put a Norman Lindsay in your shop window and you'd have the vice squad around as fast as you could shake an athletic support. Copulation, if it ever happened (and mercifully it didn't very often), was called an "indecency." There were ladies who peeped out of windows in Little Lonsdale Street but heaven knows what went on in there.

What takes place now would frighten even Gomorrah. Even in Las Vegas you have to drive 100km out of town to do what is freely available in Melbourne. Truth newspaper, which so generously gives up its pages to sexual matters, last week ran advertisements for more than 190 different establishments. For example, there is The Madame Dungeons which offer "leather, vibrator, French, Spanish, Greek, Golden Showers, Schoolgirl, Schoolmistress, French Maid, Nurse, Cross-Dressing, Baby Fantasy, Private Strips, Light or Heavy, all fetishes ... ample parking 10am to 4am."

See what I mean? It is marvellous how faithful Germs remains to her home town, always giving us wonderful publicity.

But I'm afraid I have to give her the bad news: the city has been ruined. Decent, wholesome torpor is now very hard to find.
