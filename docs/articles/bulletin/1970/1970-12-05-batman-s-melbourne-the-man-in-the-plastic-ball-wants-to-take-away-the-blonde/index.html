The Man in the Plastic Ball Wants to Take Away the Blonde

4 min read.

First published 05 Dec 1970

The opening of Neptune's Fish Bowl, a fish-and-chip shop on Toorak Road surmounted by a 20-foot fibreglass ball lit to suggest the sea, features a mermaid whose fish scales fall off during the ceremony and a Neptune whose goldfish nearly expires in the heat, raising the question of whether Melbourne's beloved Greek flake-and-chips culture can survive the arrival of upmarket 'take-away seafood.'

IT IS NOT generally realised, but our beloved Greek fish-and-chop shop is in danger. You know the place, of course. The shop is glass-fronted. There is lino on the floor and behind the counter there are cauldrons of boiling oil, one for the fish and one for the chips. Your orders sink in baskets into the much-prized oil, the aroma of cooking oil wafts up and down the street.

As you know, fish-and-chip shops are popular here because Melburnians have a special peculiarity. We are shark-eaters. Admittedly we call the shark 'flake,' but there is a point which is never made after shark tragedies — for every human eaten by a shark, humans eat at least ten million sharks.

Some months ago, Allen Aylett, famous ex-footballer and dentist of this town, opened a new-style fish-and-chip shop on the Esplanade in St. Kilda. It was air-conditioned. There was wall-to-wall carpet and, glory be, piped music. The fish was cooked in a high-speed electronic kitchen, and instead of receiving your fish and chips in utterly correct newspaper wrapping it came in a neat carton, paper napkin and all. Flake was not even mentioned.

Now it emerges that Mr. Frank Shelmerdine, formerly of the Top of the Town restaurant — the man who received a lease to build and operate a restaurant in the Royal Botanic Gardens — went into partnership with Peter Shelmerdine for their fish shop. One wondered whether Robin Boyd — had ever been asked to design a fish-and-chip shop.

The site was Toorak Road, South Yarra. The design was a building surmounted by a 20-foot-diameter plastic ball. It was made of curved triangular fibreglass panels. It caused great interest architecturally. A self-supporting 20-foot plastic ball was unique. So Mr. Boyd built his fish house to be called 'Neptune's Fish Bowl.' The ball was blue-green to represent the sea. At night it glowed magnificently in Toorak Road. What's more, he installed special lights to give the impression of swirling water inside.

As you can imagine, there had to be a grand opening. Messrs. Frank and Peter Shelmerdine contemplated various ideas: a fishing boat in the Yarra with fish brought straight to the Fish Bowl by some extraordinary conveyance, a camel, an elephant, a Moomba float procession down the street. Perhaps the Council would co-operate. Finally, they settled for a minor procession with flat-bed trucks sporting a jazz band, aqualung divers, King Neptune, and a mermaid.

One of the real developments was that one of them was former Hawthorn footballing champion Brendan Edwards. It's amazing how footballers in Melbourne dominate so many commercial ventures.

The mermaid, Miss Barbara Challinor, wore a fish tail from the waist down and nothing at all up aloft except for some little fish scales on prominent points. As Mr. Edwards carried her into the Bowl one of the fish scales dropped off and Miss Mermaid anxiously clutched her left breast.

Mr. Fred de Meyer, alias Fred Asmussen, the famous display man at Myers, was in trouble. He lost his moustache. However, he was not as embarrassed as Miss Mermaid.

Inside a tent in the parking lot, Toorak and South Yarra ladies hovered around gifts of live goldfish in bags of water. The temperature outside was around 82 degrees. It was much warmer inside. Miss Neptune, with her legs in a plastic bag, was almost expiring with the heat. She began to tip the fish bag and tip herself out. She divided the water about 50-50 between her and the fish. A fair arrangement for mutual survival.

'I feel like a fish out of water. With this thing around my legs and with this long body I can't walk unless somebody carries me,' she said. A character in the audience then pointed at the bottle of Skipping Girl vinegar and said: 'I'll take the blonde.'

The new Fish Bowl, you will be pleased to hear, didn't entirely drop the newspaper. It was printed on the outside of the cartons. This, one felt, was a sop to tradition and an insult to the 'Age.' But then as a wall covering newspaper is well known for its correctness. It is the 'News of the World' that is relatively ageless and makes much better reading when spread out fat-stained on the kitchen table.

Messrs. Frank and Shelmerdine plan to open at least five fish bowls, featuring batterless, exotic fish recipes.

'Where's the flake?' one asked. Mr. Shelmerdine replied that although flake was a very good fish he felt it gave the wrong image.

I was shocked. No flake. It was as bad as asking us to give up football.
