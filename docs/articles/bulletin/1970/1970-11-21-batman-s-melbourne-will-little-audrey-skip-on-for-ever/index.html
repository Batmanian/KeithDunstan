Will Little Audrey Skip on for Ever?

5 min read.

First published 21 Nov 1970

The return of the Skipping Girl Vinegar sign to Abbotsford, celebrated by Barry Humphries in verse, prompts a wider survey of the National Trust's classification system and a plea to extend it under 'Batman's Law' to the State Theatre, Flinders Street Station, the Exhibition Building fountain, and even the small iron pissoir at Flinders and Exhibition Streets.

HERE in Melbourne we have an advertising sign which moves us to tears of sentiment. It is called the Skipping Girl Vinegar sign and just possibly it was our first working electric sign. It went up in the early thirties. The sign depicts a dear little girl swinging a rope, who magically, really skipped. Then listen to this. In October, 1968, Mauri Bros. and Thompson Ltd., the Skipping Girl people, shifted location. Whelan the Wrecker knocked down their old Abbotsford building and the Skipping Girl was sold for $100. This spring she made a comeback. She has been re-erected on top of the Crusader Plate building just a few doors from her old site.

At first there were frightful suggestions that the Skipping Girl be modernised. One enthusiast even wanted to give her a bosom, but you will be pleased to hear she has been reconstructed precisely as she was in little jacket and near-midi-skirt.

Barry Humphries was so moved by this he even toasted her in champagne glasses of vintage Skipping Girl vinegar and penned a commemorative ode:

The trams are still pale green and And the foot of the shrine has been But most of the rest of our city Has either been ruined or demolished.

They haven't pulled down Melbourne And the shadow of Whelan's dead hammer Hangs over the old Melbourne Club.

Now we've all got a car and a telly And a fridge full of goodies and a liquor, If we concrete the whole Yarra Valley We can get to work five minutes quicker.

For though progress can sometimes be tawdry, Our town planners are brilliant and clever, Though I privately pray little Audrey Will outshine them and skip on for ever.

Naturally, of course, we are waiting for the National Trust to give Audrey the Skipping Girl a star rating. Now, the National Trust has been very good. It has a classification scheme in which almost any object can rate 'O' for an 'object of interest.' For example, there is a built-in bootscraper at 8 Collins Street which has an 'O.' So, too, does a hitching-post outside the brewery in Bouverie Street.

A Crimean War cannon outside Victoria Barracks is 'O,' Fawkner's and Lalor's graves at the Melbourne cemetery are 'Os' and Joan of Arc and St. George and the Dragon outside the State Library are beaut 'Os.'

But one feels that occasionally they need to be less aesthetic and take into account what could be described as Batman's Law. Under Batman's Law almost any object can start as a vile monstrosity; after 25 years it can become merely horrid; after 50 years a rather nice piece of history; after 75 years a lovable piece of history to be restored at all costs.

For example, under Batman's Law I now feel the old St. Moritz Ice Rink in St. Kilda Road is marvellously monstrous. Indeed, it is built in the style of an architect who had been reading Gustave Doré's illustrations to Balzac. And you'd have to give an 'A' to that entrance. It is an entrancing lesbian situation, two massive females, elbow to elbow, one of whom has a large breast exposed, then there's the message 'Restez Ici' and 'Soyez Le Bien Venu.'

Under Batman's Law the State Theatre would get an 'A.' This dates only from circa 1928, but it has had a brilliantly quick run to a monster 'A.' After all, it is the last remaining example of the Moslem influence in theatre architecture. What's more, inside there is that marvellous Eastern deep blue sky with all the little stars that blink. Are we to allow this to be lost for ever?

I am still debating about Flinders Street Station. I think it is a perfect monster, for it, too, has the Eastern influence, although of a different school to the Moslem State. I think the designer of Flinders Street Station was heavily under the influence of the Brighton Pavilion.

The Trust, rightly, has given an 'O' to the William Stanford Fountain in Carpentaria Place, by Spring Street, but there are others. How about the fountain up at the Exhibition Building, an appalling piece of design, but with what brilliance and assurance it has been built! The main bowl of this fountain is supported by gargantuan creatures with big breasts and such, and footballers' thighs that turn into fishes' tails.

Then there are other classifications that don't go far enough. George Lansell's building at Bendigo receives only 'D.' But no private house in Australia was ever as appalling as this. It should have an 'A' for its curiosity rating. And if they won't give the whole building an 'A,' how about the stained glass? I am in love with that Neapolitan delight which says 'East West — Home's Best.'

Max Beerbohm once suggested a society for covering up all London's statues. I think I would cover up that strange statue of Sir Thomas Blamey standing in a jeep in the domain, but I would give Batman rating to most of the others, and particularly those gorgeous plump marble girls in the Ballarat Gardens.

So we pass on this broadening suggestion to the National Trust, and meantime we commend them for a very worthy aim. We understand that the delectable pissoir at the corner of Flinders and Exhibition Streets is coming up for classification. This is a classic affair in stamped iron, small but delightfully shaped and scrolled.

At a time when even in Paris most of the pissoirs have disappeared, our charming little creation in Flinders Street deserves the highest grading.
