Flashpoints on the Bunyip's Intimate Parts

6 min read.

First published 17 Oct 1970

A private screening of John Murray's censored documentary film 'The Naked Bunyip' at the Palais Theatre, with the censored passages flagged by coloured lamps, reveals the contradictions of Australian film censorship in 1970, from pack rape to Barry Humphries, prostitutes and a toothpaste commercial.

ACTUALLY, the very atmosphere of the vast old Palais Theatre is enough to make one feel slightly sexy. Its architectural style could be described as early Hoyts gargantua and one's hand almost irresistibly wants to stray to a female knee next door.

Here in Melbourne we have just had a private screening of 'The Naked Bunyip' by Southern Cross Films, to see it complete and unabridged, so to speak. Just to fill you in, John B. Murray made this two-and-a-half-hour colour film on Australian attitudes to sex and censorship in 1969, finishing the actual production about December. Originally, it was to be released in February, but ever since it has been locked up at Suite 6, 449 St. Kilda Road, while wrestling operations have been taking place with the censor.

Our invitation to the Palais read: 'The kangaroo isn't the only Australian animal fighting for his survival. The bunyip, too, is currently being hunted by our censors. In many ways the censors have been extremely lenient. However, in other areas their decisions have been absurd, arbitrary and laughably inconsistent. We write this on behalf of the bunyip, who is both wounded and shocked by his treatment at the hands of officialdom. How do we explain to the poor animal that some of his most intimate parts will have to be cut?'

There was a suggestion in the 'Herald' that even a private viewing of the 'Bunyip's' intimate parts might be illegal, so on Friday morning in the deluxe lounge we had not only the film critics but the police rounds reporters as well, waiting hopefully for the Vice Squad. No Vice Squad men appeared. John Murray told us that, in all, five minutes would have to be cut from the film.

It was a fascinating operation. He put a lamp on either side of the screen. When the blue lamp flashed, that was where the film would have to be cut. When the red lamp flashed, there the sound would have to go. However, I must confess, I was so entranced with what I saw that, even though the lamps were as big as Mercedes headlights, sometimes I missed the flashes.

The story-line concerned Graeme Blundell, a research operator for an advertising agent. He is told to make a study on sex in Australia for a big American client, so we witness his Ulysses-like excursion. Mr. Blundell is very much the Graduate, a Dustin Hoffman, wide-eyed innocent.

Early in the piece he goes door-to-door with a questionnaire on sex. His first victim is Barry Humphries as Mrs. Norm Everage. He asks Mrs. Everage whether she believes in circumcision. 'Well, we had little Kenny done. Norm hasn't been done yet, but we think he should be because it's so much cleaner. In fact,' says Mrs. Everage, 'the more circumcision the better,' and with a terrible gesture she snips a gladdie in half. No lights flashed.

So we proceed, rather more seriously. Barry Jones arrives at the airport. The porno shops in Copenhagen, he says, are doing shocking business. They can't even sell their stuff at cut rates.

We get interviews with everyone in sight — Dr. Stanley Gold, a psychiatrist; Professor E. C. Wood, Professor of Obstetrics at Monash; Father Chamberlain, of the Roman Catholic Church; Dr. James Ball, of the Parkville Psychiatric Clinic.

We see Aggie Reed, the underground film-maker. He has made a film called 'I like boobs a lot.' It cost him $19 to make and it consists entirely of female breasts, shot from stills in 'Man,' 'Playboy' type magazines. All you get is boobs, and we see several dozen of them. No blue light.

Aggie Reed himself then comes on the screen and he says: 'I would like to take all the censors, put them in a cellar and fill it with concrete.' The censors were awfully decent not to censor that.

We get sequences of nude photography and fashion photography. The nude is acceptable to the censor until we go to the art gallery, where there are shots of models being sketched by the artist. These get the blue light. They are much more prim than the previous shots but they are unacceptable because the girls are showing pubic hair. If a young lady is shaved she is acceptable to the censor.

Prostitutes tell how they became prostitutes, homosexuals tell why they are homosexuals, lesbians tell of their background and why they are what they are. All this is important stuff, done with sensitivity, and often very moving. The lesbians get the blue light when it becomes clear they are in bed together with no clothes on.

Dr. Bertram Wainer talks about abortion. He says he told the police of three backyard abortionists, yet the police took no action. This gets the blue light, and one wonders why? It is not the censor's job to protect the police. Dr. Wainer talks, too, of the horror of backyard abortions and the instruments they use — coathangers and ice-picks. Censored again. Why?

The censor certainly has been liberal with some things. He has not censored sequences from a play by Jean Genet. But one suspects the thing that really breaks John Murray's heart is the cutting of the pack-rape story. The getting of this story was a remarkable thing, and it is told with such impact and revulsion to the viewer it is a very moral part of the film.

The bikie tells of a gang bang, how 25 of them 'went through' the one girl, how at the end she wanted more, screaming to go home with one of the boys, how they left her lying on the road. So it gets cut.

We go to the Pink Pussy Cat. Most of that gets cut. It is a good portrayal of the tawdry life of the stripper and anyone in Sydney can see the show live for $1. We see part of the show from Les Girls and, glory be, probably for the first time in the history of movies a bare male bosom gets banned.

The film is very, very good indeed, but is any of the censored material likely to deprave? The only sequences I found, turning me on so to speak, were depravity-making shots of TV advertisements. The worst was a shot of a couple embracing in bathing togs. The girl squeezed toothpaste on to the man's bare shoulder. I wriggled in my deluxe lounge seat. Why didn't the blue light flash?
