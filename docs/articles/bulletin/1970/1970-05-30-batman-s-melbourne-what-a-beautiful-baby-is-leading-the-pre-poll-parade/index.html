What a Beautiful Baby Is Leading the Pre-Poll Parade

5 min read.

First published 30 May 1970

In the weeks before the Victorian state election, a rush of government announcements, from the South Eastern Freeway extension to new trains and a tower hotel beside the Regent Theatre, illustrates how the pre-poll season resembles football's heady opening round of optimism.

THE FIRST WEEK of April is a beautiful time in the football season. The air is full of optimism and potential achievement. Every team has discovered new recruits, fresh from the Wimmera, the Mallee and Perth. They are 7ft. tall and can kick the ball 100 yards with either foot. Twelve coaches announce that this is their year. Yes, it's a lovely heady time.

The last weeks before an election have a similar, rich quality. Lo, suddenly all sorts of fine things begin to happen. It is eight years or more since our one and a half miles of south-eastern freeway opened to finish in a bottleneck at the MacRobertson Bridge. On May 21 we had the grand opening of section two, which takes it another two and a half miles to end in a bottleneck at Tooronga Road, Malvern. Yes, $19 million it has cost, including the diverting of the Yarra and the building of the longest elevated roadway in Europe.

One could describe the activity as frenetic. The bulldozers and graders have been out there in squadrons. Work has gone on through Saturday and Sunday; work has continued all hours of day and night and labourers have been earning $150 a week, just to get it ready in time.

Mr. Hamer, the Minister for Local Government, said all this had nothing to do with the election. He also managed, with superb timing, just in time to announce marvellous new trains for Melbourne. He had a model of one in his office and a glorious thing it was — heated, cooled, insulated, vandal-proofed with laminated-plastic interior and fibreglass seats, tinted glass and exterior of stainless steel. Victoria would buy 50 of these at a cost of $30 million. Mr. Wilcox said the timing of the announcement had nothing to do with the State elections.

On May 18, the securely rightwing Melbourne City Council called its Press conference to announce a hotel, 53 storeys high, to be built on the Regent Theatre site adjacent to the new city square. We were told that this would be Australia's tallest building, 18ft. higher than the Australia Square tower. This was in no way associated with the elections.

Yet, in the optimistic champion-coach stakes, clearly Sir Henry Bolte is easily leading all other coaches. The Henry Bolte Show was far superior to all others on TV and won the praise of the TV critics. There was no arrogance in his style. It was a soft-key, gentle performance. As he faced the cameras, very quietly he told of his Government's achievements. It was the plausible I'm-very-your-close-friend-Henry-Bolte. 'He went about his job of political fence-mending like a true man of the land,' said the 'Age' TV critic.

The ALP leader, Clyde Holding, looked beautifully trendy with long sideboards, but the 'Age' critic treated him without mercy. The Clyde Holding Show was a flop. There was no style. He had only two looks — 'the genuinely concerned look and the stern frown.' But just the same, Clyde Holding should be remembered for the best line of the election: 'The cat burglar is so rich it's a wonder Sir Henry did not invite him to his campaign dinner.'

We have had warm, human pen-portraits of both leaders. For Sir Henry it has been the racegoing, farm-loving, sporting Premier. 'I play as hard as I work. I go out duck-shooting and I'll wade a swamp and walk miles with anybody else.'

For Clyde Holding it has been the intellectual, theatre-loving, art-loving Clyde, and we have been told of his office which sports paintings by Stubbs, Boyd and Clifton Pugh.

But once again, Sir Henry has had the greatest triumph. Sir Henry Bolte produced for the newspapers his 88-year-old mother in Ballarat. Mrs. Bolte said: 'He was such a beautiful baby people always turned in the street to look at him.' Thereupon, Mrs. Bolte brought out a large photo of Henry Bolte at nine months, wearing a lace-trimmed dress. He was indeed a beautiful baby, and this picture was blown up large in the 'Sun-News Pictorial.' So far no other party leader has produced a mother or a picture of himself as a baby, so unquestionably Sir Henry Bolte has a tremendous advantage here.

Slogans have been interesting. Clyde Holding has distributed large lapel badges in the 'I Like Ike' style. They say 'BYE BYE BOLTE.' The Liberals have tried to counter-attack with a Bolte message: 'MY LUCK IS HOLDING.'

But the roughest slogan of all has come from the DLP. For the radio stations they created a song put to the 'Bonnie and Clyde' theme:

Henry and Clyde Henry's little model Sorting out the trouble He has got us in.

Henry and Clyde Clyde Holding is a smartie But the Commos run his party Round the ALP.

Henry and Clyde Show Henry you are disgusted Tell Clyde he can't be trusted Vote DLP.

However, if you are a politician the completely IN phrase is 'QUALITY OF LIFE.' Both Henry and Clyde used it in their policy speeches. Sir Henry has even said: 'The quality of life? It's peace of mind based on the home and the garden.' Neither have told that the line was actually pinched from Seneca, who came out with it nearly 2000 years ago: 'The wise man will live as long as he ought, not as long as he can. He will always reflect concerning the quality and not the quantity of life.'

Perhaps we could offer both gentlemen some ideas concerning the quality of life. How about no election speeches during peak viewing times; all election promises signed by legal contract; 65 as retiring age for politicians; remove Upper House and turn beautiful Legislative Council Chamber into Melbourne's leading nightclub. And another point — which would be of vast assistance — make Myers, Foys, Buckleys and Waltons run their January sales in the last two weeks before Christmas.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: What a Beautiful Baby Is Leading the Pre-Poll Parade, The Bulletin, 30 May 1970. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
