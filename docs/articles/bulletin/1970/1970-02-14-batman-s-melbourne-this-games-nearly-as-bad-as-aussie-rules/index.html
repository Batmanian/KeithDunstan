This Game's Nearly as Bad as Aussie Rules

5 min read.

First published 14 Feb 1970

A dispatch from the Melbourne Stock Exchange gallery during the Poseidon mining boom, comparing the feverish broking-floor atmosphere to barracking at the MCG, and gently mocking the euphemistic language finance writers reach for when the market gets nervous.

WE HAVE HAD a sobering time. There hasn't been a plummet for a week and our finance writers have been noting an easing of the market, a 'reshuffle' of mining prices.

Where is the glory of last December? The Stock Exchange suddenly has become nervous and inhibited. There is a new rule restricting gallery behaviour. In December the gallery had an eloquent atmosphere charged with expectation. It was like being behind the goals at Collingwood — everything was there but the beer cans. One could stay there all day, savouring rumours, picking brokers, barracking one's favourite stock. Now stern-faced men are on the job. It is worse than parking in Collins Street. You are restricted to 20 minutes. What's more, there used to be three red telephones just outside the gallery. They have gone. These were a barracker's lifeline to his coach. Yes, there is a different feeling abroad.

Alf Garnett, of all people, said he found this crazy buying and selling of shares 'vexed. I awful in the extreme.' This curious species of madness seems to have taken over Australia, the same three qualities which are indifference, selfishness and avarice. I know the feeling. It all seems particularly avaricious when one hasn't acquired a single Poseidon or Tasminex or even a Mr. Whippy. But, personally, I am glad to note a sobering of terms being used by finance writers. Admittedly, we did have one 'plummet' last week, but also we had six 'eases' and even 'profit-takings.'

Just to explain what I mean, some headlines from late last year: 'Magnificent performance by Poseidon . . .' 'There was an ear-splitting roar from 400 throats.' Normally, we reserve our ear-splitting roars from 400 throats to the back page for covering events at the MCG or Victoria Park. When Poseidon hit $100 the scene was precisely like the champion full-forward kicking his 100th goal for the season. Or even better, Poseidon was Bill Lawry scoring his century, doffing his cap and smiling to the crowd. The cheering brokers and the mob in the gallery was the crowd jumping the fence and running out to the wicket.

Poseidon was precisely like Lawry. It took block, and, with terrible deliberation, smartly set about making a second century. But then it fluctuated. One day it was down to $190 and it even sank to $165. Immediately one noted the irritation in the exchange gallery. I would not surprise me if we had a slow handclap up in the gallery or a sudden display of umbrellas, which is an entirely new style of barracking. But when Poseidon got back there was a wildly enthusiastic display that kept the chalkies on the run.

The most euphemistic word that has come is 'bunny.' Most brokers, cruising so beautifully that they are out on a flank, have referred to their less astute clients as 'bunnies.' Small investors are not bunnies, but I have been told on good authority that they are.

If Sir Henry Bolte really plans to ring down new legislation in the State he could do much worse than take a hand in the stock exchange. He could get re-elected for sure. We in the press have made survey on good solid terms, which have matured nicely over the years to fine advantage.

Fellow investors, prices never plummet. The market is 'easier,' 'it sets a new tone,' there is 'a slight retreat.' Poseidon 'doesn't run out of gas on a flabby market.' Shareholders merely indulge in 'profit-taking.' Nor do we ever see a recovery — prices 'improve,' 'close higher' or 'strengthen.' Please do not refer to the sensational 'star pacesetter of the seventies,' talk of 'well-favoured growth stock.' 'Well-based performance' and 'assured growth potential' is a splendid phrase.

The buyers are not 'fresh out of cash.' There's 'an absence of liquidity' or 'tight money conditions prevail.' Nor do you have a terrific buy, for right now we are looking at 'exciting short-term possibilities' and 'long-term possibilities.' Nor do you 'stave off disaster.' You 'weather the downturn,' and kindly please, please, we are not 'spec punters,' gentlemen. We are 'speculative investors.'

Goodness gracious, one day we had the line 'jewels in the star-studded metals tiara.' Could we not stick to 'mineral-oriented stocks'?

This is just a very small piece of advice, but please take note, for it will all start again shortly. According to our finance pages there will be 75 new mining floats within the next few months. But I think Sir Henry should take care. Did you read that on February 2 the brokers and jobbers of the stock exchanges in Calcutta and Bombay went on a hunger strike for 24 hours because of dissatisfaction over Government action? What an interesting idea! We could have a new headline: APPETITE EASES ON MELBOURNE EXCHANGE.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: This Game's Nearly as Bad as Aussie Rules, The Bulletin, 14 February 1970. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
