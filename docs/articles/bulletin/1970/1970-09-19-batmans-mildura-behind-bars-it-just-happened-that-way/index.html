Behind Bars — It Just Happened That Way

3 min read.

First published 19 Sep 1970

A visit to the Mildura Workingman's Club, which claims the world's longest bar at 295 feet and 8 inches, reveals an institution that has grown from a modest beginning into a vast complex of restaurants, a floor-cabaret, a billiard room and a boardroom fitted with a sliding bar, all for a $2 annual subscription.

IT WAS JUST last year that Cyril Peat announced the hideous news that the Mildura Workingman's Club no longer had the longest bar in the world. Year after year the Guinness Book of Records had given the title to Mildura — 295ft. long.

Well, the 13th edition placed the record at Ruthven's Holiday Camp, Bogner Regis, Great Yarmouth. Yet the Workingman's Club was not prepared to accept this. Norman Deering has looked upon the Workingman's Club as one of the wonders of the world — something on a par with the Eiffel Tower, the Golden Gate Bridge or the Empire State Building.

Of course, the way it happened is very interesting. When George and William Chaffey came to Mildura as pioneer irrigators on the Murray, they recognised the problem. Alfred Deakin's Irrigation Act banned all licences for Mildura in 1893. The way to circumvent this was to create a club, and the Workingman's Club came into existence in 1895. Even now there are only two licensed hotels in Mildura. The club is, in a sense, the equivalent of the pub in any ordinary Victorian town. And it grew — and grew — until it had 295ft. 8in. of bar.

Last week I went to Mildura anxious to find out what had been done concerning the world's longest bar. Mr. Deering looked much happier. He said the club had just spent money on a new bar extension, bringing it up to almost 300ft.

So your correspondent went on a tour of the wonderful Workingman's Club. The enormous drinking hall was built in stages, with 145ft. and 85ft. sections at right angles to each other, costing some $90,000. The club has a billiard room, bowling greens, dining-rooms, a dance floor and a floor-cabaret area — all for a $2 subscription a year. There is also a beautiful boardroom, where maple panels slide back to reveal a bar and refrigerator. What's more, at the end of the month there is a special club cruise aboard the Iberia to Fiji and Noumea.

One of the club's proudest innovations is the refrigeration of its beer glasses. The kegs are cooled to 32 deg., the glasses chilled to 35 deg. To cope with the rush-hour, the club established a cycle of some 10,000 refrigerated glasses.

Come to that, things are not bad for the working man.

In the old days, women were put outside in a little separate building where they could do a respectable wait and weep. Now there are 3,000 associate members and they have their own dining and drinking lounge. They are not allowed up to the sacred 295ft. 8in. altar, but even that rule is breaking down. The 'Back To Mildura' celebrations take place from October 3 to 18 and Norman Deering is planning a special day when women will be able to breast the longest bar in the world. This could be the ultimate in female emancipation in Australia.

Source: Batman's Mildura: Behind Bars — It Just Happened That Way, The Bulletin, 19 September 1970. Accessible via theNational Library of Australia's Trove database.
