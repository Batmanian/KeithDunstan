Spartan Discipline and Twenty Push-Ups for the Dedicated

4 min read.

First published 17 Apr 1971

The opening week of the 1971 VFL season, dominated by the Ron Barassi and Adrian Gallagher push-up affair at Carlton and by Brian Dixon's North Melbourne pulling off a stunning upset over the reigning premiers.

FRANKLY, from the souvenir point of view the start of the 1971 season has been disappointing. Ah, remember 1966, that was the year that nappies in club colours became available. However, many clubs felt that the actual function they nappies was not entirely creditable to the club concerned and they were discontinued. Then, in 1967, there were club jackets for dogs. These were reversible, so if things were not going well your dog could change his allegiance at half-time.

This year we have club towels, club skivvies and club pillowslips. Apparently there are large numbers of people who wish to sleep and dream with their heads upon their beloved club. Otherwise 1971 has been an utterly vintage year, with the coverage of football stupefying in its depth and range. On the weekend prior to the opening, before official matches had even started, there was footy on TV all Saturday afternoon, Saturday night and Sunday afternoon. Even 'Music for the People,' an almost sacred event in Melbourne, was pushed out of its Sunday timeslot to make room.

Then the newspapers have been filled with advertisements for TV commentators — one channel announced its entire team of half a dozen commentators in a double-page spread of a size and grandeur usually reserved only for the announcement of a new Holden or the new Maiden Cup bra, and all these paunchy gentlemen were dressed up in footy togs, complete with boots.

'Truth' did run a story which revealed that one coach had decided that players would be allowed sexual intercourse during the season, but otherwise discipline exercised for 1971 has been reminiscent of ancient Sparta. Brian Dixon, MLA, former Melbourne wing champion, took over North Melbourne, a team which languishes permanently at the bottom. Mr. Dixon is a man who trains regularly with Ron Clarke and he brought in Ron as 'fitness adviser.' From what one can gather, Melburnians have been making daily runs, virtually to Albury and back, for the Spartan cause.

But you may have heard of the Barassi-Gallagher affair. One can only say that this has been a cause celebre infinitely more important to us, in moral values, drama and news coverage, than the Dreyfus case was to Paris — and the penalties involved equally as bad.

Back on March 25, Ron Barassi, coach of Carlton, decided that his champion rover, Adrian Gallagher, who won his club's top award last year, was not training hard enough. He ordered him, in front of the team, to do 20 push-ups. Gallagher refused. He was immediately suspended from the first match of the season. All of us were aghast, but the club secretary had a fine Dreyfusian phrase: 'At all times the club must be stronger than the individual.'

The first person to say j'accuse was Adrian Gallagher's girlfriend, Miss Susan Colquitt. During the awesome silence coming from the Carlton club she was interviewed for the 'Sun News-Pictorial' by Lou Richards, a former Collingwood rover. Miss Colquitt said Gallagher had been asked to resign his job that afternoon from the firm of Talbot, Lofts and Barassi and then she let loose about the push-ups: 'It's all so childish. You can't treat grown men like that. Ron Barassi doesn't treat his players like men. He treats them like little boys.'

And Miss Colquitt added that Adrian was loyal to Carlton, loyal to Barassi, and they had even put off their marriage till November so that it wouldn't interfere with the football season.

Melbourne was split. John Goold, the Carlton and Victorian half-back, wrote in the 'Herald': 'Once wives and girlfriends start putting in their oar, all else goes. This is a man's sport. When it comes to football, they should be content to take a back seat.'

All the week there were letters and articles on the niceties of the Gallagher suspension — for and against — and Barassi's severed relations with the Gallagher family.

For the opening match, Carlton, last year's premiers, had to play Brian Dixon's Wooden Spooners, North Melbourne. With Gallagher suspended, Carlton could win this game playing on crutches. Indeed, Mr. Lou Richards wrote in the 'Sun': 'If North beat Carlton I'll take a training run at Princes Park on Tuesday night.'

Well, the unbelievable happened. Mr. Dixon's boys from Sparta beat Carlton 124 points to 98. So Lou Richards had to go to Carlton headquarters for his training run on Tuesday night, an unfortunate experience. It was like Zola doing push-ups at the War Department the day after his j'accuse. Indeed, Mr. Richards even did his 20 push-ups.

Ron Barassi had endured enough already during the past week without that. Yes, the first week of the 1971 season has been splendid. I can't see what else it has to offer.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Spartan Discipline and Twenty Push-Ups for the Dedicated, The Bulletin, 17 April 1971. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
