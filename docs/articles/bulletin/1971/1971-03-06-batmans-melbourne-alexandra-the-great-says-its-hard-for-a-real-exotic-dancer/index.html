Alexandra the Great Says It's Hard for a Real Exotic Dancer

5 min read.

First published 06 Mar 1971

For Moomba, Ferdinand Nemeth's club El Toro Espana in Russell Street has imported Alexandra the Great, billed as possessing the world's biggest bust, and Keith Dunstan goes backstage to talk with the St. Louis-born Geraldine Paredes about the decline of proper exotic dancing in an era when a girl who will take off all her clothes straight off the street is considered sufficient.

HERE IN Melbourne we have always been sensitive about two things: the inability of our large city to sustain even one genuine high-class nightclub (we all like to be tucked up in our beds by 10 p.m.) and this absurd legend that Moomba is lacking in culture.

Last year Mr. Ferdinand Nemeth, the Hungarian who owns Whiskies Au-Go-Go both at Kings Cross and St. Kilda, bought the Lido in Russell Street for $223,500. When it opened in 1965 it had the latest thing in revolving stages; it had green carpet and vivid green walls, and gold all over the place. There were six great chandeliers, antiques imported from Egypt; there were divans covered in leopardskin, and on every table gorgeous old lamps with rose-glass shades.

Mr. Nemeth's famous comment when he bought the Lido was: 'If I can make a business in a Woop Woop dump like St. Kilda, which is nowhere, I can make it for sure in the city.' He almost halved prices, put in more seats, a cocktail bar, changed the wallpaper, and now its nationality. Glory be, it is now El Toro Espana.

But now for the cultural side. For Moomba, Mr. Nemeth has brought to Melbourne (at a fee 'considerably more than $500 a week') 'Alexandra the Great,' the girl with 'The World's Biggest Bust' and 'The Toast of the U.S. Fleet.' Her real name is Geraldine Paredes and her reported measurements are 48-25-42 and height 5 feet 10 inches.

Your correspondent called at El Toro Espana and asked Mr. Nemeth if he could talk to Alexandra. Certainly, but she was about to go on for her dress rehearsal. A man gave the word not to refer to her as a stripper. She was an exotic dancer. He said the girl who took over from Alexandra in Hawaii recently did an act with a cigarette, but not in her mouth.

So I went to the dressing-room, and there she was, at least 6 feet in her shoes, thick black hair, with the big eyes, high cheekbones and generous mouth of Sophia Loren. She was wearing a splendid, brilliant red evening gown with zipper down the back.

'Please sit down,' she said. There was only one seat in the room.

'I can't sit down in all this gear, so please sit,' she ordered.

So I sat, and Alexandra the Great towered above. The bosom was like a vast mantelpiece. I was frightened.

Geraldine said she was 26 and born in St. Louis, Missouri, and she first began to get embarrassed about herself at the age of 12. At 18 she began to get walk-on parts at places like the Copacabana and the Latin Quarter in New York. 'One time,' she said, 'I was working at Flatbush Avenue in Brooklyn. It was a real rush show. I had to hold a hat high over my head. I hadn't had time to button up my dress properly and the whole lot dropped to the floor. There I was. There was a HUHHHHHH from the audience. I was fired.'

I deemed it necessary to think surely that it must really have been the start of a long-term contract. No, she said, not until some time later when working in Toledo was she discovered, by Rose la Rose. Then she broke into the big time as an exotic dancer.

'In those days,' she said sadly, 'you needed more than just a good build. I call myself an exotic dancer and I consider I have a sophisticated act. But in the U.S. they don't want legitimate acts any more, just a girl that will take off all her clothes, nothing else. They say, "You take off, you hired." Straight off the street. Feel off — and there they are, foetal.'

'Nightclubs can get these girls for just a few dollars. There are plenty of them. It's hard for a real exotic to make a decent living. But weren't there still some States in the U.S. which laid down restrictions?' 'No,' she said, 'it's topless, bottomless, everywhere. It gets more daring all the time.'

Then Alexandra, very dignified and enormously imposing, went on stage to do her act. She was a classic, as utterly correct in style as Margot Fonteyn dancing 'Les Sylphides.' She had elbow-length white gloves which came off so languidly, which were swung like a tassel before being dropped to the floor. Next to come off was the diamond choker. She swayed, she elegantly ran her fingers through her hair, softly massaged her hips, then — with a fiftieth-of-a-second action — unhooked the clasp which dropped her red gown. That was when one could see why it had been impossible to sit down.

Suffice to say that bra and girdle were actually in three layers which were shed in gasping instalments. There she stood, in all her vast glory. One could say, modestly, that all her prior claims could hardly be disputed. There was a great hush in the audience. The lady went further. She had muscle control which can only be described as remarkable.

Your correspondent left, wondering what else Moomba had to offer.
