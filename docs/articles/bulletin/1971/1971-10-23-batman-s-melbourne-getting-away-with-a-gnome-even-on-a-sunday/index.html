Getting Away with a Gnome Even on a Sunday

4 min read.

First published 23 Oct 1971

A defence of extended Sunday trading hours in Melbourne, arguing that the city's increasingly puritanical restrictions on Sunday commerce are out of step with a changing population, and that shops selling garden gnomes and concrete ornaments should be as free to open as the Dandenongs tourist trade.

THE MELBOURNE Sunday once had a God-given, creaturly, probable beauty. It had a perfect unity about it, too. The whole of Melbourne was quiet: cafes, shops, even pubs closed in the afternoon. There was some sort of law that three hours only on Sunday mornings to extend the actions to do or change anything.

The most important recreation for Melburnians on a Sunday is still either a ride out to the Dandenongs or paying a visit to Springvale Crematorium. We have had the other side in a number of ways. Last year there was a special Sunday at the Dandenongs, a visit by Queen Elizabeth and Prince Philip, but since we were never really prepared for it, the idea was never repeated.

Two years ago antique and curio shops had to wait for Sunday, because in the hills Melbourne as a service city the Department of Labor and Industry cracked down on this and there were all sorts of prosecutions.

One lady in a letter to the 'Herald' said: 'It is quite ridiculous that there can be no Sunday trading in the hills. Some people think that if there are no motor tourists they can get away with anything.' She wrote about road qualities of the Dandenongs so close to the city that having some visitors from home abroad and you come away feeling enclosed in loads of nothing.

Sincerely we have stood firm against Sunday trading. There is no changing whatever of our pub opening on Sunday. Unlike Queensland and Western Australia we have stood firm. A Gallup poll found that for men, Sunday pubs were the most vehement in the nation for men.

However, I regret to have to report that we are crumbling in other directions. Last week Mr Hamer, the Chief Secretary, introduced a Bill in Parliament which will mean that on Sunday theatres had to remain closed until recently, was the view that slowly they have crept forward seven hours ahead of the kind of time before they lose into outline.

Mr Hamer, to his credit, always recognises that there are people who can, as he said, 'only bring in the money.' However, he said, there had been strongly worded letters from the Church about the type of films that would be shown on Sundays. Therefore, the Governor has received an undertaking from the Motion Picture Exhibitors' Association that no attempt would be made to show 'the more objectionable films' on Sundays. 'He didn't precisely define what are objectionable films, but it is an intriguing idea that it is all right for your mind to be corrupted and horrified out of your mind from Monday to Saturday, but not on a Sunday.'

But the most interesting utilitarian battle in many years is taking place right now. The Department of Labour and Industry suddenly has begun to crack down on people, labourers and nurserymen. Now here the law is very clearly this was a trifle kinky. It is legal on a Sunday for a nurserymen to sell plants, fertiliser and terracotta pots, provided they are in a fenced-off area. But if he was selling garden gnomes in sand screenings, concrete pots or garden seats, he faces police prosecution as well, and liable to a fine of up to $1000.

For the Sunday trading the prosecutions have been out in force, taking garden gnome sellers to court. Actually it is the first time in 15 years that they have been booked for Sunday business. The Government has chosen a tremendously unfair time to swing action. It has happened immediately after the Spring Racing Carnival, well gone. It is well known that the busiest time for the garden, for Melbourne, is on Saturdays and Sundays immediately after the spring racing carnivals.

Just to find out how rifle things have become for selling concrete ornaments down the bayside, I found a number of nurserymen and one made the point, to make illegal transactions. Down near Frankston there are some who trade both ways slyly, nick me down the back paddock. They can't sell the terracotta planters and compost-improving tubs, but at the back in a laneway they put concrete gnomes, storks, kookaburras, elves and pixies. (Gnome men call these 'realistic concrete ornamental logs.')

The $4.50 gnome is a rather beautiful do gnome. I looked it under my arm and I'm sorry to have won in a back way. Actually the Government is planning to extend Sunday pub opening hours generally before Christmas. The Sunday Afternoon Bill was introduced by the Minister for Labour and Industry, and which way is the obvious way that the law will be changed. It's a worry, but you might say an interesting army of gnomes rally rally on Sunday will do very nicely.
