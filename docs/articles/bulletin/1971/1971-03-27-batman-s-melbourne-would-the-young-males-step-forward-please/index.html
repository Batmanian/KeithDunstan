Would the Young Males Step Forward, Please?

4 min read.

First published 27 Mar 1971

With Australia apparently devoting a quarter of its female population under 21 to beauty contests, Keith Dunstan wonders why charities have entirely overlooked young males as contestants, who are surely equally deserving of trips around the world, dream automatic sedans, and free hairdos.

FRANKLY, I have reached the stage where I just can't stand any more beauty contests. There have been so many this year that it has been even hard to get pictures of footballers in the papers. We have had Quest of Quests, Queen of the Pacific, Miss International, Miss Australia Tennis, Miss Summer Happening, Miss Moomba, Miss Personality Girl, Miss Coral Queen, Mrs. Victoria, Miss Golden Girl, Miss Australia Beach Girl and, barely, even Gallery of the Year, on St. Patrick's Day. And there are others I may have missed, such as Miss Air Hostess, Miss Teenage, and Miss Boniest Baby.

I went to a horse sale where they even had to interrupt the sale of horses for a beauty contest. Very tactfully they didn't call her Miss Horse but named her after the breed on sale.

One appreciates that these girls raise vast sums for charity, but surely it all must become self-defeating. Couldn't we begin to taper off with, say, just one beauty contest a week? Australia does not have many of the world's firsts, but it is safe to say that per head of population we do lead the world in the number of road deaths, race meetings and beauty contests.

This is the reason Australian girls win so many world beauty titles; at least 25 percent of the female population under 21 is involved in this occupation.

If you don't get involved in dinners, banquets, balls for area finals, semi-finals and grand finals you will see these great events on television. They always follow the same polished ritual. There is a male compere and a female compere. Usually there are about 20 finalists. The girls are paraded one by one in evening gowns. They come along an endless catwalk to meet the compere. Inevitably every girl has had her hair done by the same 'hair-stylist.' Every hair looks almost the same, but this hairdresser is getting plenty of plugs so the hair is piled 4 feet high and would have been spectacular enough for Helen of Troy.

The compere starts to ask probing questions. 'Raelene, what is your ambition?' Nine out of ten say they want to be models. Four out of five say they have an ambition to travel and see the world. This is a good ambition, for the prize inevitably includes a trip with chaperon around the world by Qantas, Alitalia or Pan American or at least a fortnight for two at Surfers Paradise, plus a set of hand luggage by Hickok, a wardrobe by Kenneth Pirrie, a swimsuit by Sutex, etc., etc.

Hobbies are always discussed. Sunbathing and horse-riding are always very strong. Three out of four make great claims and all of them just love the Australian countryside. At one quest the other night, a girl was asked about her ambitions and she said: 'I just want to be good at everything I do.' I tell you — these shows need more scripting. We will have to find more interests and ambitions for these girls. Couldn't we have girls who want to be the female cop in a Hector Crawford crime series, a nude model for the next Norman Lindsay, the beautiful private secretary to the next Prime Minister, or a women's liberation fighter?

Finally the parading and interviewing come to an end. A distinguished citizen or visitor is handed an envelope. After an interminable period of listing minor prizes, the distinguished guest finally announces the winner.

The winning girl looks stunned. Never for a moment did she think it would be 'me.' However, one notices that she collects herself and goes into a brilliant ten-minute speech, thanking Mummy, Daddy, everyone at work, all her sponsors, her boyfriend, right through to the milkman and the postman.

So what about the young males? There wouldn't be more than one or two beauty contests for males in the whole year and they're only for muscle-men. In these days of unisex I think charities should think seriously about this problem, and perhaps even blend the two. After all, the males, too, deserve the trips round the world, the dream automatic sedans, the complete outfits by Kenneth Pirrie, the free hairdos.

It would be interesting to hear of the ambitions of the young males today, whether they, too, all want to be models, and whether they, too, are skilled at making their own outfits. And what would happen when they won the trip round the world for two? Would the winning young man pick Daddy as his chaperon?

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Would the Young Males Step Forward, Please?, The Bulletin, 27 March 1971. Accessible via theNational Library of Australia's Trove database.
