The Beautiful Square Dream

5 min read.

First published 18 Dec 1971

A look at the long-delayed plans for Melbourne's City Square, surveying the proposals that have come and gone over 115 years and the latest design by the Theosophy Society, while questioning whether the square, once built, will become a genuinely beautiful civic space or merely a windy concrete plaza.

DOES a city really need a square? Clearly, having one is just a habit picked up by decadent older cities like Paris, London, Venice and Rome. Those opposed to the square already have noticed that squares attract only the very worst types of people — orators, various marchers, union protesters, Communists, anti-apartheiders, all sorts of scruffy people trying to starve themselves to death.

The truth is Melbourne has always suffered a dreadful feeling of inferiority because it hasn't possessed a noble square. The true hunt for it is the greatest ever perpetrated here. It outruns Dad and Dave, Blind Harry and all the rest.

Actually I have been doing some research and it seems the square hunt has been going on with some passion for 115 years. In 1835 there was a fine proposal to build Parliament House, where St Patrick's Cathedral now stands and it was to be bordered by a fine square out front. Costly waste of space was the decision.

In 1887, Mr N. Barnett, a city architect, advocated a beautiful city square in front of Parliament House in Spring Street. It would include a scaling down of the Windsor and White Hall hotels. The grandiose plan, the full statue of Queen Victoria in the centre. On Monday there was to be a dome on Parliament House, F. Kerr's sculpture among it. With a dome it would be the coup de grace in this magnificent setting for the City Square — whether an ensemble equal to it could be found in any city in the world? Costly waste of space was the decision.

It came up again in 1910 and again in 1957. This time the plan had grown and would have a fine fountain, beautiful gardens, linking Swanston and Collins Streets and equally as deep. Once again they had only the site at the Regent House. Then out at the centre of the plan was a thirty metre tower pool, flanked by four statues 150 feet high. It would be what the 'Herald' called 'monumental experiences.' There were several competitions. Each time, the Costly waste of space was the decision.

Without a Queen Victoria or a war to push things along, it wasn't easy, but there were proposals for squares in Lonsdale Street near the Queen Victoria Hospital, for a square in the space opposite the State library and many recently there have been very many fine opportunities to make superb squares out of the commercial car park blocks. Costly waste of space was the decision.

Now, at last, the impossible dream looks as if it may get somewhere. First seven ages ago, City Council started out on a city-square project for the city block bordered by Collins Streets. It is not as good a site as some of the others; a matter for it is on the side of a hill. Fountains really prefer level ground.

Completion of the square has been achieved by two incredible 'Bellbird'-like episodes. The City Council looked then at the Star Holdings of London, who planned a $40 to $50 million office-and-office tower at the eastern end. This would give the City Council no more than a sweet little building forecount.

Now the Theosophical Society, right down from there, having refused to build their tower, has refused to develop within guidelines. Initially it won an architectural award back in 1967. But they have had some doubt raised. They are Theosophists, but they have learned Theosophists are very fine people. Now the City Council has pushed its price all the way up to $150 million for the square, as if the Theosophists may accept. But in all things the Theosophists are, Sophists believe in reincarnation, they are very ancient and very old questioners and they believe that the movement of the cosmos is the largest thing on earth. They are students of all things. And the Theosophists are claimed to have the best library on the subjects of Theosophy in the country. One can only assume that this knowledge has guided their management in their present transactions.

But is it certain as if the war may be clear for the finish of our 115-yearold dream? It is by no means certain that Star Holdings may modify their plans, or that the Theosophists may agree to some money after all. There is, however, one optimistic note. The City Council very just recently, and put down a two-storey car park in the square with pre-cast concrete blocks and clearly the whole thing will be replaced when better times come stagnant. The seats are in a perfectly inoffensive position, planters are done with concrete drainpipe sides, also in a park that the city council is using. Now these square plots are really sites for gardens, fountains, the famous inauguration of Lord mayors, or revered spectators, but they're not.

The town clerk, Mr. F. Rogan, has gone so far as to announce there was to be called in to do a design. This is certainly most refreshing. We are trusting there will be fountains, beautifully designed. Once there is that, we will have no equal anywhere in the world. At night, the space of the city will still be a good place for protests and marches, too. This is true.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: The Beautiful Square Dream, The Bulletin, 18 December 1971. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
