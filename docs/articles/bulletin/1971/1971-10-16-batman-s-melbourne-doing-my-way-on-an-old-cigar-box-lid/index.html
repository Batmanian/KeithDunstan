Doing My Way on an Old Cigar-Box Lid

4 min read.

First published 16 Oct 1971

A visit to Rosalind Humphries' exhibition of paintings on cigar-box lids, which reprises a celebrated 1969 Buxton's Galleries show, while a parliamentary row over the appointment of former ASIO officer Michael Thwaites as Commonwealth Parliamentary Librarian raises questions of political discrimination in the public service.

ROSALIND HUMPHRIES, at her gallery in High Street, Armadale, has just re-created the most famous of all Australian art exhibitions. It was shown originally at Buxton's Galleries, Swanston Street, in 1969.

The entire exhibition was on cigar-box lids, which were specially provided, the lids coming from her father's own cigar collection.

The three best-known artists who exhibited were Alan Sumner, John Conder and the 23-year-old Arthur Boyd. The show was undoubtedly the most provocative Melbourne art display of the year. James Smith, a gentleman of enormous importance at the 'Argus,' is known as a close friend of Dickens — or at least it was said that he knew Thackeray.

James Smith went into Buxton's and wandered about quietly among the artists. His review charged that the show was 'without form and void.' What was this Impressionism? He argued that Rosalind Humphries delivered only scraps of composition, a sculptural rendering of what were amorphous outlines.

The publishers fought back bravely. People paid their sixpence and the National Gallery committee were heard to say to each other: 'Have you seen the "Argus" review?' As a result the show was a considerable success.

'Talk' recorded that the gallery was decorated with liberty silks and Japanese umbrellas, and Ena Hans gave a musical recital accompanied by Rosalind Humphries. An afternoon tea was served. Mr Charles Topp, from the 'Australasian,' was very impressed.

Charles Bush found the cedar boxes a beautiful surface on which to paint. Rosalind Humphries received a fine response; among those who sent in paintings were Charles Blackman, Arthur Boyd, John Boyd, Lawrence Daws, Robert Grieve, Louis James, Mirka Mora, John Olsen, John Perceval, Clifton Pugh, Rosemary Ryan, Richard Shannon, Jan Senbergs and veterans like Daryl Lindsay and William Frater.

Noel Counihan had an outrageously poignant nude. One could look at the paintings for quite some time.

A section of the ALP is trying to upset the appointment of Mr Michael Thwaites as assistant librarian of the Commonwealth Parliamentary Library. The point raised is that Mr Thwaites served 20 years as an officer of the Australian Security Intelligence Organisation.

As I see it, double standards are being applied. The position of assistant parliamentary librarian necessitates an individual of outstanding qualities. Mr Thwaites has highly distinguished qualifications and a career that would be rated from one end of Australia to the other.

The position of assistant parliamentary librarian does not involve security work. If it is proper to appoint a person of leftist sympathies to a non-security position in the Commonwealth public service based on qualifications, then it is equally proper to appoint an individual who happens to have represented the other side, on the same basis.

The question of Mr Thwaites' appointment became known in Parliament last week. It was raised from the ALP side. Speaker Anton told the House that a question was in order. As I understand the position it was raised by Opposition Leader Whitlam and commented upon by Mr McMahon.

In fairness to both men, it should be pointed out that, unlike some of their ALP colleagues who have routinely assailed ASIO, neither is on the record as questioning the need for a security service. Their objection was on a personal basis. As assistant parliamentary librarian Mr Thwaites would be in charge of a staff of professional staff for which the Speaker claimed he was particularly suited, as in his 20 years in ASIO he had 'supervision of staff engaged in research and analytical and evaluative work, supervision and direction of staff work relative to the production of reports and research papers.'

Mr Whitlam's theme was that it was essential that there should be complete trust between the Parliamentarians and the service they used. He said no Labor Parliamentarian would object to Mr Thwaites being given a clerical or other job in the Commonwealth Public Service, but the special relationship between Parliamentarians and the staff of the Parliamentary Library made a different case.

Speaker Magnus Cormack took the view that he could not discriminate against Mr Thwaites merely because he had served in ASIO, which had done its duty and was itself set up by a Labor Government.

The dilemma is not an easy one for the ALP. While one can understand the position of Mr Whitlam, Mr Cairns and the others, it is difficult to see how a senior public servant can be penalised for giving public service — especially when ASIO was established by the Chifley ALP Government in the first place.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Doing My Way on an Old Cigar-Box Lid, The Bulletin, 16 October 1971. Accessible via theNational Library of Australia's Trove database.
