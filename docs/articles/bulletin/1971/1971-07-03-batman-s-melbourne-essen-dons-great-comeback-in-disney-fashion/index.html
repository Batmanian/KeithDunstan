Essendon's Great Comeback in Disney Fashion

4 min read.

First published 03 Jul 1971

Essendon Airport closes as a major domestic terminal and Melbourne shifts to Tullamarine, with Keith Dunstan reporting on the new airport's Tweedledum-and-Tweedledee symmetry between Ansett and TAA, and Reginald Ansett's grandiose 'Megacentre' redevelopment plan for the derelict 682-acre site.

Quite the most charming letter to the newspapers was from the old pilot who said: 'We have so many airports — Tullamarine, Essendon, Moorabbin, Laverton, Point Cook ... now that we don't need Essendon any more, why don't we do the decent thing and give it to Sydney?'

Essendon, as a major domestic airport, finally ended its career on the weekend of June 19 and 20. Out on the taxiway there was an intriguing cavalcade of vehicles. There were new honey carts. A honey cart is the dear little vehicle that the airlines use to empty the aircraft lavatories. So, you see, they don't spray it in the atmosphere at 31,000ft.

Ansett, for example, insured its move for $6,480,000 and it had 125,000 items on the job and trucks were checked in and checked out with even more security than if they were carrying bullion for Qantas.

Your correspondent went out to Tullamarine to see how the new tenants had settled in. The new terminal buildings are all in the one building. However, there is a magnificent apartheid between TAA and Ansett; they are way out on either wing. So if, suddenly, you decide to swap airlines, you have to hump your bags across an endless sea of polished vinyl, past all the souvenir koala-bear shops.

It is intriguing, too, the precise care with which we treat our Tweedledum and Tweedledee airlines. Tweedledum Ansett has exactly the same amount of space as Tweedledee TAA. They have the same furniture except that Ansett is in purple and TAA is in yellow. They both have a newspaper kiosk. I thought the paperbacks were not quite as fleshy as out at Essendon. They both have a coffee shop, each with 116 chairs, and they both have intimate little bars run by Aerojet Caterers. Ansett has the Jackaroo Bar, which features a typical Wild West corral with hide and cow horns on the walls, post-and-rail fences to lean on, wall-to-wall carpets and air-conditioning. TAA has the Golden Nugget Bar, which should be an ideal resort for gold-diggers of various varieties. It has the walls decorated all over with real gold-panning dishes. Again, there is a pioneering atmosphere in air-conditioned comfort.

It is all much more comfortable, much more luxurious than Essendon. Except that modern aviation is designed for a new, hardened race. You may be asked to go out gateway number ten. So you set out 850ft. along the finger, which is like one of those polished antiseptic passages you strike in nightmares — they appear to have no ending.

But, meanwhile, what happens to Essendon, a fine, fully operational airport of 682 acres, now a lonely derelict, used only by civil aviation and a few charter airlines? At first it was thought that the light-aircraft people at Moorabbin might like it. But it would cost too much to maintain, and, anyway, who wants to be close to the jets at Tullamarine?

There was a remarkable silence on the subject from both our Sir Henry and Federal Ministers. Early in the week there was a newspaper photograph of Sir Reginald Ansett standing on the deserted tarmac at Essendon. The story underneath said he was thoughtful and resigned — neither sad nor wistful. He was not.

The next day he called a Press and TV conference in the ballroom of the Southern Cross Hotel with architects and PR men in full cry. There were booklets for everybody, there was a great scale model to tell the story of a proposed project called 'Megacentre,' a $400 million plan for Essendon over the years.

The dreams were the superbly attractive concept of a completely self-contained community of 28,000 people. The design called for self-contained, air-conditioned shopping cathedrals. There were 40-storey buildings, footpaths, with movement to different levels by escalators. There was an artificial lake. There were hospitals, technical schools, community centres, and an international hotel. Special attention would be paid to ecology, with factory gases collected and treated and expelled through common shafts. All would be landscaped so that both factories and housing occupied only one-third of the total area.

Sir Reginald's submission was as breathtaking as Disney's Tomorrowland. Meanwhile, there have been practically no other proposals. We did think, seeing Essendon was so close to Tullamarine, the area could be turned into a marvellous pleasure resort, a sort of stepped-up modern Tivoli Gardens — theatres, restaurant, casino, fun parlors, art galleries, RSLs, football grounds, a marvellous compact expression of Melburnian culture.

It would be known, of course, as Bolteland.
