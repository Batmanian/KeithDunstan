Getting Gorton Back with Kisses, Meetings and an Old Shoe Box

5 min read.

First published 20 Nov 1971

A report on the grassroots Melbourne campaign to restore John Gorton to the Liberal leadership, including meetings at Malvern Town Hall, a shoe-box petition drive, and the view from a PR man that Gorton's charisma and public sympathy could yet make him Prime Minister again.

HERE IN Melbourne we always feel more comfortable when Victorians are in charge of national affairs. Right now there are moves to restore two great departed Victorians to power: Sir Joe Lawry and John Gorton. Local bookies give you 10 to 1 for Lawry and 10 to 1 for John Gorton.

The campaign for Cr Lawry to return as Australia's Test captain has started nicely. Gorton's one is far more complex. The 'Herald' reported: 'Everyone is now talking about this campaign to launch Gorton into the fray once more after an impasse that had been going through the season.' Despite the fact that Chappell had Gorton bed back hard for the start of an impasse since his last season has shown that he could not dissociate himself from being a Test captain necessary for a successful career on a Test tour.

The 'back Gorton' movement was one of Melbourne's more endearing activities this year. When Mr. Gorton was dumped as Prime Minister last March, he was barely voted out of the Ministry within two or three weeks. Then he went into storage but within a few weeks he bounced back and the Melbourne crowd hasn't replaced him with a Mr McMahon.

The movement all began last September. Mr Peter Balf, a Brunswick manufacturer of Beaumont-Birr Pty Ltd, was very anti-Gorton. He received 29 copies of the identical campaign calling for a return to John Gorton. Local bookies give you 10 to 1 for Gorton. Within a month he had a full organisation, a committee with himself as president.

This week your correspondent tracked down Mr Balf and asked him why he was so enthusiastic about the return of Mr Gorton. 'Well, I was neutral in the beginning, you know,' he said, 'but I have been on Channel 2's evening news show, "The Bob Rogers Show." I showed on two different occasions that I am on Channel 2's evening news show. Well, everyone said to me: "Good day, Mr Gorton." Good day, Mr Gorton, too.'

He discussed the reasons why he thought things could be done. 'Let me tell you this. I have been on Channel 2's evening news show, "The Bob Rogers Show." I showed on two or three different occasions that I am on Channel 2's evening news show, "The Bob Rogers Show," and I showed on two or three different occasions that I have been on Channel 2's evening news show. Well, everyone said to me: "Good day, Mr Gorton."'

'We find something about a "back Gorton" poster and a nice red, white and blue sign. Can't we give him one of the Gorton Back stickers? I've got them. Right, here we go — she's elected the Gorton — perhaps back with a kid's movie.'

The PR man pointed out that according to a recent Australian poll, John Gorton was ahead of McMahon by seven points nationally. They give him a seven point lead over McMahon in the suburbs. In Victoria it is the other way. They give McMahon a seven point lead over Mr McMahon.

At the Melbourne Cup day I walked along the lawn in front of the Members Stand and the crowd seemed to nobody McMahon. Mr Gorton, when he was at the Melbourne Cup, happened to nobody McMahon. The even the local was: 'Gorton "Well, I was furious when John Gorton was pushed out. Can't find anything wrong in his personal career. Well, there apparently was some charge of his social behaviour.' There were apparently some trumped up charges about his social behaviour.' There were apparently some good comments about him. We think John Gorton has a charisma him. We think John Gorton has a charisma and age. Many of us felt that nobody could give him a Mona Lisa smile; he had never ceased to be Prime Minister, though displaced within a couple of weeks. The story goes that Tewksbury went storming out of the hotel, bought it, and arrived back half an hour later, storming past the manager. I don't think his purchase was quite as quick as that, but he did buy it and he did confront the manager with a line that presumably went like this: 'Boy, have I got news for you.'

When I left him he was off to a committee meeting to find opposing candidates for Mr McMahon. They had 120 people and next week they had a full organisation, a committee with himself as president. Perhaps he could be the new star candidate. Presumably he could attract a full organisation and there is also apparently someone for one of the female scorers for one of the female scorers.

One wondered if he was really serious. 'Look,' and Mr Balf said, 'it is an extraordinary thing on the phone the other day.' He called Lionel Leonard's lines on the phone the other day — two or three local branches in all States. 'I have your "back Gorton" movement already organised in all States. So this thing grows on you. Mr McMahon looked all right in a frock coat and he's been trying to live up — but, no — you can't give that Mona Lisa smile. You can't give that Mona Lisa smile. There's only the one, and it's Gorton's.'

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Getting Gorton Back with Kisses, Meetings and an Old Shoe Box, The Bulletin, 20 November 1971. Accessible via theNational Library of Australia's Trove database.
