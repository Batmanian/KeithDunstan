The Triumph of Barry Mackenzie

5 min read.

First published 04 Dec 1971

Barry Humphries' comic-strip ocker hero Barry Mackenzie is being made into a film by director Phillip Adams and actor Barry Crocker, with backing from the Australian Film Development Corporation, and the production marks a turning point for an Australian film industry finally willing to celebrate its own vulgar traditions.

BARRY HUMPHRIES looks upon Barry Mackenzie as his greatest triumph. Just consider the situation for a moment. Until last year his comic-strip book 'The Wonderful World of Barry Mackenzie' was banned as obscene, a corrupting thing for tender Australian minds. It was still banned in Victoria until only a few months ago. Then, lo, the very Government that banned the book as an obscenity, suddenly saw it as a cultural object and put up $150,000 to turn it into a film. The money came from the Film Development Corporation.

Here's the team. Bruce Beresford is the director, Phillip Adams is the producer and Barry Crocker, who is an actor exquisitely designed for the part, is the star, although Phillip Adams says Barry Humphries also has 'about 50 different parts in the film.'

Beresford is already in London, the rest of the team leave for there. Barry Humphries reports that the film will be 90 minutes long, and at 90 minutes long: 'We are filming it in winter, but I had the idea of making London as picturesquely horrible as possible. Practically all our extras will be coloured and we will have a large Fez Rent sign outside the hotel. Yes,' and we've built a special taxi set. It's a texter specially for Australians. As soon as an Australian gets into a taxi the camera can now click: over it goes up another 85 meld it: measures high.'

Naturally, one was eager to know how the delicate question of recipes would be handled. Barry said: 'We have shown a cooking machine, something a pipe wheel actually, very much lower than Crocker's trousers than around his ankles, and from that you know about the camera. The technicolor yawn will be very very effective on the large screen.'

'What about the chunder recipes?'

'We have scientific experts working on this, for I am very careful not to fall back on my own original formula. This consists of three tomatoes of sweetcorn, tinned tomatoes and Heinz tinned-vegetable soup. Cadburys have been very co-operative. You see, Fosters is so familiar now — so caring that it is so much stronger than the Pom beer. I like to think of this as improving Anglo-Australian relations.'

The Commonwealth has been kind to Barry Humphries. Great kindness above all, not only keeping Barry MacKenzie. He likes to think Barry shot to stardom entirely on literary merit, but magnificent denouncements made even from the Dean of Canterbury have pushed sales to over 50,000 copies.

Sales have been so splendid that Mr Humphries has now produced a record titled 'Bazza Pulls It Off.' You may require an eyebrow at such a title but Barry Humphries asserts in the title achievements he has performed for the old 'William' series by Richmal Crompton, but Barry turned up with a collar in his hand and with the words 'Crepeau. I come on a bit strong in these new adventures — hope the Bishop himself didn't deport this.'

The adventures are similar in character to the old books. The wandering taproom turns, cunning. Bazza is going up against the fine tradition of comic strips like Garth, Mandrake, Judge Dredd and for Dr. Foster he suffers appalling sexual frustrations and comes out wonderful.

I know you are curious about the title 'Adventures.' Don't. The version of the story where Bazza actually pulls it off happens in the earlier books. Indeed in later episodes now moving up. Bazza is consequently let on in as an immigration officer and he advises President from Weir's section. He visits a garden party at Buckingham Palace, takes sherry with the Queen Mother and passes out in the shrubbery, then wanders outside past a cold and a frosty morning.

Perhaps the most intriguing portion is the Chaplinesque in the ending. He says: 'Barry's choice words and phrases appropriate for the menu.' For Poms, magistrates, fourth-form schoolboys, James Hardie's, Milloy. Dancing officers and conserving male and female public servants.

Here are some examples:

Alec's armpit: (See under Julius's loincloth.) An egg-faced chardonnay mug. Also: An Australian dunkie.

Dog's dandruff: (See.)

Beef bayonet: (See under Percy, pork sword.) Eden salmonella, Sydney syphilis, the cattle ornament.

In Melbourne we go droive and fantastic into the bowl.

But in the oven: (See under up the Khyber.)

Bundle of lettuce: (See under greenie.) Punter: (See under Pom.)

Chilled more: (See under framing gnome, Toorak push, Swanston Street, Fosters), tiger beer.

Beef vindaloo: To make you. (See under chunder, technicolor yawn.)

Abronian: (See under frog, cooker, potato, raw.)

Abo: Newgate. A punch in the face.

Bradshaw's Mystery, as dry as a: A condition of throat price in the languid afternoon of the suburbs.

Pedals, petrol brewery: Trousers.

Pink palace: A garden near alcohol.

These are just a few examples from the glossary. There are so many that one would hesitate to print. They are offensive even to most readers, even Sidney J. Baker to the neck oil.
