With a Tear in His Eye He Said Goodbye

5 min read.

First published 18 Sep 1971

The end of the 1971 Victorian Football League season brings the simultaneous retirement of Ron Barassi from Carlton and the resignation of Bobby Rose from Collingwood, prompting wall-to-wall press coverage and intense speculation about the coaching vacancy at Collingwood, whose traditions Keith Dunstan compares with the Japanese code of the Samurai.

It always happens at the end of a Victorian Football League season. Unbelievably, coaches are very decent about it. They don't take a sack and jump off the end of the Williamstown pier.

I think we got rid of about six coaches that way at the end of the 1970 season, but 1971 has been cataclysmic. Only once or twice have occasions arisen what you might call 'extraordinary stories' managing to get on to the front pages. There was a former Prime Minister announcing a sack and a mass murder, but these stories had no staying power.

The truth is that Melburnians have been riveted to their television sets, avidly stirred by the resignations of two immortals: Ron Barassi, coach of Carlton, and Bobby Rose, coach of Collingwood.

Ron Barassi has been described as the greatest football personality since we hang up his boots. In 1970 he coached Carlton to the premiership. There was an 'Oh, ho!' Carlton still not winning one of the four. Barassi went out. He had had football 'up to the neck' and he was retiring for good.

The coverage was superb. There was a full splash on the front pages, spread all over the inside. One of our soccer writers titled a piece 'Days in the Life of Ronald Dale Barassi.' The 'Sun' took pleasure in publishing a tear from one eye.

The same day the 'Sun' had pictures on the entire front pages and stories and pictures all over the inside pages. On August 28 the 'Herald' devoted 90 columns of coverage to the coverage.

On August 30 the 'Sun' had the picture of Ron Barassi on the entire front page and there was a column-long picture of the veteran Carlton ruckman Sergio Silvagni. On September 4 the 'Sun' had a magazine titled 'What Makes Ronald Ron?' with contributions, no doubt, by even such people as the 'Sun' psychologist.

But that wasn't all — the press simultaneously documented the VFL. Most of the women's pages also sought out a former Collingwood player to give her views on the retirement. We found one who was an authority on Barassi in the bringing-up of children.

By all the same reasoning, I think the events surrounding Collingwood that past week have been more stirring still. You must appreciate that an extraordinary charisma and tradition surrounds Collingwood. To mention the traditions associated with the Royal Horse Artillery, the Scots Guards, etc., would be nothing. A fresh approach to the great traditions of Collingwood would be the Japanese code of the Samurai — it has the same death-before-dishonour ethic. E. M. Forster once said: 'If I had to choose between betraying my country and betraying a friend, I hope I should have the guts to betray my country.' At Collingwood they wouldn't hesitate. Collingwood unquestionably would have to come first.

Many years ago I went to the beginning-of-season Black and White annual dinner. It was a black-tie affair and the room was awash with handkerchiefs. From the end of the hall the fanfare. Then came the Collingwood pillow as high as embroidered cushions. And then the most important person — the No. 1 Black-and-White Collingwood worrier of all. Then came the exciting flares of trumpets, and out stepped the Collingwood officials in beige-and-black guernsey. Then came No. 2 player, and so on. Filing through the entry to the era, it tallied the conferring of degrees at the University of Melbourne.

Collingwood dressing rooms are better than the half — comparable to West Point. The team has by far the greatest winning record of any VFL club but it has won only twice since World War II. Bobby Rose in the past eight years has been within a whisker of a premiership, but twice the semi-final he failed again. Somehow Collingwood always failed to show larrikin spirit and was thrashed by Richmond.

Bobby Rose was barely to be seen in the coverage. He was barely mentioned in the Press on the Sunday and the Monday. Then the Tuesday the bombshell announcement came through — Bobby Rose resigns.

Meanwhile the spirit of bare-knit challenge is not lacking among the VFL. The Magpie captain, until halfway through the year, let fly in an interview: 'Collingwood needs new blood. It needs a new coach, new determination and some new players and fresh thinking.'

In Collingwood, which always has shown great charisma, the 6,500-member Waterside Workers' Union is planning to run for the committee along with the club's president, Tom Sherrin.

The coaching vacancy is vacant. And here's the rub. It is a case-hardened credibility difficulty that the 'Sun' found a former Collingwood player. The Collingwood committee can take it. The feeling today is that the VFL players are heard to tremble with anxiety.

I tell you, it's all too much for the cool-blooded people of Melbourne.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: With a Tear in His Eye He Said Goodbye, The Bulletin, 18 September 1971. Accessible via theNational Library of Australia's Trove database.
