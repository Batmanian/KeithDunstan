Now Here's a Dame Who Slept Around

6 min read.

First published 02 Jan 1971

A lament for four Melbourne institutions closing at once, the Oriental Hotel at the Paris end of Collins Street, the Regent Theatre with its Vintage Hoyts decor, the Tasma Guest House in Parliament Place, and Hilliers milk bar by the Regent, all falling to wreckers over the Christmas break.

AS WE HAVE pointed out before, the National Trust is a fine organisation, but it has little understanding of the meaningful importance of fine institutions to a city. There should be a special nostalgia rating. This Christmas, without even a word from the Trust, we are seeing the ultimate demise of the Oriental Hotel, the Regent Theatre, the Tasma Guest House and Hilliers' deservedly famed milk bar.

The Ress-Oriental is a large, lovely, comfortable pub situated at the Paris end of Collins Street. Just to show you how Parisian it's been, it's the only pub at that end of Collins Street and the area used to be called Parisian because the Oriental served tea and cream cakes under umbrellas out on the pavement. In Melbourne we always considered it a sin — and still do — to drink out in the open.

Well, the Ress-Oriental closes its doors for ever on December 23 and, come the New Year, wreckers will move in. The Oriental is a very old hotel. Dame Nellie Melba slept there. A remarkable woman, Dame Nellie — she appears to have slept everywhere in Melbourne except the Melbourne Club.

The Oriental used to be owned by Pearson Tewksbury, a great character who kept in his room a goldmining dish with gold dust supposedly sealed into the bottom. He had made a fortune out of the Oriental. He was a guest at the Oriental in 1910 when the management displeased him. The story goes that Tewksbury stormed out of the hotel, bought it, and arrived back half an hour later to confront the manager. I don't think his purchase was quite as quick as that, but he did buy it and he did confront the manager with a line that presumably went like this: 'Boy, have I got news for you.'

He died in 1958 and Mick Ress took over. He now moves on to the Mitre Tavern. VA and NZ, AMP and Mainline now control about 150 yards of the Paris end. They haven't announced the no doubt typically Parisian thing they have in mind, but a mighty building will go up there next year.

Many of us fought desperately to save the old Regent. As you know, it was the biggest theatre in the country. It had 3253 seats upstairs and half as many again in the Plaza downstairs. Around the Regent balcony in pressed metal there were nice topless maidens in a style known as Vintage Hoyts. There were topless girls playing lutes, there were cupids and Etruscan friezes better than the Elgin Marbles. There were chandeliers, Corinthian columns, Ionic columns and acres of scalloped burgundy curtains.

The Plaza was more Spanish Medieval, with lashings of iron galleon lamps. It looked like a scene for the torturing of Christians at the Inquisition. Well, the final act was the auction. Chandeliers went for anything from $70 to $5000. You could pick up a marble cherub for $70, a gold brocade couch for $200, scalloped burgundy curtains for $350, and an appalling framed print of Queen Elizabeth II for $16. Illuminated Tudor lavatory signs also went quite well. Actually, I was a little surprised to find that they had lavatory signs in Tudor times, but anyway, the male sign brought $16 and the female sign $15.

Then maybe you remember the hexagonal marble ticket booth that was out front. The auctioneers felt that it could be converted into a tasteful hexagonal bar to place in the middle of one's living-room or by one's swimming-pool. That went for $90. Why, even an old battered 'House Full' sign was good for $18.

One of the keenest bidders was Mr. George Frew, chairman of Commodore Hotels. Already many of the artefacts have gone into Mr. Frew's new bar, Mulligan's Pool Room, which opened recently nearby the Eureka Stockade in Bourke Street. Mulligan's has green carpet, red walls and a nude known as Mrs. Mulligan right along the bar. You must appreciate that there is a great competition in Melbourne for nude bar paintings. There is a general desire to out-nude Chloe. Mr. Frew believes Mrs. Mulligan is the biggest nude in Australia. She is 24 feet long. Interesting, but I think even Norman Lindsay would have found her proportions peculiar.

The wreckers also will move into the Regent early in the New Year and now we hear that in its place we will have a building with twin towers, no doubt to symbolise the V for victory on the part of the City Council.

Continuing our obituary column, on the same day that the Oriental closes, so too will close the old Tasma Guest House. Now Tasma is in Parliament Place, at the back of Parliament and by the Government offices. It was built in 1886 and it is one of the most gorgeous examples of cast iron we have in town.

For 25 years Maurice Branagan has produced mushroom spawn in the basement and run a guest-house and a mushroom restaurant. He has the noble record of having stood six times as an Independent candidate for the Melbourne City Council. His record is clean — he's lost every time, but 'Try Again' Branagan has always produced some of the better quotes: 'Our city council is a body that is unkind, ruthless, incompetent and avaricious.' The Government owns his building. It is putting him out by court order and there's no doubt this lovely old piece will add to the wrecker's happiness, come March or April, and we will continue our ambition of making Melbourne nearer to the pattern of Dallas, Cleveland, Miami or wherever.

Finally, just a word for Hilliers milk bar. Hilliers run a fine chocolate business in this town and for 40 years, by Regent in Collins Street, they have owned a milk bar. It closes for ever on December 31. When, in greater days, the boat race was run on the Yarra, Hilliers was a headquarters. Our crew never won. Immediately after the race it was traditional to retire to Hilliers, which had the finest milkshakes in town. It had a bar, a large brass rail, and those chocolate malteds were so rich and beautiful that they made life almost livable again.

I am told Hilliers still make the best milkshakes in town and their range has widened immensely with the years. They have a thing called a frosted chocolate which contains malted milk, syrup, ice-cream plus a thick layer of marshmallow on top, sufficient to make one forget three boat races.

However, all this finishes on the 31st and Hilliers opens again in an arcade in Bourke Street which boasts that it is Melbourne's first to have carpet. But wait — under the terms of the franchise they can't sell milkshakes. A hideous restriction, as grave as Mulligan's trying to sell beer without Mrs. Mulligan in the nude.
