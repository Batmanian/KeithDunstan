A Grand Vision for Melbourne

4 min read.

First published 04 Mar 1972

The launch of the Flinders Gate project, a plan to redevelop Melbourne's Flinders Street railway yards into hotels, cinemas and a new station, with the name 'Flinders Gate' winning a public competition after readers submitted suggestions ranging from Ned's Nook to Squizzy's Place.

SYDNEY has its Rocks scheme, but we feel we can out-rock even that. We have here a nice handout which describes our scheme as 'one of the largest developments ever planned in the world.' Which puts it on a par with the pyramids and the Great Wall of China. The idea is to develop the entire Flinders Street railway yards area for cinemas, hotels, motels, offices, gardens, fountains and, of course, a new railway station.

We have been dreaming of getting rid of the old station for a long time. It was opened in 1910, the design the result of a public competition. Most of our architectural disasters are the results of competitions. The building is cunningly described as French Renaissance, but it looks like a curious blend of the Brighton Pavilion, Luna Park and an Indian Mosque. Robert Morley once said: 'I can see what they did. They set out to build St. Paul's in London and halfway through they lost the plans.'

Who will wave this extraordinary wand? It is a joint venture by the Lend Lease Corporation and Oddenino's Property and Investment Co. Ltd. of London. Together they go under the title of Meldon Properties (the name Meldon is a combination of Melbourne and London).

Meldon started a competition through the Sun News-Pictorial to find a name for the project. It was a little like the competition to find a name for Canberra which the Sydney Star ran in 1909. They got back names such as Sydbourne, Melba, Palladium Paradise, Kookaburra, Great Stirringborough, Wheatwoolgold, Thirstyville and Eucalypta.

The range of names that poured in to the Sun was almost beyond description. There were plenty of North Banks, North Yarra Cities. There was Flindarea, Delacombe, Meldonia, Tombstone Territory, the Austral Precinct, Centropolis, Kangaroo Court, Aussie Place, Victoria's Vision Splendid, Coral Sea Towers and Flinders Folly. And there was Spaghetti Junction, 'in honor of all our wonderful migrants.'

And of course, there were those who wanted to name it after everyone in sight. Politicians, except for Sir Robert Menzies, didn't do very well. There was an occasional Bolte, a Deakin, a Lang, and even an appeal for Tommy Bent, but that's all. There was an enthusiastic desire by a great many readers to call it after Ned Kelly. One suggestion was Ned's Nook. Evonne Goolagong and Lionel Rose also did very well. So did Shane Gould, and Shane City sounded very fine. Melbourne's tsar almost as much as Ned Kelly, and so was Germaine Greer. Germaine Place also had quite a ring about it. We had Hawke's Nest, F111 and Cretootha Place. Cretootha was a word taken from toothache and the lady who sent it in said the project would have so many growing pains this would be the logical name for it.

One reader, who wished to call the project Moomba, wanted a great illuminated boomerang which you could walk through, right across Flinders Street. There were many pleas for spacious all-Australian gardens, there was a suggestion for a theatre with an electric roof which would open and close like a retina according to the light.

The grand launching of the project took place at Princes Gate plaza. The name of the project, in huge letters, was hidden under a great burgundy curtain that looked as if it might have been pinched from the stage of the Regent theatre. Sir Henry Bolte was in excellent form. Some school children were present and part of the ceremony was to present them with a scroll, addressed to a future Premier of Victoria, 25 years hence, when the whole project was complete. Sir Henry said with glee: 'There you are. I don't expect to be premier in 25 years time. You can take that as a formal announcement that I will retire.'

Thereupon he announced that the name of the project would be Flinders Gate, and at that very moment the burgundy curtains rolled up. I half expected to see a screen underneath and that we would get some Val Morgan slides. But no, there was the name with a picture of Melbourne and the grand vision of the Melbourne of 1997 behind.

It was a lady who thought of Flinders Gate, Mrs Phyllis Cartwright, of Koondrook on the Murray. She is a postmistress.

The name has the necessary advantage of being simple, dignified and easy to remember. But of course the protest letters will start rolling in. There is bound to be a letter from the gentleman who was so carried away with the fame of Squizzy Taylor that he wanted to call it Squizzy's Place. His should be a most fiery letter.

Source: out and about with Batman: A Grand Vision for Melbourne, The Bulletin, 4 March 1972. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
