Barking About a Bite

4 min read.

First published 06 May 1972

Uncle Ben's launches its new Pal Meaty Bites dog food at the Old Melbourne Motor Inn with a fashion parade inside a giant inflatable dog, a Cinemascope interview, and a banquet for 80 grocery executives and their wives flown in from around Australia.

YOU WANT to know what is the biggest dog food promotion? Such a thing took place last week at the modernly ancient Old Melbourne Motor Inn, the place Robin Boyd once described as Disneyland Downunder.

Uncle Ben's the dog food people, were carefully, carefully planning the American style promotion. They invited 80 grocery executives and their wives from all over Australia to come to Melbourne for the party. They were installed at the Old Melbourne, in suites complete with antique brass bedsteads.

The first event was the buffet lunch on Saturday and Uncle Ben took them all to the Carlton-Richmond match of the day. This was done to keep them away from the hotel where special top secret events and entertainment were being prepared.

Maybe you have heard of those plastic-style mobile hospitals that are used in Vietnam. They are as big as ballrooms, are slug-shaped and are held up by compressed air. Uncle Ben bought one of these, converted it into the shape and colour of a Disney-like puppy and erected it over the car park at the Old Melbourne.

At 7.30 p.m., after cocktails, we all went out to the enormous blow-up thing. It was called 'Pal Dog,' and it seated 200 people. One entered Pal Dog through its mouth just like a piece of dog food. I sat down by the right paw.

The show started with a spectacular fashion parade provided by five of Australia's top fashion houses. It was all compered by Maggi Eckardt. Maggi wore a finale single halter-neck evening gown which plunged to the waist. No bra of course. It inspired all sorts of pal emotions.

After an hour of soothing beauty Maggi disappeared and lo, onto the podium came Bill Peach of 'This Day Tonight.' Bill told us this was Pal's sixth birthday, and that it represented staggering success almost without equal in Australia. Yes, 1966 had been a great year. Sir Robert Menzies became Warden of the Cinque Ports, Prince Charles and L.B.J. came to Australia, St Kilda won its first ever football premiership, and Pal came to Australia. Until then nobody talked much about canned dog food, but look, now Pal was worth $35 million.

But Pal had a new gleam in its eye, and a new product — the result of vast research — a dry dog biscuit-style food: Pal Meaty Bites.

Words can only give you a faint idea of what then took place. There was an avalanche of sound such as you get in the most triumphant million dollar movie moments of films like 'Cleopatra' and 'Ben Hur.' But the electronic equipment was way beyond the capacity of Cinemascope, Cinerama and such. Until then we had not realised that the whole white inside of our vast puppy was an inter-reactive fall-back-and-forth mobile giant multi-screen.

Suddenly we had big screen colour projections all around Pal and on the ceiling as well. There were 12 separate projections: dogs of all shapes and sizes slavering over Pal Meaty Bites. It was almost total brain conditioning like something out of Stanley Kubrick's 'Clockwork Orange.' I wanted to leap out of my seat and grab a dish of Pal Meaty Bites for myself.

Then Bill Peach conducted an interview with Uncle Ben's managing director, Dr Henry Nowik. Except it was done TDT style. A full colour Cinemascope-size Dr Henry Nowik appeared on Pal's behind and Bill Peach asked the projected image questions.

Dr Nowik told Bill that Pal Meaty Bites, the result of vast research, had a soft cereal centre coated with a meat made of liver, beef and marrow-bone, fried to perfection — a sort of steak sandwich for dogs.

Bill Peach put only one devilish question: 'I understand that the preparation of dog food is a very messy business.'

Dr Nowik gave a fatherly smile. 'Even famous interviewers like you, Bill, slip up sometimes.'

We then received the 12-screen treatment. The dogs on screen were replaced by the happy faces of contented owners of contented dogs.

Then came a song:

Let's have a meal time band, Pal Meaty Bites, Pal Meaty Bites, It's the bestest food that am.

After that we all trooped off to dinner in the Calderisi restaurant. They had a real banquet: fine wines and dishes such as Duckling Hausfrau. When everyone finally went off to their brass bedsteads they found a copy of Vogue under their door with the message: 'Thank you for coming to our Presentation. We've enjoyed having you with us, and as a token of our appreciation, we've arranged to have each issue of Vogue delivered to your home for the next 12 months.'

That's just for dog food. Just wait until someone does something for humans.

Source: out and about with Batman: Barking About a Bite, The Bulletin, 6 May 1972. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
