The Jeanagers

3 min read.

First published 19 Feb 1972

A visit to Jean City, the new jeans shop in Swanston Street, Melbourne, where Carlton brewery beer-label T-shirts sit alongside Wrangler and Levis, and proprietor David Anderson's philosophy is that anything too fancy would drive customers straight back to Myers.

AN ESTABLISHMENT by the name of Jean City has opened in Swanston Street, Melbourne. In one window they have Bazza McKenzie T-shirts. If you buy one of these then you will sport on your chest a large picture of Bazza holding a pot of the foaming article. In the other window they have beer-label T-shirts. The labels are large and they cover the complete range of products from the Carlton brewery. So those who have a passion for filling themselves with beer can now have the added comfort of actually making themselves look like a beer bottle.

Inside the shop there is rack after rack after rack of jeans. On the walls there are posters. One shows two naked boys sitting back to back. One boy is making the unlikely statement: 'You put all your hang ups down when your body's wrapped in Wrangler jeans.' In another poster a character in Levis has his arm around the waist of a naked girl. In his hand he has a revolver. The revolver seems curiously unnecessary. The girl, obviously, is very friendly.

The whole place is terribly rugged; wooden barrels for decor, unstained timber for racks. The shop girls are in bare feet and the proprietor David Anderson is in jeans, open-necked shirt, and he has a week's growth of hair on his chin.

'Know why the place looks so rugged?' he said. 'Because the kids like it that way. If we had this all fancy with carpet on the floor and stuff, they wouldn't come in. The place is going like a bomb. And the reason why they come here instead of going to Myers is because they can't stand being served by someone in a suit.'

One noticed that there were four changing cubicles protected by rather flimsy screens of muslin. 'They are not labelled His and Hers,' said Mr Anderson proudly. 'All the gear here is made for males and the girls wear it too.' One gathered that if exclusively female jeans were created for the store then they would be utterly spurned.

'We also tie-dye here, to order. You can have your jeans tie-dyed, say, to match the T-shirt you are wearing.' Tie-dying, I learned, was getting that ghastly smear into your jeans as if something terribly wrong had taken place at the Chinese laundry.

Recently I met Mr David Martin of Amco, a top designer of jeans. I asked him at what point jeans attained their ultimate wretched perfection.

'Just that last second before you fade them,' said he. Indeed he shuddered a little at the thought that anyone should want to wear them new. He gave a few tips on making them desirably frightful.

Some people like to age them by walking around in the sea, then standing in the sun. Salt and sunrays seem to do something. They fade and shrink into the correctly decrepit form.

Jeans new, jeans old; jeans coming, jeans going. There's no doubt about it, jeans are the absolute badge of a generation.

Source: Lifestyle: The Jeanagers, The Bulletin, 19 February 1972. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
