Feathering the Top Eagle's Pad

5 min read.

First published 20 May 1972

Melbourne's new wave of luxury city apartment towers, from Michael Edgley's penthouse at Park Towers in Spring Street to the $250,000 24th-floor suite at 99 Spring Street with marble from Greece and tiles from Italy, and the more modest 135-flat Lonsdale Heights for those who prefer their galvanised iron roof-top views.

BACK in the old days all the very nice people of the Western District had their city apartments in Collins Street. The ultimate in city pads was Melbourne Mansions where the CRA building now stands. Alas, rising property values and falling wool prices killed all that.

Now the move is back to the city with multi-million dollar towers. And they are being occupied by a very different breed. Young Mr Michael Edgley, a millionaire at 27, and managing director of the Edgley-J. C. Williamson empire is a good example. He has the 15th floor of Park Towers at 201 Spring Street. There are 20 floors of considerable comfort. The decor is by Perth interior designer, Jill Haabdjohn. He flew her over just to do the job. The basic colour scheme is black complemented by dark mirrors. Mr Edgley even has a smoked glass, mirrored ceiling in his bedroom.

Parked down below in the basement are the cars — Michael has his bright yellow Rolls-Royce Corniche drophead and his mother has a white Jaguar. Also in the building are Sir Charles McGrath, Sir Richard Kirby, Judge Frost and Mr Fletcher Jones.

There is another Own-Your-Own apartment tower, 21 storeys, at 15 Collins Street. This has mostly one-bedroom flats and despite some beautiful prose in the brochure about a 'revolutionary new concept in city living' and the 'spectacle of the city night skyline with its flickering river and sleeping parks,' the agents have struggled to find buyers.

Nowadays city dwellers don't come from Hamilton and Camperdown anymore; they come from Toorak. Mr Jack Edgar of J. V. Edgar & Co Pty Ltd said there were a great many people living in big empty houses in Toorak. Their children had married and gone off. So he made a survey and came up with a suggestion. Why not live in large, luxurious apartments, right in Spring Street, overlooking the Fitzroy Gardens?

So 99 Spring Street, 25 storeys with 44 apartments, is the result. Each O.Y.O. is 22 squares, two apartments to a floor, and the average price is $80,000. Eighty per cent of them were sold before the building was finished. You get three bathrooms, dishwasher, refrigerator, washing machine and all mod cons in the price. You work out your own decor.

Your correspondent went on a guided tour of the new building. Mr Edgar was a little coy about revealing the names of those who had bought. We went straight to the 24th floor which is occupied entirely by a penthouse. It is 45 squares and has been sold for $250,000.

'The owner wanted it bare,' said Mr Edgar, 'no fittings at all. He wants to furnish it to his own taste. I believe he intends to spend another $100,000 on doing that. He is bringing tiles from Italy, marble from Greece and God knows what. I don't know what layout he will use, but there are fittings for six bedrooms and six bathrooms. See that corner over there — he will turn that into a half-indoor, half-outdoor living area. It will have a large plastic cupola overhead which will open up like an eye.'

Of course, the area is enormous and when finished it will unquestionably be the top eagle's pad in Melbourne. As this gentleman has the whole floor, he has glass all the way round like a lighthouse. He can see Mount Macedon and the Dandenongs; he can almost pat the spires of St Patrick's; he can look down into the garden of the Melbourne Club, and on a clear day he can see right across to the heads of Port Phillip Bay.

This apartment, like all the other apartments, has the latest in security systems. The entry by speaking box is old hat. This one has closed circuit television, as well. So the character who pressed your button downstairs is automatically revealed on your TV screen. If you don't like the look of him, or her (say, you already have a female in your flat), you don't have to answer. You can pretend nobody's home.

The telephone is also connected directly to the swimming pool, the sauna bath and the gymnasium. On the fifth floor there is a swimming pool, a poolside cafe, plus this sauna and gymnasium. The fifth floor has a series of smaller flats. Mr Edgar had the idea that some people would want separate accommodation for their chauffeurs, their housemaids and their family friends. All the apartments have been bought.

I wandered through the apartments. Some have been divided into small rooms. Some owners preferred practically no partitions, just a superb open sweep. One I noted had glass walls in the bathroom. The owner's greatest joy presumably will be to see the girls under the shower.

But if you don't want to live there, you can go round to Lonsdale Street. Here, approaching completion, is one of the biggest buildings in Melbourne, worth five and a half million dollars. It is split down the middle — half is the Chateau Hotel leased to Commodore Hotels and the other half is Lonsdale Heights, with 135 flats.

The flats are small, mainly one-bedroom, and they will rent for $30 to $35 a week. The views are hardly up to those of 99 Spring Street. From the lower floors you get a most intimate glimpse of the city's galvanised iron roof tops. But, wait, there is a glorious swimming pool on the roof with tinted glass panels around the side and you can gaze at the city there as you recline under your cabana.

And if you like collecting brochures, this one is a pearl. The message reads: 'Living at Lonsdale Heights is different from living in any other place in Melbourne. You're right on top of the excitement of the city... no more peak period rush... instead a casual free and easy stroll home, and you can watch dusk spread westward, lighting a million lamps on the way.'
