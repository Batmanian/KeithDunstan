Take Me to Your Folk Museum!

5 min read.

First published 01 Apr 1972

The heritage tourism boom sweeping Victoria's country towns, from Echuca's paddlesteamer wharf to Ballarat's Sovereign Hill, as local councils discover that history and nostalgia make marvellous motel-fillers.

DO YOU know how Sir Henry Bolte treats you if you go to him and say you have a great historic stayer in your area? Well, if you do have some beauty in your area, it is vital to adopt top security measures. If you can get Sir Henry to part with as much as $200,000 from the Treasury, it goes down in a descending scale to say around $15,000, depending on how many votes are in your area.

The folk museum thing is particularly interesting. Your pioneer has just been on a grand tour. Land out of the blue in almost any Victorian town right now and you can say: 'Take me to your folk museum!' If they haven't got one, the chances are they will show you the plans for a beauty that is coming up. History and nostalgia have become a marvellous saleable thing — the perfect idea for filling motel beds.

The latest in the market are Echuca, Stawell and Bendigo. The important thing is to get a smart idea, different from your neighbour, and go for it. Echuca is after $200,000 to restore the old part of town to those balmy days when it was Australia's greatest inland port. Most of the money will go to restoring the vast old wharf. They won't have to do much to the rest — old Echuca is exactly as it was. They even have a dear little waterfront brothel which has been much admired by the National Trust. It is brick with five little rooms downstairs, five little rooms upstairs, with the lovely comfort of a fireplace in every room. At present it is not being used for the purpose for which it was designed, but come restoration, the committee will not be shy about its origins. Perhaps they will even fill it with buxom 1880 type girls as a further tourist attraction.

Stawell has a grant of $200,000 to build a complete pioneer town. Bendigo already has the central Deborah mine as a tourist attraction. It is opening a joss house at Easter, and with the departure of the Bendigo trams, the plan is to run a special tourist tram from the mine to the joss house. You must appreciate that in the nostalgia stakes Bendigo has the most lovable trams in the whole country. They have four Birney cars, imported from the US. These were one-man affairs built in Depression times and are now very rare. Special touring parties of tram lovers from all over, even from the US, go to Bendigo just to feast their eyes on those Birney cars.

Jeparit and Warracknabeal are twin towns in the Mallee. Jeparit has a pioneer museum, Warracknabeal a farm machinery museum. Actually both are tapping the same well. The Jeparit museum is stacked with a quite marvellous array of Mallee rollers, vintage harvesters, antique tractors and steam engines. So is Warracknabeal. Now if a talker of one could nick a prized object from a neighbouring museum, it would be a matter of 'ow, Ow, the precious object went to the enemy.'

At Rutherglen there is a private venture which is worth inspection. Peter Poynton, a Melbourne restaurateur, and Bill McPherson are restoring the Land Out winery, which had its last vintage back in 1954. The winery is one of Victoria's most eloquent pieces of architecture. It looks as if it was designed by the same gentleman who did the Kremlin. Well, Messrs Poynton and McPherson have great plans for a motel, restaurant and vineyard, and one of their cunning motel hopes is to get Phar Lap's stable from Melbourne. One could think of no more sacred shrine.

Beechworth has a coach museum and Ned Kelly's old cell, and Castlemaine has restored an old market.

But the venture which has really started the nostalgia stakes is at Swan Hill. This is really something and worth coming a long way to see. It all started back in 1966 with a huge old paddle steamer and a couple of pioneer buildings. Now the town has 50 buildings, and the whole place has been done up with remarkable style. Every night now they have a 'Sound and Light' performance. The tourists can also take trips around the town in a specially designed open train. The place is a marvel of modern electronics with spotlights and special sound effects provided by microphones hidden in trees. But as a business it is a success almost beyond belief. Over 200,000 people came through last year and their takings soared.

The biggest improver and a near rival for Swan Hill is Ballarat's Sovereign Hill. Ballarat is spending big on a recreation of the days of mining, and now they are completing a mighty steam-driven ten-head battery which operates at 700 blows a minute. Ballarat citizens don't know what they are in for. When that goes into action the sound will be heard almost to Bacchus Marsh.

The park has been successful beyond the committee's happiest dreams — they had half a million people through in the first 14 months. There is just one saddening thing. Ballarat is not far enough away from Melbourne. Most of those half million did not stay overnight, so it is terribly necessary to make the museum big enough for a two-day stay.

But the nostalgia stakes are becoming too competitive. Just make a noise like a traction engine, a vintage candle machine, or a dear old horse-drawn buggy and you will have 200 talent scouts from as many museums on your doorstep. Funny, in the old days they only used to scout the bush for footballers.
