How Groceries Went Foreign

5 min read.

First published 08 Apr 1972

Jim Cairns' warning that foreign interests would control half of Australian manufacturing within a decade, illustrated by a walk around a local supermarket where almost nothing from cornflakes to pet food is still Australian-owned.

THERE HAS been a melancholy air about Dr Jim Cairns these past six months. What with the Vietnam War expiring like an old car tyre, he has been light on eloquent causes. But last week he was inflating back to top form, saying foreign interests within ten years would control half of Australia's manufacturing industry. They had at least 25 per cent already so they had only to expand at their present rate to get 50 per cent within ten years. But, he said, there was no precise detailed information, no tabulated facts on foreign capital in Australia, and he attacked the Government for making no attempt to find out what was going on.

The next day your correspondent called at the local supermarket. Our family grocer was burning. 'Did you read Jim Cairns on foreign industry taking over Australia? Twenty-five per cent! He doesn't know what's going on. I can't get any detailed information either, but I would guarantee that 90 per cent of all the stuff sold in an entirely Australian supermarket is produced by foreign-controlled companies. There's damn little that is Australian left. Look, come and have a walk around and I'll show you.'

The first glittering display island was covered with corn flakes, Coco Pops, Yummy Bits — all that sort of thing. Our grocer went on: 'Kellogg-Nabisco, Quaker Oats and John Bull are American. The only Australian firm left is Sanitarium.

'Soups? They've got a pretty good grip there. Heinz — American. Campbells — Canadian. Rosella is really Unilever, which is European. We have Edgells, which is a good seller. That belongs to Petersville, but their shares are pretty depressed at the moment. In the packet soups, Continental is Unilever and Maggi is Nestlé's, which is Swiss.'

The next gorgeous island was covered in boxes of detergent. 'None of this is Australian, not a damn thing,' he thundered. 'Now look at this: Surf, Softly, Rinso, Drive, Omo, Lux, Torrent — they're all Lever and Kitchen. Don't you think that's fun? They all advertise like mad and compete against each other and they're all made by the one firm. And here's Velvet Soap. Dear old Velvet Soap. I bet you thought that was Australian. Nope — Lever and Kitchen. And here we have Punch, Spree, Fab, Ajax and Cold Power — all Colgate-Palmolive. There are a few little ones that are Australian but over there you have the bulk of the market.

'Deep frozen foods? Except for a couple of local ones, Deep Freeze is almost all controlled by foreign interests.' He ran his eye over the deep freeze bins and spotted Sara Lee Blueberry Cheese, Devil's Food Cake and Apple Danish. 'What about these?' I asked. 'I'll give you one teeny weeny guess,' said he. 'American.'

'Biscuits? They're not so bad. Arnott's is a very big firm. Tinned fruits? They're not too bad either with firms like AJC. But here's something interesting. Mrs Trewern's jams — they're produced by Cadbury-Schweppes. Now there's a foreign combine for you. This stuff always used to be labelled "Mrs Trewern's home-made jam" — now they have dropped the "home-made."

'Here are the beauty aids. Everything we sell here is owned by foreign companies — Johnson and Johnson, Bristol Myers, Colgate-Palmolive, Unilever-Rexona.

'You want some tissues? Scotties — Bowater-Scott, American. Or Kleenex? Kimberly-Clark — American.

'Pet food — my dear friends your little dog and your little puss is almost entirely eating foreign. Luv is owned by Carnation Milk which is American. Uncle Ben's is also American. Whiskas, Chunky Chum, Jets, Crunchy — all those are foreign owned.'

'But here's one,' I cried, grabbing a beaut pet food box labelled 'Good-O.' 'You're dreaming,' said our man. 'That's produced by Uncle Ben's. If you want Australian pet food, the best ones left are Pets, Tucker Box and Biff.'

We stopped at the soft drinks section. Cottees are General Foods; Coca Cola and Schweppes are foreign; but Tarax is still Australian. 'Someone ought to buy shares in Tarax,' said our man. 'Someone must want it.'

'Milk,' he continued, 'is practically all foreign. Tongala Milk products are owned by Nestlé's. Then things like Milo and Ovaltine — Nestlé's. The instant coffees such as Nescafé, MJB, Maxwell House — all foreign. Robert Tims have been taken over by Tetley. Tuckfield? That's Australian.

'In tinned food, Imperial is foreign, Mayfair is Intercontinental Packers. Harvest Label is Plumrose, and that's Danish. Tom Piper is Australian. And, of course, you know about Rosella and Heinz. Masterfoods have been taken over by an American firm. My dear boy, the only old solid firms left in our industry are Arnott's, Marrickville, Kiwi and Tom Piper. And if you like to look at wine, there are only three really big ones left which are Australian — Penfolds, Seppelts and Mildara.'

'But the point is,' as Jim Cairns says, 'you don't really know. Nobody has ever looked into the whole thing properly. We may not own anything.'

I bought a Stars and Stripes T-shirt which was a special at the check-out desk and fled.

Source: out and about with Batman: How Groceries Went Foreign, The Bulletin, 8 April 1972. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
