A New Religion for Winter

5 min read.

First published 22 Apr 1972

Melbourne's devotion to Australian Rules football described as a full-blown religion, from a widow's request to inter her husband's ashes in Richmond's goal square to the Victorian government's approval of VFL Football Pools and the imminent launch of Tattslotto.

FOOTBALL is the most deeply held religious faith in Melbourne. Oh, there are other faiths. Horse worship is also very strong, but football is the more intense.

Last week the Herald reported the case of a gentleman who, just before he died last October, expressed the wish that his ashes be buried on the Richmond football ground. Richmond now uses the Melbourne Cricket Ground so the widow went to Ian Johnson, secretary of the Melbourne Cricket Club. Ian Johnson refused the request. The club was unable to cope with the number of football lovers who wanted their ashes to be buried there. So the lady went to the secretary of the Richmond Football Club, Alan Schwab. The club still uses the old Richmond ground for practice matches and such. Mr Schwab gave permission. And so the ashes were buried right in the goal square.

If this sort of thing proceeds any further with worshippers of the game then the goal squares of our football grounds could take on the sanctity of the crypt of St Paul's or Westminster Abbey.

Kindly don't look upon this as surprising. There is in fact a very clear trend towards the Church of The Holy Australian Rules in Melbourne, with Sir Maurice Nathan as the first football bishop. Sir Maurice has just resigned from the Melbourne City Council so that he can give more time to his task as president of the Victorian Football League and his Victorian archdiocese.

You have read already of the conflict between GTV-9 and the broadcasting Control Board over football replay telecasts on Sunday mornings. The Television Act permits the broadcasting of only religious or educational programs before noon on Sunday. The Control Board has objected to Channel Nine's use of replays and for a time there were threats of legal action. This now has subsided.

However, the board should realise it is interfering with deeply held religious beliefs. The football replay is actually a religious service. Michael Willesee on Channel Nine's 'A Current Affair' demonstrated this the other night. He had a priest intoning from the gospel out in the centre of the ground. I don't know what gospel it was; perhaps it was the Gospel of the Early Commandments as written in the 1850s by some of the original holy disciples like Harrison and Wills, when they dictated that cricketers shall not play in the winter.

Willesee gave a few shots of typical scenes of worship at the Melbourne cricket temple. When a goal was scored the chanting and moaning noises made by the congregation were not unlike what you hear if you worship Mohammed. There were many devout acts reminiscent of eastern religions. Many people carried idols to which were attached long coloured streamers. These they waved during the most sacred moments of the service.

Sadly the Church of Australian Rules, like most religions, is not receiving enough money in the collection plate. One of the greatest agonies is finding sufficient funds for the new cathedral at Waverley. The church is hoping eventually it will take congregations of well over 100,000.

But the dream for the past five years has been football pools. Sir Henry Bolte, a racehorse owner, and a good Victorian Reform Council man has always refused the legislation. But last week football pools finally received the Victorian government's blessing. You see, until now we have had a frustrating time. We have been able to bet on dogs. We have been able to bet on horses. We have been able to bet on horses and carts at the trots. But this is the first time we are able to bet on humans.

Details of the scheme have yet to be announced but it is believed you will have to choose the order in which you think the teams will score most points. For a 50 cent entry you could win $150,000.

The government has not announced the rate of impost — it is believed the Football Commission will give the VFL one per cent. Already this is a matter of awful grief at Harrison Chapter House. The pay-out percentage to hospitals and charities is almost twice that of the TAB. Public and municipal grounds do very nicely but the VFL gets only half a cent from every 50 cents staked.

The football pools will be run by Tattersalls. Indeed we are moving into an interesting period. New South Wales is the fruit machines state and you can describe Victoria as the Tattersalls state. Indeed the time is approaching when Tattersalls may be able to take over the entire Treasury. The annual budget could be handled by a Tattersalls consultation with Sir Henry drawing the Marble. Next month Tattersalls is introducing Tattslotto. If you pick six numbers right you can win $90,000.

So that is the way religion is heading in Melbourne. Do you think it is odd for us to worship football? I don't think so. The footballs are made of pigskin and animal worship is widely and reverently accepted in many countries.

Source: out and about with Batman: A New Religion for Winter, The Bulletin, 22 April 1972. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
