Sock It to Me, Pharaoh!

5 min read.

First published 16 Sep 1972

A report on St Paul's Cathedral's rock opera season in which Russell Morris performed 'Joseph and the Amazing Technicolor Dreamcoat', Johnny Farnham starred as the Prodigal Son and Colleen Hewett followed in 'David and a Golden Throne', all to packed houses amid protests outside.

THE CHURCHES are trying very hard in Melbourne. The Wesley Church has a billboard out asking: 'Was God an astronaut?' And, if he was, you could well ask: 'Was he a Methodist astronaut?' Then last week Scots Church had Mr Ross Smith reading the lesson. Mr Smith is captain of the St Kilda football team. God could call on no greater drawcard.

But wait, you should see what is happening at St Paul's Cathedral. It is becoming such an entertainment centre it would surprise no one if Harry Miller made a takeover bid. As they say down at Hoyts: 'As hard-tops go, it's a big'n with 1200-seat capacity.' The location too is splendid. It is on the intersection of two tramlines. Flinders Street station is on the opposite corner and immediately over the road there is Young and Jackson's Hotel, fantastically convenient for getting a beer during the interval.

In recent times they have had rock music, drama and ballet. Right now the cathedral is in the midst of a rock opera season. They opened with Russell Morris in Joseph and the amazing technicolor dreamcoat. If you haven't heard of Russell Morris then ask anyone under 25. He's big deal. The opera was by the same team that wrote Jesus Christ Superstar.

It was a little startling to observe what they had done to the old cathedral. Right in the middle of the nave by the lectern they built a stage. On either side by the 'In loving memory of' brass plates, usually to people who had given handsomely in the 1890s, were two huge amplifiers. Then 15 feet up on scaffolding were batteries of lights.

They had a rock group called 'Chain' which could really pump out the big sound. For the first half of the show they gave us straight rock. The bearded pianist in his T-shirt opened with a little message about Shane Gould... 'Great news about Shane Gould... I reckon Shane Gould is worth a prayer tonight.'

Meanwhile outside the cathedral there were protest groups. They were just as young as those inside and they carried posters: 'It is written my house shall be called a house of prayer' and 'Real power is only in Christ.' They said the cathedral was intended for the worship of God and not as a venue for pop stars and rock.

But Joseph was an opera that really moved. The audience loved it when Nic Gazzana as Pharaoh rolled over on his back on the floor with the microphone high in the air, and Russell Morris shouted the line: 'Sock it to me, Pharaoh.'

Theatrically it was spectacular. Joseph's technicolor dreamcoat came down on wires from an enormous height way up aloft under the main spire.

Oh, it was a big success. On four nights they averaged an audience of 1000 a night. The precentor, David Sankey, said he was very happy with the behaviour. They had one agonising problem. What would happen if the be-jeaned rock audience wanted to smoke? Not a very nice thing to do in a cathedral, and it wouldn't have been easy stopping it. 'Don't smoke in church, kids, sounds a bit wet coming from a bloke in a dog collar,' he said. Fortunately the whole thing was beautifully solved by the health commission, which forbade it anyhow.

The next opera was The Prodigal Son with Johnny Farnham in the title role. Of Mr Farnham the program said: 'The king of Australian op/pop... Older women want to mother him, and the teeny-boppers say he's the sexiest young man who ever trod the stage of a concert hall.'

This time the show was entirely locally-created — music by Brian Adamson, and lyrics by Ray Lee. Once again the place was packed. The ushers wore white flared trousers, black shirts and big velvet bow ties.

Each member of the cast moved with a microphone nursing it like a special lolly. The old biblical story had quite a shot in the arm.

John, the prodigal son, gets mighty bored with life on the farm: 'Baby John couldn't give a damn, bored with life ad nauseam.' So he skips off to a beaut casbah in Tangier. Next you see Johnny in a fantastic check suit with iridescent lurex lapels and he sings:

Be 'in' — go gay and hetero.
Porno is there if ya wanna glow,
As long as everyone's got the dough.
Swap wives — bigamy makes you thrive;
Grog on — forget about point 05;
Get boozed — enjoy every camel-drive.
That dirty 'r' laugh's a good epitaph.

Then he gets on to the drugs. This song was sung Al Jolsen style, banjo and all.

Mescalin, heroin, pethedrine and dexedrine,
Morphine, methedrine, benzedrine, amphetamine;
barbiturate to opiate, hallucinate and escalate;
You'll be greater by far.
Cocaine in the vein; paradise is just a grain.
Mary Jane will ease your pain and help you out to blow your brain.
Hallucinogen again, again, again and once again,
To find your Shangri-La.

Then little Johnny goes on an awe-inspiring trip. He writhes on the floor, screams, moans and shudders. Meanwhile you get about 2000 watts of sheer organ, guitar and amplifier power plus flickering lights. Good stuff. It must have very nearly shattered the angels in the stained glass windows.

Well, Johnny eventually comes to. He realises the shocking errors of his ways and returns home to the joy of dad and the near-fury of his elder brother. There is some nice soothing stuff from the cathedral choir, dressed in T-shirts.

Then there is the rollicking coming-home all-is-forgiven party. Johnny comes out in the most sensational kaftan gown you ever saw — reds, greens, gold, with half the lurex and sequins in Melbourne, plus a huge, gorgeous star of David hanging around his neck.

The audience went crazy. They whistled, clapped, called for four encores, threw streamers, showed an enthusiasm rarely seen before in a cathedral.

Now we get Colleen Hewett, the Godspell girl, in David and a Golden Throne. I have one request — they shift a few lounge seats across from the Hoyts Regent next door. Anglicans must suffer awful posterior penance during sermons.

Source: out and about with Batman: Sock it to me, Pharaoh!, The Bulletin, 16 September 1972. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
