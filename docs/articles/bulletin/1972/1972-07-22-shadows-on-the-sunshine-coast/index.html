Shadows on the Sunshine Coast

5 min read.

First published 22 Jul 1972

A visit to the Gold Coast during the Winter Bowls Carnival finds mayor Bruce Small championing $99 package tours to offset cyclone damage and lost tourists, while the construction of a 17-storey beachfront tower sparks a protest campaign over shadows, erosion and the relentless ugliness of outdoor advertising.

IT IS overwhelming, even a little depressing, to meet Alderman Bruce Small, mayor of the Gold Coast. He is now the Gold Coast representative in Queensland state parliament. The final counting was a long drawn out affair. Weeks went by until finally Bruce Small just managed to edge out Alderman Jack McIlwain who stood for the Liberal Party. Bruce Small may have spent most of his life in Melbourne but very wisely he stood for the Country Party.

Oh, he campaigned cleverly. He used his old slogan: 'Think big — vote Small,' then added another particularly useful line for Small lovers: 'Better the devil you know than the devil you don't know.' So he became a member of parliament at 76 years — an Australian record. Bruce Small goes further. He claims it is a world record. No doubt it gives him a feeling of humility when he considers that other politicians like Sir Henry Bolte drop out at an almost infant 64.

He looks ferociously fit, like an ageing weightlifter. He awakens at 6.30am, does keep-fit exercises, swims, prefers stairs to lifts, and doesn't smoke, drink, chew or gamble. He serves on over 40 committees around the coast and works a 16-hour day. But he does not plan to give up his job as mayor — he sees no problem in adding MP duties to his already crowded 24 hours.

It hasn't been a good year for the Gold Coast. They had cyclones and eternal rain. It is not easy to find out exact rainfall figures. You see at Surfers Paradise there is no official rain gauge. The tourist department maintains the sound public relations view of continual sunshine on the Coast so a rain gauge is unnecessary.

But it is not only the weather. There are so many nice cheap package tours to Fiji, Hong Kong, Singapore, Paris. The nice people of Toorak who would normally spend their winters in paradise now skip off to Europe instead.

Bruce Small estimates they have lost $20 million worth of business this year so he has been down in Melbourne beating the drum for Sun Fun Pty Ltd which is offering package tours from $99 upwards.

Right now they are doing rather better because the Gold Coast is host to the Gold Coast Winter Bowls Carnival and 4000 have come in from all over Australia and New Zealand. Up and down Cavill Avenue you see nice old gentlemen in their white hats, white trousers and blazers. Then there are the ladies in white hats and skirts that come right down within hello distance of their shoes. They look a curious leftover from the 19th century.

Meanwhile the council has made the dramatic move of abolishing parking meters. For one terrible moment we concluded that this would mean the end of the meter maids. But no, you will be pleased to hear that these girls in their gold lamé bikinis are still on the prowl. But now they put polite cards under your windscreen wiper asking you not to stay longer than two hours.

However the place needs more parking space so they have worked out an ingenious plan. Say you have a development site, the council will offer $30 per week for each free parking space you provide. This is a good deal and people are pulling down buildings to make parking space. You know Sea Breeze, the three storey block right on the esplanade? That is coming down. The swimming pool where John and Ilsa Konrads once trained is now being used for burning off rubbish. If this develops much more there will be marvellous accommodation for cars at Surfers but not much for people. You will be able to send your car up to the Coast for a nice holiday.

Actually Surfers Paradise would attract more people if it were not so determinedly ugly. To the innocent eye, planning does not appear to exist unless they are planning a horror. The sheer gurgitation of outdoor advertising outdoes even Kings Cross. They have advertising all over the buildings, over the litter bins, over the seats, over the bus stop shelters and they even have hoardings on poles also covered with signs.

The row of the month is over the construction of the new 'Allawee,' a 17 storey 'prestigious' block of units. You may remember cyclones did devastating things to the beach here last autumn and the observer's still innocent mind might have thought no one would ever again build close to the beach. But this block is closer to the beach than any of the other Miami towers. It is virtually right on it. Workmen have been sinking piles which have not only disfigured the beach but caused further erosion with constant water-pumping. At a time when the council and its mayor are seeking Commonwealth assistance for beach restoration it is strange that this sort of thing is allowed to happen.

A strong protest group is fighting 'Allawee,' claiming that such buildings throw shadows on the sand, and that such shadows turn the poor sunbather into a nomad. They claim too that such buildings with their absentee landlords are future slums.

I'm not worried by that word 'prestigious' on the hoarding. I looked it up in my Shorter Oxford dictionary. 'Prestige' or 'Prestigious' means a delusion; an illusion to blindfold, to dazzle the eyes; a deception, an imposture; influence or reputation derived from previous character.

But otherwise the place hasn't changed much. Big, busty girls swelling out of their bikinis pad bare-footed down the main street. And in the Beachcomber Beer Garden I spotted this sign: 'The proprietor regrets that he cannot accept any responsibility for customers injured in the rush to leave this premises at closing time.' I am told they really mean it. They get up to 1000 people in that beer garden. At 10pm a holiday maker is pretty desperate to get to a bar that hasn't closed.
