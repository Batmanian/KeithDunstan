The Winter of Their Discontent

4 min read.

First published 24 Jun 1972

Melbourne's warm and snowless June of 1972 leaves ski-resort operators nursing heavy losses, while Keith Dunstan reports from Falls Creek on the fashion designer lodges, ski-gear merchants and the Thai-roofed retreat of the city's 'Duckfeather king.'

CAN YOU picture a June scene on the Yarra? Early morning mists with the sun filtering through, giving priceless McCubbin-like impressionist scenes. Can you imagine our City Square in early winter? Chaps sunbathing in front of St Paul's, in the foreground while members of the Hare Krishna movement chant happily in the background.

We are trying to think of new slogans for our town: 'Come to Luscious Melbourne — Land of the Winter Sun.' Some are even considering changing the name of St Kilda to Sunstroke Strip.

While these last few weeks have been enchanting for sun lovers, for others they have been a horror. In Collins and Burke Street the ski retailers have big, sad, heartbreaking eyes. They had hoped for the most exquisite blizzards to come some time around Easter, so that people will rush into Myer's, Buckley's and Georges for their thick winter coats and tweed outfits. If you don't get that sort of weather by June the good citizens of Victoria are convinced that they can skip the winter season and save their money for the spring. And that is what's happening.

For snow lovers the situation is even more grave. By tradition the Queen's Birthday weekend is the official opening of the snow season. For the fortnight before snow people burned their candles and prayed for superbly hideous weather with savagely satisfying gales direct from Mawson.

Day after day they looked at those extraordinary weather maps covered with near-motionless big circles. The result was practically zero snow at any skiing resort in Victoria.

Your correspondent went to Falls Creek, which is way up in the north-east of the state, more than 200 miles from Melbourne. Of course, it is not Thredbo. We have heard rumours about these NSW resorts; that they have chandeliers, indoor heated swimming pools with monsteria deliciosa all over the place, piped music in every room, and lashings of pink marble.

Falls Creek is not a large resort. It is smaller and more exclusive with only 2000 beds and the committee management there insists that it will never be allowed to go beyond 3000 beds.

You could describe Falls Creek as the Portsea of the ski resorts. It is not true that you have to announce your clubs before they will let you in, but it is very nice. Keith Hyland, the famous 'Duckfeather king,' has a lodge there. The word is that he had it built in Thailand, dismantled it, shipped it to Falls Creek and rebuilt it. Can you imagine a Thai ski lodge? It has a roof in pink tiles, it has upturned dragon tails, and little brass bells tinkling in the alpine breeze — probably to ward off the après-ski spirits.

Fashion designer Norma Tullo has a lodge with timber balconies over stone and it is pretty enough to get a grading from the National Trust. It is lovely inside. Even the bathroom has ankle-deep carpet.

As for the long weekend, the mountain was totally green under brilliant sunshine. Looking at the motionless ski tows, the idle ski instructors, the non-working over-snow vehicles, the fashion shops with unsold ski gear and the booked-up lodges, nobody could estimate the cost of a snowless weekend.

Businessman Barry Lawther said it cost him $6000 alone in unhired gear. He told of the precarious life in snow country. 'Look at it this way,' he said, 'farmers have four seasons to make their way. We have only one and it lasts barely 10 weeks. People in Melbourne and Adelaide watch the weather reports and as soon as they see there's no snow they cancel. If there's snow, I'm fine. If I go through the season without it then I'm history on this mountain.'

But what do you do when there's no snow? Well, there were some damned good chop picnics on the weekend your correspondent went up. There was also a cycle of parties where, by inviting back and forth, you met the same people throughout the day.

There was a great deal of time to discuss ski fashions. Last year we went through the jeans scene, and even the hippy look. It was important to look impoverished. The quality of your skiing was in indirect proportion to the dowdiness of your gear. The fellow in jeans could be another Malcolm Milne.

That's finished now, I'm told. You can be brilliant again. You can wear flared trousers, Bondi Jets and big black floppy hats — that's the gear straight from the smart resorts of Europe. One would suggest you get something large, black and floppy with a very wide brim.

Anyway if there is no snow it won't be a problem will it?
