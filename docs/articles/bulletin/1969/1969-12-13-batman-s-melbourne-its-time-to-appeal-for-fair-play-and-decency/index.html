It's Time to Appeal for Fair Play and Decency

6 min read.

First published 13 Dec 1969

The Albert Park Lake weed Elodea canadensis has driven the rowing club to the Yarra, the yacht club to despair and the power boat club to Lake Eppalock, despite $60,000 spent over ten years on copper sulphate, a $5000 English weed-cutting boat that only distributed the growth, Senator Kennelly's rejected proposal to import South American manatees, and a yabbie scheme abandoned when universities and fishermen offered three times the purchase price, leading Keith Dunstan to suggest appealing to the weed's sense of decency and fair play.

FRIENDS, in recent times a great deal of publicity has been given to the Loch Ness Monster. Some experts claim, indeed, that there could be as many as five monsters in Loch Ness. But how many people have heard of the Albert Park Lake Monster?

We have warned before of the Albert Park Lake Monster, but its menace is growing by the hour. As you know, the dreadful all-enveloping weed that takes on human characteristics is a favourite in science-fiction. Some years back, BBC-TV had a show called 'The Quatermass Experiment.' It all started when an astronaut returned in his capsule from outer space. When the scientists opened the capsule the spaceman was absorbed by a frightening growing thing.

The English Apollo people obviously did not take the proper quarantine measures adopted by the Americans and his growth thing grew and grew all over London. The Londoners tried everything from artillery to germ warfare — nothing could stop it. Worst of all, it swallowed up people and absorbed their brains in the process. Finally, just as it was going right over the city, creeping over Westminster Abbey, something terribly British happened. They appealed to the weed's decency and fair play. The authorities spoke to those brains which had been absorbed and they committed an act of self-sacrifice. They destroyed themselves from within and the weed shrivelled up and died.

Well, some time in the past an Armstrong or an Aldrin has done the wrong thing, because an awful, malevolent weed has taken full control of Albert Park Lake. It is a strange green slime that spreads across the surface of the water. Rowing has now ceased. The Albert Park Rowing Club, famous on the lake all this century, has shifted its headquarters to the Yarra and is building over there a new $35,000 boathouse. The Albert Park Yacht Club announced last week that it had reached the end of its tether and no longer could compete against the monster. The Victorian Power Speed Boat Club, which has 250 boats on the lake, announced that their last Sunday meeting would probably be their last on the lake. The weed choked up their motors and they would have to go to Lake Eppalock.

Oh, we are not just standing back. A powerful indignation society has formed, the Albert Park Protection League, known as Apple for short. Note how in times of trouble we invoke powerful mystic forces like the Beatles to help us.

But let me tell you something about this park. Recently the Melbourne 'Herald,' in its nice Melburnian way, said Albert Park wasn't as beautiful as London's Hyde Park or New York's Central Park, or as lovely as the Bois de Boulogne of Paris, clearly implying it was better than any of them.

Brochure after brochure has spoken of its glories. I remember one dated 1881 which noted how this splendid expanse of water so perfectly modified the prejudicial hot winds which blew from the north-east. 'How beautiful is Albert Park Lake. Here you might see a flock of black swans spearing their way across the surface of the glassy water.' Once it was a swamp, but in 1873 it was dredged, remade, turned into an area of glory for regattas and such, and named after Prince Albert the Good.

I am not sure when the monster first crept in. I think it was about 1960. No doubt he came to earth aboard one of those wayward, not adequately explained misfirings from Woomera. It was in 1962 that it first became a real danger and began to interfere with boating. They called it Canadian water weed or Elodea canadensis, and there was a constant parade down to the water's edge to inspect the thing — botanists, entomologists, university professors, heads of departments, minor Ministers, senior Ministers.

We tried tipping in 2½ tons of copper sulphate. We imported from England a weed-cutting boat, which cost $5000, and tore up and down. This was a naive move. Those who read their science-fiction know all too well that if you chop up a weed monster it only serves to distribute the growth.

Senator Kennelly, the chairman of the Albert Park Trust, searched the world for solutions. There was a great move in 1963 to import South American manatees, dugong-like creatures that live on water and weeds in the lagoons, lakes and shallows of South America. Two fat manatees, we were told, would mow down a mile strip of weed 22 feet wide in 17 weeks. Senator Kennelly wasn't impressed by that one: 'I am not prepared to mount an expedition to the jungles of Guatemala or anywhere else just to give the lake its own monsters. Impractical!'

Then came the theory that the weed had grown because we had upset the balance of wildlife in the water. Yabbies were the answer. They muddied the water and made weed growth impossible. So the Trust paid $1 a hundred for yabbies and put them in the pool. The trouble was, universities and fishermen were offering three times that price for good yabbies. And, besides, a young man could conduct a beautiful permanent business — sell his yabbies to the Trust, fish them back out of the lake, sell his yabbies to the Trust, fish them back out.

In ten years the Trust spent something like $60,000 in fighting the weed. In 1963 one of the oil companies did succeed in clearing it, but once they stopped pouring in chemicals back it came.

Apple is all for importing Chinese grass carp, which apparently adores Elodea canadensis. It can be found in two areas; in China's Pearl River — the Mao type of carp — or in the Amur River, Siberia — the Moscow persuasion. However, the carp is a prohibited fish in Victoria and the Fisheries and Wild Life Department will not have it, not even in this time of crisis.

Now we hear the weed has even reached the Prime Minister. Mr. Gorton, we are told, has written to the newly appointed Minister for Education and Science, Mr. Bowen, asking him to discuss the possibility of a scientific inquiry with CSIRO officers.

But, of course, he is utterly wasting his time. The thing to do is to appeal to the weed's sense of decency and fair play before it is too late. I would suggest that next Sunday morning a committee composed of Sir Rohan Delacombe, Sir Henry Bolte, Archbishop Woods, Archbishop Knox and Sir Irving Benson should get down there and give it a try.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: It's Time to Appeal for Fair Play and Decency, The Bulletin, 13 December 1969. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
