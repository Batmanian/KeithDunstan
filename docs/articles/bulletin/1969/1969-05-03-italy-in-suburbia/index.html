Italy in Suburbia

3 min read.

First published 03 May 1969

Melbourne has become one of the major Italian cities of the world, with 200,000 first- and second-generation Italians, over 500 Continental delicatessens, Italian-language cinemas in Footscray and Ascot Vale, and an estimated one million gallons of home-made wine produced annually in backyards from Carlton to Reservoir from grenache and muscatel grapes trucked from the Murray.

A MAJOR REASON why Melbourne is not as dull and as staid as it used to be is that it has become one of the major Italian cities of the world.

How many Italians? That's an awkward one, but if you take an average of the estimates of the first- and second-generation Italians it comes to nigh on 200,000.

It's not just Fitzroy, Carlton, and Brunswick that have Italian flavour and feeling — they are seeping out everywhere. Our urban diet used to be the bottle of Fosters and the meat pie. This meat pie was a very curious article made out of thin, brittle cardboard; on the ground floor there was a smear of gravy and a great attic of air above. Now more and more, the urban diet is the bottle of red and a pizza. In Swanston Street, in Russell Street, in Lygon Street, there is the Pizza Roma, Pizza Napoli, Pizza Venezia.

In Melbourne there are over 500 'Continental' delicatessens, and even in outer suburbs like Reservoir, Syndal, and Broadmeadows one encounters the pungent rich smells of Sicily and Naples — salami, provolone, prosciutto, and mozzarella. There are Italian picture theatres which show nothing else but Italian-language movies — Cinema La Scala at Footscray, Cinema Colosseo at Ascot Vale, Cinema Italia at Clifton Hill, and Cinema Liberty at Brunswick.

Easter time is vintage time, and the great mass of grapes all pass through the market over a short period of three weeks. The grapes are mostly grenache and muscatel. They come from South Australia and all along the Murray, Renmark, Red Cliffs, Swan Hill.

I called at the market at 5 a.m. before light on a Saturday. Peel Street was blocked solid with people, trucks, and cars; the predominant language was Italian. The smell was heady enough to be overpowering — the sweet, sickly smell of fresh grape juice.

I went to the home of Joe Mento in Moonee Ponds, who, with two other families, was crushing 100 cases of grapes. They were treading in a flat-bottomed concrete trough in the traditional style, by foot. But, you will be interested to hear, not bare feet — the men were wearing gumboots.

Melbourne is the capital; it is Australia's Rome. Commonwealth Statistics for Italians who still hold Italian nationality: Canberra 1315, Melbourne 90,205, Sydney 42,240, Brisbane 6830, and Perth 2820.

Don Dimattina, Melbourne's biggest dealer in wine grapes, puts total home-made production at over a million gallons: 'The Italian who makes his own will also buy more in the bottle, and it is his interest in wine that has helped make the industry in Australia.'

Source: Italy in Suburbia, The Bulletin, 3 May 1969. Accessible via theNational Library of Australia's Trove database.
