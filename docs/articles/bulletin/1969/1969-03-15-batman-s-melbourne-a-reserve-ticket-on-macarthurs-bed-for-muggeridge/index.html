A Reserve Ticket on MacArthur's Bed for Muggeridge

3 min read.

First published 15 Mar 1969

The four-day auction of the Menzies Hotel's 4000 lots by closed-circuit television, covering twin beds slept in by General MacArthur, Danny Kaye and Sophie Tucker, a $35,000 Czech chandelier, chamber pots with scroll work, the two statues Prudence and Phoebe separated after 102 years, and a bottle of Bass ale from 1902 that nobody thought worth opening for 67 years.

WOULD YOU mind if first I have another brandy? Thank you. It has been a hard week. It all began four days ago, and is still going, the Menzies Hotel auction, over 4000 lots, the biggest auction this town has seen.

The auction started on the sixth floor and worked down. It was the first auction to use closed-circuit television. They had 20 TV sets and 800 chairs in the Chandelier Room and the auctioneer kept in touch with scattered platoon commanders. One could even take in the auction in the comfort of the cocktail lounge, where there was also a TV set and sound was piped through.

The four most beautiful rooms in Melbourne are the ballroom at Flemington, the dining-room at the Melbourne Club, the Legislative Council Chamber, and, with its superb proportions, its gilt, its Ionic columns, the Chandelier Room at the Menzies.

Among the prize items were the twin beds of 501, actually slept in by General Douglas MacArthur, Danny Kaye, and Sophie Tucker, although not at the same time. They went to Barry Jones for $70. Mr. Jones said that Malcolm Muggeridge was to be his guest for a TV series later in the year and Mr. M. would sleep in the MacArthur bed: 'He'll probably utter a strangled oath if I tell him who was there before him.'

There were 380 champagne glasses and they went in lots of 20 for $8. These were used by the yachting Livingston brothers, who used to run a fantastic Menzies party every Melbourne Cup night, and nothing but French champagne flowed all night.

Even better were lavatory signs. A 'Ladies' Boudoir' sign brought $12. Mr. Robert Humphrey, of Toorak, bought the big Club Bar sign for $7. And the doors with the Menzies crest were popular. There's a nice little door on the side marked 'Governor's Suite Entrance' — it was tradition that the Governor never came in the front entrance because it wasn't done to be seen entering a place that sold the demon drink. Mr. John Barber, of Coliban Park, near Kyneton, bought the Governor's door for $24 and said he was going to put it on the shearers' shed: 'For years the shearers have been complaining that the place is not as good as Menzies.'

The bidding was keenest for the chandeliers. The big fellow, imported from Czechoslovakia, a 60-branch festoon affair, worth $35,000, went to a Sydney syndicate for $11,500.

On Thursday night there was the wine sale — surprisingly poor cellar, some '34 Margaux, some '55 Latour, but little else. A bottle of Bass King's ale, brew of 1902, sold for $180. You have to feel for a bottle of Bass that nobody thought worth opening for 67 years.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: A Reserve Ticket on MacArthur's Bed for Muggeridge, The Bulletin, 15 March 1969. Accessible via theNational Library of Australia's Trove database.
