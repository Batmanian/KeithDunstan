The Wedding, the Greatest Thing Since the Bogle Case

3 min read.

First published 01 Mar 1969

Keith Dunstan covers the Zara Holt wedding at Millicent Avenue, Toorak, a press circus of 62 journalists, live television cameras, uninvited spectators climbing through windows, and the great friesian calf Meadow Glen Dame Zara afflicted with dissipated dyspepsia — all presided over by Nicky Holt in a four-inch tie.

OLD HANDS long skilled at covering circulation-builders believe there has been nothing to equal it since LBJ's visit or even the Bogle Case. The pure beauty of the Jeff-Zara Case was their superb skill in being able to provide a fresh lead for every edition. Just when the story was beginning to die, Jeff pulled a friesian calf out of his hat. Then Dame Zara struck back with the watch wedding present: 'A watch he can wear underwater. He loves diving into water for an impulsive swim, and this will be just the thing.'

On the wedding day at 3.30 p.m., pastoralist firms, terribly impressed by the arrival of the young friesian, Meadow Glen Dame Zara, had sent gifts: a cold branding kit, a great bag of Denkavit calf fodder, a calfeteria, and a plastic bucket for feeding. However, Meadow Glen was unwell. Mr. Bate was sitting on the back steps feeding the young lady milk. A university professor had been called in for consultation. He said Meadow Glen had aggravated dissipated dyspepsia — begging your pardon, that's diarrhoea.

There were 62 Press, radio, and TV people in the front garden. A complete all-star cast of the major feature writers, on-camera men, radio, etc. There was a nose against every window, ladies walking their poodles, and soon it was like Viet Cong infiltrating.

Nicky Holt was equally spectacular. His shirt was a red-ochre colour, the same as used for painting fireplaces. His Terence Stamp tie was 4in. from east to west, and this was with a dark chalk-stripe suit. He was to drive the bride to Millicent Avenue, but at St. George's Road the Mercedes would not move: 'The damn thing got stuck in P,' he said. The Mercedes, a lovely chocolate-brown, was a 280SE, six months old, with only 2000 miles on the clock.

Next came Mr. and Mrs. Gorton, Mr. and Mrs. McMahon, Tony Eggleston, Dame Mabel Brookes, Mr. and Mrs. John Bate, Sir Rupert and Lady Clarke. The food was set out on beds of ice — lobster, avocado, crab, smoked salmon, red and black caviar, oysters, pate de foie gras, and then hot mussels and Continental sausages.

Mr. Arthur Calwell was first out with his coat off. He rumbled: 'Press coverage has been outrageous. Both on this and Hasluck. I'd like to see a picture depicting a cartoonist, a Pressman, and the cow and I'd label it The Bovine Breed.'

It was a good wedding, friendly, relaxed, no formality. During the day there was only one complaint and this was from Denver Dixon, Melbourne's famous professional stunt man, who was in the city dressed as a native chief, coal-black with horns on his head. He said during the day three out of four people had asked him if he was in town for Dame Zara's wedding.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: The Wedding, the Greatest Thing Since the Bogle Case, The Bulletin, 1 March 1969. Accessible via theNational Library of Australia's Trove database.
