The colorful cloying of company reports

5 min read.

First published 10 Dec 1977

Annual reports in 1977 are 22.8 percent bigger than 1976, with color up 29.7 percent — EMI's features a National Geographic seascape and a gentleman wielding an electron into a cathode ray tube, Carlton and United Breweries shows a million empty pottles, BHP's board poses like a Marylebone cricket team of the 1880s, and Peter Kurts Properties labels their executives "DYNAMIC," "A LARGE YOUNG LION," and "NOT EASILY RUFFLED" in the chairman's own handwriting.

YOU WILL have noticed that bank forecasts for 1978 were laden with misery. The "confidential" report from the Department of Overseas Trade was laden with misery and, if you believe the economic experts, so is the new year.

Do not despair. As yet you have not noted the Batman company annual report index. Annual reports are 22.8 percent bigger in 1977 than they were in 1976 and they show a definite upswing in the number of pictures used, 13.95 percent; and we are going through boom conditions for color, a 29.7 percent increase. Indeed, as a company annual report observer, one could say that conditions rarely have been more promising.

Many of them clearly have been inspired by illustrious magazines like Fortune or even Vogue, and they drop into the letter box with a heavy clunk.

I mean, let's just take the EMI annual report. On the cover there's a marvellous seascape you'd never see in National Geographic. Turn over and, WOW, there's this fantastic color shot of Sydney Harbor. Turn to page two and you get a gentleman "wielding an electron into a color cathode ray tube at the New South Wales plant." Terrific stuff. But there are lots of pictures. I particularly liked the one of the lovely family all gathered round having a sing-song with their Hammond organ.

You almost get to the stage where you forget to look at the figures, but these are nicely done too, on heavy quality Vogue-type paper, black type on a soft yellow.

EZ Industries, on the other hand, put their figures in pink, and seeing that Sir Edward Cohen predicted a big drop in profits, one trusts that the color wasn't being too symbolic. But never mind, there are good color shots of an automatic strapping and stacking machine, the MV Zincmaster, being loaded with zinc concentrate and a picture of no 4 ball mill at Rosebery you will love.

Elder Smith-Goldsborough Mort gets our prize for the best cover. You get a country scene that goes all over the front and back, better than anything you'd see in National Geographic. Turn over and, WOW, there's this fantastic color shot of Sydney Harbor. There are more pictures in the middle, including your souvenir shot of the stud merino ram which sold at the Royal Adelaide Show for a world record price of $46,000.

What else was good? Well, you'd go for the Carlton and United Breweries report. It has beautiful full-color artwork, brewery wagons with Clydesdales, chaps serving behind the bar, that sort of thing and all the pages have a pretty light blue border.

I went for the color shot at the back which depicted a million empty CUB bottles (a very human Australian photograph) and I liked the little feature complete with color shots on Fosters' Lager, how it is now going to 40 overseas countries and how we sold a million 25oz cans in one hit to the U.S. in July, 1976.

The TNT report is about as big as the Women's Weekly, with rather more color. It is packed with shots of TNT operations all around the globe and I must say I was touched by the picture of the purple Kwikasair van with the kangaroo on the side, parked in front of Windsor Castle. Undoubtedly it was taking home all the gifts presented to Prince Charles while he was out here, like the sterling silver boomerangs with the genuine Aboriginal motifs on the side.

Farley and Lewers Ltd have a cover in gold, then a tremendous double-page spread showing us Circular Quay in 1927. Turn over and you get a tremendous double-page spread of Circular Quay in 1977. I don't know that the 1927 scene isn't better.

But if you want my commendation for my best for 1977, then you should go to the BHP report. There they are, all gathered round, looking friendly, easy, some sitting, some standing, rich color with a very good painting on the wall. You get the feeling at once that they are a lovable board of directors and it has all the relaxed style of a Marylebone cricket team of the 1880s.

Our medal for originality goes to Peter Kurts Properties Ltd. Pages two to five give you pictures of Kurts executives. A Gold Coast manager is labelled "DYNAMIC," A Brisbane manager gets the caption "A LARGE YOUNG LION," their construction manager is "NOT EASILY RUFFLED" and the home finance man is "GREAT IN HEAVY GOING."

But wait. If you really want the happiness of close personal contact, then listen to this. You get the chairman's report from Peter Kurts himself in his very own handwriting, on the company's own letterhead. Isn't that something?

Source: Batman's Melbourne: The colorful cloying of company reports, The Bulletin, 10 December 1977. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
