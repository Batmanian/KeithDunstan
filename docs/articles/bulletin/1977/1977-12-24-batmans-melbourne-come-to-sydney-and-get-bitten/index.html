Come to Sydney and get bitten

4 min read.

First published 24 Dec 1977

Melbourne's new Tourism Authority and Sydney's new Visitors Bureau launch simultaneously — Melbourne sends Lord Mayor Kennedy, Cr Rockman, league footballers and local beauties to Hyde Park to give away 2200 carnations and kick footballs into the crowd while playing "Why Don't You Come And See Melbourne" over loudspeakers — prompting Keith Dunstan to propose Sydney reciprocate with mock funnel-web spiders, Manly sharks in a tank, and Madame Justice Evatt as compere.

IT IS WELL KNOWN that there is no rivalry between Melbourne and Sydney and, thank God, this column never mentions it.

Just by curious coincidence two publications arrived on my desk the other day. One is titled All About Melbourne, the first newsletter from the Melbourne Tourism Authority, the other is Compass, announcing the launching of the new Sydney Visitors Bureau.

Now under the leadership of Mr Hamer, Melbourne launched its tourism authority, with great eclat, film and all, back at the beginning of July. Sydney with great eclat, under the leadership of Mr Wran, launched its visitors bureau just a couple of weeks later.

Melbourne, I notice, is going all out in every place throughout Australia and interstate, to attract visitors. Its target is 300,000 extra visitors plus $26 million worth of spending money a year.

I notice that Compass is just slightly impressive. It says these things: "The bureau's task is to actively promote the City of Sydney around the world, interstate and throughout New South Wales... despite competition from other State capitals, Sydney remains Australia's premier tourist attraction. Seventy-five percent of all visitors to Australia never leave Sydney. This year the city will have more than three million visitors spending more than $220 million."

Melbourne is working hard on it. We are concentrating on the fact that our registration plates registered here have "the garden State" message on them. The plates are being pushed hard. We have that beyond the bluestone there exists all this green.

Also being pushed very hard is the idea that in Melbourne we have the best food, the best restaurants, and the most remarkable sporting facilities, to say nothing of the greatest art collection.

You may have noticed that in Sydney, Adelaide, and almost inevitably elsewhere, we have been turning on displays of Melburnian fervor. On October 25 a team which included Kennedy, the Lord Mayor, Cr Rockman, some league footballers and Melburnian beauties, went to Sydney. They went into Hyde Park and to lunch-time crowds they gave away 2200 fresh carnations. The footballers kicked 18 genuine Australian Rules footballs into the crowd. They played a specially composed song "Why Don't You Come And See Melbourne" over the loudspeakers, and according to the Melbourne newsletter, "The crowds loved it."

A newspaper commented: "Melbourne's been around for long enough. If people haven't discovered it by now will this expensive promotion make any difference?"

Well, that is something which is impossible to tell. Personally, just to balance things up, I do like the idea of a Sydney Expeditionary Force coming here. I see the outline of an outstanding public relations concept. The Marco Polo expedition to China, the Rock band, something that could also be tied in with the expeditionary force here. Don Beresford, chief of a NSW tourism thing, agreed that almost certainly there would be one, but maybe it would have to wait until February or March. Keith Dunstan should be brought in as consultant on those things. I could act as director.

For a start I would suggest that Leo Port, Lord Mayor of Sydney, should mount his expedition in a series of your beautiful double-decker buses. Here in Melbourne we have been promised a series of "lightning" tram and train strikes going on into infinity, so it would be nice to get a picture of actual public transport working.

We have been rubbed silly by massage parlors, but Kings Cross is still a pure, romantic dream for us. We could have boys and lovely, touched strippers from the nicest establishments in the Cross.

Also being pushed very hard is the idea that in Melbourne we have the best food, the best restaurants, and the most remarkable sporting facilities, to say nothing of the greatest art collection.

Some things are utterly irresistible. I would like to have a mock-up of the Gap, your marvellous rescue team that climbs up and down, pulling people back to the joys of Sydney. I would like to see funnel-web spiders; very few people here have ever had the opportunity, and what about a tank with a couple of Manly sharks?

I don't know who you would get as compere, but I tell you, almost the best-known Sydney person over here now is Madame Justice Evatt.

There should be many more of these exchanges, it is what we both need.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Come to Sydney and get bitten, The Bulletin, 24 December 1977. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
