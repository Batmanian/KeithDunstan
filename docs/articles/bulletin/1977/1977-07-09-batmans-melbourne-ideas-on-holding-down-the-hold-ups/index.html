Ideas on holding down the hold-ups

4 min read.

First published 09 Jul 1977

Melbourne's new Tourism Authority launches amid a city famous for being the armed hold-up capital of the world — over 228 armed hold-ups this year — with the State Savings Bank's PR man cataloguing the daily public suggestions: nerve gas, marbles, trap-doors, fall-in grills, dye capsules, radio transmitters in banknotes, and the ejector seat for tellers.

YOU WILL BE interested to hear that we now have a Melbourne Tourist Authority. The theory is that Melbourne always has suffered from bad PR. People have had the erroneous idea that Melbourne is a cold, wet, dull place whereas in truth its charms are enormous.

So we set up an authority, engaged an advertising agency, and we gave the whole affair a splendid send off at Parliament House on Thursday night. The undoubted plot is to snare away some of Sydney's tourist trade. Our passion for football and horse racing is already famous, but a new point which might intrigue the tourist is this, for after all, tourists love a sense of sin — we are now unquestionably the armed hold-up capital of the world. We have them at a rate of better than one a day.

It's very hard to keep pace with the rapid progress, but I believe these are the latest figures. This year we have had 28 bank robberies and 228 actual armed hold-ups.

Ex-Queensland Police Commissioner Ray Whitrod has pointed out that Melbourne always has been the crime capital. We have a nice tradition for it going right back to Ned Kelly and continuing through "Squizzy" Taylor, but so far nobody has been able to pin down a "Mr Big" who might be behind all these robberies.

Obviously robbing a bank is so easy. Almost anybody can do it, simply by reading the daily reports in the newspapers and nobody, least of all the banks, is coming up with a solution.

Meanwhile the banks are being inundated with suggestions from the public. Your correspondent spoke to Bill Patey, public relations officer at the State Savings Bank.

"The ideas come in daily," said Bill. "Perhaps the most interesting was nerve gas. The idea came from a company, and they were perfectly serious about it. There would be a cylinder fixed to the front door to be activated by the teller.

"The cloud of gas would hit the fleeing bandit and leave him paralysed. Great if you hit the bandit but what happens if you paralyse some of your customers as well?"

Another interesting suggestion was marbles. The teller would press a button and hundreds of marbles would shoot all over the floor with the result that the obliging bandits would step on the little balls, fall and break their necks.

Trap-doors were the most popular suggestion. Press a button and, lo! — a trap-door would open and the bandit would drop into a ready-made cell waiting down below. (It would be embarrassing if you pressed a button in error and dropped a valuable customer, but as Bill points out, the trap-door theory breaks down because always there is more than one bandit and the fellow who is left is likely to start shooting.)

The most fascinating suggestion, according to Bill, was the fall-in grill. When the bandit puts his hand in to take the money, the teller presses a button and — ZUNK! — down comes the spiked grill and he is trapped like a rabbit.

The same gentleman suggested a mechanical fist complete with boxing glove, which would belt the bandit in the stomach.

Bill said: "We get an awful lot of suggestions for hooters and sirens, a light outside flashing ROBBERY IN PROGRESS.

"We had a suggestion the other day that we should include a tiny radio transmitter among the bank notes. The notes would be beeping and it would be possible to find the bandits with direction finders. What with minaturisation, that's not such a bad idea."

There has been a proposal that a great net covered in fish hooks should drop from the ceiling, which compared rather well with the idea that the teller should have an ejector seat. A foot button would catapult him over a screen into a safety compartment.

But the best suggestion to date has been dye, and this has worked on a number of occasions.

"A lot of thieves have been caught trying to present green money," said Bill. "The idea is to place a tiny capsule in the money bag. It blasts off in a sort of green powder and if it gets on you its terrible. We heard from the police that one fellow took showers for 24 hours before he could get it off."

The bank tellers' solution is to fight for the installation of armored glass. The government solution is to increase penalties to 25 years, but there was yet another bank hold-up while they were actually debating the bill.

The social solution was for Tattersalls to offer a million-dollar prize lottery. It all made something for nothing sound so easy.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Ideas on holding down the hold-ups, The Bulletin, 9 July 1977. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
