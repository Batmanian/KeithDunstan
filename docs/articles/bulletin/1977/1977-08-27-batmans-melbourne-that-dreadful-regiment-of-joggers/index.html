That dreadful regiment of joggers

5 min read.

First published 27 Aug 1977

The jogging and fitness craze has swept Melbourne, Lufthansa runs in-flight exercises, Prahran Market fathers arrive in matching crimson tracksuits, Toorak couples commission made-to-measure His-and-Hers wool sets at $80, and even the young attend 21sts in Adidas gear, while Keith Dunstan offers advice on the correct track-suit aesthetic, from the form-fitting leg to the ski resort badge.

I HAVE just been reading about the latest big attraction in international jet flying — get-fit programs. Until now the airlines have always fought it out with the same lethal methods: stuff them with good food and expensive liquor until they are absolutely comatose.

Yes, the competition has been deadly, the weapons Krug for Krug, Veuve Cliquot for Veuve Cliquot, smoked salmon for smoked salmon, venison, pheasant, duck a l'orange. But don't you see, this past year the Americans have gone nutty about jogging, tennis, physical fitness, health foods.

Apparently Lufthansa were the first to get on to the idea. They have recorded physical fitness programs aloft. I read the story in London's Daily Mail. At the right moment, the order went out to everyone to lay back their seats, then to gentle music came the instructions for toe-touching, knee bends, legs-ups, all that sort of thing.

What's more, many of the air travellers took it so seriously they wore special gear, towelling tops, track suits, and there was this ghastly sight of a jumbo jet, packed with writhing humans trying to recapture their youth. The theory behind it is that jet lag has nothing to do with the difference in clock times. It has all to do with too many martinis and too much veal cordon bleu, camembert cheese and VSOP Cognac.

Other airlines are now getting on to the idea. So from here on don't expect pretty air hostesses in saris, sarongs, beautiful gowns and such; expect well-muscled females in track-suits.

Actually the physical fitness thing has hit here in a big way. I can't sleep in the morning for all the pounding and panting going on past our front door. You get the evening joggers by the thousand panting past the Shrine and the Botanic Gardens. Honestly, you can be in danger in Flinders Street and Swanston Street at noon from running, perspiring gentlemen who in the old days used to be in happy comfort in the Florentino.

The politicians have woken up to it. Did you notice the pre-budget pictures of Mr Lynch? We had him in a surf cap paddling a surf ski or whatever up at Surfers Paradise. Then, just before he was due to deliver the sombre news, one of the current affairs programs interviewed him jogging along the beach. Mr Lynch was in his tracksuit.

Indeed, you see a lot of Mr Lynch being interviewed in his tracksuit. And if you want to keep up with this modern — I'm groping for the right word — lifestyle, that's it, then you must get yourself a tracksuit.

Even if you never run a yard it gives the impression that you are fit, and an exciting person to know. Over the years in Toorak and South Yarra it has been correct to wear mink, ermine and dress right up to one's false eye-lashes just to go down to buy a cabbage or a bottle of gin, particularly on Saturday mornings. It is more correct now to wear a tracksuit.

The Prahran Market is the same. Now it is the done thing for elegant males to go to the market early and get the vegetables for the whole family. I treasure one market memory of several weeks back. Father arrived in his crimson red Volvo. He stepped out and, lo, there he was in his matching red track-suit — the total look.

Of course, track-suits have come on. Maybe you remember your school days. You only rated a track-suit if you were a really big deal, representing the school in the 100-yard dash or the high jump. It was a shapeless thing lined with fleecy cotton, and you always made a great show of taking it off when they put the bar up to 5ft 10in.

Ye gods, some friends of ours have just bought His and Hers track-suits! Beautiful they are, made to measure in Carlton in pure wool, crimson tops and back legs. I think they cost around $80 a set.

Our track-suits aren't up to that standard, and when we set out we feel slightly humiliated.

If you are going in for this track-suit idea, then let me give you some advice. It is no longer done for your track-suit to look worn and slightly sweat-stained. Until last year it was a good idea to have a faded green-and-gold suit, then someone might get the idea that you won the pole-vault at the Berlin Olympics. That doesn't work any more. Your track-suit should be beautifully-cut. The legs should be form-fitting, but it is all right to have a flared bottom. (This indicates that you are also a skier.) There are some excellent Italian ones. Don't overdo the stripes. A symbol like Peugeot at Le Mans on the back is good. Badges? Yes, but one is enough. This could suggest that you have been skiing on the Tyrol or maybe you spent last week in Colorado at Aspen or some fancy resort. A hint that you were an oarsman at the America's Cup is also nice.

We have an armed bank robbery rate of one-a-week, and I think that almost 50 percent of them have been done in track-suits.

Several weeks back the young in our family were taking off for a dance. They all came in wearing tracksuits and Adidas jogging shoes. In my day any form of dancing was considered impossible unless you wore black patent leather dancing pumps.

"Where are you going?"

"We're going to a 21st."

"Good grief. A formal party. You can't possibly go like that in track-suits."

"Ahhh, come off it. Everybody'll be wearing track-suits. Parents too, probably."

I retreated, as always, defeated, humiliated. I tell you, Phil Lynch knows when he's on to a good thing.
