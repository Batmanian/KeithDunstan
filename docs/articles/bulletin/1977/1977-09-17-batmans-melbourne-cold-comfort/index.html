Cold comfort

5 min read.

First published 17 Sep 1977

A letter from Trans Time Inc. of Canberra prompts a conversation with Australian agent Dr Thomas Donaldson, a mathematics lecturer at the ANU, about cryonic suspension — $50,000 for the full body, $15,000 for the head only, 28 Americans frozen so far with 14 still in thermos-bottle storage at minus 169 degrees — leading Keith Dunstan to doubt whether waking in the year 3000 would be worth the trouble.

JUST occasionally a letter comes into this office which makes an offer of regarding my eternal remains — a niche here, a rose garden there, a little plot over yonder. But this one was a beauty. It was headed: Trans Time Inc., PO Box 1286 Civic, Canberra, ACT.

It began: "Dear Sir, you have probably heard or read of cryonics, the freezing of the newly-dead in the hope that medical science may someday restore them to active life, health and youth. Trans Time, a California corporation, has offered full cryonic suspension for several years. Significant numbers of people have made complete prearrangements to be suspended under the Trans Time program in the U.S.

"Recently Trans Time has also become a foreign corporation registered in Australia... Trans Time has also made a legally-binding commitment by which they will fly to Australia and freeze on location any member resident in Australia who has made prearrangements for them to do so."

This sounded much more interesting than my niche, so naturally I telephoned Trans Time at once and spoke to the Australian agent, Dr Thomas Donaldson. Now Dr Donaldson turned out to be a non-medical doctor, an American, and a lecturer in mathematics at the ANU.

Our conversation went like this:

"Doctor, what sort of progress has been made with this thing? How many people are there in the U.S. deep frozen for all eternity?"

"So far 28 have been frozen and 14 are still frozen."

"What happened to those who got unfrozen?"

"The people concerned made the mistake of leaving the arrangements to their relatives and they didn't keep up the commitments."

"How much does it cost?"

"Fifty thousand dollars. That takes care of everything for all time. If you want head only then that's cheaper... only $15,000."

"Head only?????"

"Well yair, the idea is that medical science eventually will catch up with head transplants, so this is the logical way of doing it."

"Do you mean to say some people already have left their heads frozen?"

"Yes, two that I know of."

Dr Donaldson explained that there were several organisations involved in cryonics but Trans Time was the biggest. It had five bodies and two heads in suspension. The best way to make sure of your situation legally, so that it was binding on the relatives, was to join a cryonics society. This meant a joining fee of $1000 and from then on they did everything. The society then had the legal power to take away the body.

"I belong to the Bay Area Cryonic Association," he said.

"Do you mean you've signed up too and your body will be frozen?"

"Of course."

"How old are you now?" — "Thirty three."

"Well you've got a little time yet. How is the body stored?"

He explained that the bodies were stored outside of San Francisco. They were frozen to minus 169 degrees centigrade and put into a large container like a thermos bottle. This alone costs $5000.

Once in there the body was right until future science uncovered the secret for restoring health and youth.

"The large bottle is filled with liquid nitrogen," said our man. "A little boils off from time to time, so it has to be topped up every couple of months."

"But do you mean to say you are paying $50,000 to have this done to you?" asked Batman.

Dr Donaldson replied in the manner as if everyone does this thing. "Yair, I do it through an insurance policy and that's the best way. I pay $800 a year."

He admitted that two people already had signed up in Australia, but he didn't say who. He also agreed that he received a generous commission for every new suspension member, but he was donating the money to the advancement of cryonics.

And what happened when the grim reaper arrived and one dropped dead in Collins Street or wherever? The cryonics people would fly here, freeze your body and fly it back to the Eternity Mausoleum outside San Francisco.

Well, I have done some deep thinking about this. I'm prepared to concede that in the year 3000 it will be no trouble to unfreeze a body and restore it to life, but I'm sceptical about the youth bit. It would be better to have yourself deep frozen smartly before you reach 30.

The other problem is that I'm a conservative, maybe even worse than Bjelke-Petersen. I think that the deterioration in the world just during the past decade has been beyond belief. Do you reckon it would be worth waking up for in 3000?

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Cold comfort, The Bulletin, 17 September 1977. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
