The "Old dark blue" goes pink

4 min read.

First published 16 Jul 1977

Melbourne Grammar announces 25 girls will start at Grimwade House next year — a momentous break for Australia's oldest Anglican public school, which has produced three Prime Ministers — while cold economics drives the co-ed trend across Victoria's private schools, with over 200 girls already at Geelong Grammar and female crews heading for the Head of the River.

MELBOURNE GRAMMAR has announced that next year little girls will start at Grimwade House. Just a timid, minor beginning mind you. Twenty-five of them, $389 a term, will start at grade one. They will infiltrate until by grade six, Grimwade will have 150 females. There is no suggestion at this stage that the girls will go on into the senior school, but as an old co-ed watcher, I would say it is as inevitable as the coming of an all-female Head of the River.

The news rated little space in the newspapers, but for stately old Melbourne Grammar to go co-ed, this is the big break-through. Can the others be far behind? Melbourne Grammar, founded in 1858, is the oldest Anglican public school. Geelong Grammar was founded to look after the "nice people" of the Western District. Melbourne Grammar was created for the sons of the well-to-do professional people and businessmen.

It has Australia's track record for Prime Ministers, three of them — Alfred Deakin, Lord Bruce and Malcolm Fraser. The tradition of the great being spawned at Melbourne Grammar is fairly awe-inspiring.

But let's give you a report on how this co-ed thing is progressing. You don't want to get the idea that it is all sheer enlightened, liberated thinking that has brought it about. Cold hard economics has thrown the boys and the girls into each others arms. Ten to 15 years ago most of these schools had long waiting lists; now they are looking for pupils and if they are occupying desks, it doesn't matter so much whether they are male or female.

The veterans at this, of course, are Geelong Grammar. At old boys' day this year, for heaven's sake, they had quite a sprinkling of old girls. The boat race on the Barwon was really something. There are over 200 girls at Geelong Grammar. Well, you know how it is the done thing to get into a huddle, chant crazy battlecries and jump up and down. The Geelong Grammar girls were doing precisely that. What's more, some of them with long hair flowing down their backs, were wearing GGS school caps. (These school caps are now priceless antiques and it is very fashionable to possess one.)

As you know, Geelong Grammar absorbed the girl schools Clyde and Hermitage, and female school sport is a very big thing. They are particularly strong in swimming and tennis. During the past five terms some of the young gentlemen at other schools have been stunned out of their minds when suddenly they have found themselves in competition against a Geelong Grammar female, and the competition has been serious at that.

Rowing at Geelong Grammar is very big. They have more than 30 female rowers, and what with Geelong College sporting female crews that female Head of the River is not so far off. GGS also goes in for female yachting, golf, hockey, baseball, athletics and what seems a little awe-inspiring, karate.

Who else is taking in females? As we say, Geelong College, that Presbyterian headquarters in Geelong has gone fully co-ed. Assumption College in Kilmore is co-ed. Ballarat College took in the girls from Clarendon College. Ballarat combined with the girls from St Leonard's Brighton Girls' School has also gone fully co-ed.

How long will the others hold back? You could probably work it out on a graph, the falling-off of pupils directly related to the upward rise of school fees. Scotch College, locked in the battle between the continuing and the uniting Church, is the obvious thought. We hear that they are very careful these times when they add new buildings. Facilities are there for the possibility that they might have to provide male and female lavatories.

Melbourne Grammar and Scotch are the grand old rivals. Why, back in 1858 they initiated Australian rules football with the very first match. It was a sort of prolonged brawl spread over the Domain between 80 players.

It would be nice to have Melbourne Grammar, though, and Scotch girls in contest. Melbourne Grammar has a school song of terrible ferocity. It is sung to the tune of Men of Harlech. The chorus goes:

None our ranks shall sunder, Who will shirk or blunder If all are true To our dark blue Our foemen shall go under...

Geelong Grammar girls, I am sure, would be nicer. The GGS school song is in Latin and nobody really knows what it means.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: The "Old dark blue" goes pink, The Bulletin, 16 July 1977. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
