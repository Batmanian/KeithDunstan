Is nothing sacred?

4 min read.

First published 03 Sep 1977

The Herald reports that Moonee Valley Racing Club may go heterosexual, allowing men and women to mix in the betting rings and bars, which would cascade through Flemington, Caulfield and Sandown, ending Melbourne's cherished tradition of sexual apartheid in the members' enclosures, a curiosity as distinctive as the city's trams.

THERE'S some breath-taking news here in Melbourne. The Herald has made the suggestion that the Moonee Valley Racing Club is about to go heterosexual, an unheard of thing for one of our clubs. Do you know what this would mean? Men and women would mix together in all the betting rings, bars and eating houses in the Members' Reserve.

Jack Elliott, chief racing writer on the Herald, says that if the Moonee Valley Racing Club does this thing, then Flemington, Caulfield and Sandown will be forced to follow.

Frankly I don't believe it, that would be too earth shaking... and even something of a pity. This sexual segregation has become a marvellous curiosity like our trams. Visiting newspapermen, when they are looking for a fascinating topic, get on to it at once.

No city race clubs anywhere else in Australia have this splendid camp approach, and, according to Mr Elliott, you don't see it happen anywhere else in the world.

Flemington makes a good story because it is designed precisely on the lines of sexual apartheid. On the pavement passing through the Members' Stand there is a broad white line. It is as though one expected the message of Petain's cry in the First World War: "Ils ne passeront pas." Well-bred Melburnian males know their place and females know their place.

A serious belief still prevails in this town — there are many occasions when the male should be allowed to be exclusive with his own sex... when he is drinking, when he is having lunch and most of all when he is betting.

Betting, you must understand, is a very serious matter and the feeling here is that females tend to be frivolous. For example, at Flemington the males have this precious members' area with their bookmakers. The theory is that they can wander up and down and bet $2000 here, $3000 there and do it in such style that they don't even have to accept a betting ticket. They are so well-known to the bookmakers that a nod is good enough.

They don't want to be interrupted by females trotting up and down saying: "I'll have 50 cents each way on... what's the name of that horse again?"

This distrust of females goes back a long way. Maurice Cavanough in his book The Story of the Caulfield Cup recalls that in 1907 the Victorian Government proclaimed an anti-gambling act which prohibited newspapers from publishing pre-post betting odds, a prohibition that lasted for more than half a century. At the same time they made it illegal for women to bet on racehorses at all. The frail creatures didn't have the strength of character to withstand the temptations.

That act stayed on the books until a couple of years ago. It was one of those things they ignored, like baring their knees on the beaches, but it remained there just the same.

You might say there are glimmerings of hope. In November, 1975, the Victorian Government passed a rule which made it legal for female bookmakers to field on Victorian tracks. So far no female has actually taken up the challenge, but we do have female clerks.

What's more we do allow horses of both sexes to co-mingle on the track and actually race together. I understand that a mare by the name of Surround is doing rather well at the moment.

Where else can the male escape? There used to be the corner public bar, but most of these have been open to females since 1970. According to some recent figures the female office worker is more likely to drop in for a drink on the way home than the male. So that's out. There's still your club. The Melbourne Cricket Club still maintains its exclusive male rule and you're lucky to get in there unless you are Queen Elizabeth II.

Most of the top clubs such as the Melbourne, Athenaeum, Savage and Australian remain exclusively male until about 4 pm. Then they allow females in for dinner, a matter of pure economics, you understand — otherwise they would be deserted every night.

Personally I don't care about Moonee Valley. I'm waiting to see what the Victoria Racing Club does. That's the big step. If the VRC goes heterosexual, it's all over. Only the male lavatories would remain single sexed, and I wouldn't count on that either.
