We're in a parlors state

4 min read.

First published 10 Sep 1977

A massage parlour has opened in Keith Dunstan's South Yarra street, prompting reflection on how Melbourne has grown from 12 parlours five years ago to around 200, the Town Planning Appeals Tribunal calls them "thinly disguised brothels", while the Sunday Observer's four pages of advertisements offer everything from Tommy with a 48-inch bust to firelight massage and escorts for the discriminating.

WE HAVE a massage parlor now in our very own street. Oh, I must admit there has been a stir. A petition has gone from door to door which we have all been asked to sign. You must appreciate we are in South Yarra, and we are a most respectable street.

This petition has done the rounds for one reason. There's not so much terror over our morals. A massage parlor is a business, practically a factory, so it is lowering the value of all our houses.

The damn things are popping up everywhere. The Town Planning Appeals Tribunal, the State's top planning arbiter, has just announced that the parlors are "thinly disguised brothels." To be perfectly accurate, other businesses are going poorly here. The rag trade is utterly dejected, the timber trade is on the point of ruin, building is stagnant, the automotive industry is miserable, but the rub trade is the one boom industry.

Five years ago we had only 12 massage parlors and now there are just on 200. There is fairly considerable evidence that Melbourne is the massage parlor capital of the world.

There was great fuss last January. The former chief of the Vice Squad, Chief Inspector Fred Silvester, made a report to the government, and he recommended tighter controls. Chief Secretary Dicke said he and Health Minister Haughton were working on the matter and were planning to register and license the parlors. Not a thing has been done. The police have carried out a few raids, but the number of convictions has been few.

Personally, I would like to see the whole thing clarified, for this reason. I mean, what is the difference between a massage parlor and a health studio? What happens if you are a sexually satisfied chap and all you have is an aching back? You really want someone to rub your third vertebra or whatever, and certainly not any other parts.

You could go into a place labelled "health studio" and suddenly find yourself being assaulted by a girl, naked except for G-string, mobile hands, whip and all.

I don't know whether Mr Dicke is having trouble in finding these establishments, but could I refer him to a newspaper in this town called the Sunday Observer. It was, until recently, owned by Maxwell Newton, but every week it carries four pages of sex advertisements, mostly from massage parlors. Sometimes the pages are labelled "studios," sometimes discreetly "home service" and other times, for God's sake, "health."

Just take a note of these:

Pinocchios. Strippers for bucks' nights. Topless waitresses and double acts. Home, motel, 24-hour service.

Sharron is my name, home massage is my game and my aim is nothing less than your satisfaction.

Emperor's Palace. Shery-Rebecca-Allani-Linda-Fiona-Sharon. Special guest for seven weeks only, Tommy with 48-inch bust.

Home of the rising sun — massage by firelight — Kate — Julie — Rene.

To be perfectly clean have a shower with me. Ring me any time after 12 midday.

Some specialise in foreign girls. They offer French, German, Chinese, and they are terribly careful about allowing no suggestion of racial prejudice. I notice that the ads never frankly refer to black girls. You can get an "island" massage.

Some of the ads give the prices: $20 for the more routine standards but there are specials at $14. Or you can get an all-in feature show. The Trafalgar Club offers a three-hour show. Full "Removie" and massage. $25 inclusive. Closed Sundays.

There are the men's ads too. Such messages as: "I have a dozen guys working for me who are clean, discreet and generous, here's your chance."

"Teeny-boppers, teenagers — earn extra money. Generous businessman wants one-to-one under your own terms."

It is a wonder to me that some of the tourists authorities haven't made use of the news that we are now the massage capital. I mean, the Sunday Observer makes Hamburg, Copenhagen, Hong Kong, Stockholm and other centres sound miserably dull by comparison.

We could hardly refer to ourselves as "swinging Melbourne," that's been done, but I do think "rubbing Melbourne" would go down rather well.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: We're in a parlors state, The Bulletin, 10 September 1977. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
