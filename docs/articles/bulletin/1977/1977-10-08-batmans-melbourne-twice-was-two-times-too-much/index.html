Twice was two times too much

4 min read.

First published 08 Oct 1977

Melbourne endures two grand finals in a row — Channel 7's football marathon runs from midnight Friday through nine replayed finals and a champagne breakfast before the actual match draws 106,000 to the MCG — leaving wedding guests in St Raphael's Preston exchanging thumb signals about Collingwood's score during the ceremony, and brides displaced to 9 am starts so grooms can still make the game.

OH, I TELL you it has been a very hard couple of weeks in Melbourne. There were some valiant attempts to break into the news. Bill Hayden said some inflammatory things about the economy, a fashion model had her navel misplaced and there were some jolly good strikes. Yet I had the impression that if Queen Elizabeth had gone off for a naughty weekend with Jimmy Carter it still would have made only page 17.

You have never seen such coverage. Not only was the dreary stuff on pages one, two and three, in the centre and filling the entire sporting section, there were special lift-out supplements and complete color wrap-arounds. Don't think it has only been in the Sun and the Age. The Bulletin itself devoted its front cover to the football players as if anyone has had the slightest opportunity to forget what they look like.

Channel 7 was almost beyond belief. It had what they called the "football marathon." It began around midnight on the Friday. Then the theory was that they would give the viewers all the grand finals of the past 10 years. Actually they got through only nine.

After that they went to the North Melbourne Football Club champagne breakfast. They replayed the Truth Cazaly awards dinner which had been on the previous Monday. This was filled with filmed highlights of the performances of great players. Next they went straight to the MCG for the reserves grand final and after that the grand final. Bounce, bounce, bounce, on, on, on. There was a brief respite for news which was mostly football. Then they had a replay of the grand final just in case anyone wanted to reassure their eyesight followed by World of Sport, mainly football, which ran through to 3pm.

I asked Gordon Bennett, producer of this show, whether many people had observed the whole performance. He had no doubt about it. The telephone calls all through the night proved that. Even before it started a number of people who were going interstate contacted him. When they discovered he was putting on a footy marathon they cancelled their trips.

To tell you the truth, when they were actually playing the thing Melbourne was lovely. There were 106,000 inside the MCG and maybe several more million watching it on television. I went for a ride on my bicycle. The sun was shining, the air was warm, you could hear the birds chirping and not a human or an automobile was astir.

A gentleman in a parked car wound down his window and shouted: "The game. Didja hear the news. It's a draw."

And so we had two grand finals. Never before had it happened, and you would think one would have been enough.

But there were the thousands of posters both announcing a Magpie or a Kangaroo victory. These had the date on them and they were to be sold for the children's hospital. They had to be abandoned.

Apart from that, the Saturday after the grand final by tradition is the biggest grand day of the year at garden nurseries. Husbands at last literally return to their petunia duties. Not for another week. Again it is just like Easter. You get your thousands of Easter brides and you get your thousands of post-grand-final brides.

I had a call from one very distinguished lady whose wedding was set for grand final day No 2. "Do you know what the blighter did?" she said. "He called me up and asked, did I mind if we set the wedding at 9am. This would still give us time to have the wedding, the wedding reception and still get to the grand final."

There was chaos in some churches even during the first grand final, let alone the second. A female wedding guest confessed to me: "We had to go to a wedding at St Raphael's in Preston on Saturday afternoon. My husband had a trannie and he tuned in. The church was full of Collingwood supporters and he gave signals. When Collingwood was in front he grinned and gave thumbs up. When North was in front he looked miserable and gave thumbs down."

My informant intimated that even the priest was aware of the state of the game.

Oh mercy, when will it all end? It is surprising that some enterprising manufacturer has not produced ear muffs — in neutral, non-football colors, naturally.
