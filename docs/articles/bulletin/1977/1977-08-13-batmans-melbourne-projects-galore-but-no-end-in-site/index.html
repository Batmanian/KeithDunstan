Projects galore but no end in site

4 min read.

First published 13 Aug 1977

An adman proposes harnessing Melbourne's hot air from parliament, the City Council, the BLF and the Save the Regent Committee to power a turbine, which captures the city's crisis of confidence perfectly, as Keith Dunstan opens a form guide on its projects: Collins Place 3/1, underground 100/1, arts centre 250/1, and Newport power station at 1/1992.

AN ADMAN by name of Leo Keane made a fascinating suggestion in the Age the other day. Said he: "What we should do is put pipelines into State parliament, the Melbourne City Council, the Builders Laborers' Federation and Save the Regent Committee, and have all participants blow together. The amount of hot air so gathered would drive an enormous Regent-centred turbine to generate the power we need.

Marvellous Melbourne right now is suffering from a lack of confidence, and it seeps out everywhere. The Melbourne City Council is even having trouble in meeting its electricity bills, so the chairman of the council electricity supply committee, Cr Ralph Bernardi, suggested that the council remove 3661 street light bulbs — that is, every second light. This would save $330,000 a year. So if Melbourne has a reputation for being a dull city, this would make it beautifully symbolic.

But it goes deeper than that. We really need to finish something to make us feel better. Your correspondent is wondering whether it would be all right to open a little book and accept bets on the city's current projects. Which will be finished first? Perhaps you could make your bets on the nose or for a place.

Here's the list of the current starters:

Collins Place project 3/1 Duplication of Hume highway 200/1 Regent Theatre re-opening 50/1 Westgate bridge 2/1 Melbourne underground 100/1 Arts centre 250/1 Yarra Bank bicycle path 100/1 Newport power station 1/1992

We are very sensitive about bridges. You will remember how the Kings St bridge collapsed back in 1962. That happened when a gentleman named Ray Noble, 48, of Francis St, Ascot Vale, drove across in a low-loader which carried a 28-ton power shovel. Is Ray Noble still around? I think it would be a marvellous idea if we could get him to bring out his low-loader and his power shovel and be first to drive across the Westgate bridge.

Our number two confidence-sapper is the Melbourne underground. Current official cost $301 million. Original estimate $117.2 million. The form guide hope is that it will be finished in 1981, strikes permitting.

Arts centre. The three-stage project began in 1962 and when finished, God willing in 1981, will have cost more than 10 times the original target price. Finishing price will be more than $110 million.

We can't give you any reliable thoughts for the finish of the bicycle path or the ring road. The twin towers of Collins Place, which played a solid part in the wrecking of Mainline Corporation, is our form guide tip to win the big race. Its cost is over $250 million and seems to indicate the inexhaustible source of funds of insurance companies. It is proceeding relatively strike-free under E. A. Watts and should be open in 1979-80.

Then there is the bicycle path along the Yarra. Originally there were dreams that this would take us right out to Warrandyte or wherever. At the moment, spasmodically, construction is taking place between Chapel St and Grange Road, Toorak. Apparently out of sympathy with the Hume highway the path they have made already is beginning to break up.

Regent Theatre? There is a conflict between the municipal officers who have black-banned it because they want it pulled down and the builders' laborers who have green-banned it because they want it to stay up. As of the day before yesterday, the city council still wanted it to proceed and become a theatre again. Date of completion unknown. When was the Regent first sold to the City Council? — In 1969.

City square. Architects have produced a lovely plan. Form guide estimate of completion? Give them at least three years.

Last year we opened a fine, new 34.1km of freeway from Wallan to Broadford, part of the duplication right through to Albury. Now we learn that already the new freeway is breaking up and the cost of repairs is around $1 million dollars.

But Newport is your fun bet, the Superman of the Melbourne Project Cup. I would estimate the finishing date at 1992 and the cost around $1000 million, with 2,752,000 working days lost.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Projects galore but no end in site, The Bulletin, 13 August 1977. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
