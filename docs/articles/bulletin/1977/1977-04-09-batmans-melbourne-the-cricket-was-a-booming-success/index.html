The cricket was a booming success

4 min read.

First published 09 Apr 1977

The Melbourne Cricket Club revives its 1890s tradition of playing the Walhalla miners at the mountain-top Walhalla Cricket Ground — where sixes are lost forever in the bush, a cannon fires to celebrate wickets, and the MCC is dismissed for 119 before Walhalla United also collapses for 86.

IF ONLY Alan McGilvray had been there our bliss would have been complete. He could have described the lengthening shadows of the gums across the ground, the galahs strutting across the cricket pitch. As for the clouds of rosellas and currawongs, they would have just blown his mind.

We have just held an historic cricket match of deeper significance than the centenary affair at the Melbourne Cricket Ground. It all took place at the Walhalla Cricket Ground and there's no question that for atmosphere and sheer drama the WCG kills the MCG.

Now Walhalla is an old gold town 45 kilometres north of Moe up in the Baw Baws. Its Long Tunnel Mine was the richest thing this State has ever seen and back at the turn of the century it had a population of between 5000 and 7000. Now it has 18, a resident wombat at the hotel and its fire protection consists of a genuine horse-drawn hand-pumped fire cart. It's a beauty, the sort of thing you could sell for $100,000 on the U.S. vintage market.

Walhalla was such a big deal that every year from 1890 to 1909 the Melbourne Cricket Club used to send its crack team to play the miners.

Now Walhalla is in a ravine and there was never enough flat ground for a cricket pitch. So the miners built their cricket ground on top of a mountain above the main street. Just getting there is a 30-minute climb.

Last year the Victorian forests officer at nearby Erica, Ray Baker, restored the old ground and Ian Johnson led an MCC team and the old tradition was revived again.

Conditions were interesting. Back in Warwick Armstrong's day there was hardly any timber on the mountain because it had all gone into the mine. Now it's so thick you're lucky even to find the ground.

Hit a four and you have to be very careful to watch where the ball goes. Hit a six and it is gone forever. In the 1976 match Paul Sheahan hit six sixes in one over and they lost 23 cricket balls in one afternoon.

Naturally for the 1977 match every past player who had ever performed in the Walhalla-MCC Test series was invited. None turned up but we did get Tom Trumble, son of the great Hugh Trumble who played on the WCG, and we did get Leo O'Brien who performed in the Test series against Jardine's men back in 1932.

Leo was most impressed by the WCG wicket. There was matting over antique concrete and even the leg spinners were bowling bumpers.

Play started at noon and Lindsay George, captain of Walhalla United, sent Jeff Moss and his men of the MCC in to bat. At that moment there was an earth-shaking explosion so loud many of us dropped our cans in terror.

The workshop at the long tunnel mine had a genuine dinkum cannon that they could celebrate the golden wickets. It fired up several times during the afternoon.

The Walhalla men tore through the great MCC side causing the greatest collapse since England went for 95 in the Centenary Test. State opener John Anderson went for only two. There was a brief moment of glory when Barry Matters made 45 runs in 26 minutes including two sixes (both balls lost) but the side was all out for 119.

Walhalla was in a state of delirious cannon-firing excitement, but the miners didn't fare any better than the MCC. There was a good opening stand of 18 runs, but after that came a controversy, which will probably make a full chapter in the cricket books of such people as Crawford White and Ray Robinson.

The MCC had a bowler named Peter who was bowling so fast and keenly at the Walhalla batsmen. The local team chanted "Kay-bee, Kay-bee" from bay 13 at the WCG and I can't tell you how awesome and terrifying it was.

Lindsay George, Walhalla's star batsman, was at the crease. A sharply rising ball hit him on the pad. KB, leaping 20 feet in the air, shouting howzzzzzat, and Tom gave him out. Some keen observers in bay 13 reckoned it would have hit the stump by two metres, others thought it might have missed the leg stump by two metres, but there was great rumbling.

Walhalla United fell for only 86.

At stumps the wombats and the wallabies were just starting to come out. It will be on again in 1978 and I wouldn't be surprised if the Walhalla militia armed themselves with half a dozen cannon next time.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: The cricket was a booming success, The Bulletin, 9 April 1977. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
