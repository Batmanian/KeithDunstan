I tell you, myxo didn't get 'em all

6 min read.

First published 15 Jan 1977

A visit to the World Championship Rabbit Skinning Contest at Moyston, population 63, where former world champion "Killer" Crawford, the graceful Ray Bath, and a fearless women's competitor named Doris Tognan demonstrate that the old art of rabbiting is alive and well in pre-myxomatosis scenes just beyond Ararat.

YOU MUST appreciate that everyone has their own kinky interests. For some years now I have been a devotee of Moyston and the Rabbit Skinning Championships of the World. Moyston is 15 kilometres beyond Ararat and it is middling famous because Seppelts put out a wine of that name.

The truth is that Moyston hasn't had any grapes since heaven knows when. Its population is only 63 souls. It used to have a pub, but it is closed down. It used to have a Catholic church but that closed last year, and now a city character is turning the building into a trendy residence.

But Moyston still has Eric "Whirly" Wilson. Eric has a famous old lizzie of a motor car that he puts into the Moomba parade. It is covered all over with unbelievable moving gadgetry, which drives at least 30 propellers. Hence the name "Whirly." Well Whirly is president of the Moyston Gymkhana Committee.

Apart from Whirly, by golly, Moyston has rabbits. It is almost a pre-myxomatosis scene. You even see them splattered across the roads as you drive towards the rabbit capital.

The gymkhana, however, has become a very big day. There are 42 horse events, 18 dog events, wood chopping and some quite awe-inspiring folk events like the "Man Against Horse" 50 yards dash and the "Big Belly Fella" competition. They put the local characters with the biggest guts up on a platform and invite the public to pick the winner. The winner was an axeman and his belly rolled forward in suspended animation above his belt like a full kilderkin, although he winsomely insisted that he was a non-drinker.

But everything else is nothing compared with the great classic event — the World Championship Rabbit Skinning Contest. This is like the Melbourne Cup.

The important man behind the scenes is Ron "Killer" Crawford, getting on in years now, but nevertheless a former world champion. Killer goes out the night before and brings in the rabbits. It was pretty tough this time. He reckoned there had been a lot of eagles and foxes about, and it took him until after midnight to get his bag of 105.

There is a delicate art in this. The rabbits have to be brought in alive by ferret or whatever and if you want to get a good look at them beforehand there's not much chance. Killer wrings the necks of all the rabbits about a quarter of an hour before the show, then brings them straight to the ground in hessian bags. It is important for a world champion to have a nice, still-warm rabbit to do his skinning.

The big event takes place on the tray of a semi-trailer. You get a nice variety. First they have the Miss Tiny Tot, Miss Junior Show Girl and the Miss Moyston contests, then — straight into the rabbit skinning.

There were heats and carefully placed positions for each contestant. Each position was noted by a little effigy of Bugs Bunny. (This was a frightening piece of symbolism, like having Mickey Mouse on the job as a rat exterminator.) Stewards at the VRC couldn't have been more strict. They laid down very careful rules. Each contestant had to skin two rabbits, leaving the carcass in "export condition," then stretch and tie the two skins over wires. What's more, those skins had to be undamaged.

Most of the contestants were middle-aged, wore the classic old felt hat and had Joseph Rogers pen knives. You see, the Joseph Rogers knife, like the Burdy gun to the gun shooter, or the Stradivarius violin to the fiddler, is the absolute classic. Killer Crawford showed me his. "Over 50 years old," he said. "Bit worn but still perfect. They don't put steel like that in 'em any more."

The first heat was won in 70.5 seconds, the second heat was carried off by Killer in 90.5 but he lost points because of a nick in one of his skins and was disqualified. The third heat went to Ray Bath, a farmer from Cressy, in 55.5 seconds, a new world record.

I tell you, it gives one a feeling of awe to be present when a new world record is set and it hasn't happened to me since the days of Dawn Fraser. You could see it with Ray Bath, quiet, unflamboyant, aged 57. His speed and beautiful economy of movement was almost beyond belief. I haven't seen anyone faster except possibly an Italian making pizzas in Carlton.

Ray was world champion in 1974. He lost on a technicality in 1975 and he almost lost this time because he forgot to tie on his skins correctly at the finish.

Meanwhile we had the Ladies Skinning Contest. The number of export carcasses out the back was beginning to mount, and the blowies were adding to the indigenous flavor of the scene. I had rather hoped that the winner of the Miss Moyston contest would be in the rabbit skinning for this would have made a lovely double, but it wasn't to be. We had three competitors, Marilyn Clark (local stringer for BTV-6), Daphne Blackman and Doris Tognan.

Marilyn went well until in a violent burst of speed she pulled the leg off her second rabbit. Doris Tognan however was smooth, assured, and brilliant. She skinned her pair in 100.5 seconds and took the title. It wasn't any trouble — she said she had been skinning rabbits all her life at Mt William.

Next we had the ceremony of conferring the world title. Ray Bath received a cheque for $100 and a magnificent champion's belt, the sort they give to world title boxers. It had metal studs all over it and two silver rabbits on the buckle.

I was fascinated to learn how Ray had gathered such skill. He said that back in the pre-myxo days he had 109 dogs and worked as a professional rabbiter. He said once he worked out the total number of rabbits he killed in a year and his average was 295 rabbits a day.

He said that his 109 dogs as they set out for the attack was a scene to be remembered.
