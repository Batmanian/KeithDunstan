The language of screwballs

4 min read.

First published 27 Jul 1974

Frank Sinatra's use of 'hooker' to describe female journalists at Festival Hall sends Melbourne newsrooms to their dictionaries, while a letter from 'Irate Purist, Dandenong' prompts Keith Dunstan to consult Roget's Thesaurus for clean alternatives to queer, fairy, screw, pervert, bang, pot, intercourse, grass, fuzz, boob and other words that cause constant sniggering, before turning to the parlous shortage of genuine swear words.

YOU MAY HAVE noticed that in our beloved Festival Hall some of us were journalists — in fact a good number of scribblers, hookers, etc. — creatures who had never done a day's work in our lives. We were considerably puzzled at the word 'hooker' and journalists all over Melbourne, male and female, hurried to their dictionaries.

We found that a hooker was the muscular character who won the ball out of the pack in a game of Rugby League football or, alternatively, the dictionary said, 'Hooker N. A two-masted Dutch coasting or fishing vessel: a one masted fishing smack. Often a derisive term used for any old vessel.'

Naturally female journalists were hugely insulted at this, and complained to the Australian Journalists' Association. Nobody likes to be referred to derisively as an old vessel. Only later did they calm down when it was explained to them that Frank Sinatra was merely referring to them as prostitutes.

On the very same day your correspondent received this letter. 'Dear Editor, English is a noble language, and where is it going to with all these basic words that one can no longer use any more because of their sniggering reception. I refer to words like Queer, fairy, screw, pervert, camp, bang, pot, deviate, intercourse, bums, grass, fuzz, boob, high, jack, clap . . . oh, the list is interminable.

'I mean, where is it going to end? The possibilities are horrifying. Soon people will be struck dumb and our glorious language bastardised. Yours etc, Irate Purist, Dandenong.'

One could see exactly what Irate Purist, Dandenong, was driving at. I got out my dictionary and Roget Thesaurus to see if I could help. 'Queer' certainly was no problem — that could be overcome with odd or strange, but 'fairy' was not so easy. Monsieur Roget offers troll, dwerger, sprite, changeling, will-o-the-wisp or pigwidgeon. None of these seemed satisfactory and who wants pigwidgeons or trolls at the bottom of their garden.

Then there's 'screw.' In New York I once asked a 19-year-old typist: 'What sort of screw do you earn?' and she burst into tears. In the context the word can be overcome easily — say salary or wages. And Roget gives you 'convert,' meaning connecting one piece of wood to another via a cylinder with spiral ridge and thread. 'Pervert' — one who has lead astray, turned especially in religion. So all your perverts from now on could become heretics.

'Bang?' Explosion, slam, thump, plump, toot, back-fire or fringe of hair cut straight across the forehead.

'Pot?' Salamander, warming-pan, cauldron, chafing-dish, urn, retort, crucible, alembic, still. Alternatively you might have to 'shoot' or 'fire at' and then there are those of us who 'go to pot.' In that case you will have to go to ruin. Furthermore some of us still keep the pot under the bed. I would suggest in the most refined manner possible that this become the receptacle.

'Intercourse?' Be very careful of this word. If you plan to have 'intercourse' with somebody, especially of the other sex, I would suggest you talk or communicate.

'Grass?' From now it would be wise to refer to it as ecological herbiage. 'Fuzz?' Minute particles of down. 'Boob?' A blockhead. A dull stupid fellow. A dunce. A small mind, or 'a small kind of companion for the half-decks of merchant ships.' When referring to boobs just put into plural.

'Jack' — Knave, rib or plate with holes, jacket formerly worn by foot soldiers, East Indian fruit like a coarser breadfruit.

There are many other words which raise a constant snigger, such as Roget offers for 'screw' — spurious or bulb.

As Irate Purist, Dandenong, pointed out, there are countless more examples. Please feel free to use this page as a consulting service. If there are any other words you are nervous about, say for example 'tit,' which can be anything from a small bird to a small horse, please feel free to consult me.

We must keep the language decent.

But what worries me even more is the lack of scope for being honestly vulgar. There are only eight or nine real swear words in the English language and they are being used to death. Do you know that our most popular Australian tabloid even used the words 'shit' and 'bugger' the other day.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: The language of screwballs, The Bulletin, 27 July 1974. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
