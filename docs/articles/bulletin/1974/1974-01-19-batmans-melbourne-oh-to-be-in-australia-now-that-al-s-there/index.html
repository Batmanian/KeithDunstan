Oh to be in Australia now that Al's there

5 min read.

First published 19 Jan 1974

Al Grassby's push to attract 250,000 expatriate Australians home inspires Keith Dunstan to propose a network of Australian Nostalgia Centres in London, Paris and New York, stocked with Fosters, Four 'n' Twenty pies, footy replays and Violet Crumble Bars.

YOU HAVE read that Mr Al Grassby wants to boost the Australian migrant intake by an extra 20,000 in the next six months. You have read too of a remarkable change in policy. He intends now to bring in maybe 1000 migrant workers from the Philippines to feed the prawning factories. He has also appealed to 250,000 expatriate Australians to come home again. He has suggested to the expatriates that they could have assisted passages, yet so far there does not appear to have been a great response to this scheme. I wonder if I could help. Just before Christmas there was a remarkable case in Melbourne.

A homesick Australian, who works for Tradax, the grain people, in Geneva, sent a telex message to his head office in Melbourne asking them for four dozen Four 'n' Twenty pies. They refused at first to believe him, but as he became more insistent, demanding 'Where are my pies?' they acted. A crate of deep frozen Four 'n' Twenties from the Melbourne factory, went by taxi to Tullamarine then by Lufthansa to Geneva. Cost: around $50, over $1 a pie.

I don't believe that this case is unusual. Australians when they go overseas become very strange. They are terribly disparaging, knocking, anti-national and they find all forms of national celebration absurd. The minute they step off their 707s abroad they become aggressive, clannish, eager to express their Australianism and the superiority of all things Australian. I remember an Englishman reporting 'What I like about Australians is that they all wear kangaroos in their buttonholes. Convenient. This enables one to avoid them.' Nobody wears kangaroos in their buttonholes in Australia.

Earls Court, Kangaroo Valley, is only one example of the way in which Australians herd together overseas. I have attended a party in London, where one of the clan had actually written home for gumtips. The climax came when the gumtips were burned in the narrow grate. The scene outside was typically London-winter-horrid, and as the marvellous smell of burned eucalyptus filled the air, tears of nostalgia for sunshine, Bondi, koala bears, Young & Jacksons, cricket at the MCG, gallons of Wynvale . . . hell, we were even sentimental for Henry Bolte.

Do you see what I mean? Al Grassby can conquer this thing by setting up a series of nostalgia centres. There could be NCs in London, Manchester, Glasgow, Paris, Rome, Amsterdam, San Francisco, New York. They would have to be very carefully planned because different things get to different people. One portion of the NC would have to cater for the country bloke. There would need to be galvanised on supported by poles of stringy bark. There would be old sheep skins lying around, a wooden grid underneath, plus a superb odor coming through of sheep droppings.

For city types you would need a building maybe with a tile roof, scalloped curtains, cream brick, triple-fronted veneer. Then inside you could have the typical Australian pub, wall-to-wall burgundy patterned carpet, imitation oak panelling, Rothmans' country scene posters on the wall, illuminated cigarette machine and radio or TV set up to play permanent footy replays or tapes of racing broadcasts in the shrieking tones of the finest Oz sportscasters.

The bar would supply Fosters, Reschs, Four X, Bulimba, Swan, Coopers, Cascade, plus the finest pies and most dearly beloved counter lunches. I would suggest fried, dried up T-bones with well-greased chips or Flinders St curry and rice.

We would import the best Port Phillip mercury-laden battered flake wrapped in newspaper. Around the bar there would be footy sweaters, genuine relics from Sydney and Melbourne, worn by deeply revered saints of the field, like Hudson, Barassi, Wayne Richardson or say, Norm Provan from Sydney.

If possible I would like to keep the entire surrounds of Nostalgia Centres littered with empty cans of Fosters, empties of Tarax Black Label plus some wrappings here and there of Violet Crumble Bars and Freddo Frogs.

For Sydney ex-pats it would be important to have some good quality Nutt & Muddle one-armed bandits in the corner. The noise, the rattle of the arms, the crash back into position, would bring on waves of unbearable nostalgia for the Rabbitohs and the St George Leagues Club.

Over the PA system we would have tapes running constantly, giving typical background noises ... the beautiful music of 3AK ... rain on a galvanised iron roof ... a go ya mug ... Up there Cazaly ... the radio broadcast of Parliament ... a Melbourne horse drawn milk cart ... the marvellous Wheeeeeeeeee of an 18-gallon keg expiring ... a good Australian PMG voice saying — 'the number you have just dialled is not a working line please consult your directory and dial again' ... a total fire ban day being declared in five languages ... the sound of kangaroos being shot ... the screech of summer crickets ... an auctioneer at a cattle sale ... Norman Banks ... a speech by Sir Robert Askin or Joh Bjelke-Petersen.

Throw in a few pictures of Don Dunstan and Al Grassby in their latest swinging gear. Oh, I don't think it would be hard to get everyone back home.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Oh to be in Australia now that Al's there, The Bulletin, 19 January 1974. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
