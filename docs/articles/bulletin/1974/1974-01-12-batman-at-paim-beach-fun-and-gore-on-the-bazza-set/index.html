Fun and gore on the Bazza set

6 min read.

First published 12 Jan 1974

Keith Dunstan watches the first day of filming of 'Barry McKenzie Holds His Own' at Palm Beach, where director Bruce Beresford orchestrates scores of blood-drenched extras and an imported CSIRO blowfly to create scenes of carnage on the beach and an idyllic barbecue beside Pittwater.

F YOU WERE hoping for a quieter, more morally uplifting experience in the Number Two Bazza McKenzie film, then I fear you will be disappointed. I have witnessed the first day of the filming of Barry McKenzie Holds His Own, at Palm Beach, just north of Sydney.

The plot is still largely secret, but I have managed to glean a few scraps. Barry, after his unusual behaviour in the first episode, is now declared an 'undesirable alien' and he is not permitted into Britain. He overcomes this by disguising himself as a Pakistani, making a secret moonlight voyage across the Channel with a team of other Pakistanis. They, very sensibly, are disguised as Poms with umbrellas, briefcases and bowler hats. Barry, however, thinking a Pakistani would be like a Lawrence of Arabia, arrives on a camel and gets caught by the British secret police. These secret police are hardly over-secret; they are played by the famous English TV cops Lionel Jeffries and others.

Barry then travels across Europe and while standing amidst the glories of the Champs Elysées he wonders how the Frosbos could plan a place like that. No decent beaches, no proper restaurants, no place where you can crack a decent tube of Fosters.

Part of the film will be set somewhere near Budapest. Count Plasma, Director of Tours to Transylvania, is desperately trying to boost the intake of tourists to his country, because for many years his nation has been suffering from bad PR. If only he could entice the Queen of England to come, Transylvania would be restored to the end. He decides to kidnap her and in so doing mistakes Aunty Edna Everage for the royal lady.

Bazza pursues to rescue Aunty Edna and is captured also and gory things happen. Count Plasma, while sucking Bazza's blood, is surprised that it comes out a strange amber colour with a head on it. Meanwhile, Aunty Edna, who is showing Transylvania such an adorable little place, shows Count Plasma her home movies.

And this is where shooting started at Palm Beach, the making of the home movie. Barry Humphries was the first on the scene, dressed as Aunty Edna. He had been into David Jones' for an entirely new Edna outfit. 'A somewhat delicate enterprise,' he explained. He chose a brilliant yellow, green and black mu-mu, yellow straw hat with sash around the crown, flower ear-rings, yellow and black platform shoes and wig with... yes, Edna is now showing just a touch of grey.

Barry said: 'The difficult moment came when I asked for pantie hose. "The biggest you have in the shop," I said. That stunned the shop girl. And she said with a giggle, "Do you want the double gusset?" "Yes," I replied firmly. Anyway, they showed me to a quiet back office rather than the ladies or gentlemen's changing rooms, where I could try things on.'

Barry plays Count Plasma and at least three other parts in the film. 'Bruce Beresford doesn't know it yet, but I am trying to write in a few more parts for myself,' he says. Meanwhile, they are shooting the home movie, which consists of a series of lovable, homely, everyday Oz scenes.

It begins with Aunty Edna sitting peacefully on the beach and all is blissful until suddenly hundreds of people come running out of the water covered in blood, screaming. They have been attacked by the deadly sea wasp. This is so normal in Sydney the crowds on the beach don't even look up.

The director in charge was Bruce Beresford, the same man who directed Bazza No 1. Bruce, a man of great charm, has a gift for wooing people into doing undesirable things. He had no trouble at all in persuading half the beach of sun-tanned bathers into taking part.

He herded them together and explained what they had to do, almost like a football coach: 'Now you have just been bitten by the deadly sea wasp. When I give the signal I want you to come out of the water writhing, hands out grotesquely. And when I say writhe, I mean writhe.'

The make-up department had cinema blood in large kerosene tins. They poured this into the waves until the sea looked like a Viking massacre.

He gave the signal for everyone to come running out of the water. As amateurs they were marvellous. Perhaps they even did it with too much gusto. They rolled on the beach, arched their backs, twisted in pitiful fashion.

He stopped the action there. Then he brought in his team with car fridges filled with hunks of octopus bought at the fish market. These he draped over his victims.

The make-up ladies went round very appropriately, I thought, with blood in plastic drinking cups. These they poured over the suffering bodies. Some of the victims felt they weren't getting enough blood and asked for more.

'Now once more — scream and writhe,' shouted Bruce. 'And for God's sake don't smile when you writhe.'

This was the cue for the medical team. Up on the top of the beach was an ambulance. Doctors in white jackets with stethoscopes, stretchers and enormous hypodermic syringes came running down the beach to gather up the fallen. On the backs of their jackets were the words SHARK SQUAD. As they carried away the bloody ones, the crowds on the beach showed little interest.

Barry Humphries said: 'When this goes on the screen in Transylvania I say: "It is not as bad as it looks, only half of these nice young people are maimed for life."'

I didn't see the next day's shooting, but I am told that it was a typical Australian barbecue. Bruce Beresford found the most expensive swimming pool available with a magnificent view across Pittwater. The sort of nice home all Australians have. The barbecue took place at water's edge.

The steaks were cut so that they were actually 3ft long, and unbelievably mouth-watering. The climax came when a huge blowfly settled on a steak. Bruce Beresford got the fly from the CSIRO. They trained it for three days until it was utterly ravenous. So when it settled on the steak the camera zoomed in until it filled the entire screen.

Aunty Edna then sprayed it all over with her Aerosol can, doused it in tomato sauce and gave it to one of the unwitting ladies to eat.

No doubt there will be complaints from the Ministers for Tourism over the new movie. But there is one consolation. Count Plasma, after seeing the blood scenes at Palm Beach, immediately decides to migrate.

Source: Batman at Palm Beach: Fun and gore on the Bazza set, The Bulletin, 12 January 1974. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
