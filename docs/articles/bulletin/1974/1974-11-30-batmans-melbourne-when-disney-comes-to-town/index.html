When Disney comes to town

4 min read.

First published 30 Nov 1974

Australia's first Walt Disney production, Ride a Wild Pony, budget $1 million, filming around Chiltern near Wodonga, sends 30 vehicles of crew including cinematographer Jack Cardiff to cover the bitumen roads with earth, repaint all the shops in gold and black, install antique Plume and Atlantic petrol pumps and create a Crystal Palace Theatre with Pola Negri posters, transforming a nearly-demolished country town into a 1928 period piece that Disney plans to leave intact as a tourist attraction.

YOU WILL BE pleased to hear that a movie is being made in Victoria that won't have an R certificate. There's no sex, not even a shot of Jackie Weaver in the nude. This is Australia's first Walt Disney Productions movie, Ride a Wild Pony. Budget $1 million.

It appears to have all the warm, lovable Disney overtones . . . a rich girl, a poor 13-year-old boy and a pony that gets lost. It is an adaptation of a book by James Aldrich.

They have been filming around Scone in NSW for the past five weeks and now they have come to Chiltern, which is just off the Hume Highway, near Wodonga. The Disney people hunted three States to find a little Australian country town that was superbly dusty, vintage and suburban verandah-posted.

Well, Chiltern has been in a state of stiffening arteries and rigor mortis for the past 20 years. Many of the shops in the main street have been unoccupied. There was even a suggestion that they might put a bulldozer through half of it.

Chiltern is actually a gorgeous place. It should have been preserved under glass or formaldehyde years ago. It has the now de-licensed Vine Hotel, which allegedly has the biggest darn grape vine in the world growing in its courtyard. Next door there's an old picture theatre and the pub proprietor used to show the flicks and pull his beer at the same time.

Can you imagine what happened when the Disney crowd moved in? They looked splendid. For example there was the top camera operator Urnee Robinson who had a goatee beard, a velvet jacket, and sailor's cap. He looked like a new model Joseph Conrad. There was the English director, Don Chaffey. He too had a Colonel Sanders beard and moustache and looked like an old model Joseph Conrad. There was the Academy Award 'cinematographer' Jack Cardiff, famous for Red Shoes and The African Queen, he looked marvellous — denim jacket, denim shorts, neckchief, short socks, sand shoes, cowboy hat and dark glasses.

They rolled in with all their gear, a convoy of 30 vehicles which included caravans, mobile school room, cook house, generator trucks, giant brute arc lamps and six portable flush toilets.

Chiltern had to represent the bush town of Barambogie, circa 1928. Now I would have thought Chiltern was just perfect as it was. They might have had to update it a little to bring it forward to 1928. Not on your life — it took 30 workmen five weeks to get the place just right.

For a start they covered all the bitumen roads with earth to get that nice old Russell Drysdale look. They re-painted all the shops, all the signs, a dazzling black lettering with gold, and black lettering. What they did to the garage for example was a work of art. They put in antique hand petrol pumps labelled Plume and Atlantic, put up signs announcing they were agents for Essex cars and Perdriau tyres.

Another building became the Crystal Palace Theatre: Admission 3d Adults, Children 1d, three performances weekly. Their posters were very correct. You could see Pola Negri in The Beggar and D. W. Griffith in his latest and greatest old photo play Love Flower. The coming attraction was Gloria Swanson in Her Husband's Trademark.

The building next door they converted into The Jolly Swagman Hotel, a little out of character that name. It is the sort they give now to 1970ish $10 million suburban beer palaces. It should have been the Railway, the Albion or the Commercial.

Then you see, there are 200 extras. Practically everyone for miles around has a part, right up to mid-December, all being paid Equity rates, up to about $26 a day. I tell you, it's the biggest thing that has happened in Chiltern since the gold rush.

For shooting, the extras have had to walk up and down the street, chat in doorways. One woman in the pub, looking in the direction of Mr Chaffey's beard, tried to look like Gloria Swanson and said: 'Who knows, perhaps this afternoon I will be discovered.'

Those in the movie that have already been discovered are Michael Craig, Lorraine Bayly, John Meillon, Graham Rouse and Robert Bettles, 13, who plays the boy in the film. Oh yes, and there's wonderful Neva Carr Glynn, dare one call her the veteran actress?

What will happen when Disney Productions moves out? Chiltern just can't go back to the old days. Disney Productions plan to give them everything. The local shopkeepers love their new vintage signs. So the plan is to leave everything exactly as it is now to make Chiltern a better tourist attraction: the town where they made the film, a sort of Disney capital of Australia.
