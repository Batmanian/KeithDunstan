Topweight of turf books

4 min read.

First published 16 Nov 1974

The Australasian Book of Thoroughbred Racing — 453 pages, $19.95, with chapters by Bart Cummings, Roy Higgins, Scobie Breasley, T. J. Smith and Lady Clarke — includes the story of how Lady Clarke's family bet on Counsel for the Caulfield Cup through bookie Phil Moon, Sir Frank Packer chasing a committeeman around the birdcage with an umbrella, and jockey Roy Higgins naming Light Fingers as the best horse he ever rode.

OCTOBER-NOVEMBER are the cruel months for book reviewers. Australian books rain down like TAB tickets. If your book isn't out by November the book sellers won't order and you'll never make it for that sweet Christmas market. And that's the truth — but for that Christmas gift to Aunty the entire book trade, Commonwealth Literary Board and all, would turn up its hard covers and die.

What's more, one notices in the book review department, the books continue to get bigger and bigger. A whopper arrived this week — The Australasian Book of Thoroughbred Racing, compiled and edited by Derek Ballantine and Alan Trengrove, Stockwell Press.

It is hugely illustrated in color, 453 pages, and it incorporates the first Who's Who of the turf. Price $19.95. Apart from the selection of famous authors, this book is fascinating because of the variety of authors — Bart Cummings, Roy Higgins, T. J. Smith, Scobie Breasley, Sir Chester Manifold.

There's a marvellous chapter with the by-line Kathleen Clarke, who is Lady Clarke, wife of Sir Rupert. Take this extract: 'My father bred a horse called Counsel by Lawnmaker which he gave to my eldest sister, Pat. Old Lou Robertson had Lawrence and Counsel in the same stable, and he told my father Counsel was a good horse. Scobie asked to visit my sister. In those days women didn't talk to jockeys — that was left to the fathers and husbands. But my sister said: "That would be very nice."

'Well, do you know, he said to my sister, "If you have 100 pounds on Counsel, I think you'll be pleased." In those days that was a lot of money. We went to Phil Moon, who was everybody's bookie. All the ladies used to ring Mr Moon, they had fun. They never put on much, so when we got the news about Counsel my mother rang Phil Moon and said: "I'll have 100 pounds each way on Counsel in the Caulfield Cup."

'Somebody else in the family rang and said: "I'll have 50 pounds on Counsel." As he was then about 200 to one, Mr Moon felt something was going on in the family.

'So he went and laid off and backed Counsel himself. Well do you know, he won enough money on Counsel to buy the New Treasury Hotel. All because the family had 100 pounds each way. Isn't that funny?'

There's a chapter written with Sir Frank Packer just before he died. Here's a commendable story: 'My paper, the Telegraph, was constantly being attacked by Mr Norton, and often the attacks became personal. One day I was standing in the birdcage at the races when there was a noisy demonstration, with members of the public crowding against the fence, jeering and booing.

'As a photographer tried to take a picture of the crowd, Pat Osborne, a committeeman and a fine old gentleman, tried to stop him. This offended my Press instincts, so I said: "You can't do that. He's there. He's entitled to take a photograph." Mr Osborne thereupon turned on me and said, "Who are you, you young whipper snapper?"

'"Our little difference ended with him chasing me round the birdcage brandishing his umbrella. Some time later I discovered that the photographer I was defending was employed by my opponent Norman.'

Then there's a chapter by the great Jockey Roy Higgins, a man who has ridden 1600 winners and starved himself since he was a 15-year-old.

His best horse: Light Fingers. 'She was the best horse I ever rode and I think I will always feel that way.

'Some people say to me that Gunsynd must have taken over supremacy in my affections. No. Whatever the other horse might be, Light Fingers will always be special to me. There was not one flaw in her. I keep only one race photograph, and that is the finish of the Melbourne Cup showing Light Fingers winning — actually, I built a bar around her.'

Australia's top 10 horses? Derek Ballantine and a panel of experts, including Kevin Ryan, Bart Cummings and Kenneth Cox, discussed the matter and came up with these in order: Phar Lap, Tulloch, Vain, Carbine, Galilee, Manfred, Comic Court, Bernborough, Light Fingers, Wakeful.

The low listing for Carbine would grieve some old hands. There's a band in Melbourne called the Carbine Club, which is still dedicated to the belief that Carbine was the greatest horse that ever drew breath.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Topweight of turf books, The Bulletin, 16 November 1974. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
