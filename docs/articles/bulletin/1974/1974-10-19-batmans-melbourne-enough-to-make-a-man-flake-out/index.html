Enough to make a man flake out

4 min read.

First published 19 Oct 1974

Melbourne's three million shark-eating humans, some consuming flake 14 or 15 times a week, face a second crisis after the Victorian Health Minister seizes $20,000 worth of school shark for exceeding the 0.5 parts per million mercury limit, two years after a similar ban; a letter to the Herald demands to know how many Melburnians have actually died from flake and suggests labelling it 'Eating Bolte is a health hazard.'

I HAVE OFTEN wondered why some of the great French and American photographers, both above and below the water, have not come to Victoria to do the ideal series. The waters along the Victorian coastline through to South Australia are the world's most perilous for the shark.

In the Melbourne area alone there are nigh on three million rapacious shark-eating humans. Some are so ravenous for the flesh of the shark they eat it as often as 14 or 15 times a week. There have been seasons when so many sharks have been captured and butchered there have been fears for the future of the species. Humans have continued to multiply — return attacks by sharks on humans have been comparatively rare.

I see already that you have noticed the look about my eyes and the length of my teeth. I confess that I, too, adore shark flesh. It is pure white, almost boneless and fresh from the water it has a succulent, salty, delicious flavour. It is quite addictive. A week with no shark and you tend to feel wan and deprived.

I prefer to buy it in the classic manner from the local Greek shark centre. He dips it lightly in a yellow batter, pops it into a wire basket then sinks it into a boiling cauldron of deep frying oil.

My children also have been raised on shark flesh, just like all the children in our street. Perhaps this is why Melburnians are so savage on the football field, why we have an extra sense of bite and predatory desire in financial affairs.

I have confessed a good deal to you this morning. Shark is spurned in almost all other States. Sydney people, harborsiders particularly, seem to recoil in horror when you mention it. Only we Victorians have ever understood its true quality. That is why I am now telling you of our lust so that a good documentary could be made.

You don't hear a great deal about it because we are sensitive. The most commonly caught shark is the dog or school shark, known biologically as Flakeus Megalops and Flakeus Tasmanicus. Years ago some inspired official in government had the idea of coining the popular term flake. I have always believed that it deserved a more distinguished name. We could have honoured one of our more agile, fast-moving local political performers. We could have called it Bolte or Hawke.

But I have told you all this to explain the agony that has struck us. In 1972 we were eating 24 million pieces of Bolte a year. Our thirst for shark flesh was so great we also imported many tons from other States, including NSW. In August of that year the Victorian Government banned the catching or selling of school shark more than 28 inches long for food. Tests had shown that nearly all school shark over 28 inches had a higher mercury content than the permissible level of 0.5 parts per million.

Can you imagine the misery? Not only did this very nearly ruin the livelihoods of most of the fishermen and fish shops. It was tragedy for us. It was taking the batter out of our mouths . . . like banning hamburgers in Brooklyn.

Well, two years went by. The situation seemed to subside. I found that whenever I went to the local fish shop there was plenty of flake available. We went on eating our flake, mercury and chips and no harm came to us.

Now it has all been condemned by our Minister for Health, Mr Scanlan. We have been told that this shark, too, is dangerous to health, well over the 0.5 limit. Inspectors have seized $20,000 worth of the illegal shark.

So misery will set in again. Where will we get our plentiful supplies of hot shark now? Will we have shark-runners secretly selling it around the city squares?

This letter which appeared in the Herald was typical of what many felt. 'I think it is absurd. How many of us so far have died from flake-eating? One? or none? How many thousands have died and are dying every year from heart attacks and cancer, caused mainly by excessive cigarette smoking and consumption of alcohol, apart from the hundreds of the greatly deformed children due to the same health hazard and still huge newspaper, TV and other advertisements are allowed without any restriction or prohibition' — Jim Makis, High St, Doncaster.

Yes, why can't we carry on as before with every piece of battered shark flesh labelled: 'Eating Bolte is a health hazard.'

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Enough to make a man flake out, The Bulletin, 19 October 1974. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
