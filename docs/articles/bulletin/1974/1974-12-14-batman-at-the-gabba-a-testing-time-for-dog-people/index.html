A testing time for dog people

5 min read.

First published 14 Dec 1974

The Queensland Greyhound Owners and Trainers' Association threatens a black ban after spectators at the First Test dropped beer-can tops and broken glass on their dog track, while Keith Dunstan surveys the 'Gabba's transformation from a mound of red dirt under Moreton Bay figs to a ground with grandstands, petunia beds, barbecue facilities and excellent Press box — proposing Test matches and dog races could be run simultaneously, and noting the new innovation of foam car fridges being broken over heads on a Saturday afternoon.

THE QUEENSLAND Greyhound Owners and Trainers' Association is in a state of fury over the way spectators sat on their dog track at the 'Gabba during the First Test. Spectators dropped the tops of their beer cans and even left broken glass on the track.

You can put this down as yet another difference between the 'Gabba and Lord's. At Lord's you don't see signs all around the ground asking you not to drop bottles and cans in order to protect the feet of greyhounds.

It is interesting, also, at the 'Gabba that there are no signs urging you to save the feet of humans.

I know that I am an old 'Gabba man and I have attended every Test match there for the past 25 years. I find its metamorphosis into dogdom shattering. In the old days it had tremendous character. There was only one grandstand, the outer was a mound of red dirt under the Moreton Bay figs and over yonder you could see the train crossing the main road at Woolloongabba.

This was a marvellous scene. A guard with a flag would walk in front of the train and this gentleman would ring a bell. The conditions for the Press were rather similar to those provided at the Birdsville races. Back around 1954 militant cricket writers denounced the nice old 'Gabba saying it was no longer worthy of a Test match. Well, the smart Queenslanders didn't improve conditions for the public. But they did build a club and on top of it the best Press box in Australia.

What's more they provided fresh fruit for Press men, good lunches and very decent comforts. I think these blessings came for the first day of the First Test in 1958. An ex-Australian Test star looked round happily at the other cricket writers and said: 'What do you say, fellers — two Tests for Brisbane?'

I ask you to picture the scene now. There are grandstands almost all the way around the 'Gabba. Some are for cricket watchers, some are exclusively for dog people. Around the cricket boundary there is another fenced-off grassy area. This is the dog track. For the Test match they had planted grass, and put in lots of flower beds. Down near the scoreboard end I ask you to believe — they had large beds of petunias.

The comforts are marvellous. You can sit down, eat a meal and watch the game at the same time. At one spot you can even watch your steak being barbecued while Lillee is bowling his bouncers. A great aid to your animal passions.

In the old days you used to come dressed and disrobe when you arrived. Not any more. Correct site now for a cricket person is a towelling hat, a pair of black football shorts and nothing else. Bare feet you see. That's why I think the Queensland Cricket Ground Trust should counter-attack against the Queensland Greyhound Owners and Trainers' Association and put up signs asking dog-lovers not to drop bottles for fear of cutting the tender feet of cricket humans.

However, the dog people are so annoyed that they have threatened a total black ban for their December 19 meeting. I think this is a great pity. I don't see why the dog people and the cricket people should not work more closely together.

Indeed, it would be entirely possible to operate Test matches and dog meetings at the same time. I don't know how long a dog race takes, but it wouldn't be more than seven to 10 seconds. There are frequent times of great boredom during Test matches. They could run a dog race at the end of each over or perhaps even between balls when the fast men are operating.

Furthermore the 'Gabba has all the facilities both for the TAB and bookmakers. I think it would be very nice to be able to place your bets on Lillee, Thomson, Underwood, and Amiss as well as your favourite dog.

Cultural behaviour at the 'Gabba has always been interesting. I believe it was here that the shower of beer cans was first introduced as a form of public expression. It was here that the slow can clap was first discovered. Clever Brisbane spectators found that banging two cans together was far more meaningful and eloquent than the mere beating of hands.

If I remember correctly it was here, too, that we had the first sale of beer at cricket on a Sunday, thus maintaining the standard of noise throughout the entire five days. Furthermore at the 'Gabba I have always been impressed by the devotion of the spectators to the loads that tirelessly they have carried into the ground.

At one Test match I noticed some gentlemen carrying in an entire ice chest filled with the appropriate article. Even though cans were available this time at numerous points all around the ground the Brisbane people very sensibly continued their dogged fight against inflation. Cans outside the ground were 31 cents — inside they were 40 cents. So almost every human carried in one of those plastic foam car fridges.

Here again late on the Saturday afternoon, we saw yet another 'Gabba Australian first. The brawls in the old days always used to take place under the Moreton Bay figs down at the school end. Now, because of the dog building, there is insufficient room and the accepted quality fight area is in front of the scoreboard.

On Saturday I observed one gentleman break his empty car fridge over another gentleman's head. This became most popular. Soon car fridges were being broken all over the place and the thing to do was to throw the pieces at each other. There was quite a shower of plastic foams and the white pieces looked very pretty as they caught the light. As I say, another Gabba first.

But taking all things into consideration, the 'Gabba is now quite an attractive ground. The Adelaide oval has an advantage in that there is a superb cathedral to gaze upon and the commentators can talk about the cathedral end.

So let's have a cathedral for the 'Gabba. It shouldn't be beyond the powers of Clem Jones.
