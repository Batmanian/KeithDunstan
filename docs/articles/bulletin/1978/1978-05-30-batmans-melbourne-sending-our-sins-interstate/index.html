Sending our sins interstate

5 min read.

First published 30 May 1978

The VFL's plan to shift Sunday football to the Sydney Cricket Ground — thus keeping Melbourne's Sundays pure while exporting the violence and mayhem to NSW — leads Dunstan to propose turning Albury-Wodonga into a hermetically sealed national TV sporting capital, freeing Melbourne's Flemington and Randwick for subdivision.

SEVERAL weeks back this column lamented that the pure beauty of the sepulchral Melbourne Sunday was being desecrated. The Victorian Football League wanted to play football on Sundays and, glory be, there was even a move to open Victorian hotels on Sundays.

Mercifully our worthy Premier, Mr Hamer, put down the move for Sunday VFL football and it seems certain that our pubs also will stay somnolently closed.

But wait, the VFL had a brain wave. It decided to shift our Sunday football to the Sydney Cricket Ground and then relay it back here by television. There has been a small delay while they settle on terms and conditions but the plan is to have at least two Sunday VFL matches at the Sydney Cricket Ground this winter.

Don't you agree that this is a marvellous idea? Melburnians always have had the feeling that there is something wicked about Sydney, a dangerous area infected with slot machines. So how satisfying to be able to exorcise our dangerous Sunday tendencies and deliver them across the border. Perhaps with a little more encouragement, Sydney might open its pubs on Sunday and do Melbourne's Sunday drinking for us as well.

Last week I inspected the grand old Sydney Cricket Ground to check whether it was adequate for our beloved game. The spread of the ground is magnificent and the suspicion is that once upon a time it was even designed with Australian Rules Football in mind. The stand accommodation, however, is a little outdated and there does not appear to be any wire-enclosed tunnels from the changing rooms to the ground. We provide these in Melbourne for the protection of our players. Many Sydney supporters there adore their players to such a degree they like to caress them on the head with bottles as they run out.

Yet on the other hand the SCG is as physically attractive as the MCG and expatriate Victorians will surely feel at home. No spare space is left crudely unadorned. There are large messages to make use of Ampol, Craven Mild, Toohey Draught, GIO Government Insurance, Bridgestone Tyres, and to have a Cold Gold KB Today.

But how many people will go to the SCG to witness this foreign code? On television the other night a shrink of psychiatrists put forward the view that it is the thought of violence and the possibility of blood that actually drives crowds along to football. It is a nice release for their own awful passions. Watch other fellows flattening each other and it makes you feel better.

On Sunday, May 7, Sydney had a round of rugby league which the newspapers described as "Sunday Bloody Sunday" with the odd punching, kicking, gouging, biting and kneeing, followed by lots of suspensions and coaches describing their opposing teams as "animals." Can Melbourne compete with this? On Saturday, May 13, we had Saturday Bloody Saturday with one coach accusing the other team of being a bunch of animals. The violence was so spectacular the TV stations kept playing it over and over again just so that viewers could enjoy how violent it was.

Australian rules players tend to fly higher and fall harder, but we do lack the intimate violence of the scrums. Sydney crowds may prefer biting, which was particularly prevalent on May 7, and our players have received little training in this. We expect a crowd of at least 20,000 to 25,000 for the first SCG match.

However there is another angle to this, and your Batman has a brilliant idea which could be of benefit to the nation. Always it has been a problem indulging in live telecasts of events in your own city. Until now Melbourne people have not been able to see their VFL football live, nor their racing live, particularly the Melbourne Cup.

Indeed TAA and Ansett have even run special excursions to Tasmania so that people can watch the Cup and the VFL Grand Final on television. Television in sport is more important than the gate money, and that, rather than the possibility of sin is very likely the real reason why we are transporting our football to Sydney.

All right, that Albury-Wodonga complex is not going too well. Why not turn it into our TV-sporting capital? Build several weather-proof superb sporting grounds there. Design them for the TV camera so that they can home in to the very punch, to the closest mouthful. Albury-Wodonga is nicely poised to big population areas — Melbourne, Sydney, Adelaide — and perfect for telecasting all over the nation. In Albury-Wodonga we could hermetically seal all our tennis, football, golf, racing. Then think of all the agony it would save in our cities, the horror of the parking, the blocked suburban streets; think of the saving in litter, dropped beer cans and mess.

Think of the money to be made in some particularly choice areas of our cities. Flemington and Randwick would subdivide quite beautifully.
