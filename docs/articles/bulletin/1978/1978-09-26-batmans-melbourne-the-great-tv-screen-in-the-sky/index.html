The great TV screen in the sky

4 min read.

First published 26 Sep 1978

Melbourne is about to get two enormous outdoor Video Matrix screens, one at the MCG for instant replays at $1 million, and one for the City Square at $1.3 million, raising alarming possibilities about Big Brother, seditious protestors being drowned out by 60-metre images of Dick Hamer, and the death of the great Melbourne custom of meeting your girlfriend "under the clocks" at Flinders Street.

THE GREAT V. S. Pritchett, writing in the Sunday Times a few weeks back, was lamenting the lost art of conversation. He told of the days when he had listened spellbound to the sparkling exchanges between Yeats, AE, and other noble performers of the day.

Now the electronic age has taken over and in every window you see the shining of a blue square. The talking is done for us and we live in our own cells.

This is not entirely correct. You must appreciate that some of us, like delaying our first whisky, don't turn the knob until 6 pm. We have long stretches of daylight with no TV at all when an occasional conversation is possible.

But as Eliza Doolittle said: "Just you wait." We are coming up to 1979, and very well, because this will be the year of the great outdoors electronic machine.

We will get at least two enormous outdoor TV screens, one at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, the other in the City Square. The system is called Video Matrix.

The Melbourne Cricket Club is planning to spend a million dollars on its VM. It will replace the grand old scoreboard that we have enjoyed these past couple of decades. The club secretary, Ian Johnson, says it will be utterly computerised and it will speed up all kinds of information. The entire MCG crowd will be able to watch instant replays just as if they were at home. There will be no need for you to discuss whether Toohey was really out when he gave a touch to slips: VM will have done it all for you.

They came up with the idea that the City Square wall should be covered with an interesting electronic device. The City Council decided on the VM made by the Stewart-Warner Corporation in the US and this one will cost $1.3 million.

Melbourne's Town Clerk, Frank Rogan, is very enthusiastic and he has told me of the magic powers. It can take video tape, film, cartoons, out of newspapers, still photographs, anything. It could pick up a football match or a cricket match at the MCG. And of course, there will be news. "This will be run in the printed word straight off the typewriter or teleprinter."

Naturally there will be commercials; there will be plenty of those and he has no doubt that they will service the $1.3 million cost very handsomely.

There are some alarming possibilities. I naturally asked where would be the ideal spot for the VM machine. What happens to that little group in the square with its puny banners and faintly amplified voice when it starts providing seditious thoughts about the City Council or the State Government? A 60-metre-deep picture of Dick Hamer or the Lord Mayor could appear saying: "Not true my friends."

Lord Mayor Rockman insists that VM will never be used for the Big Brother technique. Oh, he is full of enthusiasm. Its light intensifies automatically according to the natural light in the square. He sees a captive audience of at least 60,000 people a day, to say nothing of all the passing motorists.

See what I mean? We have a saying in Melbourne: "I'll meet you under the clocks." This means meeting your girlfriend at Flinders Street Railway Station. Come autumn, 1979, you will be able to meet your girlfriend in front of the VM, say at lunch time, sit there, hold hands, and not say anything.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: The great TV screen in the sky, The Bulletin, 26 September 1978. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
