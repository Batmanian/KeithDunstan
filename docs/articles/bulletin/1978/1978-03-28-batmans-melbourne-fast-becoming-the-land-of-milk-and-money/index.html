Fast becoming the land of milk and money

5 min read.

First published 28 Mar 1978

The Victorian Dairy Industry Authority's Big M campaign — four flavoured milk flavours in handy cartons aimed at teenagers — has captured 13 percent of the soft drink market in four weeks, and now they want to put milk cocktails into hotel smorgasbords, served as a Misky or a Cooper Special.

SENSATIONAL things are happening here in Victoria. Milk is making its biggest comeback in more than 30 years. It all brings back memories of the days when I used to be an addict. The social centre of the universe used to be a milk bar called Hilliers in Collins Street.

It was here that one always went to drink and forget after the boat race, for our crew never won. The creation which eased the misery came in a long aluminium vessel and it contained double quantities of malt, double quantities of chocolate and a great blob of ice cream. It really needed to be consumed with a spoon. Bliss.

Percy Cerutty was one of those who helped to end the milk era. If he caught any of his athletes milk-devouring he would cry: "Milk is for babies, not grown men like you." And he gave us those terrible warnings of its effect on the male animal. "The deep parts of his consciousness retain the link with his mother and once he has the taste for milk he will never develop properly as an individual... That is why Australia has become a matriarchal society. I would close every dairy farm in the country and put the farmers on a pension."

What with this and talk of cholesterol we went on to safer things like whisky. All right, on January 4 of this year the Victorian Dairy Industry Authority launched a mighty advertising campaign inspired by George Patterson's. The plot was simple enough. Start by grabbing the teenage market. They introduced four flavours of milk in handy little cartons, just as Patra had done for orange juice. They called it Big M and we had Big M Iced Coffee, Big M Strawberry, Big M Chocolate and Big M Banana.

There was a great sexy poster printed in color in the newspapers showing a grinning teenage blonde wearing the most miserably inadequate string bikini you ever saw. Ahhh, but she was hugging her cartons of Big M and this ad contained a free voucher for you to go to your corner store and get a free Big M.

Well, I tell you, this campaign took off better than almost any campaign I can ever remember. This week your malted milk lover went to see Brian Purtell, general manager of the Victorian Dairy Industry Authority, and he looked very happy.

He said in the first four weeks they had gained 13 percent of the soft drink market. Before, their flavoured milk was literally nowhere. They had boosted milk by 8 percent and when the next figures came in, he was confident they would show even greater improvement. They unashamedly went out to meet the pop-fizz market head on and already there have been some cries of bias.

It began when Cadbury-Schweppes officials told Mr Ted Bytheway of the Armadale Kitchen that they would remove his refrigerator unless he stopped using it to display Big M. This is the deal. Soft drink firms lease out these smart double-door glass refrigerators for around $15 a month, provided they are packed with their own products.

A director of the Trades Practices Commission claimed milk bar owners "were being leant on" but the regional director of Cadbury-Schweppes, Bruce Burnett, said this sort of thing had been going on since God made little apples — the shops were perfectly free to get other refrigerators for their other products. And that is exactly what will happen. Any milk bar that loses a refrigerator will get a Big M fridge under precisely the same deal, according to Brian Purtell.

I asked him what would happen when the novelty thing wore off. "It won't," he said. "We have lots of new flavours on the drawing board."

And that's not all. The latest plot is to put Big M into the hotels. In a phrase that sounded distressingly familiar Brian Purtell said: "Milk is a marvellous mixer. It will go with anything — whisky, rum, brandy. I see a milk combination as the perfect drink for the businessman who doesn't want something too heavy at lunch for the drink to be served at the hotel smorgasbord.

"Would you like to try the Cooper Special?" he said enthusiastically. "This is named after Des Cooper, our chairman — cognac plus our Big M iced coffee."

I tried that and, by golly, it was jolly good. I downed it just like an old-time Hilliers job. "All right," said Brian, "now I am going to give you a Misky." That turned out to be whisky and milk and it came in a long glass. Frankly one tended to lose the Scotch taste and it seemed like particularly zippy milk.

There will be a big promotion for all this in mid-April. Hotel keepers will be invited to a show at the Hilton and there will be a great range of drinks — Coffee Marsala Float, Hot Rum Punch, Peppermint Nog, Choc-Orange Warm Up, and many others still in the hands of the designers.

Brian Purtell did offer me another drink, but I left in a hurry. There was the fear of confronting the policeman and breathalyser with the words: "I'm all right, officer, I've just been drinking my milk."
