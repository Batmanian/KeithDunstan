Living off the fat of the land

4 min read.

First published 05 Sep 1978

A road trip up the Newell Highway to Queensland — delayed by a smashed windscreen at West Wyalong, the broken windscreen capital of the world — reveals that Australia is very nearly the worst place for roadside eating, but that the great evolution of the caravan park, with its four-star facilities and heated bathroom floors, now puts more beds in country parks than in motels.

IT WAS at West Wyalong that I discovered the essential difference between Victorians and New South Welsh people.

The take-away food shop offered a number of culinary delights. There were pies, there was an extraordinary creation called a battered frankfurt on a stick and there were deep fried dim sims.

I felt that the frying of a dim sim was a crime against humanity. "Could I have mine boiled?" I asked. "Can't help you," said the proprietor with an air of shock. "You gotta be a Victorian. They're the only people who would ask."

Donald Horne, Max Harris, John Pringle and others have tried to probe the psychological depths of Sydney and Melbourne but the real difference is a layer of hard fat.

I did find another place which offered hamburgers with 12 different varieties of sauce. I have come to the conclusion that Australia is very nearly the worst place in the world for on-the-road eating. Even in darkest Indonesia you get more variety.

Actually, we were looking for a meal in Wyalong because we had a broken windscreen. I would suspect that Wyalong is the broken windscreen capital of the world. There is a marvellous collection of stones all along the edges of the highways in and out of town, perhaps even provided by the local council. The garages have signs offering INSTANT WINDSCREEN repairs. One establishment told us they had mended 17 the previous Sunday.

Their efficiency in replacing the windscreen was remarkable. First they moved in with a great suction machine that whisked away all the broken glass. They had a superb collection of replacement screens for all types of cars and they had us on the road again in under an hour. $106 please. Then if you want an insurance against your next smashed screen they can also provide a stick-on plastic screen, which will get you to the next garage. Price: $2.50.

We travelled the Newell Highway to Queensland and, apart from the lack of eating houses, I recommend it as a delightful way to go. What impressed me more than anything else was the grand evolution of the caravan park. Every town has one and any place over 5000 people has at least four. I would suspect there are now more beds in caravan parks than in motels.

If you intend to travel living in on-site vans then you must have a survival kit. I would suggest: You need sleeping bags. You should have a little electric heater. Those central-west NSW towns are like Mawson at night. A torch. That's to find the public dunny at 2 am. Also when you have four caravans plugged in to the one source, the power fuses when the character in the 20 foot 8-berther turns on his triple bar heater. A hook fitted to a suction pad. There is a curious Australian antipathy to providing hooks in public places.

A plug. The first thing in the basis of civilisation is a plug in the wash basin. This is something that is fairly general across the nation. In the wash basins there were small square pieces of black rubber cut out of a tyre. At first I thought these were somewhat inadequate for the soap. No, they were to keep the water in your basin. Apparently on a forecast of Mildew providing plugs in caravan park toilet blocks is the first thing to go.

The basic on-site van costs $21 more a night per family. The tent and power was $3.50 a night for two people. An on-site van was $8.50 a night for two and with that there was a refrigerator, stove, dishes and cutlery.

Some of them are very grand. The caravan park at Narrandera has beautiful views across Lake Talbot. The NRMA awards stars like the Michelin Guide and you get anything up to four stars. A four-starrer will have laundry, washing machines, dryer, kerbing, shop, barbecue, individual drainage for each site, slides, swings, and tiled bathrooms that would knock your eye out. I understand that at Pambula there's a caravan park which even has a toilet block with heated floors.

We travelled in a VW Campervan which was a great little machine, but here is one excellent piece of advice I can give you. When you drive off in the morning don't forget to disconnect your umbilical cord, your electricity plug. The results can be quite spectacular. Your VW Campervan is like a straining dog on a lead.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Living off the fat of the land, The Bulletin, 5 September 1978. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
