Knockers held in high regard

5 min read.

First published 17 Oct 1978

Lord Mayor Rockman's campaign against Melbourne knockers — with his proposed "I love Melbourne" bumper stickers and appeals for positive slogans — misunderstands the fundamental psychology of Melburnians, who are kygmies by nature and would draw far more tourists with an international knocking competition than with any amount of civic boosterism.

MELBOURNE is going through another remorseless period of self-examination. Our officials want it to be loved. Our Lord Mayor, Cr Rockman, announced last week that he was fed up with Melbourne knockers. "I'm tired of people being negative," he said. "The city has so many natural assets that people tend to forget about. I'm incensed by attacks on Melbourne in the media and I'm going to fight back."

So Rocky is planning to have some type of DON'T KNOCK MELBOURNE car stickers printed at his own expense. He doesn't want anything actually as crude as "Don't Knock Melbourne." He thinks that is a little negative. He likes "I love Melbourne" with a heart attached, and he is appealing to the public for ideas.

Already there has been a rush of suggestions. One sombre citizen offered: "Don't Hang Your City Before it is Sentenced."

As The Age pointed out, one fellow seemed terrified that we were all about to leave. He suggested: "Stay in Melbourne, It's Nice."

Also we had: "Don't Knock Our Scene, Stay Where It's Green" and "Before You Blow, Give Melbourne A Go."

Oh, yes, there were those who considered the steady precipitation an asset. We had, "Melbourne, Australia's Garden of Eden... Great Green Glorious Melbourne" and "Melbourne, Flower of the Garden State." There was even "Melbourne, Super City of the South," "Melbourne Where the Action is," "Melbourne Says it With Style" and "Melbourne, the Sophisticated South."

If I might dare risk the criticism of knockers I am sceptical about the value of these bumper stickers. Once upon a time your pioneer wrote a book about knocking and before you work on this subject it is important to study the psychology of the problem.

There is little doubt that of all people Melburnians adore to be knocked. You will remember Al Capp and his famous comic strip Li'l Abner. He created a mythical creature called the kygmy. The kygmy had an enormous, rotund, tempting behind. As soon as one spotted a kygmy one just had to kick it, and this was good for both parties. The kygmy liked being kicked and it relieved the frustrations of the kicker.

Other cities turn knocks aside. You could go to Paris and say quite truthfully that the citizens are rude, they detest tourists, the climate is filthy in winter, the place is dirty and larded with prostitutes. Nobody would care, not a single newspaper would print the story. The same would go for London or New York. They don't care what you think.

Now if you come to Melbourne and say the girls are gorgeous, plump and over-sexed, the fashions are brilliant, the restaurants are great and the gardens the finest the world has ever seen, we won't print that.

But if you announce that Melbourne is dull, that it is incapable of finishing anything whether it is a bridge, a mall, a city square or an underground railway, if you tell us that our Arts Centre before it is through will be an expensive botch at twice the cost of the Sydney Opera House, we'll print that. We'll go out of our way to print it, even if we have to find it in the Nome, Alaska, Tribune. We adore to be kicked, you see.

There have been lots of them lately. Remember when Barry Humphries gave his talk to the National Press Club. He designed something called an "Abysmal" for Melbourne. Instead of a city square he suggested we should have an "Abyss." This was a vast pit in the centre of the city with an abysstaurant at the bottom. Then instead of a Moomba parade we could have a Doomba parade which fell into it. This was only a small part of Mr Humphries' talk, but you should have seen the run it got in Melbourne. It was all over page three in our most famous morning daily, complete with artist's sketch. We all liked that.

The Age ran a poll the other day and they announced with some glee I thought, that 60 percent of Melburnians, if they had the choice, would live somewhere else.

You've got to understand that deep down we all love the place, but it is not something we care to have advertised or spread abroad. We'd be terribly embarrassed.

No really, if Rocky wants to get tourists, he should be more crafty. I would suggest an international knocking competition with a first prize of a month in Melbourne. We would call it the great Melbourne Knock Up or Knock In. Americans for a start would mis-interpret what it meant and come rushing by the million.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Knockers held in high regard, The Bulletin, 17 October 1978. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
