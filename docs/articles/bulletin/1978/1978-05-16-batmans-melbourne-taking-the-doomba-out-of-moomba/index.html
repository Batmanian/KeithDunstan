Taking the Doomba out of Moomba

4 min read.

First published 16 May 1978

Melbourne Tourism Authority director Sue Calwell fights to change Melburnians' self-deprecating habit of knocking their own city, a habit so ingrained that Barry Humphries' proposal to turn City Square into an abyss called "Doomba" received more press than any promotional campaign.

THERE'S DEEP distress here over Barry Humphries' plan for our City Square. You will remember that Mr Humphries spoke to the National Press Club and revealed his plan for turning Melbourne's City Square into a giant pit, a tourist attraction to be compared with the Black Hole of Calcutta and the Grand Canyon.

He said: "A gigantic excavation is what I have in mind, in short, an abyss. Then Melbourne truthfully can be called an abysmal city." The hole would be called the Abyss-Mall or the Town Hole. It would include a caveteria, a radio station — the Abyss-C, and a fibreglass cliff face. He had an idea for leading the Moomba Parade straight into the pit and this would be Doomba.

This was the most devastating assault on our beloved city since Clement Freud said: "No wonder Biggs used this place as a hide-out — Melbourne is an ideal city for an escaped convict." Or since Max Bygraves said: "I've always wanted to see a ghost town." Or since Terence Stamp said: "Melbourne is such a conventional town, I'm surprised you didn't ask the Beatles to shave off their hair."

Actually I have always had the theory that Melburnians deeply love their city, but they enjoy being knocked. Remember Al Capp's kygmy? The kygmy used to bend over and let people kick him in the behind because it made the other person feel better. That's us — we're kygmies and we love to bring happiness to folk in Sydney and elsewhere.

This has to change. Last July we acquired the Melbourne Tourism Authority. Its director is Sue Calwell, a most dynamic, energetic young lady who has an office on the 31st floor of Nauru House, where she presides, looking just literally down on her splendid client.

She said sadly: "That Barry Humphries story did us a lot of harm. It is exactly what we are fighting against."

Sue Calwell frequently used the word "negative." You could be either positive or negative in your approach. She said: "In Sydney we found that most people were positive about their city, proud of their beaches, harbor and everything. We did a similar one in Melbourne and most people were apologetic about the place. We asked them if they would like to live anywhere else, whether they would like to go to Sydney. No, they loved living here. They wouldn't want to live in Sydney in a fit. So that's our biggest problem. Change this negative approach. Make every Melburnian an ambassador for his city. I reckon it will take us five years.

"Melbourne has so much to offer. It's the fashion capital, it's the eating capital, it's the financial capital, it's the sporting capital, it's the arts capital, it's the..."

"The weather capital," one suggested.

"Oh come on," she almost shouted, warming to a cold subject. "It's the four-season capital. Melbourne's not frigid New York. We don't get snow. It's a great place for warming in front of log fires, rugging up for football, for cycling, for horse riding..."

Sue Calwell's plan is to bring an extra 300,000 tourists a year into Melbourne and she claims already they have had great success. George Patterson's have done two 60-second commercials which are being shown all around Australia and New Zealand. They are packed with quick-fire pictures of Melburnians having a beaut time — shots of the Len French ceiling in the Arts Centre, feeding in restaurants, gorgeous sunlit scenes on the Yarra, Flemington, footy, a bicycle path, yachting, breasty beauties on the beach, golf, and it is all played to a tune of Wonderful Melbourne.

All sorts of ideas are afoot. The Tramways Board is preparing some special vintage tourist trams, superbly decorated by local artists. What's more they are setting up all sorts of package tours. For example they are preparing golfing tours for the Japanese. "We have a package offering 15 golf courses — Melbourne undoubtedly is the world's best golf city and the Japanese just love it."

As I departed the enthusiastic Miss Calwell waved her arm towards the great panorama of Port Phillip Bay: "Just look at that place. Is it so hard to sell?"

Well one doesn't know. If the gentle approach doesn't work we can always go to a reverse Melbourne Kygmy Campaign. People could come here by the million to kick us and make themselves feel better.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Taking the Doomba out of Moomba, The Bulletin, 16 May 1978. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
