The language takes a mauling

5 min read.

First published 28 Feb 1978

The opening of the first block of the Bourke Street Mall, complete with orange trams still running through it, prompts a meditation on the word "mall," the years of furious political warfare between Bourke Street and Collins Street factions, and the dream of one day turning Melbourne's entire Golden Mile into a pedestrian paradise.

HERE IN Melbourne we have had all the ghastly puns — mall-contents, mall-formation, mall-practice. It has been mall-odorous and, of course, the City Council has mall-lingered no end.

These past years Melbourne has yearned for a mall. I mean, everybody else has one. There's Rundle Street, Adelaide; there's Martin Place, Sydney; there's Hay Street, Perth; and there is even Brisbane Street, Launceston. And we have been through the same terrible battle of how to pronounce it. If you are English and you think of Pall Mall, where malls all began, then it should rhyme with "shall." But if you are under the American influence then you pronounce mall like "maul."

There are those who say that Melbourne has this superb system of rectangles as laid down by Mr Hoddle in 1837, so why change it now. But we had this City Strategy Plan back in 1974. It produced the appalling word "Pedestrianisation" and malls have been on the go ever since.

The first idea was to turn lovely old Collins Street into a mall. Then we had a furious battle (mallee), and I ask you to believe that one newspaper even worked "Pell Mall" into a headline. First there was a six-week trial run in Bourke Street with the Bourke Streeters not entirely sure whether they wanted it.

Then the word seemed to get around that if you have a mall retail business in the area goes up anything between 20 and 40 percent. So the struggle settled down between Bourke and Collins Streets — some councillors on one side, some on the other.

The Herald said: "Make no mistake it's been total warfare, with big guns blazing out public petitions, secret drawings and reports, agents infiltrating enemy territory, and furious guerilla action in-between." The Businessmen's Association for the mall took up more than 10,000 signatures and a rival show in Collins Street took up a counter petition.

The leader of the Collins Street push, Mr Allan Forbes, of Georges, pointed out that Collins Street had the atmosphere, the trees and would become "a gentle oasis in the business district to which all people in the city would gravitate for a restful interlude." Bourke Street came back with the telling stuff that they had more theatres, more shops, and, even more deadly, they had a superior battery of public lavatories. The telling blow was when the Hotel Australia took out a Supreme Court writ against the City Council on the grounds that the mall would mean the end of their hotel. It was an interesting comment on the muscular 1970s. If people had to walk 50 yards to their hotel then they wouldn't go at all.

Anyway the Bourke Street push had the big guns of Myer behind them, and it was locked in. The first stage of the Bourke Street Mall was opened on Monday, February 13. The City Council had hired the firm of architects Yuncken Freeman and Everist Pty Ltd, and eventually they will spend $1.4 million with waterfalls, iridescent awnings, fancy paving and such.

It will go at least from Elizabeth Street to Russell Street and maybe further. But right now the council is spending only $70,000 and there is only one block, Elizabeth to Swanston.

So you see the mall at this stage is just a blocked off street. It has a number of disadvantages. Number one disadvantage is that you are likely to be banged in the bum at any second by a 20-ton orange tram. The traffic not only crosses it laterally as it does in Martin Place but the trams run straight down Bourke Street as well.

Our Lord Mayor Cr Irwin Rockman says trams pass through some of the great malls of the world like the Bahnhofstrasse in Zurich, but they hardly add to the restfulness of the scene. The second disadvantage to me is that this mall is right in the commercial, raw, retail heart of Melbourne.

There you are pinned between Myers, Buckleys, Darrods, Coles and Edments. Never will it be a "restful interlude" because there will always be that feeling of guilt that I haven't paid enough attention to the bargain table.

Please don't think I am against malls. The automobile unquestionably is the most loathsome thing produced in the 20th century and anything that gets it out of the way is a blessing, but this miserable little block in front of Myers is nothing. Bliss would be to turn the entire Golden Mile into a mall and leave all panting polluters on the other side of the Yarra and up beyond Victoria Street. You would be allowed to bring a car into the city only if you had a super gold-plated pass, the sort perhaps which would get you into Bowral.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: The language takes a mauling, The Bulletin, 28 February 1978. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
