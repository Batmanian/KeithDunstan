These Oranges are no lemons

5 min read.

First published 06 Jun 1978

A tram traveller of vintage antiquity defends the new Clockwork Orange trams against their hostile press — finding them smooth, silent and an improvement over the old green rattlers — while proposing that decorated tourist trams serve tea and cakes on runs to St Kilda and Toorak, pointing out the grand homes and finest tax dodges along the way.

IF YOU are not sympathetic toward our beloved Melburnian trams, I suggest you turn elsewhere. I'm a tram traveller of such antiquity I can remember when we travelled all the way from Glenferrie Road to the city for fourpence or twopence for a junior. I'm so ancient I can remember when we were forced almost under pain of death to stand up for adults. Even then the same vicious dear old lady for whom you stood up was likely to report you to your headmaster if you weren't wearing your cap.

I can remember, too, the great Melburnian floods — was it 1936? — when Toorak Road, South Yarra, was under water for more than a week and trams were considered so important there was a link-up with buses where the trams came to a halt at the edge of the lake.

All through the 60s the anti-tram lobby fought for their removal. The leaders of the anti-tram lobby were the motorists from Sydney Road, Toorak Road and High Street, who almost died from frustration daily as they joined the huge crocodile behind the trams moving along sedately at 15kmh, and stopping every 50 metres.

The anti-tram lobby is still fuming with frustration, but I reckon they have lost the battle well into the next century. The reason? For the first time since I have been alive the Melbourne & Metropolitan Tramways Board is expanding the network, and the grand opening should be about eight weeks from now. The Burwood Road line is being extended 3.4 km eastwards from Warrigal Road to Middleborough Road and that is costing $2 million. Here in Melbourne we don't do grave things like that unless we expect it to last at least a hundred years.

This line is very fancy. The rails are in their own little island down the centre of the double lane highway, utterly separated from motor traffic. They call this the "light rail" system and it can go like the wind without the usual trouble from tiresome automobiles parking on the rails in front of the tram waiting to turn right.

But wait, this is just the start. There will be a further extension right through to Springvale Road. Further extensions are being surveyed out at Preston to new housing areas and La Trobe University.

The trams are still losing better than half a million dollars a week, but the board is trying very hard and it is promoting the new image of "pollution-free transport."

The other move which will give us trams almost for eternity is this — the MMTB is buying 200 modern trams at around $300,000 each. Already these trams are appearing on routes all around Melbourne. They had a first experimental run on the East Brunswick line and out there they caused a rise in ticket sales of 30 percent.

The new trams are bright orange as opposed to the dear, old green trams — which have been trundling since the 1920s. The new jobs have copped the name "Clockwork Orange," and there has been an extraordinary hostile campaign against them in the Press. We have been told that the heating is oppressive, that the ventilation is inadequate, that they are vile in summer. When you enter them you have to pass through the front door and move on to a little bar at the left where the conductor sits with his electronic ticket machine. This makes non-payment of fare almost impossible. So there are further complaints that this queueing to pass the conductor holds up traffic and makes the new trams infinitely slower than the old.

So we have had many a tearful article, many a sobbing letter pleading for the old green trams and a pox on the Clockwork Oranges. It is true the Clockwork Oranges are being modified for better ventilation and faster fare-paying, but I still can't understand the reasoning.

On the nostalgia scene I'm a bad offender, I have nostalgia practically dripping out of my ears, but in my innocence I find the Clockwork Oranges a vast improvement, so much so that if there is an orange tram in the distance I wait for them by preference. You don't get wet, you don't get howling southerlies on their way up from Mawson, and glory be, the Clockwork Oranges are smooth and almost silent. It seems to me that travelling along Nicholson Street from Fitzroy they are much faster.

The greenies, one by one, are now being put up for sale. If you would like to live in a tram on your block at Lorne or Rosebud they are now available. Two trams, numbers 401 and 484, have gone to Seattle and there they will operate a 2.1km tourist line along the waterfront beside Puget Sound. They cost $5000 each and another $25,000 each to land them in the US.

Now if we want to make special use of the nostalgia market we can do the same thing here. We have even been promised a number of tourist trams for Melbourne but they are taking a long time to arrive. I would suggest to the chairman of the board, Mr Snell, that they be like this:

The raw material would be an old green tram but someone with the skill of Ronald Searle, Emmett or Petty would be called in to do the design. I would like it to be tasteful and with all the grace of an old Drummond Street terrace house, it would have lots of cast iron and twiddledy bits.

Inside there would be deep leather armchairs, a bar, and the opportunity to buy tea and cakes. The tourist trams would leave Town Hall at 10 am, noon and 2 pm with maybe a greeting from the Lord Mayor in his robes. Then the illustrious vehicle would go down to St Kilda, or off to Wattle Park, or why not out past the Botanic Gardens to Toorak where a charming guide could point out the stately homes and all the finest tax dodges?

I reckon it would put the Tramways Board into the black again.
