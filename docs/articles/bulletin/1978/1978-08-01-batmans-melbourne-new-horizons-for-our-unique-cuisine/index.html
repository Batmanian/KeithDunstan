New horizons for our unique cuisine

4 min read.

First published 01 Aug 1978

Premier Dick Hamer's report from Japan — that Westernisation has replaced rice and raw fish with McDonalds and Kentucky Fried, making Japanese children tubby and creating vast cheese demand — leads Dunstan to propose an Australian Trade Delegation introducing Japan to crumpets, dim sims, the barbecue, Vegemite, Violet Crumbles and Eskimo Pies.

OUR DICK HAMER has been in Japan for 12 days and he has brought back some tremendously exciting news. He said the Westernisation of Japanese eating had really moved on apace. The "old rice and fish syndrome" had given way to McDonalds, Kentucky Fried Chicken and even pizzas.

Already this new style of eating was making Japanese children tubby and Mr Hamer is delighted. What with chains of pizza houses across Japan he sees a golden future and he is advising Victorian dairy farmers to get ready. The Japanese are going to need vast quantities of cheese to fill those pizzas.

Personally I find this very comforting. My last visit to Japan was in 1966 and south of Tokyo not only was it impossible to get Western food, nobody spoke English. For several weeks, knees cracking as I sat on the floor, I lived on raw fish, tempura, sukiyaki, shio-yaki and such delectable items as dried seaweed and bean curd.

The climax of the trip was a flight from Osaka to Tokyo. The Japanese air hostess went down the aisle and handed each passenger a carton. Inside was a fine piece of bamboo with two ridiculously short chop sticks and a bubble of soya sauce. Inside the bamboo was a mess of cold rice and oh no, raw fish.

It is difficult enough trying to eat raw fish and cold rice in a Chinese restaurant, but in a bumpy aircraft with a carton on your knee and mini chop sticks it's like trying to eat peas with a butter knife. It was a situation where a visiting Australian could not afford to lose face, but even finding my face was difficult and most of the raw fish and cold rice landed down my shirt and on my trousers.

Clearly, this now is all changed, and I don't think we should hesitate on this one. If the Japanese are looking for a lead in a change of eating habits, then we should be helping our closest trading partner.

Mr Anthony should lead a special Australian Trade Delegation which would introduce Japanese to our eating culture. This delegation would include people like myself who are expert in these matters. I think I would start by teaching them to eat crumpets. It is not generally realised that Melbourne is the crumpet capital of the world. One firm alone in Melbourne produces three quarters of a million crumpets each and every week. A crumpet is a carbohydrate sponge designed to absorb gallons of hot butter. When honey is applied on top of that it is a dish fit for the Emperor himself and once again, very good for several Australian industries.

There are undreamed of opportunities with the meat pie. The correct way of eating a pie in a paper bag without dribbling is a unique Melbourne skill. However, if we are to win a permanent Japanese market I would suggest that we fall back on classic items like the dim sim. Melbourne again is the dim sim capital of the world. Admittedly they do look like contraceptives, but I think I would rather have half a dozen dims for lunch, correctly boiled not fried, than any other delicacy you can name. Here in Melbourne you will even find barristers wolfing them in their rooms.

I suppose we could introduce the Japanese to the full horror of the Australian barbecue and this would help our beef industry. We could take across some of our gas-fired barbecues and show the Japanese the correct method of singeing meat and how to wash it down with rough barbecue red. We have coin-in-the-slot barbecues along the Yarra Bank. I am sure the Tokyo City Council could be persuaded to instal coin-in-the-slot barbecues along the banks of the Sumida River.

There are undreamed of opportunities. Has anyone ever thought of exporting Vegemite to Japan? It is well known that it is highly addictive. Or have the Japanese tried the Hoadley's Violet Crumble which I have always thought was one of the finest Australian cultural creations. And how about our ice cream? Last summer I counted 67 different ice cream concoctions available at our local milk bars, but the greatest of them all was still the classic Eskimo Pie which dated back to the early thirties. We could make a killing with Eskimo Pies in Japan.

If Mr Anthony likes to include me in his delegation as special cultural adviser I have lots more ideas.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: New horizons for our unique cuisine, The Bulletin, 1 August 1978. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
