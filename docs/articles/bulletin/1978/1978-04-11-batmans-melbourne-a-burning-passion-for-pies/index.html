A burning passion for pies

5 min read.

First published 11 Apr 1978

Two visiting food experts, American diet guru Lelord Kordel who condemns pies, and British food scientist Dr Magnus Pyke who enthusiastically endorses them, give Dunstan the perfect excuse to confess a lifelong addiction to junk food, from penny Nestlé bars through liquorice fishing lines to the greatest Melbourne contribution to mankind, the Little Bourke Street dim sim.

I HAVE just invented a fine new rule which may help you to live to be 100. Don't believe anything any expert says on fitness or diet. As soon as they mention magic words like vitamin C, vitamin E, cholesterol, fibre, jogging, yoghurt, dried apricots — FLEE.

Let us just concentrate on two experts. We had a visit from Lelord Kordel, the American diet expert, and he had some bitter things to say about my greatest love, the Australian pie. "Pies," said he, "are turning Australia's paragons of fitness into candidates for the scrapheap." His comments on the filling were even more regrettable: "It resembles what little babies do in their nappies."

All right, now we have had a visit from a toothy, grinning "author of 20 books," British food scientist Dr Magnus Pyke. On pies: "I am thoroughly in favour of meat pies. For a hungry man I can hardly see anything better. I have a suspicion they are very good value for money." On hamburgers: "If we were locked up to six months in a MacDonald's hamburger shop our health would be just about complete when they let us out."

Dr Pyke thereupon claimed that our affluent society was far too pre-occupied with health and diet.

All right, after all this I am just going back to the things I love. The Americans have a name for it — junk food. The latest information is that maybe 60 percent of all that Americans eat is junk food. The line-up of fast food houses — MacDonalds, Colonel Sanders, Pizza Hut, Big Boy Hamburger, Dairy Queen — has become so vast that now the supermarkets are beginning to suffer. The supermarkets killed off the corner store and now the junkeramas are starting to kill off the supermarkets, because that's where Americans are eating. We had another expert out here, a Dr Bill Rathie, of all things a specialist on garbage. He made a study of garbage in the city of Tucson, Arizona, and came up with the figure that 40 percent of the food consumed by the people of Tucson had no food value whatever.

Isn't it fascinating when you look back on the things that really gave you the greatest joy? Magnus Pyke gives me heart. I have been a junkie all my life. I started very early on those slim little penny Nestlés chocolate bars. Always they contained a pretty picture in each one. From there I moved on to MacRobertson's Freddo frogs, then on to Violet Crumbles and then on to a junk item from which I have never recovered, licorice. Back in the dreamtime we used to get it in the form of fishing lines — put the bulk of your fishing line in your pocket, the long string in your mouth and just keep on chewing.

One can talk of great dishes like Duck a l'orange or Chicken Kiev but a junkie thinks back almost with tears in his eyes to great feeds like tomato sauce spread on white bread, or crumpets. I have heard a Wettex being described as the world's most absorbent cloth but the Australian crumpet is the world's most absorbent material for melted butter. All right, as soon as your crumpet has reached the squelchy stage then you put honey on top.

There's no question that the passion for sugar and carbohydrates is more deadly an addiction than that for hash, tobacco or alcohol. A true food junkie is never satisfied.

I am very interested in bread junkies. You will have seen them often. They go into a fancy restaurant and the average price of a dish is around $6, but the truth is that all they're interested in is the bread rolls. Red-eyed and panting they cannot wait for the waiter to put it down.

Bath buns are good too. Maybe you have forgotten the pure beauty of a bath bun as a mid-afternoon snack. I have friends who are chipoholics. They eat potato chips all day long and they carry on analysing the various brands as Len Evans does with his Krug, Perrier, and Cliquot.

Personally, if I need a genuine, junk food orgasm of the palate I go for a slice of home-made brown bread, cover it with peanut butter — not just the ordinary peanut butter but the stuff with the crunchy nuts in it — then on top of that spread honey. Then if one is in a genuine calorie Kamikaze mood, add as a third layer, sliced banana. Bliss.

I don't know whether anyone introduced Dr Magnus Pyke to the Little Bourke Street dim sim. I honestly believe that a dozen of these on a Saturday night is the greatest Australian junk contribution to mankind. Never go for them fried. Look out for the boiled variety, the dimmie which — begging your pardon — looks as if it has been cooked in a contraceptive.

I was very happy to see Dr Pyke supporting our meat pie. I am told that during a big match at the MCG they can still go through 40,000 in an afternoon. There is little harm in a pie junkie. I have friends who won't leave Melbourne because they believe the pies elsewhere would never be the same. Then there is a TV producer here who so believes in Sydney pies that he has them flown over every day by air freight.

Oh yes, I believe it is one of the beautiful cultural skills that a Melburnian junkie learns early — how to eat a pie from a paper bag without dribbling. These esoteric arguments about whether they contain meat or not are hardly important.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: A burning passion for pies, The Bulletin, 11 April 1978. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
