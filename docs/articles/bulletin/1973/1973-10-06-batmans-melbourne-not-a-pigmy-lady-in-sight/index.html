Not a pigmy lady in sight

5 min read.

First published 06 Oct 1973

Tom Wittingslow, who has attended 41 Melbourne Shows with 40 rides, side-shows and 180 employees, laments that the sideshow has been killed off by strip clubs, TV boxing money and leagues clubs, leaving only a Siamese twins illusion done by mirrors and his prize new Turbo ride, a $180,000 double ferris wheel designed to create maximum discomfort and inspire the greatest terror.

FRANKLY, I'M terribly disappointed in the entire slow scene. Where's Zenobia the thought-provoking shapely brunette? Zenobia used to go on circuit from Adelaide right through to north Queensland and she had her head cut off 40 times a day. For a matinee change she wasn't above having herself separated amidships by a circular saw.

Where's Vanessa the Undresser? She was awfully popular with the young policemen. They used to get free admission and stand up there by the dozen just to make sure ... ahhh ... that she put on a respectable show.

I miss Flaming Mamie the Tropical Heatwave of Love. I search in vain for the pigmy lady. And where's Tomahawk Joe, the wild snake man from Queensland? He's not there. There's not a dwarf, not even a fat lady. I tell you, the Melbourne Show this year has been a total disaster.

I went out to the Show to see Tom Wittingslow. He's the Harry Mailer of the side-show business. He has been to 41 Melbourne Shows. He has 40 rides, side-shows and games and he employs 180 people. This year there is only one freak show, a Siamese twins show. Outside there is a marvellous picture which depicts a near-nude busty female and a chesty male connected by the one neck, and no head.

The poster reads: 'See the Freak Travesty of the Century.' Tom Wittingslow took me inside. Stretched out on a bed was a young male in T-shirt and jeans, and a young female in T-shirt and jeans, looking utterly headless and oddly attached by the one neck.

'Of course it's an illusion,' said Tom, 'all done by mirrors, and you'd have to be a flaming nitwit to believe it. We've got six people to do this. They lie there doing absolutely nothing — one hour on and two hours off. The most boring job ever, but you wouldn't believe how many volunteer to do it.'

'But what's happened to all the side shows?' one asked. 'Where's Vanessa the Undresser?'

'Ah yes,' said Tom. 'Vanessa wasn't a bad sort either. Don't know where she is. But what can you do with strippers now. There are strip shows all up and down St Kilda and if you want to see someone with no clothes on you've only got to go to the movies.'

'But — but what about the boxing troupes?'

'You'd have to be joking. Kids can go on TV-Ringside now and get $30 or more for three rounds. Jimmy Sharman would have taken maybe $50 for five fights.'

'That goes for the Wild West shows, too. Look, all the best acts either go to the Leagues clubs or to TV.'

'But surely a nice freak or two?'

'Look, you just can't find a decent freak any more,' said Tom. 'I'm thinking of sending my son over to America to look for some. They're always very popular.'

We reminisced a little about famous freaks. For example, there was the Pinhead Chinaman. He had a head no bigger than an orange. Tom Wittingslow swears that it was that small. The Pinhead Chinaman was only four feet tall and you could pick him up with one hand. There was Claudie Alba, a marvellous big blonde girl of 12 stone. The event would go out to the he-men. Anyone who could kiss the beautiful Claudie on her sweet, virtuous, rosebud lips within the time of 60 seconds would receive one pound. It was very rarely that Claudie got kissed. Usually the male finished on the flat of his back.

There was Zimmie, the half-man, the legless wonder. He used to eat bananas, drink a bottle of soda and smoke a cigar under water. There were all the beaut fat girls, like Jolly Letty. Letty was quite small actually, just over 20 stone, but she had two sisters at home who were bigger than she was.

Of course, there was Big Flo, an awe-inspiring 7ft 6in Negro, who was 40 stone. She talked with a southern accent and the way she downed whisky was awe-inspiring. Girls like that don't come any more.

Tom Wittingslow insists that rides are the big money spinners now — haunted trains, moon walks, go-carts and such. He took me out and showed me with pride his new Turbo. It was about 40 feet high and was like a double ferris wheel. It sprouted two enormous umbrella arms of spokes. At the end of each spoke was a wire cage which revolved. The cages spun, turned upside down and whirled from 40 feet up to within inches of the ground.

The object of the machine was to create the maximum discomfort and inspire the greatest terror. Tom said: 'We only got Turbo this year. We bought it in America and by the time we transported it here, paid the duty, etc, it has cost us $180,000.'

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Not a pigmy lady in sight, The Bulletin, 6 October 1973. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
