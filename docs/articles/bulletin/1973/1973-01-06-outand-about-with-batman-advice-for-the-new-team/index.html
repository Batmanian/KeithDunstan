Advice for the new team

5 min read.

First published 06 Jan 1973

A satirical manifesto of advice to the newly elected Whitlam government, proposing new ministries for quality of life, gambling, sex, sport and protests, with nominations for permanent heads including George Moore, Germaine Greer and Barry Humphries.

THERE was a heady brilliance about those first 100 hours. When Messrs Whitlam and Barnard were in sole command, suddenly all things seemed possible. There was even the suggestion that the title for our new national anthem could be: 'Nearer my Gough to thee.'

There was a warm feeling of satisfaction too in some government departments. When the pilots' strike was on, a senior official in the department of civil aviation was in a state of bliss. He said: 'This is the first time in the history of the department that we have had the prime minister as our minister.'

Now instead of the marvellous tight efficiency we have 27 ministers and 37 departments. Colette, cartoonist for the Melbourne Herald, depicted Gough Whitlam looking out his window in Canberra with these words: 'You might say the unemployment figure has already started to drop appreciably since we took over.'

Outside there was a queue composed of characters with brief cases going off into infinity. And there's no question — there will be a job boom. Think of the secretarial jobs to be had; all the appointments of press secretaries, speech writers, PR men, researchers, secretaries to secretaries and secretaries to secretaries' secretaries.

But the ministers chosen generally are dull. They lack adventure and seem to have little to do with the deep things which motivate Australians.

The top cliché during the electioneering was 'quality of life.' So why is there no minister for quality of life? It could cover the full range of cultural matters such as the drinking of beer and the standardisation of glasses throughout the country. The New South Welshman for example who comes to Melbourne and asks for a 'middy' is greeted with a disbelieving stare.

The party could also seek an improvement in the degrading quality of cheap foods. We have meat pies and chicken rolls, sold out at sporting grounds, which would be spurned even in the poverty-stricken bazaars of poorest Java.

The QOL minister could also fight for the Australian language in the same manner that Welsh patriots are fighting for their national tongue. Instead of the disc jockey patois now adored by the young we could return to 'bonzer, dinkum, cobber, scone hot, extra grouse' and 'coming the raw prawn.' Mr Humphries might be persuaded to become the QOL permanent head.

It is also extraordinary that no department of gambling has been established. Gambling, obviously, has become the important financial activity in the country. There is a need to co-ordinate all gambling. Mr Whitlam has talked eloquently of putting together all medical benefits schemes, but what has he suggested about all the state lotteries, the Opera House lottery, the Golden Casket, Tattersalls and the rest? Nothing. Obviously a leading bookmaker or jockey should be made permanent head here. I would suggest a great Australian, respected everywhere, like George Moore, would be ideal.

Under the guidance of Dr Coombs the gambling ministry could take over much of the country's finances. We could have the Education Lottery, the Defence Lottery, and the Social Services Lottery which would solve the entire financing of these expensive activities.

The ministry could also provide a smooth, co-ordinated national TAB, plus nationalised one-armed bandits. This should solve most of the financial problems of the treasury.

After gambling the predominant national interest is sex. This can be discerned quickly from observing any bookstall. The minister could assist here by tidying up and smoothing the flow across state borders of the latest literary sexual creations. Seeing that the tax has now gone on contraceptives and the pill, government field teams could give advice on their use. One is unsure who should be the permanent head of this department, but perhaps Dr Germaine Greer would accept office.

What else? There is a ministry for recreation, but this is an absurdly loose term. We should have a ministry of sport, especially seeing that sporting figures are the most admired in Australia.

Labor is unfortunate that it does not have a famous Victorian footballer to put in charge of this department. The Country Party at least had Mac Holten, a former Collingwood star. But the ministry could work toward giving the nation more football, more race meetings and providing facilities in neglected country areas.

It might even consider chartering an underworked Qantas 747, filling it with tennis players, golfers, athletes, footballers and such and sending them on a grand sporting tour. It has already shown how deeply the government feels on this matter by sending Dawn Fraser on a free trip to the United States.

There are many other ideas that come to mind. A ministry for interstate antipathy could do much towards placating angry state premiers. A ministry for protests would also be useful. Students who want to take over the administration of universities always need help and advice.

Teams of protesters could also be a useful arm of government as in China or the USSR. Truckloads of protesters could be moved quickly to areas that required a smart demonstration, such as an embassy, or a match being played by a visiting racist team.

These are just a few tiny ideas for the prime minister. Plenty more could be made quickly available should he desire to appoint Batman as another of his advisers.

Source: out and about with Batman: Advice for the new team, The Bulletin, 6 January 1973. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
