Sliding into corruption

6 min read.

First published 05 May 1973

A formerly clean-minded journalist surveys his local newsagent's paperback rack, finds 197 of 300 covers show bared women, reads a Number 96 tie-in novel's breathless prose, and concludes that sex has killed off the wholesome Western, and that only cleverly promoted immorality in Westerns could save it.

TO TELL YOU the truth I don't think I can hold out much longer. All the things Sir Arthur Rylah, the Reverend Alan Walker, Sir Irving Benson, Mr Meagher said are coming true.

I have been corrupted. Now everything turns me on. I want to do the things Marlon Brando did in 'Last Tango in Paris.' I want to be in a real live orgy like Dan Greenburg in the December issue of Playboy. I mean there's no stopping my desperate slide. I'd even like to be the male nude centrespread in a female magazine.

Nothing like this happened five years ago and it's all due to one thing, as one of the above gentlemen said, 'the opening of the floodgates of sex.'

Some psychiatrists have said that the average male thinks of sex once every 10 minutes. I am down now to once every 10 seconds. It is all due to the remarkable confrontation on TV, radio, in the magazines and even the newspapers. The other night we had an abortion done live on commercial TV, then we had more abortion on ABV. We had a live childbirth and then we had a female psychologist who discussed sex problems using terms like 'premature ejaculation,' 'erection' and so on.

Can you begin to imagine the effect of all this on a nicely brought up journalist who until four years ago was compelled to use words like 'house of ill fame,' 'a social disease' and 'self pollution.' Very young journalists don't even know what 'self pollution' is, they think such terms refer to a by-product of the Yarra.

Oh yes, all the talk until now has been on the corrupting of the young, but look here, the young have been brought up with it. It's we old chaps who are the danger, who are on the brink of corruption and hell fire.

But where are we to turn? I am trying to cut down on R certificate films for instance. This Easter there were 17 R movies and 15 only slightly less erotic ones showing in Melbourne. And I think there have been 117 reviews of 'Last Tango in Paris.' Some critics have reviewed it three times.

Where are you to go for a clean, wholesome influence? I don't buy my books at Flinders Street Railway Station any more. They have all these 'How to do it,' 'All you need to know' and 'Fun in the Sun' magazines.

So this week I decided instead to make my purchases at our local newsagent. He has a mixed business. He is agent for the Commonwealth Bank, he has a sub-agency for the Post Office and he sells exercise books, rubbers and geometry sets. Well, he has converted one wall into a rack for paperbacks. The result is startling. The rack holds 300 different titles, and I did a quick survey. Of the 300, 197 actually had girls on the cover who were either bared to the waist or bared altogether.

One I remember especially vividly. She was young, very extravagantly shaped up front (notice how nervous I am still about using R certificate terms) and she was stretched out on the floor. Around her ankle there was a padlock and chain. Indeed it looked a very curious situation.

Others had titles like 'Warm Bodies,' 'Girl in a Big Brass Bed' and 'Swamp Girl — Bold, Daring, Brutal.' Then there were paperbacks which were based on the TV series Number 96. One showed a naked girl leaning over a chap who still had his pants on. A great many of them are featured this way. I believe this is a pertinent piece of psychology aimed at the old chaps. It is part of the Walter Mitty dream that a nymphomaniac is always waiting for him or her.

Now the Number 96 series, cutely sells for 96 cents and the blurb on the back does not vary greatly in theme. One of them reads: Bev Houghton, lolling on the settee in the briefest set of underwear Janie had ever seen her wear, looked up. 'He asked you to ring him. This producer — what's his name, Lederer? You said he asked you to ring him.' Janie bit her lip. Alex Lederer had asked her to stay behind when the others left. He'd looked her over as if she were on display in a slave market. The little ribbed sweater she'd been wearing had felt indecently clinging, providing no protection at all against his searching eyes and speculative thoughts. 'Would you?' Janie asked. 'What a question.' Bev reappeared. Her negligee hid nothing and the bikini set was now dangling from her hand. 'You would,' Janie said. 'If a girl has to she has to doesn't she? How do you imagine you get yourself a job like mine? You'll have to face the fact that this town is full of sexy bodies and pretty faces. If you want to make it you have to give that bit extra.' Janie Sommers wanted to be an actress. Very much. But she had to give that little bit extra.

After reading this my mind roved back. It's not much more than 20 years since the news stand was covered with Westerns and mysteries... you know, decent, clean killings. Out of the 300 titles now in front of me there were no Zane Greys, no Edgar Wallaces. There were never any searching eyes or speculative thoughts in Grey or Wallace.

Then the thought suddenly occurred to me, whatever happened to the Western? Back in the golden days of TV around 1960 there were better than five Westerns a night. I checked on my TV guide and now there are only four for the whole week and these mostly appear only at those dead hours when nice people are in bed. The Western, so clean, pure and decent, has been killed off by sex.

Could I only suggest to the vice squad that instead of pursuing nice bare ladies in discotheques they concentrate their energies on getting a few Zane Grey novels banned; could not a few tomes of them be burned by the Melbourne vice squad. It would be easy to find some Marlon Brando-like association with Indians, for example.

As soon as the Western was known to be daring, forbidden — capable of inspiring worry and fear in the Establishment, everybody would want it. The Western would be back on TV, in the movies, clothed centrespreads of John Wayne would be in the female magazines.

I think that the Western, cleverly promoted, could kill sex in about four weeks.
