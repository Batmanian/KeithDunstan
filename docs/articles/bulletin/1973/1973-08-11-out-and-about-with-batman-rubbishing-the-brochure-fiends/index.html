Rubbishing the brochure fiends

5 min read.

First published 11 Aug 1973

Keith Dunstan proposes a Cram device, a combination rubbish bin and letterbox with a ball-bearing swivel allowing one swift flick to tip the entire morning's mail into the rubbish, as a remedy for the flood of direct mail targeting newly engaged, newly expecting and house-building households with everything from medieval-themed wedding menus to bronze-dipped baby booties.

FOR YEARS NOW I have been working on an invention. It is an invention that could sweep the world — like the safety pin or the skinless hot-dog. It is the combination rubbish and mail gadget, hereinafter referred to as Cram. Putting it in very simple terms for the non-technical, Cram is divided into two parts. On top is a rubbish tin. On the bottom is a letter box. In the centre there is a ball-bearing swivel. This means that by a swift flick you can up-end the whole thing and tip your entire morning's mail into the rubbish compartment.

The need for Cram is desperate and the situation is worsening daily. There are fleets of small boys on bicycles who appear to do little else but deliver direct mail junk to our letter box. Here's a range of just some of the unwanted things that have come into our letter box in the past week — a packet of detergent, an invitation to buy a new American miracle atlas, a message offering everlasting friendship from our neighborhood petrol dealer, two religious tracts, three give-away newspapers, a charity lottery offering us a dream house with swimming pool in Surfers Paradise and several invitations to buy our house at dream prices. Most of this junk has ended up floating around our garden.

The need for Cram is strong because the direct mail firms have such crafty methods. I have seen a brochure from an advertising firm which says direct mail is the boom way to advertise. The firm can provide addressograph lists of all doctors, painters, garage men, carpenters, confectioners, chemists — anyone. And being even more crafty — heaven knows how they do this one — they can provide income groups. For example, they have lists of those people who earn more than $10,000 a year, more than $15,000 a year and so on.

Once you get on a list you've had it — the stuff keeps coming 10 years after you're dead: an easy way of acquiring immortality.

The three most dangerous situations are to get married, to have a baby, or to start building a house. The second your name receives public notice the direct mail boys move in. In recent weeks two girls in our office have become engaged. The engagement notice in the newspaper was all that was needed, so, to keep the Cram feasibility study, they saved the mountains of literature that came in.

The word went around the entire wedding market — reception houses, photographers, hotels, spec. builders, and jewellers — and all had their pamphlets.

One brochure came with an enormous heart on the cover. It started off: 'You have chosen your life partner. Now choose a venue for your wedding reception that will make that wonderful day live long in your heart. Romance and memories. Be it large or small, our hotels offer the most gracious and most romantic environment for your wedding banquet.'

Here was the really moving line: 'If you have your wedding reception at this hotel, the bride and groom get a complimentary guest room for the night with champagne provided. We will make the treasured memories of your wedding day so much more to remember.'

Then there were: 'Dear bride-to-be, would you like to wing your way to a happy future with us?'

'Dear bride-to-be — we can offer the most romantic of coach holidays.'

Or how about this: 'Dear bride-to-be, we would like to extend best wishes on the announcement of your engagement. May the days that unfold bring joy and happiness in the planning of your future together. Would you like to be our guest for a complimentary luncheon for two any day of the week or Sunday with your fiance or parent to discuss the complete arrangement for your marriage?'

One firm had its letter personally written and it had the nice lines, 'May the many joys that engagement days now hold increase for both of you as the years ahead unfold.'

There were 10 bridal menus on offer and they all had pretty names like 'Menu Sir John,' 'Menu Celicia,' 'Menu Baron,' and 'Menu Sir Topaz.' They ranged from $6.25 a head through to $9.95 which was the 'Menu Henry III.' On this medieval menu you get langouste a la Santa Barbara, soup a l'onion, filet de boeuf Wellington and savarin a la Parisienne. So if you have 100 guests you are up for $1000 for a start. It would be better to name a program after a stockbroker.

When your name appears in the birth columns that is the signal for the next avalanche. You get pamphlets for cots, baby baths, cribs and blankets, and I treasured a pamphlet from a nappy wash outfit which had the slogan, 'We cater for little folk who ain't house broke.'

Another firm wanted to 'permanise' baby's booties. You could have baby's first pair of booties dipped in bronze, preserving every lovable wrinkle. You can also get the same thing done for your hero's football boots.

It is interesting how the direct mail system discovers you are about to build a house. A friend of ours was inundated the moment he paid his deposit on a block of land. He presumes the direct mail people received the tip-off when his name appeared on the council records.

In came brochures on all the various types of fences — brick, wire, or wood, brochures on wrought iron gates, on furniture, bedroom suites, gardening tools, motor mowers, ready-made garages, roll-type doors, venetian blinds, dream kitchens, lighting fixtures. There were the gentlemen who wanted to paint this still unmade house and those who wanted to insure it. There were the floor tile men, the patio men, the glaziers, and, of course, the firms that offered the complete house.

Personally I don't think we have seen anything yet. The need for Cram will grow and grow. Advertising it is a problem, and obviously the best way to spread the news is by a four-color brochure, delivered to every mail box.
