Suffocated by awful anthems

5 min read.

First published 30 Jun 1973

Whitlam's national anthem competition produces terrible entries dominated by the words "God save," "Gough," and odes to nickel mines, but Keith Dunstan argues the best solution is to make Australia unique as the one country in the world that inflicts no national anthem on its subjects, since making 'Waltzing Matilda' official would ruin it forever.

GOD PRESERVE us from national anthems of all kinds. It's all right for Mr Whitlam, he's talking about the subject daily and receives a nice nationalistic, vote-getting wash-off. But for some extraordinary reason anthem lovers are unaware of his address. Entries for his anthem competition keep coming to me. Presuming that he has had four times as many as I have had, I think we should all pause for two minutes of utter unmusical silence in deep sympathy for the terrible agony that is being suffered right at this moment by his judges.

I can't tell you how awful are the entries that I have seen. For your benefit I have put them through the computer and come up with the favorite words and phrases. I am surprised to discover that the favorite word in my entries is 'Gough.' There is a feeling that if only Gough can be worked in somewhere then a grateful prime minister would almost certainly award them the $5000. After 'Gough' the favorite words are 'God save,' 'God bless,' 'God help,' and 'God defence.' Despite rumors that God is out of favor most entrants are using him in a big way. We're strong, too, on 'Long live,' 'Our Australia down under,' 'Our Anzacs,' summer skies, sweeping beaches, emus, koalas and kangaroos no end, and a strange, disturbing influx of 'golden' anythings.

There is an RSL fringe with slouch hats and eternal flames kept alight, but on the other hand there is a new young element which goes for nickel mines, iron ore and BHP. Only one anthem, curiously enough, mentions the pure glory of Australian Rules football.

Here is one of the offerings:

Y'wanna know bloody something? Of all the countries in the world, Australia's bloody best.

That was signed 'Dinkum,' North Carlton.

Yet, the best so far that Melbourne has seen has come from Mr Brian St Alban Smith, of Albert Ave, Boronia:

Sweet southern clime (though grey suburban) Storied in rhyme from Leeds to Durban, A bushman's land of fighting fools, Dear land of pies, and Aussie Rules, Land of the chiming golden wattle, Whose streets are paved with broken bottle, Whose plains, the desert golden sands, Are spread with aluminium cans, How can we sing of the love we bear? We'd have the world our feeling share. For though we love not nuclear bombs We trace descent from whingeing Poms Still ... we watch the world go by on telly While growing our soft white under-belly Which brimming with grog, o'er hangs our trousers, No sight for sensitive modern wowsers!

But if some nation's in a plight And calls for antipodean might To help to snatch them from the pickle, We then, like death, do swing our sickle And send forth our puppet-figures, Our Tiger Moths, our fifty Diggers. And boast in parliamentary session, On how we've taught the wogs a lesson.

Now providence takes up the game! Help us preserve sweet Austral's fame, From those who'd make her wear the collar, A harlot for the yankee dollar.

Oh, there are several more verses to this, but frankly I'd like to make Australia unique — the one country in the world that did not inflict on its subjects and others the suffering of a national anthem.

Archbishop Arnott, of Brisbane, the other day, talked of the necessity of a national anthem for Olympic and Empire Games. Anyone who has ever attended these nationalistic orgies knows the agony of these anthems. Imagine how refreshing it would be for all concerned after Australia had won the 100 metres freestyle for the band to play Australia's two minutes of silence. Everybody would appreciate that.

As for the playing of anthems at concerts and formal affairs, a distinguished woman music critic once made this complaint to me: 'Every time they play that song about the Queen I have to stand up. My gloves fall to the floor and I forget them. You wouldn't believe how many pairs of gloves I have lost through the national anthem.'

Mr Whitlam, obviously, wants something to play at state funerals. Heaven knows why, but he is now wondering whether 'Waltzing Matilda' is suitable. Well, of course, it is. After all, it is the tale of a suicide, and very gloomy at that.

The awful truth is that 'Waltzing Matilda' has everything required of an Australian anthem. It is totally expressive of Australia — fear of coppers, knocking off of sheep, dislike of work, suspicion of a swaggie being boozed, courage in ending it all rather than face the boss, and finally the triumphant protection of property.

But for heaven's sake don't let them pinch our song. Why isn't the National Trust doing something? Once 'Waltzing Matilda' is made official, played by 100,000 brass bands and 100-instrument symphony orchestras it will be ruined for all time.
