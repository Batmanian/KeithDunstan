Cashing in on Kelly

5 min read.

First published 18 Aug 1973

Glenrowan, long notorious for turning its back on its most famous son, has been transformed by Don and Valda Tibbits's Glenrowan Tourist Centre into a Kelly shrine with a giant fibreglass figure on the roof, while visitors can buy Ned Kelly T-shirts, beer mugs, tea towels and candles, and the star exhibit is Mick Jagger's fibreglass armour from the Kelly film.

BACK IN 1880, just before Ned Kelly was hanged, Melbourne Punch ran an 'authentic' list of Kelly relics then in existence:

'The actual threepenny bit taken from Ned Kelly. Now in existence — 14,805. Ned Kelly spurs, 170. Ned Kelly revolver — 7242. Armor (complete suit), 18. Outlaw's horses, although they were all shot at the beginning of the fray, 1500. Ned Kelly's boots, 18,500. Bullets picked up in Glenrowan Inn, 78,000 million.'

The relics are still coming in. Last October a Ned Kelly rifle turned up for auction at Joels. Several weeks ago the long lost missing cummerbund or sash was found in Benalla. This sash, green, seven foot long with gold tassels and blood-stained was worn by Ned during the last stand. The doctor who attended him, John Nicholson, souvenired it, while Kelly lay unconscious.

The discovery of this cummerbund caused some controversy in Victoria. Rabbi R. Lubofsky of St Kilda wrote to The Age: 'It is my pleasure to announce the discovery of an even more significant Kelly relic, namely his left sock, which although in an advanced stage of decomposition (the salt content fortunately preventing complete decay), must be accepted prime status as this was actually worn on the body proper. As I am concerned for the welfare of this unique memento and in order not to deprive the public the rare privilege of viewing the inestimable piece, I suggest that it be preserved in a reliquary and placed on exhibition in Melbourne's National Gallery. Here, under suitable lighting, one will actually be able to see the imprint of his foot and from an oblique frontal angle, the outline of his big toenail.'

The letter drew some angry replies from Kelly historians, but you will be interested to hear that the Kelly souvenir business is making remarkable progress. The astonishing example is Glenrowan. Now the little town of Glenrowan was the scene of the 'Last Stand' and I remember going there many a time for a Kelly story. People would turn their backs on you in the bar. Others would be more blunt. They would tell you to get going and cut out your newspaper prying. There was not a plaque, not a sign, not a photograph, to record that Glenrowan had ever been associated with Kelly.

That situation was unchanged until three years ago, now the transformation is almost beyond belief. You have the Kelly Country Motel. The Glenrowan Hotel is divided into Ned Kelly's bar, Dan Kelly's saloon bar and the Kate Kelly ladies lounge. But the most remarkable development is the Glenrowan Tourist Centre which has been open only since last Easter. This is virtually a shrine to Ned Kelly.

It is being operated with some skill by Don and Valda Tibbits. Don used to work for a printing business in Brighton, Melbourne, and he got the idea that the Kelly country had strangely ignored its most famous son, so last December he resigned his job and went to Glenrowan.

Now as you enter Glenrowan your first sight is the centre with a great figure of Ned Kelly on the roof complete with helmet and arm defiantly outstretched holding a six-gun. Inside he has charts which tell the Kelly story, maps which show where every Kelly incident took place and on the walls there are great Kelly blow-ups taken from the 1880 Australasian Sketcher. Admission is free, but Don makes his living from the sale of souvenirs and books. You would be amazed how many there are. You can get yourself a Ned Kelly T-shirt, complete with helmet and gun on the chest. There are replica Ned Kelly pistols, Kelly beer mugs, bottle openers, tin openers, key rings, paintings, wall hangings, posters, wanted notices, tasteful cast-iron Ned Kelly heads, ash trays, calendars, and you can dry up the dishes with a Ned Kelly tea towel. Remember the Henry Bolte and Bob Hawke candles? — you can do the same with Ned, burn a wick down into his skull. You can also buy Ned Kelly Christmas cards.

What else? The life of Kelly has been studied more carefully than that of any other Australian and Don Tibbits has an extraordinary range of Kelly literature priced $1.25 for a paperback through to $69 for a complete transcript of the famous 1881 royal commission into the police force.

Don Tibbits does not have a genuine suit of Kelly armor, but wait, he does have on exhibit the genuine suit worn by Mick Jagger in the Ned Kelly film. It is made of fibreglass and looks even more real than the real thing, except that for Mr Jagger it weighed only 21 pounds compared with 97 pounds for the suit used by Mr Kelly.

Don Tibbits says that young folk get a tremendous thrill when they try it on. They find it more significant to put on a suit once worn by Mick Jagger.

He plans to develop his centre, run Kelly tours round the district, put in a barbecue area and make it a real Kelly folk museum.

And there are other opportunities for development. In the toy shops you can buy Billy the Kid and Matt Dillon wild west suits. Nobody yet has put out Mick Jagger fibreglass Ned Kelly suits.

Also I find it astonishing, there is no plaque, not a sign, nothing, on the site of the old Glenrowan Inn where the great battle took place, all you see there is rubbish, and a few rusted car hulks. The old pub could be rebuilt — they could have re-enactments. I tell you what — at Disneyland they would be burning it down at 2 pm, 4 pm, 6 pm, and 8 pm daily. Admission $1.50, 60c for children.
