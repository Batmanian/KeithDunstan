Tasmanian sin needs a fair go

5 min read.

First published 07 Jul 1973

Every time Tasmania pioneers a decently sinful enterprise, the other states steal it, from the Tattersalls lottery to off-course betting to 10 o'clock closing, and now Wrest Point is offering Australia's first legal two-up but hesitating over who to appoint as ringmaster, while New South Wales threatens to legalise gambling houses and claim the sin capital mantle.

IT IS JUST a matter of time before we will be able to run the entire economy on gambling. Here in Victoria we have the Tattersalls lottery, the Tattslotto on TV, the footy pools and we have the happy choice of being able to bet on horses, dogs and humans. If only we ran the railways and tramways on a system of lucky tickets I am sure we would quickly get rid of the vast deficits there. One-armed bandits in all our schoolrooms would also take care of the shortage of funds for education.

But one wonders if New South Wales is not going too far. In recent weeks three out of four journalists have attended gambling casinos in Sydney, and the Daily Telegraph in an industrious crusading campaign has suggested that it is a sure bet that gambling houses will soon become legal in New South Wales.

I would like to make a small protest on behalf of Tasmania. Every time they come up with something decently sinful someone steals it from them. You see, for the past 80 years Tasmania has been on the verge of bankruptcy, so it has always been important to be Australia's sin capital. Tasmania was practically destitute in 1893 when it invited George Adams to start the Tattersalls lottery on a 60,000-pounds-a-year deal.

In 1953, the Reverend Crichton Barr, at Scots Church, Melbourne, said: 'A lottery is equal in potential ruin to persistent drunkenness.' The Reverend Dr A. Leewood said: 'No self-respecting government would stoop so low as to give over the raising of public finance to a private lottery.' Wouldn't they indeed! That was the year Victoria pinched the Tattersalls lottery from Tasmania.

Tasmania was the first state to legalise off-course betting. Under the Bookmakers' Act of 1932 they appointed a betting control board with bookmakers' clubs and licensed bookmakers in each city. This was a daringly ingenious method for getting out of the depression of the 1930s, but later the other states had to spoil their originality by creating their TABs. Tasmania had 10 o'clock closing way back in 1937, a courageous tourist attraction.

The casino which they opened in Hobart earlier this year was in this great tradition of making money by daring to do it first. So don't you feel that rich Sydney is acting in rather a churlish way towards Tasmania?

It is hard to know where they can turn from here. Obviously they will make Salamanca Place one of the most charming sin streets in the world. If they were clever enough, Tasmania's idea could be a free port where we could all go to get our cheap trannies, TV sets, watches, cameras and such. I would suggest that some of the finest A-graded National Trust houses be turned into beautiful vintage brothels. Salamanca Place would become the most utterly charming sin street in the world.

Then, just like Hong Kong, all sorts of young chaps could be waiting at the airport. 'I take you to hotel. You want to buy good cheap camera. You like to meet my sister. No? You like to meet my brother.' But they wouldn't want to make things over-legal. It might be wise to arrest a tourist or two, every so often. Otherwise visitors might not get that enjoyable feeling of breaking the law.

Meanwhile the casino is trying to get ahead once more by offering Australia's first legal games of two-up, and the P.R. department suggests that this could come about by the end of the month. Originally it was thought that we would have two-up along with the other games when the casino first opened.

So why have they waited so long? Obviously they are scared. This is an indigenous Australian game over which hangs much mystical folk lore, and they are terrified of doing it badly. I mean who would they get to act as ringmaster? Obviously they couldn't get a prissie type in a black tie like an ABC news reader. They would want someone who knew the terms like 'come in spinner,' 'ringie,' 'alley loafer,' someone who knew a 'nob' from a 'grey.' It has been suggested here that Paul Hogan might be the man, but he's too young; he wouldn't know the great two-up tradition.

We have been told, too, that the Wrest Point people have decided that they will use English pennies and the game will take place in a pit near one of the bars. I think this is wrong. In Melbourne, two-up used to take place at the back of the old Williamstown racecourse or all around Dudley Flats in the 'Quarries' and the 'Starch Factory.' In Sydney, they used to be held in the Paddington rubbish dump at the back of Trumper Park and for nigh on 40 years there was Thommo's. That was like Ring Lardner's 'Greatest movable crap game of New York.' Well, Joe Thomas's two-up game was in a different place every night — schools, empty factories, back yards, building sites — and the catering was good. There was always a sign up: DRINKS AND SMOKES.

I think it is important that the whole thing should feel vaguely illegal. In the centre of this shed there would be a bare concrete ring with a wooden surround. The game would be held in a shed. Outside the ring there would be a series of standing-room only positions. Outside I would have a couple of shifty looking characters in broad-brimmed pork pies. They would be the cockatoos.

Not necessary now, of course, but as I say, unless there is a nice sense of sin the whole thing won't work.

And I believe we all have to work most unselfishly to keep Tasmania the sin capital of Australia.
