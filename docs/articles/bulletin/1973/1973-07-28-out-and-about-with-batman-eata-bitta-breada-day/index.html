Eata bitta breada day

5 min read.

First published 28 Jul 1973

Amid the great diet bore of 1973, with 235 household diets in two years and Henry Bolte's grapefruit causing an Israeli fortune, the bread manufacturers fight back with a six-slices-a-day diet launched in the rain at Caulfield railway station with Ron Barassi present, though Keith Dunstan suggests the real PR solution would be spreading a rumour that wholemeal bread is the most potent aphrodisiac known to mankind.

DID YOU READ that Margaret Whitlam opened Weight Watchers' Week? While her husband was up at Surfers Paradise dealing with the great problems of our time, she was in Sydney coping with that other problem — too much affluence and not enough self denial. It was reported that Mrs Whitlam had a cup of coffee, selected a cracker with curried egg on top, spurned the skim milk, rejected saccharine and left the sandwiches untouched.

I tell you the diet thing is becoming the greatest bore in history. You get it at every party, every lunch, every food occasion where people try to kid themselves they are not eating. At our house this past two years I swear we have had 235 different diets — egg diets, spinach diets, health food diets, probiotic diets. We've eaten seaweed and rose hips, wheat germ and muesli like gravel.

And things move in and out of fashion. One week sugar is going to kill you — then it's all right. Next week, fats, eggs and such are going to kill you. Then some doctor tells you — that's after he's threatened you with death for the past five years — that the cholesterol thing is OK — it's something else that is going to kill you, like too much carbohydrates.

We have listened to those who say you've got to jog or you'll die of a heart attack and we have listened to those who say, don't jog you will die of a heart attack. We have counted calories, we have counted units on the carbohydrate diet. Ye gods, we've drunk herbal tea and anything is better than that. We have drunk carrot juice, and fought off hunger with an acre of pineapples.

Henry Bolte came back from the U.S., a year or two ago. He was a slimmer, racy, utterly more dashing and fetching Henry Bolte. He told the world he had lost a stone by eating grapefruit. You have no idea what magic this caused with the sale of grapefruit. The only trouble was he gave the good news at a time when our grapefruit were out of season. We found ourselves paying a daily fortune for grapefruit grown in Israel. Unquestionably, some Israeli grapefruit millionaires are immensely grateful to Henry Bolte.

And if there are any young writers in the audience here is the recipe how to get rich. According to our local book seller, here are the current charts for top sales: way up high are books on sex, particularly scientific how-it-is-done books. Next come cook books, then diet and get fit books are third. It is, at least, a trifle reassuring that cook books are still slightly in front of un-cook books.

Of course the egg diet was one of the most interesting. It was sponsored by the egg producers and you eat three or four eggs a day — about 24 a week. Keep it up for six months and they make you a member of the Egg Board.

Now the bread manufacturers are fighting back. They are worried that we are not eating enough bread and their public relations team has come up with the idea of a bread diet. In my innocence I believed, and I had always been told, bread makes you as fat as a tick. But the Bread Manufacturers' Association announced: 'For many people counting calories has become a fashionable part of modern living. However counting calories in itself is not the recipe for a slim figure or good health. A balance of foods is essential in any reducing diet... most people have the idea that bread is fattening and should be excluded. This is not so. One slice of bread contains no more calories than the average-sized piece of fruit.'

And they go on to tell you what marvellous stuff bread is and people have been eating it right back to Biblical times.

On the bread diet you get to eat six slices of bread a day. For example: on day one, you get 4oz orange juice, a boiled egg and two thin slices of buttered toast for breakfast. For morning tea you get a slice of bread and fish paste. For lunch you get two slices of bread and butter or margarine with lettuce, tomato and cucumber. Dinner you get iced water, 5oz lean steak, spinach, marrow, green salad, and, lo, another slice of bread.

This style you lose two to three pounds a week. Along with the diet chart comes a list of forbidden foods: sugar, sweets, chocolates, cakes, honey, chutney, jam, marmalade, tinned fruits, ice cream, alcoholic drinks, pastries, scones, desserts, spaghetti, potato chips, nuts, peanut butter.

I think this is terribly unfair on other industries. I am sure Cadburys will fight back with the chocolate peppermint cream diet, the spaghetti manufacturers with the spaghetti diet and the jam people with the marmalade diet. And I would be very interested in the peanut butter and honey diet.

But meanwhile the bread people are in there fighting. They launched their diet in the rain at Caulfield railway station with the announcement that too many people went off to work on inadequate breakfasts. So for some days they served coffee and hot toast to commuters. Present on the first morning was Ron Barassi, coach of the North Melbourne football team. It is very difficult to launch anything in Melbourne unless you have a footballer present.

As the commuters went by, bread girls offered them coffee and toast. There were a number of comments that went like this: 'You've got to be kidding. I've had my breakfast and I've cleaned my teeth.'

Mr Barassi was a little unhappy because they had only vegemite or marmalade to put on the toast and he didn't like either.

However, one wishes the bread manufacturers well with their campaign. If it fails could one suggest to the PR team they spread the rumor that say, wholemeal bread is the most potent aphrodisiac known to mankind, and even white bread restores sexual virility? After all, sex is still doing better on the charts than diet books.
