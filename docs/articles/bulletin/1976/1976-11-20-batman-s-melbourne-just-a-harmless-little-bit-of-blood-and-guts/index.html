Just a harmless little bit of blood and guts

5 min read.

First published 20 Nov 1976

A sentimental moviegoer's fruitless search for a decent picture in Melbourne's current release schedule, which offers nothing but demonic children, splatter films, Nazi brothels and sexually transmitted disease comedies, with the only hope a 100-foot gorilla arriving on Christmas Eve.

A FILM called Deep Red with David Hemmings arrived in town last Thursday. The film ads say: 'When was the last time you were really scared? The Exorcist? Psycho? Jaws? Now there is Deep Red. You will never forget it.' The ad shows a screaming female head upside down in a mess of blood with a wild character chopping away at her neck with a butcher's meat axe.

Deep Red is the latest in the spook, demon, awfully evil movies. They're coming in a vomit-green rush just like the age-old gallop of westerns.

There is another one in town called The Omen. This has made $50 million in the U.S. and it is making a bundle here. It is all about a repulsive little five-year-old called Damien who wears a school cap and short pants. Apparently he is a pint-sized Satan who makes Frankenstein look sweeter than Jimmy Carter. There are a series of macabre and suitably bloody big screen deaths. Dear little Damien forces his nanny to commit suicide. He pushes his mum over a balcony and there's some good stuff like a character being impaled by a lightning conductor and a slow decapitation by glass.

According to Time, Harvey Bernhard, the producer, is now working on three sequels to The Omen and little Damien is making a triumphant return, our new satanic Shirley Temple.

And if you want more green vomit we are to get another Exorcist to be titled The Heretic — Exorcist II.

I am telling you this because I am one of those who really loved Sound of Music. I was even turned on by Richard Burton singing How Do You Handle A Woman in Camelot. It's worse than that. I even used to adore Bob, Bing and Dotty in Road To Bali and all that sort of thing.

Obviously I'm a sentimental idiot. I don't get enjoyment out of the beaut things of life, like watching blood spread across the water and nice girls being eaten by sharks, or a skyscraper on fire with everyone screaming and jumping out of the upper floors. Or a mass earthquake in which by some marvellous jiggery-pokery they make you believe that you too are part of it and you will die along with the thousands on the screen.

Oh we need help. Last weekend my wife and I wanted to take in a movie. I think that's the correct term. We rejected The Omen and Deep Red, because our local parish church is taking a sufficient interest in Satanic bashing now without Satan winning them all.

I suggested we should go to see Griffen and Phoenix. You see Peter Falk is in it. He's Colombo on the TV series and I'm crazy about him because he drives an ancient Peugeot and he's so polite to his criminals.

'What's it about?' she said.

'It's about two people in love who are dying of terminal cancer,' I said.

'That's lovely,' she said. 'Just what we need.'

'Well, wait a minute,' I replied. 'There's another Peter Falk show in town. Let's go to that. It's called A Woman Under The Influence.'

'What's it about?'

'Um, according to the reviews it's all about this nice housewife who is going progressively mad. Her blundering husband, in his stumbling aggressive way... that's Peter Falk... tries to help, but don't worry she goes to the nuthouse.'

'You can keep that one too.'

So I suggest Survive. The ads show the picture of this crashed airliner in the snow. I can talk with authority now because I can remember the real story on which it was based. 'Well you see, this airliner gets lost in the Andes and it crashes into a mountain top, about 17,000 feet up. Their radio's out. They can't be located. Finally their only hope of survival is to start eating the dead frozen bodies.'

'You get that on film?'

'Sure.'

'Ye Gods.'

So we don't go to Survive. But there's still hope. We like the look of The Sailor Who Fell From Grace With The Sea. That stars Kris Kristofferson and Sarah Miles. OK. Sarah Miles is a lonely widow, who, according to the review, has had her sexual emotions repressed. Her young son spies on his mother's sexual acts by peeping through a keyhole and with all sorts of psychological sadism the happy relationship is ruined.

The review says: 'The grim dissection of family cat and an explosive death of a seagull are grizzly preludes to a tearful finale.'

We looked on and on. There was Margaux Hemingway in Lipstick. That's about a lovely model who gets violently and viciously raped. She is so incensed she wreaks her terrible revenge, said my wife reading the blurb. 'No, I only like happy rapes,' I said.

There were a couple of Nazi movies. One was about a surgeon whose wife was slaughtered by the Nazis. Years later he got his revenge by hunting down her killers and systematically butchering all 10 of them. Then there was another chiller about villainy in a Berlin Nazi brothel.

I read to my wife a few more ads.

'Look here, there's Adam and Nicole... "A luscious look at naked greed. People with a lust for constant sexual release indulge in an orgy you will never forget."'

'Or how about this. There is a lovely picture of a naked boy and girl holding each other. It's quite tender really. The blurb says: "Only the British could extract so much humor from the situations in which these people find themselves involved. It could happen to you. It is a film with a message about sexually transmitted diseases and contains vital information for people of all ages."'

'Oh lovely,' she said.

'Very well then,' I continued, 'our only hope is to wait for King Kong. That's a movie about a 100 foot high gorilla who tears aeroplanes out of the sky and has a lovely relationship with a blonde. I think it opens as a goodwill gesture on Christmas eve.'

'Well, I suppose the rack does have its compensations.'
