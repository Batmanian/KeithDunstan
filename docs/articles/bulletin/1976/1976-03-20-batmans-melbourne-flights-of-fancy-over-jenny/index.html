Flights of fancy over Jenny...

6 min read.

First published 20 Mar 1976

Toy company Toltoys hires a Boeing 747 to secretly launch their new Jenny doll to 365 toy buyers mid-flight over the east coast, with film screens, saturation TV advertising and a duty-free bag for every buyer, while the Sydney Toy Fair also reveals the booming Six Million Dollar Man range and a quarter-million Sindy dolls already sold in Australia.

THE QANTAS folk say it is quite a regular thing now. If you are swinging a big deal, if you want to impress your buyers, you hire a Boeing 747. Anyone can do it for around $6000 an hour. It has this beautiful advantage — you have a captive audience. It is very difficult to leave a Boeing 747 at 32,000 feet.

The Channel 7 network hired one some weeks back. Last week Toltoys did it for the toy buyers in the midst of the Sydney Toy Fair. You must realise this is a deadly serious time of the year. Come March, all the preparations, all the big buying is done for Christmas.

OK then, Toltoys filled the Jenny aeroplane, invited 365 toy-buyers from all over the nation to go for an hour-and-a-half flight in the jumbo jet. They weren't told why. The purpose of the flight was top secret. Oh, competition is big in the toy industry. Preparations to preserve secrecy would make ASIO operations seem tame indeed. The flight was on the Tuesday — the movie commercial wasn't completed until the previous Friday night. Right up until the previous day or two there was only one copy of the product that anyone could see.

During the toy fair they had two dummy releases to put rivals off the scent. The suggestions were that the big promotion was for (a) the Sonny and Cher toy kit (b) the Six Million Dollar Man.

Oh it was very nice. Trip out to the international airport. Everybody had their Qantas international ticket with their seat numbers. As we were seated we were told we would fly down the coast to Wollongong, across to Canberra, to Albury, then back home again. Hostesses came by with drink trolleys — whatever anything you want. Then came the big deal. Up went five movie screens throughout the aircraft. We were all issued with our sterilised and clean headphones. There were 10 channels. We plugged into channel three. Lights, camera, music. In full color — the director of Toltoys, wearing a Qantas skipper's hat. He is sitting up on the controls with Bert Newton also in a skipper's hat.

We are told that Toltoys is introducing Jenny (begging their pardon, a Barbie-type doll) who is based on Jenny Tregaskis, a real live Qantas air hostess.

Jenny has fun. Jenny goes all over the world — to South Africa, Austria, Hawaii, Japan. Every little girl's dream is to be an air hostess, so she can get her Jenny doll and buy the gear for all these situations. She can have her big-game South Africa Safari outfit. She can have her Austrian skiing gear complete with skis, her Hawaiian grass skirt and also surf board, her Japanese kimono, to say nothing of her fair-dinkum Pucci Qantas air hostess uniform.

We are told on this film that there will be saturation TV advertising for Jenny from July right through to September. What's more, the commercials will be twice as long as commercials normally are. 'I promise you,' says Ken Fee, 'this thing is really going to make Jenny take off. We are going to promote Jenny like nothing ever before.'

The suggestion is that every buyer reach right now for his order book.

Everybody is pretty happy by now. There are cheers and claps for the film.

For Toltoys and General Mills is very big indeed. Paul Curran is tall, quietly spoken and conservatively dressed in dark blue like a Wall Street banker. He doesn't look anything like a toy buyer. He was once a champion gridiron footballer and played for Notre Dame. These days he spends much of his life travelling. Recently he went from Minneapolis to Rome for a two-hour board meeting, just straight there and back, using the same aircraft. He also did something similar in London, there and back without sleeping in England. He says you get no jet lag that way.

We felt pretty honoured. He was spending three whole days in Australia.

I asked him what was the biggest selling toy in the world. There was no doubt about that at all. Monopoly continued to be the top seller. It far outstripped them all.

'Selling toys though is a nightmare,' he said. 'You have to keep finding new products. You have to plan it 18 months ahead and few toys last longer than three years.

'This means that every year 35 per cent of your output is involved in handling a new product. What other manufacturing industry is involved in a situation like that?'

He said the new current best-seller was the Six Million Dollar Man. He comes with a whole range of accessories — rocket ship, bionic head, bionic eye, etc. There is also the Six Million Dollar Woman and Maskatron — the man's villain rival.

All the 365 buyers are wandering all over the jumbo jet, eating, drinking, having a fine old time. As we are about to land we are told: 'You have been on an international flight so naturally you are entitled to duty free stores.'

It is a gag. As everyone leaves the aircraft they get a 'duty free' bag. It contains all sorts of free gifts like panty hose, nail lacquer, bottle of wine and curiously a packet of French onion soup — probably a secret Toltoy aphrodisiac to make them fill out order forms.

IN ADDITION to all the other toys at the Toy Fair at the Sydney Hilton last week — the boats, planes, cars, trains, tanks, cranes, pools, pool-tables, pogo sticks, bikes, guns, games, cowboys, Indians, castles, houses, horses, footballs, lettering sets, typewriters, balloons, puzzles, paints, perfumes, darts and dartboards, transfers, tents and trampolines, to name but a few — there were dolls: leopards, lambs, fish tortoises, panthers, monkeys, dogs, rabbits, seals, ducks, hedgehogs, chooks, badgers, dinosaurs, kangaroos, Wombats, Snufflepaguses, Grouches, Sunbonnet Babies, Holly Hobbies and her friends named Heather, bears, elephants, and a pretty good looking line in owls.

All of which were pretty dowdy compared with the Sindy dolls.

Sindy is not so much a doll as a way of wife. She should be a splendid primer for the little girl who one day will make life a hell upon earth for two or three husbands. More than a quarter of a million of them have been bought in Australia so far. It may explain the increase in the divorce rate.

The Sindy-fancier begins with $3.98 worth of doll and, $140 later, has about 30 outfits including a tutu, shortie pyjamas, a bridal gown and riding gear; and boots, shoes, sandals and slippers.
