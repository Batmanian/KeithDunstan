Melbourne returns to silent Sundays

4 min read.

First published 14 Feb 1976

Victoria's new noise control legislation sets decibel limits for lawnmowers, power tools and amplified music, prompting a rooftop test session that reveals the four-stroke Rover mower as the suburban Sunday's prime villain at 91 decibels, while Melbourne simultaneously prepares to welcome the Concorde.

MELBOURNE always used to be renowned for its beautiful sepulchral qualities.

As the late Brian Fitzpatrick once said, the Melbourne Sunday was a thing of timeless beauty and perfection, like the winged victory of Samothrace. One can remember too a saying that the only sign of life in Melbourne on a Sunday was the wisp of smoke from the Springvale crematorium.

Then did you know the original reason why Sunday newspapers were banned in Victoria? When the government brought down the Sunday Newspapers Bill it was decided Sunday publications were intolerable because newspaper boys would scream out crude headlines within earshot of churches.

How interesting then that the government has decided it necessary to introduce noise control legislation to maintain the purity of suburbs. The new bill was proclaimed last week.

Now we have provisions like this: motor mowers, you can operate from Monday to Friday only between 7 am and 8 pm, on Saturdays and Sundays, only between 9 am and 8 pm.

Electric power tools: 7 am to 10 pm weekdays. Weekends 9 am to 8 pm.

Electrically amplified musical instruments: weekdays, 7 am to 11 pm, weekends, 9 am to 11 pm.

Car and truck noise levels will be decided some time in April.

The penalty for infringement is $400. Many of us are wondering how this will work. Will your neighbor turn into a cobber dobber? As soon as junior has a party and turns up Skyhooks to 105 decibels will your friends next door anonymously call the police?

Will the sound meter, rather than the breathalyser, be the new dreaded instrument of the suburbs?

The man who has the worry of all this is Bob Snow, 32, a sound engineer, the head of the Environment Protection Authority. Snow has been quoted as saying 'I don't want the new legislation to stop people enjoying themselves. I expect most breaches of the legislation to be settled by discussions between the parties involved. When such talks fail, the police and local council officials will have power to charge offenders.'

But when does noise become offensive? It is terribly hard to define. Batman consulted Graeme Moss, an acoustics engineer.

'A lot of it can be psychological,' he said. 'An air conditioning unit in a hotel room, which is making only a comparatively small noise, can drive you out of your mind if you're trying to sleep.

'A lawnmower noise in an industrial suburb, which already has a fair level of background noise, may not sound very loud. In a quiet residential suburb it is earth shattering.'

We thought it might be a good idea to have a noise party. To re-create on the rooftop of Myers, Bourke Street, the do-it-yourself noises that were likely to come across a garden fence.

Graeme Moss had a very sophisticated $1000 noise recording instrument and we chose a range of three metres as if the noise were coming from a carport below the bedroom window.

Now 40 decibels is what is considered a generally acceptable after hours noise for the suburbs.

We started with a small Japanese hand planer which operated at 15,000 RPM. That gave off a steady 75DB. A Black and Decker 5 inch grinder at 5000 RPM gave off 70DB when running and 78DB when grinding metal. A two-speed drill was good for 70DB. A small Homelite chainsaw, a classic Sunday morning nerve-shatterer out our way, did 83DB when idling and 88 when slashing wood. This was surprising. I thought it might have scored higher.

A flymo Electrolux, a floating-on-air type of lawnmower gave off a shrill 82DB. This was the particularly interesting test. We wanted to compare the electric with two-stroke and four-stroke motors. A two-stroke 160cc Victa machine operated at 79DB when idling and 89DB when actually mowing. A four-stroke, 171cc Rover was 79 when idling but gave off a noble blast of 91 when mowing.

Indeed this was the prime villain of the day. Even a Black and Decker 7½in saw when screaming through a 2 by 4 piece of old hardwood only reached 88.

But who are we to talk about noise? Meanwhile Melbourne is one of the few cities that is actively and vigorously welcoming the Concorde. One trusts that British Airways won't choose to bring it across the suburbs on one of our sepulchral Sunday mornings.
