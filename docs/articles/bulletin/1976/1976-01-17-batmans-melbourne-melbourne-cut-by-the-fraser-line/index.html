Melbourne cut by the Fraser line

4 min read.

First published 17 Jan 1976

The Federal election results have split Melbourne cleanly along the Yarra, with Liberal blue on the wealthy south and east and Labor grey across the working-class north and west, producing a battle map that resembles the fall of Europe to the Germans in 1940.

FRANKLY HERE in Melbourne we have always been a snooty lot. In some cities, such as Manila, the rich live close to each other because they have no other choice. They do so for their own protection.

In Melbourne it is a matter of prestige. The well-to-do, the rich retailers, manufacturers, the land-owners have always lived in Toorak. It is desperately important to live there. South Yarra, too, is equally as good. That's just an extension of Toorak, really. You can get away with Armadale. Friends like to see you there.

Brighton is fine. Beaumaris is the suburb for the newly rich, the young rich who like to sail boats. Balwyn is for the comfortable middle class, and so too is Camberwell. That's a fact that the people of Balwyn find hard to take. Right down the other side at Dandenong Road, which is Glen Iris/East Malvern, then cuts its way through Hawthorn, Camberwell, Balwyn, across to Ivanhoe. A professor at Monash University once described it as the longest stretch of well-stuffed suburbia in the world.

I bet it is, too. Right through there your standards of nicely behaved, lawn-clipped, solid brick, brick-veneer, Peace rose, white azalea suburbia never fades or sinks. What I am getting at is this. The dividing line between the rich and the other people is the Yarra. All the public schools, all the best recreation areas, all the best swimming beaches, all the best golf clubs are on one side — on the other you have worthy areas — Essendon, Footscray, Brunswick, Preston, Collingwood, Keilor. Decent enough perhaps, but people with white collars and their eyes on glory and a CBE don't live there. There are a few trifling exceptions. Heidelberg is over the Yarra, just over. That's an OK suburb. Sunbury, too, is good because that is close to Romsey and you are getting close to the Rupert Clarkes and...

Am I telling you all this? Because the Melbourne results of the Federal election were so fascinating. We are now split entirely down the middle. The eight seats entirely on the right are entirely on the right. Diamond Valley, Casey, Henty, Latrobe, Isaacs and Holt all went to the Liberals. Diamond Valley and Casey, for example, took in the more trendy mud brick belt like Eltham, Kangaroo Ground, Warrandyte, Croydon, Lilydale and Points East. The hip pocket nerve swung them securely back to the right again, as it did Isaacs and Holt.

On the other side of the Yarra — Scullin, Batman, Melbourne, Melbourne Ports, Gellibrand, Maribyrnong, over in the industrial west and north of the city, have remained unflinchingly Labor.

The map now has this line down the Yarra. Some people are calling it the Fraser Line. It runs down Plenty Road, follows Salt Creek past the Rosanna Golf Club, then follows the Yarra all the way to the Botanic Gardens, where it swings down Punt Road, back along Toorak Road, does a zig-zag around Elsternwick golf course and goes straight into the bay at Mordialloc.

The maps published after the elections were shaded dark grey for areas still held by Labor and virgin white for areas taken by the Liberals. They disturbed me a great deal for they looked just like battle maps we used to publish when the Germans were overrunning Europe.

I mean, there is quite a salient: there where Labor holds Richmond. This reminds one of the Battle of the Bulge, where Field-Marshal Cairns no doubt can make an assault into aromatic tree-lined areas of Higgins.

Oh, I see great battle areas forming. I would suspect that the finest tank battles would be waged on the open territory around Yarra Bend national park hoping, of course, that hospitals in that area would be declared neutral ground.

Labor has Williamstown Dockyard, the main wharves, St Kilda Marina, Luna Park, General Motors, Tullamarine and Essendon Airports.

The Liberal Party, on the other hand, has a small amount of naval strength, Royal Brighton and Sandringham. They also have that famous flagship of the Royal Victorian Colonial Navy — HMVS Cerberus, rusting on a sandbar at Half Moon Bay, Black Rock, but not to be forgotten as it was at one time the one-time flagship of the Royal Victorian Colonial Navy.

There is also Flinders Naval Depot, the Officer Training Camp at Portsea — purely Liberal. Liberals have Moorabbin Airport and they could attack with more than 200 Cessnas.

The actual city down town area at this stage is in the hands of Labor, which means that all the best clubs, the Athenaeum, the Australian and the Melbourne clubs are in ALP hands. This makes life most uncomfortable for the top Liberal forces — led by Generals Fraser, Peacock and Lynch — but never fear, they are grouping their tanks.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Melbourne cut by the Fraser line, The Bulletin, 17 January 1976. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
