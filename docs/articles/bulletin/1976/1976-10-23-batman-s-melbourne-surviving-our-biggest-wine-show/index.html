Surviving our biggest wine show

4 min read.

First published 23 Oct 1976

Expovin 76, Australia's biggest international wine exhibition, draws 70,000 visitors to Melbourne's Exhibition Building, where judges from four countries award Wolf Blass a third consecutive Jimmy Watson Trophy and the city narrowly avoids watching its prized fountain flow with 400 gallons of indifferent red wine.

WE HAVE JUST survived the biggest international wine exhibition seen in this country. It had a nice trendy name, Expovin 76, and it took place in the grand old Exhibition Building. In those areas where we normally have Kingswood Holdens, caravans, twin Merc speedboats and such, there were exhibits trying desperately to look like German wine cellars, French chateaux or 17th century Spanish missions.

It required a special act of parliament to turn the great hall into a vast licensed area and at first there were terrible fears it could become like the Rutherglen wine festival, a sort of Super Bowl of Rutherglen. But they charged a $2-admission fee and for this you received 10 tasting tickets and a plastic wine glass. So you became very selective, deciding whether you would have Wolf Blass on the way to visit Petaluma or if Petaluma was better on the way back. Indeed, often it was a challenge to get through your 10 tickets in the one lunch hour.

Wine promotion in Australia has always been anything but spectacular. Expovin was the idea of a young and small Melbourne company, Selected Estate Wines. When they launched it back in 1975 they got a chilling response. Not one of the big companies came in. However most of the smaller companies and vineyards exhibited.

The problem was to get prestige and recognition. This year they did this by gathering big-name international wine judges. Expovin director Robin Bardley went touring and persuaded these people to come — Dr Maynard Amerine from the U.S., Countess Ines de Lupe from France, Dr Richard Muller from Germany, Mr Harry Waugh from England. The chairman of the panel was Dr Max Lake from Australia.

The idea was that these judges would give papers and they would have awards for wines entered from an entire international field. Over several days they judged 300 wines and the winners were announced at a great banquet with Premier Hamer at the Southern Cross hotel. They had 10 awards of 'outstanding merit' and two for the best wine of the show — one for the best potential and one for drinking right now.

The one with the best potential was a Chateau Coutet (Barsac) 1971. The other was a Wolf Blass Langhorne Creek cabernet-shiraz 1971.

Max Lake handed over the prizes and it was a lot of fun. To be frank, we've had a good deal of Wolf Blass lately. He has won the coveted Jimmy Watson award three years in a row. When he came up to receive the big Expovin award he had all the submissiveness of a football coach who had just won the grand final.

He told a marvellous story of how tough things used to be. How, needing some quick cash pretty badly, he went to one Melbourne dealer and offered him a large batch of riesling at under a dollar a bottle. He was told it was acidic and not worth drinking.

'That same dealer,' shouted Wolf Blass, 'paid $12 a bottle for the same wine at an Adelaide auction. He could have saved himself the trouble and bought it for less than a dollar.'

At the Exhibition Building there is a voluptuous old fountain. The idea was that for several days they would have it flowing with red wine. They even purchased 400 gallons of, shall we say, somewhat indifferent red to make this possible.

David Castle, one of the directors, said the previous Lord Mayor, Cr Ron Walker, was enthusiastic over the idea. Right at the last moment the council said 'No.'

I think they visualised booze-crazed Melburnian winos swimming against the tide right under those busty fountain dryads.

We still haven't been advised what Expovin has done with its 400 gallons of ghastly red. However, Jim Duggan, chairman of Expovin, did announce: 'There is no truth in the rumour that we are bottling it for special use by city councillors.'

They had to get 50,000 people through the exhibition to break even. The final figure was over 70,000 and they had to extend the show for another day. The response from the public was beyond belief.

Already the building is booked for another Expovin in 1978. I do hope they can get the fountain going then. Perhaps the council would agree if they used a nice fizzy Barossa Pearl.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Surviving our biggest wine show, The Bulletin, 23 October 1976. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
