The dog show

2 min read.

First published 27 Sep 1975

At the Melbourne Royal Dog Show, Afghan hounds dominate the entries with 300 dogs competing, Irish setters are staging a resurgence over German shepherds, and judge Bill Crowley presides over what Keith Dunstan describes as the most socially revealing event in Melbourne's dog calendar.

THE Melbourne Royal Dog Show. This is one of the great occasions. It is held at the Royal Melbourne Showgrounds. It draws dogs from all over Australia and some from overseas. It is a world in itself.

Afghan hounds are the great story of this year's show. There are 300 Afghan entries. This is extraordinary. The Afghan hound boom is one of the more surprising social phenomena of the 1970s. Five years ago the Afghan was relatively rare in Australia. Now it is everywhere.

The Afghan hound is a magnificent animal — tall, graceful, with a long silky coat and a noble, slightly distant expression. It is not an easy dog to keep. The coat requires constant attention. The dog requires considerable exercise. It has a mind of its own. These qualities have not deterred the enthusiasts.

The Irish setter is staging a comeback. The German shepherd has been dominant in Australia for years — it is the country's most popular breed, used by police and defence forces as well as kept as a family dog. But the Irish setter, with its flowing red coat and enormous good nature, is gaining ground.

Bill Crowley was judging. He is one of the most respected dog judges in Australia. He moved through the rings with the quiet authority of long experience. He knew exactly what he was looking for and he found it.

The dog show is a revealing social event. The owners are as interesting as the dogs. They groom, they present, they compete with an intensity that suggests the dog show is not merely a hobby but an identity.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: The dog show, The Bulletin, 27 September 1975. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
