The opal town

2 min read.

First published 03 May 1975

Keith Dunstan visits Andamooka, the South Australian opal mining town where Rudi Duke has built a stubby hotel into an unlikely desert institution, prospector Frank Douglas hunts opals in the barren landscape, and promoters dream of holding a car race across the dry bed of Lake Torrens.

ANDAMOOKA is the most unlikely place in Australia to find a hotel. It is a small opal mining town in the South Australian desert, north of Port Augusta, in country that is as remote and barren as anywhere in the continent.

But Rudi Duke has a hotel there. It is called the Andamooka Hotel and it is a remarkably cheerful place. Duke is a large, ebullient man who seems entirely at home in the desert. His hotel is built, in the fashion of Andamooka, into the hillside. The interior is cool even in the great heat. His clientele are miners, travellers, tourists who have driven enormous distances to see the opal fields.

The hotel serves mainly stubbies — the small bottles of beer that are the drink of the outback. Duke stocks an enormous supply. In the heat of an Andamooka summer, men can drink a great many stubbies.

Frank Douglas is a prospector. He has been at Andamooka for years. He knows every part of the field. He can read the country, understand what the rocks are telling him. He finds opals. Not always. Some days nothing. But sometimes magnificent stones, worth thousands of dollars.

The opals of Andamooka include some of the finest in the world. The colours — the blues and greens and reds and oranges — are extraordinary. Queen Elizabeth II has a magnificent Andamooka opal, presented to her during the Royal Tour.

There is a plan, apparently serious, to hold a motor race across the dry bed of Lake Torrens. Lake Torrens is one of the great salt lakes of South Australia, normally dry and hard-surfaced. The organisers believe it could provide a track suitable for speed attempts or even a racing event. The logistics are formidable but the plan is not entirely absurd.
