The crisis in literature

2 min read.

First published 15 Nov 1975

The 1975 constitutional crisis inspires a survey of the literary allusions being deployed by both Whitlam and Fraser supporters, with an Ovid quotation surfacing alongside comparisons to Caligula, a Richard III Society mobilising, and Percy Shelley being misquoted in a political advertisement.

THE constitutional crisis of 1975 has produced remarkable literary activity. Everyone is reaching for quotations.

Ovid has been quoted. Ovid, the Roman poet of the first century, is not normally a resource in Australian political journalism. But someone found something relevant and there it was in the newspapers.

Caligula has been mentioned. Caligula, the Roman emperor, is always available as a comparison when political behaviour seems extreme. The comparison is not entirely fair to whoever is being compared — Caligula was notably worse than any Australian politician — but the name has a satisfying ring.

The Richard III Society has been active. There is an Australian branch of the Richard III Society. They believe that Richard III has been unjustly maligned by history and by Shakespeare. The crisis has given them an opportunity to point out that deposing a legitimately constituted authority is not unprecedented in history.

Percy Shelley has been misquoted in a political advertisement. The misquotation was not entirely harmless. The original Shelley line said something rather different from what was attributed to him. This sort of thing happens in the heat of political crisis.

Gough Whitlam has been very good on literary references. He speaks with authority and passion and he deploys quotations effectively. Malcolm Fraser is less literary but more effective in other ways.

The crisis will pass. The literary references will remain. Australia's constitutional crisis of 1975 will be remembered and written about and argued over for generations.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: The crisis in literature, The Bulletin, 15 November 1975. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
