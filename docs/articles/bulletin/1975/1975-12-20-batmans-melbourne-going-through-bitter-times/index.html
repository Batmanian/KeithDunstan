Going through bitter times

3 min read.

First published 20 Dec 1975

A beer strike has left 75 percent of Victoria's hotels dry, with 120,000 casual workers stood down and extraordinary scenes of drinkers chasing Courage Brewery trucks through the suburbs, as Keith Dunstan surveys the drinking statistics showing beer accounts for 17.5 percent of all liquid consumed and the state endures a drought worse than Christmas 1962.

THESE ARE hard, parlous times. I have tried experimenting with riesling, soda and ice. I have been drinking vermouth on the rocks. I have tried mead and even awful, sickly cider. Newspapers here are offering all sorts of substitutes, but it is not the same.

As I write, 75 percent of the State's 1440 hotels have gone dry and 120,000 casual workers in hotels are being stood down. No trade, so no barmen. There are pathetic tales about boys who sell Heralds around hotel bars. They are not earning their Christmas money.

There have been some stirring tales. The Bayswater Football Club was having its annual Christmas party. Their beer had been correctly ordered for months. The time came to collect it and there was none to be had. They tried at hotels all over Melbourne — no luck.

Well, they heard that a pub on King Island down in Bass Strait still had some beer. So the club president went to Moorabbin, chartered a twin-engined Piper Aztec, flew there and acquired three kegs of Courage plus a pile of crayfish. Football clubs are used to this dramatic sort of stuff.

Courage, the once unloved brewery, the one that has only a minuscule share of the market, is now dearly loved. The Courage workers are not on strike, hence Courage is bottling day and night. Even so, the big game is to discover somewhere a well of Courage.

There have been extraordinary scenes outside the Courage brewery. The other morning a fleet of 28 trucks carrying 224 tons of beer left Courage and went off on their rounds. There was a whole fleet of thirsty drinkers waiting, their engines revving. They followed these trucks wherever they went on their rounds. As soon as a truck stopped at a pub anywhere the drinkers sprinted inside to be first in the queue for the bottles.

Meanwhile Market Services in Melbourne has made a 700-page survey of our drinking habits, and beer is 17.5 percent of all we drink. Coffee is on top with 31, tea 27; milk 12; soft drink 5.2; fruit juice 2.3; cocoa 1.2; wine 1.2; and spirits 0.3.

We drink 1943 million litres of beer a year. Only a third of us actually drink beer so the truly devoted drinker gets through 391 litres a year — better than a litre a day and that costs him around $688 a year.

Think of us kindly here in Melbourne. Think of us dry, parched, miserably trying to console ourselves with bitter lemon and an Alka Seltzer on the rocks. Should not bottles-for-Melbourne be the new cry?

Source: Batman's Melbourne: Going through bitter times, The Bulletin, 20 December 1975. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
