The secret of long life

2 min read.

First published 22 Feb 1975

Keith Dunstan debunks health food myths with help from Percy Cerutty and Murray Rose, while celebrating the longevity of old sailor Charlie Rouse, who survived to 94 on the ship Polly Woodside, and concludes that moderate alcohol consumption may be the true key to a long life.

THE great Australian health food movement is upon us. There are restaurants that serve only natural foods. There are shops that sell only health foods. The whole business is based on the premise that if you ate the right things you would live longer, feel better and be generally superior to other people.

Percy Cerutty, the great athletics trainer of Herb Elliott and other champions, was always a health food man. He was the man who put athletes on raw oatmeal and made them run up sandhills. He believed that a natural diet was the secret of athletic performance. He was wrong in some particulars but his methods produced champions.

Murray Rose, the great swimmer, was a vegetarian. When Rose was a world-class swimmer and world record holder this was thought to be extraordinary. How could a man be a champion athlete without meat? Rose answered the question by being a champion.

But there are doubts about the whole health food proposition. I have been talking to Charlie Rouse. Charlie Rouse is a retired sailor who lives on the ship Polly Woodside at the docks. He is 94 years of age and his health is excellent. His diet has never been what health food enthusiasts would approve. He has eaten what was available, mostly the kind of rough, plain food that sailors ate. He has drunk his share of liquor.

The Polly Woodside is a beautiful old iron sailing ship, built in Belfast in 1885, that served the seas for years before she retired to Melbourne. She is now a museum ship. Charlie Rouse lives aboard and minds her.

What is Charlie's secret? He says it is simple. He never worried. He worked hard but he never worried. He drank in moderation, ate in moderation, slept well and kept occupied. This seems far more convincing than the health food argument. The secret of long life may be the absence of anxiety rather than the presence of wheatgerm.

Source: Batman's Melbourne: The secret of long life, The Bulletin, 22 February 1975. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
