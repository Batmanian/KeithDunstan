What's Been Missin' from Kissin'

4 min read.

First published 17 Oct 1964

A report on the Cutex flavoured-lipstick launch party in Melbourne, where Cherry Kiss, Peppermint Kiss and Caramel Kiss were unveiled to an audience including Footscray captain-coach Ted Whitten, with the assembled press left to judge whether taste and fragrance really did add a new dimension to cosmetics.

YOU may as well know the truth: your correspondent always has had a weakness for lush parties turned on in the name of Public Relations. PR-wise, it had been a most unusual week. First came the invitation to the Cutex flavoured-lipstick party at the oh-so-smart hotel.

At the party there was a strong gathering. As the representative of Australian Rules football there was Ted Whitten — 'Mr Football' — the captain and coach of Footscray. For the Sydney show there was Mike Lingham, handsome Parker representative. Then Wendy Lingham from the Pty Ltd side of things.

For many years now the name of Cutex has been synonymous with fine nail care. We had to peruse the press kit...

'For many years now the name of Cutex has been synonymous with fine nail care. We are proud to announce a product that will start an exciting new trend: gay, new lipstick as fresh as tomorrow and as pretty as the blossom of spring. A Cutex flavoured lipstick — it's what's been missin' from kissin'.'

As advertising copy this was exciting reading; one had to continue.

'For the first time in the cosmetic industry, Cutex has turned lipstick-wearing into an exciting adventure by making taste and fragrance an integral part of its total appeal... Cherry Kiss — a coral-pink soufflé with a ripe cherry flavour. Peppermint Kiss — a soft, summer pink, delectably cooled with peppermint. Caramel Kiss — toasted mocha, deliciously flavoured with caramel.'

We read on: 'Never before have candy jar flavours been distilled with all their original freshness into lipstick taste-scents so intriguingly, instantly definite. This makes "forbidden fruits" the season's most subtle weapons... lovely to look at... fun to wear... and destined to give the kiss a new dimension. Look for excitement at lip-level.'

Mr Vernon Casey told the gathering how the flavoured lipsticks had swept the United States and Great Britain, how they were particularly relished by the teen market (from 14 years to 25). Indeed the sales were going so well in the US they were even introducing four new flavours: grapefruit, cola and strawberry.

'It's been suggested that we try some real Australian flavours,' he said, 'but flavours like that are hard to do.'

Mr Casey then introduced the ladies. Wendy Lingham, the hostess, was in peppermint and she had with her a team of 'the season's most subtle weapons.' One was in cherry, one in peppermint, one in caramel. If you are interested in the trend of modern lipsticks, the orange was pale orange, the peppermint pale pink, the cherry was red and the caramel was the colour of mud.

At the appropriate moment the record went on. It built up to a max, it goes like this:

GIRL: Johnny?

BOY: Yes.

GIRL: Come closer...

(Drums getting louder and more intense.)

GIRL: I've got a secret... a brand new lipstick secret. I'll put some on and you can know the news.

BOY: Mmmmmm-a-n! It's a new kind of kiss! It's fantastic!

As you can imagine, after such a build up the audience was in a high state of excitement, urgently waiting to find out what had been missin' from kissin'. Alas, the girls were unresponsive. They gave no practical demonstrations. However, one pretty flavour did remark: 'Will I use it? What do you think? Look, brother, the day I need flavour to get favours from the boys then I'll give the game away.'

Source: Around Melbourne WHAT'S BEEN MISSIN' FROM KISSIN', The Bulletin, 17 October 1964. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
