David Penman's Sunday

3 min read.

First published 26 Jul 1986

A Good Weekend "My Sunday" profile of David Penman, Anglican Archbishop of Melbourne, tracing his weekend from midnight NBC news to parish rounds, Wimbledon tennis, and his growing collection of more than 40 wombat figurines.

THERE WAS a time when the day off for the clergy was inevitably Monday. Out there at the golf club, you had to be very careful with your language on Mondays. You knew there would be a clergyman on almost every tee.

Times have changed. David Penman, Anglican Archbishop of Melbourne, says that he took Fridays off when he was a parish priest. There would be weddings on Saturdays and almost always funerals on Mondays. Yes, people tended to die during weekends. And, of course, there was always that extra day. Now he works the clock round but he tries to preserve Saturday, 9 to 5, as a family day. Then he's quite fanatical about sport, with a deep devotion for the Carlton football team.

Both Melbourne's archbishops have this failing. The Catholic Archbishop, Frank Little, goes for Essendon. David Penman says he's sorry for Frank Little. The defending premiers are not having a very good season.

David Penman's home at Bishopscourt is beautifully situated in East Melbourne, Fitzroy Gardens opposite and the Cricket Ground just down the road. 'I like to go down there in civvies — broad hat, dark glasses.'

The weekend starts with the NBC Today Show at midnight. 'I get it on Channel 7 every night and I watch it from 12 to 12.30. They give a terrific world cover of what's going on. And they have the best weather person I have ever seen. He's male, fat and bald. I have never been to the United States but he helps give me an understanding of that country.'

But TV sport has kept him awake just lately, Wimbledon tennis and the final of the World Cup from Mexico. 'Soccer is such a fascinating artistic game.'

He has always had this love for sport. 'I used to be a Phys Ed teacher before I was ordained,' he said.

Sunday, almost inevitably, he is invited to some parish in the archdiocese. 'Jean (his wife) always comes with me.' He meets as many people as possible and usually stays for lunch with the vicar.

There could be another service in the afternoon, a confirmation, consecration or grand anniversary. If that's not on, maybe he can wind down with a glimpse of the Sydney Swans on television. Then comes Evensong.

David Penman is poised on his 50th birthday. Staff members describe him as a human dynamo and not a bit frightened of big issues such as uranium mining and capital punishment and he has been out front in the drive for the ordination of women priests.

He became Archbishop in 1984 after a full year's deliberation by the selection committee. One writer described him as 'the last wombat in the paddock.'

Immediately after the announcement, Channel 10 sent him a toy wombat with the message 'Welcome to Melbourne.' Other wombats began to flow in and he has become a collector with more than 40. He has ceramic wombats, a wombat in a jigsaw, wombat tiepins, an Australian flag in the shape of a wombat. He has been made a member of the Wombat Underground Movement.

The activities of WUM are not entirely clear but the Archbishop is proud to be a member. Best of all, he has a road sign: BEWARE OF WOMBATS FOR NEXT 5 KM.

This Archbishop is a strong, resourceful man. It would be wise to be beware of him over a longer distance than that.
