Peter Russell-Clarke's Sunday

4 min read.

First published 30 May 1986

A Good Weekend "My Sunday" profile of Peter Russell-Clarke at Soho, his century-old farm on Victoria's Bellarine Peninsula, where cooking experiments, Queenscliff fish, long lunches and a mob of more than fifty peacocks define his country weekends.

RUSSELL-CLARKE is cook, cartoonist, TV showman, advertising man and business entrepreneur but deep down he's mostly a cook.

He says, if you are really going to be a successful cook, you must adore everything about it. You must love the look of fresh food, the shopping. You must love all the preparation, the chopping, the slicing, the rolling, the kneading, the mixing.

'It's just like sex,' he says. 'The orgasm isn't everything. Most of the fun is in getting there, all the wooing that goes beforehand.'

Peter and Jan Russell-Clarke have this place called Soho on Victoria's Bellarine Peninsula. Soho is their love and dream and they use it a little like the old county squires of England. A farm, says Peter, is the perfect place for entertaining. For one thing, it doesn't matter if the house is gloriously untidy. And another thing: after eating, you can get up and go for a walk and look at other creatures feeding.

Soho was the original grand mansion of the peninsula. It is more than 100 years old. It has lofty ceilings, big verandas all around. Nothing is quite level and nothing is quite perpendicular. Peter is a great collector of cooking gadgets. He has, too, a huge old radio — a console, I think, was the correct term — operated by valves. It handles Bing Crosby and the Andrew Sisters very well but you suspect it might have a heart attack when expected to produce rock.

Peter and Jan have always been jogging nuts. They have jogged 10 kilometres a day as a sacred ritual. Peter's back has started to disintegrate from all that pavement pounding. So now he walks for an hour every day and covers six kilometres. They particularly like walking along the beach at Point Nepean, which can be wonderful and lonely.

The Russell-Clarke menage is devoted to eating and just thinking about eating. They don't get up early; rising time will move past 9 on to 9.30 or later. Breakfast is toast, butter and honey. 'We eat a lot of wholewheat bread. In the old days, they used to say bread made you fat. Now you eat it because it's good for your bum. We like honey because it's a natural food and has vitamin B and gives us protein.'

Then, during the morning, he likes to experiment in the kitchen. 'You might be making a pie and then think, "Hey, what would that be like in a filo pastry? How would it be with cheese drops on top?"'

'We go to the wharf at Queenscliff to get our fish. There's a little boat there called the Compass Rose. Fantastic for mussels, scallops and I reckon our whiting is just as good as the South Australian grass whiting.

'There's tennis before lunch and lunch sort of continues. You know, a fair bit of talking and we don't leave the table until about 4. That's what's good about the country. Nothing is so urgent, you're not rushing anywhere and there's time to talk to people.'

We forgot to tell you about the peacocks. You have never seen so many peacocks. He started with a nice breeding pair and they have grown like the population of China. He has lost count but he knows there are more than 50 and he hasn't the heart to get rid of one of them. They perch on the cars and they get up in the trees and they sit on the roof like a crowd of barrackers in a football grandstand. 'They are very intelligent,' said Peter. 'They know exactly when you are trying to sleep. They walk up and down on the galvanised iron.'

No intruder could get into Soho unobserved. The peacocks turn on their Hallelujah Chorus as soon as a foot approaches the property. But, apart from the peacocks, there are geese. Julius Caesar used geese as 'watchdogs.' The geese shriek almost as loudly as the peacocks. Peter says he loves Soho, he goes there because it's so quiet and peaceful after the city.
