A new clutch of ugly ducklings blots the cityscape

4 min read.

First published 08 Aug 1998

A nomination round for Melbourne's ugliest sights, from the Foxtel and Optus advertising signs looming over the historic Young and Jackson's Hotel to the kitsch interior of the new Imax Theatre and the warring columns of 101 Collins Street.

Flinders Street looks splendid without the ugly twins, the Gas and Fuel buildings.

Surely that is just the start. What else could we remove? Melbourne has ugliness all over.

Back in the 1980s we gave annual awards for the ugliest things in town. We called them UGHs, for Un-Godly Horror.

Now where would we start? Perhaps our top UGH, our Golden Ugh, should go to the Foxtel sign. It would have to be the biggest advertising hoarding. It is there beside Punt Road bridge, and it spreads itself right across six grain solos. It is fanned by 20th Century Fox searchlights and glares across the river to South Yarra. The Yarra is supposed to be one of the glories of Melbourne and we are confronted by this thing.

We should have another UGH for the awful Optus sign on top of Young & Jackson's Hotel. There is a blue globe and the obscure message "Yes Today - Yes Tomorrow". It is criminal that the succession of affluent owners and lessees have not looked after this old pub. They have used it mainly as a grandstand for frightful advertising signs.

It is one of the last of our very old pubs. It began as warehouses in 1851-52, then opened in 1861 as the Princes Bridge Hotel, which is its real name. It was there before the present Princes Bridge, Flinders Street Station and St Paul's Cathedral. It is an old treasure. It should be authentically restored and given some dignity.

Have you noticed what the Public Transport Corporation has been doing to our trams, apart from getting rid of the conductors? Our trams are supposed to be green. They are supposed to melt into the landscape. The artists' trams were perhaps a bit of fun, but we got rid of all those. Now we have trams that are just in it for the money, perambulating billboards and a shock on the landscape.

Could one commend to your suffering eyes the Toyota Camry Tram? When we are persuading our citizens to forget their cars and use public transport, we have a blue tram with two great Toyotas on either side.

There is another tram that has been on the Toorak line. It is advertising mobile telephones. It is brilliant yellow with red spots, plus conversation balloons down either side. Or how about the Cadbury tram, a big chocolate, advertising the latest confection, called a Fuse. You think the tram is about to explode.

The new Imax Theatre in Rathdowne Street, Carlton, deserves an UGH. Imax is brilliant technology and is worth seeing. It deserves better design. The interior of the building is the most awful piece of kitsch since the Jam Factory. There are squares, stars and diamonds all competing with each other. There are dinosaur-sized icecreams, lollipops and buckets of popcorn. There is a whole area called Lollywood, where you buy your lollies. There are TV screens in the shape of eyes pumping out images and nerve-bruising noise. Then, right across the floor travelling from wall to wall, there is the message, THE WORLD'S BIGGEST SCREEN.

The casino deserves an UGH. What Crown has done along the Yarra is working very nicely. The line of restaurants and the broad bluestone space between the buildings and the Yarra give one a feeling of peace, provided those towers don't start belching fire. But the foyer is a worry. Perhaps they should pull it out and start again.

There are those sinister waterfalls, there is all that black marble, and there is the ceiling way above with its changing lights. From hidden recesses way aloft comes strange music. This gives one a sense of doom.

One gets a similar sense of worry at 101 Collins Street. Those topless columns, which don't support anything, have a Salvador Dali surrealist feel about them. It is accentuated when you go inside, where there are another 24 columns beside pools of water, lonely, supporting nothing. There are some haunting, emaciated sculptures plus a board giving the latest stock prices to add to your unease.

Of course, columns are all the go at the moment, particularly in Toorak. Architecture has a desperate design to go backwards and return to a previous century.

If you want to observe a perfect example of what could be called Italianate or Museum architecture, go to Hopetoun Road, Toorak. There are three buildings there, Doric, Ionic and Corinthian columns, splendid porticos. A couple of the buildings could even have been built by Mussolini.

Another fine suburban UGH could be the Moorabbin Town Hall. They started off thinking they might be a bit of Florence with a campanile, except it was made of concrete instead of fine old stone. Then they put in a 1990s addition in colored glass, so that all the pieces of architecture are nicely at war with each other.

Very likely you have your own UGHs. You should tell us about it so that we have a better city.
