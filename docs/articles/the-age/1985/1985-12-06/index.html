The Gallery of Sport Waits for Norm

3 min read.

First published 06 Dec 1985

A wry lament over the Victorian Gallery of Sport at the MCG, still unfinished after months of BLF stoppages, demarcation disputes, and a walkout triggered by Premier John Cain's site visit, with completion now pushed to April 1986 at a cost overrun of $1.5 million.

AH YES, of all the 5,000 projects in Victoria's 150th year unquestionably the strawberry on the cream was to be the Gallery of Sport.

We feel more deeply, more passionately about sport, than even money. Well, almost. So we thought what a glorious tourist attraction for Melbourne — Gallery to the Arts in St Kilda Road, Gallery to Sport in Richmond Park.

But, alas, it has had to fight battles against the National Trust, against the city council and above all, against Norm.

The trust felt it destroyed the character of the beloved old MCC area, thought it was too close to the main building and Norm Gallagher didn't like John Cain.

The Gallery of Sport is being built for nigh on $3 million just outside the members' entrance at the Melbourne Cricket Ground. It will be a museum and a gallery, covering 19 sports, everything from cricket and football through to squash and lawn bowls.

I believe John Landy, for example, has donated the spikes he used to run his first four-minute mile. There's a hall of fame which will kick off with 120 illustrious greats — Don Bradman, Dawn Fraser, Robert de Castella, Hubert Opperman, Jimmy Carruthers, Frank Sedgman, that sort of thing.

The theory was that it would be opened during the 150th year, before the end of last month. There were months approving the niceties of the design and whether it would fit into the park and whether it would be in harmony with the park-grabbing old members' stand. Then came the building of the lights at the MCG.

There was the demarcation dispute with the ironworkers and the AWU, the BLF and Norm Gallagher; $15 million worth of projects at the MCG were declared black.

In January–February there were delays to MCG projects because of over-award payments. Eventually workers received a promise of over-award payments of $84 a week, two free tickets to VFL finals, safety boots and overalls. That was settled in April. Then, in July, there were further stoppages over the jailing of Norm Gallagher.

In August, September–October there was splendid progress, lots of concrete poured, so much that on October 23 Premier John Cain was able to lay a foundation stone. He announced that the gallery would be a lasting memento to Victoria's 150th and enhance the historic significance and great traditions associated with the MCG.

John Cain, 'the enemy,' had invaded the site. By walking on to the site Mr Cain had made it 'unsafe.' Bryce Thomas, assistant secretary of the Melbourne Cricket Club, told us that if a site is unsafe under the award, the workers must still be paid. The BLF members were not paid, so a strike was on again. They did not return until Thursday, November 14.

They worked two days but not on the following Monday. Now they were not working Mondays, only Tuesday to Friday. This was to point up Norm's battle then for the 35-hour week.

We consulted a despairing Stan Evans. When will we see the completed Gallery of Sport?

No hope before April 1986, said Stan. An equally sad Bryce Thomas said delays on MCG projects had cost the club an extra $1.5 million.
