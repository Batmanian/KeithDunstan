Good Lord! — or, an appeal to Mr Packer on a silly cut-off

5 min read.

First published 28 Jul 1985

An open letter to Kerry Packer protesting Channel Nine's threatened withdrawal of cricket television coverage, arguing Australians are so devoted to the sport that any government permitting its removal from free-to-air TV would face electoral consequences.

DEAR Mr Packer, Please don't take your bat and ball and go off home. That's not your style. You have had a few Test matches of your own this year and you haven't lost any of them yet.

We do love you for putting both the cricket and the tennis on the screen.

As a cricket fan I am shocked at my fellow man, but I guess you get better ratings out of shows like The Love Boat and The Mike Walsh Show. There are those who have to get their dreadful glob of Love Boat romance before they can absorb the true serious action of cricket.

You claim that your cover of sport is the best in the world. It probably is, but then you know and I know, that Australians, man for man, are nuttier about sport than any other creatures on earth. Sport to us is the way we establish our national identity. When we pick up victories on sporting fields it helps us conquer our curious national inferiority complex.

I think you discovered very early it was good business to sponsor cricket, tennis and golf, especially cricket. It's what you have done with cricket that has made you the best-known Australian along with Rupert Murdoch, Nellie Melba and Ned Kelly. Is there anyone in Britain who hasn't heard of Kerry Packer?

I can quote ladies I know who are bordering on 90 and they are still there watching it at 2 am. You say the BBC, with its two channels, still doesn't cover it. But the English are different from us, aren't they? Even at Lord's you are lucky to get a crowd of 30,000. They seem to prefer the madness of soccer.

They haven't the delicious hang-ups that we have. Many of us believe the greatest bliss in life is to witness the thrashing of the Poms. Maybe that's what keeps me up until 3 am. I must confess my eyes begin to glaze over when Messrs Gooch and Gower keep hitting our boys all over the ground.

We used to get the cricket on television given to us by the ABC. I am sure the ABC would be covering it even now except that you outbid them for the rights back in 1979.

Lovely, it was, no advertisements. We didn't have every over interrupted by that dreadful fellow who is always in a state of ecstasy because he has made an STD call. We didn't get that mum who kept telling her daughter that her hands would no longer look like rats' feet if only she used hand lotion. We didn't have Bill Peach raving on, strangely comparing the wonders of outback Australia to the goodness of Cadbury's Dairy Milk Chocolate. We didn't have all those near-naked girls and boys in a dangerous state of euphoria jumping over a giant soft drink can. Nor did we get all those replays and those terrible flashbacks. I get confused.

Half the time with your cover I don't know whether they are covering cricket right now, yesterday or tomorrow.

There was that occasion during the second innings. Suddenly Ian Chappell screamed: 'He's gone.' Gooch was out. 'He's got him, he's got him.' Someone else went and Chappell shrieked: 'It's Lawson, he's struck again.'

My God, I thought, this is marvellous. We're going to win this Test. Then the full horror of it hit me. They were just playing these damn flashbacks. Gooch, Robinson and company were still there, as safe as the House of Lords.

I hate to have to tell you this, because now you will really cut us off for ever. But at our place we watch the Nine cricket with the sound off. We have our radio on, tuned in to dear old Alan McGilvray giving us the ABC cover.

You can't put your radio too close to the television set because it picks up interference from the cathode tube. We have the radio on the other side of the room by the fireplace and it's just fine.

This way we get perfect continuity without the worry of commercials and all the flashbacks. You would be surprised at the number of people who do the same thing.

You and Mike Willesee rather ganged up there — you murdered poor Mr Duffy on the Willesee show. The trouble is, I don't think Mr Duffy knew much about it. I bet he hadn't shown the true marathon courage of sitting up for both the cricket and the tennis all through that brutal nocturnal fortnight.

Only those of us with fearless 'desire for the ball' had the strength to do it.

I made it myself only by going to bed at 6 pm, then setting the alarm to get up, refreshed for the bounce, at 1 am.

I remember only too well the clash with Wimbledon and the cricket. But look, we could have had both with reasonable comfort. Wimbledon still operates in the Victorian era. They don't start tennis until 2 pm, 1 pm our time. So you could have given us the cricket until lunch. Anyway, at Wimbledon it was nearly always raining.

The real reason for not giving us cricket before lunch, I suspect, was ratings and advertising contracts.

I am grateful to Mr Duffy. He was doing his duty as Minister for Communications. You have put the ball fairly on his middle stump. If you go ahead with the threat not to give us the GTV9 cricket, Mr Duffy will have to communicate mighty fast and find somebody else to do it.

I have the feeling the ABC can hardly wait to get back into the game. Perhaps we could pay for it with a 10 cent increase in admission charges to the cricket during the summer, or how about 10 cents extra a can? It would be worth it.

I mean, it's all very well to have squalling summit meetings on tax and have brawls with the BLF. We can even overlook the decline in the Australian dollar and cope with the collapse in the ANZUS treaty, but any government which fails to give us television coverage of the cricket is in deadly peril. I wouldn't vote for it in a fit.

Yours sincerely and deeply, KEITH DUNSTAN
