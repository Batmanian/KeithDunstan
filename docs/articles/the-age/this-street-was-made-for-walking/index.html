This street was made for walking

4 min read.

First published 13 Oct 1997

Here we go again. Nigh on half the city councillors and perhaps (shudder) a majority of the council want to put traffic back into Swanston Walk.

Aren't we putting in a City Link? Aren't we building a ring road for the very reason we want the city to breathe?

Have they forgotten the frightful Swanston Street we knew in 1992? Every creature on wheels was trying to get from one side of Melbourne to the other and they all went down Swanston Street.

The footpaths were so narrow you needed Plugger-like shoulders to survive. Remember the pedestrian islands for the trams? At 5pm, you deserved a VC just for getting there, and once you arrived, humanity was so thick you couldn't squeeze through to the tram.

On the hideous rating, Swanston Street was a 10. Maybe it is still hideous but the rating has gone from 10 to two. Councillor David Bardas is shocked because it is called Swanston Walk. What does he want, Swanston Street Struggle? Swanston Street Carbon Monoxide?

It is a sweet piece of irony that we have Messrs Burke and Wills looking massive and disconsolate at the corner of Collins and Swanston. Maybe they're looking for a car. Wouldn't they have loved a nice 4WD to get them on their way from Cooper's Creek?

But wait, there is more walking going on now in Swanston Walk than anywhere else in Melbourne. According to an independent traffic survey, pedestrian traffic between 1993 and '96 increased 42 per cent on the west side and 35 per cent on the east side. Since then, it has risen a further 16.2 per cent.

The busiest place for people in Melbourne is at the corner of Bourke and Swanston, with 31,000 passenger movements per day. And it wins by a country kilometre; the busiest suburban railway station is Box Hill with 18,000 movements.

The biggest complaints come from shopkeepers. Business isn't good. The vacancy rate at the moment is 1 per cent. How about that! Normally, in the city, when the vacancy rate drops to 5 per cent, they go mad putting up a gaggle of skyscrapers. Ask Just Jeans, ask Country Road, ask any long-faced chain retailer, crook business has nothing to do with traffic in Swanston Street.

If you haven't noticed, the shops have improved remarkably. Levi Strauss has taken a prime position on Swanston's east side. Big honor, I'm told; the first designated Levi temple in the country. Nike is moving in over the road, the first pure Nike holy of holies. Surely, they must believe in the Walk.

Yes, it's getting better. The trees have taken a time, but they are reaching the stage when they will start to branch out over the street. Some of those so-called Paris-style restaurants need to lift their game, perhaps. But they are quite ingenious. They do everything at the kerbside. Nothing has to be carted across the footpath. They are ideal spots for observing all the white shirts and black stockings. The Crown Cafe on the west side beyond Bourke is another good spot, splendid for coffee and pastries.

But could you imagine back in 1992 actually sitting down in Swanston Street? There are seats every 50 yards. You often see an old bloke near the corner of Collins feeding pigeons, birds all over him, squatting on his shoulders. That flower stall outside the Town Hall must be one of the most beautiful in any city.

It's the kinky bits I like. Have you ever studied that iron seat, let's see, it's on the east side right up near Little Lon. It is composed entirely of leaves and lovely metal sun flowers hover over your head. Unquestionably made for lovers.

You keep discovering little bits, such as a sheep, a whale and a tugboat set into the pavement or the way people like to get intimate with those three sculptured characters on the corner of Bourke. It's fun to slip a newspaper under their arms.

But here are some predictions. Three weeks from now, the council will vote to keep Swanston as it is. It would be too complicated and too expensive to do otherwise. After pondering a while, they will extend the Walk all the way to Victoria Street. They will make it more fun at night with open-air markets with emphasis on young chefs serving great food.

And the Walk eventually will be such a success that the council will start widening the footpaths in Elizabeth Street, reducing the traffic and actually making the whole Golden Mile fit for humans.

You just wait. We'll get rid of those cars.

A defence of the pedestrianised Swanston Walk against councillors pushing to reopen it to traffic, citing survey figures showing it as the busiest pedestrian corner in Melbourne and the return of retailers like Levi Strauss and Nike.
