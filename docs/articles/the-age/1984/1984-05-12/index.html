Stand by as the get-away becomes a give-away

3 min read.

First published 12 May 1984

A tour through airline brochures reveals the extraordinary lengths carriers are going to in 1984 to win custom, from Dom Perignon in Waterford Crystal to Delft liqueur houses and Sony Walkman headsets.

This is the time of year when almost everyone is heading off to the Northern Hemisphere. Business mates on 'fact-finding' tours, doctors at medical conferences in Cannes, Tahiti, Santa Barbara and Lake Como, many of them conveniently timed around the Los Angeles Olympics.

Actually there is no need to go anywhere at all. I am a brochure collector. Friday nights, when the television is particularly awful, we have Walter Mitty dreams and travel comfortably around the globe on the contents of the letterbox. The brochures have never been better, with at least a dozen airlines desperate for your custom.

The Thai brochures are marvellous. 'We fly in a different atmosphere where the intrigue of Thai culture creates exotic airborne pleasures.' Royal Orchid service, slinky Thai hostesses in long gowns wearing orchids. This alone is worth the trip.

The food and drink battle is awesome. All airlines are giving drink away, whether you are in steerage or 'Royal Sheikh' class. In first class you receive French champagne and a flower as you sit down. Pan Am and Qantas are pouring Moët, British Airways are on Veuve Clicquot, Singapore goes for Lanson, KLM for the expensive Taittinger Brut.

The food is beyond belief. British Airways and SAS are pursuing what can only be called a 'Great Chefs' gastronomic struggle. One British Airways menu comes from Hermann and Faye Schneider of 'Two Faces' in Melbourne: Homard Thermidor with Chassagne Montrachet 1979, or Contrefilet de Boeuf Rôti with Côtes de Nuits 1980. KLM offers five main courses to choose from: hare, scallops, guinea fowl, saddle of lamb, and fried chicken Szechuan. Lufthansa has assembled a French grand cru wine list.

The toiletry bag battle is also worth following. I am personally a 'struggle' class man, so this is of particular interest. British Airways has a neat zipper bag containing a sewing kit, razor, shaving cream, toothbrush kit, spot remover, mouth gargle, three sachets of eau de cologne, a comb, a nail file, and an eye shade. In first class the perfumes are Cartier, with Dunhill and Burberry aftershave; Business class gets Elizabeth Arden. KLM gives you a Delft miniature of a house facade containing liqueur. Cathay Pacific has dreamy His and Hers bags in burgundy. Qantas has His and Hers bags in refined blue with sockettes, eye shades, comb and mirror, toothbrush and toothpaste, nail file, shoe horn, and what the brochure describes as 'exotic unguents.'

Pan Am is now providing Sony Walkman headsets. Continental has put in a real bar with a brass rail in the centre of its DC-10s. Singapore Airlines is serving Dom Pérignon and Chivas Regal in Waterford Crystal.

The seat battle continues alongside all this. KLM reports that Business Class seats cost six thousand dollars each. Qantas has announced that the seat pitch has been altered from thirty-six to thirty-eight inches, giving passengers two extra inches of leg room, with full pictures of contented, dozing passengers under divine woollen blankets to prove the point.

Qantas also claims to be the only airline offering a full-scale library aboard. Toothpicks with dental floss. Radio plus a Reuters news office. SAS actually has telephones, so you can ring anyone anywhere while aloft. As Groucho Marx used to say: 'Is that you, Mum? I won't be home for dinner.'
