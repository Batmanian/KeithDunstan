After 500 kilometres, still the kings (and queens) of the road

4 min read.

First published 05 Dec 1998

They will trail into town tomorrow via Heidelberg, 2100 cyclists who will have pushed their muscle motors all the way, 500 kilometres from Wahgunyah on the NSW border.

Even on the longest day, 100 kilometres from Myrtleford to Benalla, only 28 failed to make it and had to be picked up by the "sag wagon".

This is the Vic Roads' 15th Great Victorian Bike Ride. Noel Reid, the ride director, claims it is the biggest mass migration of cyclists in the world. He is responsible for making about 70,000 meals for the riders and transporting 70,000 kilos of luggage.

The numbers are down a little this year. There were rides when they had nigh on 4000 bikers. But the faithful are still there, like Alan "Curly" Hartup, of Newstead.

Frankly, he doesn't look like a 500-kilometre cyclist. He is a little on the tubby side. And as for being curly, there's hardly a hair on his head. This is his eighth GVBR trip and he is 83 years old.

He has no time for suggestions that the Myrtleford-Benalla run must have been a real exhauster. "Not a bit," he said.

Mr Hartup has a regular training run - Newstead-Campbelltown-Clunes-Talbot-Carisbrook and back to Newstead. Exactly 100 kilometres. He has a big worry though. The GVBR form says they will accept entries from people aged 10 years to 84.

This means they cut him out next year. "I bet the insurance companies are behind it. They think if you're around 84 you will drop dead on the road. Not me."

He is determined to be back next time.

What does he think about when he is riding? He loves talking to the young girls, who give him encouragement.

"Keep on pedalling, mister," they call out.

Jack Campbell, 77, of Elsternwick is another rider. A retired industrial chemist, he is a regular on the ride. He couldn't make it last year because he was on a cricket tour with Rotary in England.

There were a number of Rotarians on the ride. One brought along his son, so that they could get to know each other better. And how did it work out? Jack laughed: "I believe he saw his son only four times. Twice he had a girl on his arm, and twice he had a glass of beer in his hand."

It is fascinating to watch the ride's progression across Victoria. The rest day was at Alexandra and the cyclists camped at the showgrounds. The sight of 2000 multi-colored tents huddled together is remarkable. The bikers sweep across the countryside like locusts eating every town out of its supply of Mars Bars, Magnums, Cherry Ripes and hamburgers. Benalla turned on a fireworks display opposite the football ground where they camped. Two bands provided the music.

Alexandra blocked off the main street through town. The bicycle was king, no cars. The three pubs, the Shamrock, Mt Pleasant and the Commercial, spread themselves right across the street with tables and chairs. The band, Destiny, played in the little bandstand.

People of all ages took part in the ride. Several parents brought along their babies and young children and carted them along in trailers attached to their bikes. There were 700 school children, escorted by noble teachers and parents.

The biggest contingent was Geelong Grammar School with 26 riders. The youngest rider to complete the trip was Anna Clements, of Surrey Hills. Born on 20 December 1988, Anna beat Nadia Bonato, of Kew, by one day.

There were few complaints. The facilities have improved. One year the ride became known as the great Victorian bike queue.

But the queues have eased a little with the years. There are 54 mobile toilets and 66 showers that consume 250,000 litres of water. Noel Reid is proud of his latest shower unit, mounted on a semi-trailer. It has 16 showers, girls up one end, boys down the other, and it is a hi-tech marvel.

It used to be one of the excitements, hot water one moment, downright frigid the next. Not any more. Noel says this unit keeps hot and can wash 1100 people in four hours.

There will be some sore knees and some very sore behinds for a few days. But Bike Victoria is already planning next year's ride. I believe they intend to pedal through the gold fields.

The 15th Great Victorian Bike Ride brings 2100 cyclists 500 kilometres from Wahgunyah to Melbourne, among them 83-year-old veteran Alan "Curly" Hartup and the ride's youngest finisher, nine-year-old Anna Clements.
