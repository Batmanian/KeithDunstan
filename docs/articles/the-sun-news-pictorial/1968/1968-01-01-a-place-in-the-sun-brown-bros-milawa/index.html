A Place in the Sun — Brown Bros., Milawa

4 min read.

First published 01 Jan 1968

Keith Dunstan attends the opening of a new underground cellar at Brown Bros., Milawa, months after a November frost wiped out the vintage, and watches John Brown seal a cache of wines under concrete to be dug up on 1 January 2001.

THIS column believes deeply in attending significant historic occasions. On Saturday it went to the opening of a new cellar at Brown Bros., Milawa.

Milawa is 10 miles east of Wangaratta and there have been Browns there since 1857. Even more important, they have served mankind by producing wine since 1889.

John Brown built the cellar with the aid of his four sons and, underground, it is vast.

It has 50 bins and each will hold 250 dozen bottles. It is comforting to see wine producers showing such faith in the future.

However, it will take a little time to fill this reservoir.

Last year, on November 20, the entire vintage was wiped out by frost. There hadn't been a frost like it in November for 35 years.

John Brown said he heard the day before that there had been a blizzard and four inches of snow on Mt. Hotham and he felt a frost was on the way.

He got up at 4 a.m., looked at the thermometer and the temperature was only 34. Everything seemed all right and he went back to bed.

But, by dawn, the temperature sank to below 27 deg.

When he went out, the vines — 100 acres of them — were limp and there was an acrid smell in the air, like you get in a flower shop.

Two days later everything was black. It was as though a bushfire had been through. So he didn't have to worry about grape picking this year — and the loss was 150,000 bottles.

★

SO, after that Frost Report, let's get back to the Brown act of defiance in expanding the vineyard for even bigger operations.

Wine lovers from all over came for the underground opening.

For example, Dr Max Lake came from Sydney. He has his own vineyard on the Hunter River known as Lake's Folly.

His bottle label features the A-frame winery at Pokolbin, which is generally referred to as the Pokolbin Opera House.

As for the folly of a doctor starting a vineyard on the side, it may not be such a folly after all.

The first wine to go on sale — and 1967 wine at that — has been selling at $2.50 a bottle.

Also present was the wine consultant, Len Evans, and the House of Lords, plus Allan Watson, from Watson's restaurant in Carlton.

In the House of Lords there are such men as Prof. Harold Hunt, Prof. Max Hargreaves, Dr Sam Benwell, Dr Dick Gutch and Max Marginson, the biochemist.

Prof. Hunt says they received this label in the early fifties.

They used to eat down at the back of Watson's and it seemed to the others that they received special treatment.

Contemptuously, they were labelled 'The Lords.'

Now they have their own room and there's still a suspicion that they get special treatment.

The Lords and lesser aristocracy gathered in the new Brown cellar and the thing to do was to study the wines.

One began with the 1965 vintage, comparing it in color and style with the 1964 vintage and, in turn, comparing it in color and style with the 1963 vintage and . . .

To make a long story short, eventually one sipped one's way right back to 1952.

And as one journeyed backwards to the years of one's youth, the wines appeared to become mellower, softer, and the cold cellar somehow cosier.

The true enthusiasts took out pencil and paper and noted down the great years, but, alas, those wines will never be seen again.

★

THEN, when we had all reached the proper stage of mellowness, John Brown performed the grand ceremony.

A special cache had been prepared in one bin.

Into this he put, in a moisture-proofed carton, 14 bottles of his medal winning wines — two of Riesling, two of late-picked Riesling, eight of claret 1961-64 and two of 1958 vintage port.

Another bottle went in which contained the names of all those present and then, I regret to say, Mr Brown showed little trust.

He did not merely cover the cache with a handy lid.

He covered it with a thick layer of concrete.

Then, using house-name letters, John Brown jun. made this message in the wet concrete:

'DIG HERE' — 1.1. 2001.

★

MR BROWN believes by that time, the Riesling will be in a sad state.

The late-picked Riesling will still be drinkable, two bottles of 1964 Everton claret will be fine, but he would make no predictions for the others.

However, all those present were invited to return on January 1, 2001, to note the results of the experiment.

Your correspondent will be there, of course. So please order your Sun for 2-1-2001 so that you can hear the next episode of this gripping serial.
