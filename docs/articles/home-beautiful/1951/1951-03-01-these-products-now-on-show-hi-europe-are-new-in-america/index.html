These Products, Now on Show in Europe, Are New in America

3 min read.

First published 01 Mar 1951

Reports on the Museum of Modern Art's touring exhibition of 500 items of American industrial design, refrigerators, radios, furniture, crockery and can openers, sent to Stuttgart and other European cities to demonstrate that American design has outgrown its inferiority complex about European taste.

America, this year, has sent 12 tons of refrigerators, radios, dish washers, furniture, toasters, crockery and even can openers to show Europe what it can do in the field of industrial design.

Normally, Americans like Australians, tend to have a sense of inferiority about their own products. They believe the best in art, furniture, and modern design must naturally come from Europe and Scandinavia. Yet businessmen here, especially since the early thirties, have discovered that beauty in design can triple or quadruple the sale of goods. In fact they now take the design of household goods — including everything from armchairs to bottle tops — so seriously that good design competitions have become regular events in most cities.

Because of this, last year the Museum of Modern Art received many requests from Europeans to stage a show of American home furnishings in the principal cities of Europe and Great Britain during 1951.

This show, with 500 different items, left New York and opened in Stuttgart last month. The purpose of the show is to give Europeans some idea of the most progressive trends in design that have taken place here since World War Two, with an emphasis on items which are typically, or even peculiarly, American.

The show will have two advantages. First it will be a valuable exchange of ideas, and second it will show European designers what has already been done here. This will help them in the selling of their own goods in America.

Edgar Kaufman Jr., of the Museum of Modern Art's staff, selected the various items. They include woven plastics, woven fibres, meat slicers, luggage, kitchen furniture, toys, fabrics, pottery, lamps, plastic bowls and all kinds of mass-manufactured goods.

Among the furniture on display are simple, low-cost storage units designed by Charles Eames for the Herman Miller Furniture Company. The uprights and struts are of cadmium-plated steel; drawer fronts, side-panels and other components are of plastic-coated mauve plywood. Drawer pulls are of brushed aluminium. All pieces are available in a variety of colours and finishes.

There is also a Calfab "knock-down" chair, with solid hardwood frame strung with cord, alongside a coffee table designed by Franziska Hosken and made by the Calfab Furniture Company. The twin floor lamp with adjustable reflectors is a low-cost example of a well-designed lamp. A streamlined safety can opener slips on to a special wall bracket when in use.

Source: These Products, Now on Show in Europe, Are New in America, The Australian Home Beautiful, March 1951. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
