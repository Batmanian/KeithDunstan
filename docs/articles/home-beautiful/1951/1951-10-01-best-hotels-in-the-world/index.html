Best Hotels in the World

5 min read.

First published 01 Oct 1951

Reports on the two most modern hotels in the world, the El Panama in Panama City, designed by Ed Stone with no windows, louvred "breeze-ways" and no lobby, and the Caribe Hilton in San Juan, Puerto Rico, with 300 air-conditioned rooms, a private beach and 14-acre gardens, arguing that such American investment could transform Australian tourism.

When I first came to the United States two years ago I decided I wanted to taste American comfort and feel the real luxury of an American hotel.

So I walked into a hotel in San Francisco (not the best) and said in a firm voice: "Would you give me a room ... with a bath please?"

The receptionist looked at me just a little disdainfully and replied: "Sir, we have NO rooms without baths."

That was the end of it. I got my room with a bath whether I liked it or not and the situation is the same all over America. A room with a bath is a very small thing and the travelling American expects far more than this. He wants his private radio and television set and a room-service which can meet his every fantastic wish.

While Australia is fighting to provide small houses by the thousand, the United States, in most areas, has conquered its housing problem. But, curiously, two of the finest American hotels have just opened outside the United States. They are the Caribe Hilton in San Juan, Puerto Rico, and the El Panama in Panama City.

They are perhaps the most modern hotels in the world and a fair example of what could happen if American capital is ever allowed to build hotels in Australia.

The most interesting of the two is the El Panama. It has 271 rooms and looks like a long white honeycomb in the sky. The architect, Ed Stone, said he wanted to build a hotel which had no windows, no doors and no corridors.

This was something of an exaggeration, but as it turned out it wasn't far from the truth. Most of the rooms do not have windows, and winds sweep through in one side and out the other. At last we have a hotel in the tropics designed for tropical conditions.

Except for a few air-conditioned rooms in the central block, the hotel is only one room thick. The rooms, called "breeze-ways," have louvred folding screens which open towards the balconies on the south and plain louvred screens on outside companionways or decks to the north.

Few architects would have the courage to build a hotel without windows in Panama. It rains in torrents down there and, of course, there are all kinds of insects. But the insects don't fly above the tree tops and 9 ft. overhangs stop the rain from sweeping in. Most people who take the air-conditioned rooms ask to be switched to a "breeze-way" after the first few days. They find it more intriguing and more comfortable.

The El Panama has caused a stir in hotel and architectural circles, for there is another odd thing about it — it has no lobby. The hotel planners decided the only use for lobbies was to attract old women who liked to gossip and see what the young things were up to.

So, in place of a lobby, the whole ground floor is divided between a lounge, a restaurant and a bar. Nothing divides one from the other beyond a few movable trellises and plant boxes. People can move freely through the area.

It is easy to move this area outside where there is another bar, a dance floor, an extension to the restaurant, beds of flowers, pools, gardens, and long shady walks. The swimming pool is like one in a Hollywood film. Chairs and tables are all round, so while the energetic swim you can sit, sun-bathe and drink an aperitif. The building cost £2,500,000.

The Caribe Hilton is just as modern and just as luxurious. It is slightly larger with 300 double rooms. It cost £A2,700,000 compared with £A2,500,000 for the El Panama.

Each room is air-conditioned and each room has its own private balcony with glass from floor to ceiling, radio, bath, shower and running iced water. The ground floor and lobby space is completely open to the air, so you can walk straight through to the gardens and patios outside. Although it is within San Juan's city limits, the hotel gardens run to 14 acres.

The biggest attraction about the place is the Caribe Hilton's private stretch of beach. At first the beach wasn't everything a beach ought to be, so the Hilton organisation blasted all coral rock out of the swimming area. Then they covered the beach with an unusual type of soft coral sand which does not stick to the body.

Of course, hotel guests don't have to change in their rooms. There is a series of locker rooms and cabanas fitted out with hot and cold showers, radios and the same modern furniture used for the inside of the hotel.

Both hotels have done much for their respective countries. The El Panama has done much the same for Panama. There are no hotels comparable for many hundreds of miles. The rates at the Caribe Hilton range from 10 to 16 dollars a day for single rooms and 12 to 19 for double rooms. The rates are much the same for the El Panama.

Source: Best Hotels in the World, The Australian Home Beautiful, October 1951. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
