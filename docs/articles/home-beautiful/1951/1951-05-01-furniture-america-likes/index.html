Furniture America Likes

5 min read.

First published 01 May 1951

Reports from Chicago's Merchandise Mart winter furniture exhibitions, where the Museum of Modern Art's "Good Design" show and the growing taste for simple steel-and-fabric pieces are gradually pushing aside the old Louis XIV reproductions and "Ranch" style sets, with television quietly reshaping everything from sofas to floor coverings.

Chicago, not to be outdone by the Empire State Building, claims to have the world's largest building — the Merchandise Mart. It is the great centre of America's wholesale market, where professional buyers can make their selections from the best merchandise this nation has to offer. It rises 25 storeys above the street, covers two city blocks, and houses 25,000 people. Freight trains run underneath direct to freight elevators, and it has over four million square feet of office space.

This is the great centre where America holds its furniture exhibitions, and naturally the best place to gain some idea of the types of furniture Americans will be buying during the next 12 months. In recent months the winter exhibitions have been taking place, and the Museum of Modern Art, in conjunction with the Merchandise Mart, is holding its second exhibition on "Good Design."

The "Good Design" exhibition is featuring the best in household goods and furniture sent from cities all over America and Europe. The display consists of more than 250 articles needed for furnishing a home. They have been on sale in America since last June, and you can see some of them on these pages.

Naturally the main thought worrying buyers at the Merchandise Mart is how long home furnishings will be in good supply. Manufacturers believe there will be no real shortage for at least six months. So far only 25 per cent. of the manufacturers have found it necessary to cut down on their products, and some have been able to carry on by substituting different materials.

This year more and more people are buying modern furniture. They are looking for simple, comfortable furniture which is easy to handle, and which will stand up to hard treatment. Furniture which is easy to clean and doesn't get in the way in a small flat is much in demand.

This means that heavy armchairs are no longer popular. For one thing they are too costly and for another they take up too much space. Instead, many of the armchairs and tables are made of steel. They are comfortable, attractive and consist of little else but fabric or cord draped around steel tubing.

People seem to be shying away from anything that is hard to clean. Manufacturers are therefore showing an array of tables made of steel and glass — tables with scar, scratch and stain-resisting tops. It wasn't easy to take at first, but chairs with plastic upholstery are now selling well. Manufacturers claim these will last a lifetime, and they can be cleaned by a quick scrub down.

Television is having its influence. Everything is now mounted on ball-bearings, including chairs, tables, lamps, sofas and television sets. This enables furniture to be rearranged to give the whole family a front view of the television screen.

Despite the yearning for space, sofas are still popular. There are several reasons for this. It is easy to convert them into beds. In a small room they will seat three or four people, and take up less space than the equivalent number of armchairs, and they are handy also for watching television. Many of them have no arms and are mounted on thin metal legs. They come in sweeping curves or in right angles to fit in a corner. Crescent shapes are popular for television, and some of these are nine feet in length.

Naturally the high price of wool has hit the carpet market. Manufacturers are going all out for new floor coverings made of synthetic materials. They are all experimenting with carpets made of rayon, orlon, wool blends and cotton. Attractive cotton twist rugs sell cheaply. They are in high demand because it is easy to send them to the dry cleaners when they get dirty.

Salesmen say that the all-synthetics and blends take brighter colours, clean and wear as well or better than wool carpets. At the moment cotton twist carpets sell for 7 dollars a square yard, 40 per cent. rayon–60 per cent. wool combination 10 dollars a square yard, and all wool 11.25 dollars a square yard. There is no doubt that synthetic coverings are going to become very popular in the US.

The winter shows at the Merchandise Mart prove that a great many Americans still have not taken to modern furniture. Many manufacturers are still making huge quantities of "antique" Louis XIV style chairs, tables and bedroom suites. Another popular style is the all-American Ranch furniture. This is bulky, solid and usually made of oak, hickory or pine. The advertisements say "it gives the kind of western feeling which bespeaks open-hearted hospitality." Some of it has an attractive simplicity, but on the other hand, several manufacturers have gone for this style with such gusto that the "open-hearted hospitality" is apt to give one's house the appearance of the old curiosity shop!

This furniture is hardly the product of our time, and certainly not the type which is making American designers well known throughout the world. For the best that America now has to offer one need hardly look further than the Museum of Modern Art's "Good Design" exhibition.

Source: Furniture America Likes, The Australian Home Beautiful, May 1951. Accessible via the National Library of Australia's Trove database.
