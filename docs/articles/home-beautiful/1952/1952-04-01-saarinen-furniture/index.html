Saarinen Furniture

3 min read.

First published 01 Apr 1952

Examines Eero Saarinen's approach to furniture design, his moulded plastic shell chairs with foam rubber padding, manufactured for Knoll Associates, designed to replace the great stuffed armchair of a different era and to accommodate the way modern people actually sit, rest and squirm.

Saarinen has been winning furniture design competitions for the past 10 years and there is no doubt that his work has had an effect on the living habits of Americans.

Modern furniture is becoming more and more popular in New York, so much so that several stores sell nothing else. It has none of the bulk of the furniture of 20 years ago. Instead, it is light, made of steel, plastics and sponge rubber. Imagine a sofa that you can lift with one hand! It is a style of furniture more suited to our style of living. It makes up for lack of space and suits modern construction methods.

Saarinen is best-known as a designer of chairs. If you are not used to modern furniture, a Saarinen chair may look strange, unnatural, with its weird moulding and plastic shape. It may look fragile and you may sit down gingerly, thinking it will never support your weight. But it does; it is stronger than most chairs and superbly comfortable.

One of Saarinen's chief aims was to design something which could replace the great stuffed armchair, designed for a different era. Then chairs had an exaggerated size to impress by sheer mass — made to look right in outsize rooms with lofty ceilings.

Now you see few great stuffed armchairs in modern American rooms. Apart from the fact that rooms are too small to cope with them, most people cannot afford them. Hundreds of hand operations go to making a stuffed armchair and prices for them have risen beyond all reason. So this type of furniture is going out along with cooks, maids and 12-room houses.

Saarinen believes people sit differently from the way they did in the Victorian era. They want to sit much lower, relax their bodies more and rest their heads. This calls for a design with three planes of support which will let you sit comfortably in all sorts of positions.

Most people can sit in the one position for just so long. No matter how comfortable they were, they must start squirming, twisting, and throwing their legs or even pulling their legs up on the chair. It is simply a matter of stretching stiff muscles and gaining exercise.

Saarinen believes in catering for all this. A chair should leave room for plenty of movement, otherwise it is like wearing a tight pair of shoes, which may be comfortable when you first put them on but become agony when you want to start wriggling your toes.

A chair is a background for the person sitting in it, Saarinen says. If the lines of the chair can be so made that they become a flattering instead of a negative background, this makes a great deal of difference, particularly for a beautiful woman.

The Saarinen armchairs have laminated fibre shells on frames of steel tubing, padded with sponge rubber. The chairs are springy and resilient, so that they give to the shape of the body. The fibre shells are stamped out in steam heated moulds. Almost all the materials are products from the laboratory. Like the helicopter or the deep freeze, the Saarinen chair is a post-war product, a far cry from the pre-war armchair made from wood, springs, horse hair and leather.

They are hardly a novelty here any more. Saarinen said that when he saw one of his chairs in a Li'l Abner comic strip, he knew he had arrived.
