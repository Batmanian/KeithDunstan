The Age

Keith Dunstan wrote for The Age from 1986 until his death in 2013.

The Age is a Melbourne broadsheet founded in 1854, long regarded as the city's paper of record and the natural home for Dunstan's later columns after a career built at its rival, the Herald and Weekly Times stable. Collections of his columns for The Age can also be found in his books "No Brains on Tuesday", where for a period he wrote a weekly column and "A Day in the Life of Australia" - a series to commemorate Australia's Bicentenary in 1988.

  • Know him? We've just kissed. — 20 May 1984 A 1984 photograph of Prince Charles kissing Prince Edward on the cheek prompts a survey of the social kissing boom, from Brezhnev lifting Kissinger bodily off the ground to the art of the cocktail party fake-kiss.
  • The Punks at the Automatic Teller — 10 Jun 1984 A visit to Melbourne's punk hairdressers reveals the engineering behind the Apache-strip, the bobtail, and the scrunch, all of it driven not by political protest but by the desire to look like Boy George.
  • Good Lord! — or, an appeal to Mr Packer on a silly cut-off — 28 Jul 1985 An open letter to Kerry Packer protesting Channel Nine's threatened withdrawal of cricket television coverage, arguing Australians are so devoted to the sport that any government permitting its removal from free-to-air TV would face electoral consequences.
  • Worm Fans Rout Norm — 27 Sep 1985 A wry observation on Melbourne's graffiti war over imprisoned BLF leader Norm Gallagher, whose "Free Norm" slogans rivals were altering by turning the N into a W, prompting a wish for a CSIRO spray that could erase all such propaganda from the city's walls.
  • The Gallery of Sport Waits for Norm — 06 Dec 1985 A wry lament over the Victorian Gallery of Sport at the MCG, still unfinished after months of BLF stoppages, demarcation disputes, and a walkout triggered by Premier John Cain's site visit, with completion now pushed to April 1986 at a cost overrun of $1.5 million.
  • Elephants Return to the Fold — 24 Jan 1986 Hildegard Kamphausen, owner of the Chateau Wyuna restaurant at Mt Evelyn, acquired a deconsecrated 1909 Church of Christ from Montrose and had it moved two and a half kilometres to her property, transforming it into a wedding chapel booked months in advance.
  • Herman Schneider's Sunday — 25 Jan 1986 A profile of Swiss-born Melbourne restaurateur Herman Schneider, whose supposedly sacred Sunday off from running Two Faces in South Yarra and Roesti in Hardware Street was inevitably eaten up by market runs, wine tastings, and anxious checks on both restaurants.
  • Hector Crawford's Sunday — 14 Feb 1986 A profile of Hector Crawford and soprano Glenda Raymond, the Melbourne entertainment power couple whose Sunday tennis club on Toorak Road attracted judges, academics and portrait painters, followed by an evening screening Crawford Productions footage for the Nine Network.
  • As Organic and Good Earth as All Hell — 28 Feb 1986 A visit to the Red Hill Market on the Mornington Peninsula, the monthly produce and craft market that began in 1976 with 22 stalls and by its tenth anniversary had grown to 210, drawing crowds from across Victoria and as far as Adelaide.
  • Peter Russell-Clarke's Sunday — 30 May 1986 A Good Weekend "My Sunday" profile of Peter Russell-Clarke at Soho, his century-old farm on Victoria's Bellarine Peninsula, where cooking experiments, Queenscliff fish, long lunches and a mob of more than fifty peacocks define his country weekends.
  • David Penman's Sunday — 26 Jul 1986 A Good Weekend "My Sunday" profile of David Penman, Anglican Archbishop of Melbourne, tracing his weekend from midnight NBC news to parish rounds, Wimbledon tennis, and his growing collection of more than 40 wombat figurines.
  • Frank Little's Sunday — 12 Dec 1986 A Good Weekend "My Sunday" profile of Frank Little, Catholic Archbishop of Melbourne, whose Sundays run to three or four different homilies, an Italian Mass he preaches in Italian, and a Monday golf game at Metropolitan that recently ended when he stepped off the sixth tee.

  • This street was made for walking — 13 Oct 1997 A defence of the pedestrianised Swanston Walk against councillors pushing to reopen it to traffic, citing survey figures showing it as the busiest pedestrian corner in Melbourne and the return of retailers like Levi Strauss and Nike.
  • The happy thwack of cash on willow — 27 Oct 1997 A meditation on cricket's transformation from a game of beautifully organised boredom, best enjoyed dozing at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, into a business fought over by players threatening strike action for a share of ballooning sponsorship money.
  • Harbor no grudge, it's better here; The bay and me — 29 Dec 1997 A Boxing Day case for Port Phillip Bay over its more celebrated rival, touring favourite spots from Sea Winds and Point Nepean to the Sorrento ferry and a flathead-fishing trip with mate Doug Crittenden.
  • Moombas and shakers — 05 Mar 1998 A profile of the Wittingslow family, the fairground dynasty who have run Moomba's rides and sideshows since patriarch Tom Wittingslow started out guessing weights at Penny Land in 1932, and who now stage the festival itself.
  • A new clutch of ugly ducklings blots the cityscape — 08 Aug 1998 A nomination round for Melbourne's ugliest sights, from the Foxtel and Optus advertising signs looming over the historic Young and Jackson's Hotel to the kitsch interior of the new Imax Theatre and the warring columns of 101 Collins Street.
  • On the right track at last — 31 Oct 1998 The Lilydale-Warburton Rail Trail opens along a disused line closed by Henry Bolte in 1964, part of a growing Victorian movement converting old railway corridors into 36 kilometres of bush trail free of motor vehicles.

Dennis's Sentimental Bloke first met Doreen.
  • After 500 kilometres, still the kings (and queens) of the road — 05 Dec 1998 The 15th Great Victorian Bike Ride brings 2100 cyclists 500 kilometres from Wahgunyah to Melbourne, among them 83-year-old veteran Alan "Curly" Hartup and the ride's youngest finisher, nine-year-old Anna Clements.
  • Obituary: Richard Cudlipp — 12 May 1999 An obituary for advertising executive Dick Cudlipp, who rose to managing director of George Patterson Bates in 1965, drove Carlton and United Breweries' campaign against the Courage Brewery, and later became a Western District cattle and vineyard grazier.

  • Joy of sport lost in a country that takes it far too seriously — 29 Dec 2010 Reflects on Australia's outsized reaction to the 2010-11 Ashes loss under Ricky Ponting, arguing the country has forgotten that sport, unlike genuine disasters such as Cyclone Tracy, is meant to be enjoyed regardless of the result.
  • Plagues be upon us, let's hope for no divine retribution — 04 Jan 2011 A mock-biblical catalogue of Melbourne's afflictions during a wet 2010, led by a possum population boom under the Wildlife Act, and running through cars, mobile phones, football and noisy restaurants via the ten plagues of Exodus.
  • It's crunch time for Melbourne: we need a congestion tax — 27 Jan 2011 Argues Melbourne should follow London and Stockholm in adopting a congestion tax to break the city's addiction to the motor car, citing Bicycle Victoria figures on the rising share of peak-hour commuter cyclists.
  • Swear fines a nonsense when invective is so ineffective — 06 Jun 2011 Marks new Victorian on-the-spot fines for swearing by recalling editor Michael Davie's landmark 1979 decision to print an obscenity in The Age, and proposes Paul Keating chair an inquiry into supplying Australians with fresh invective.
  • All I know about ethics, I learned at sword-point — 17 Jul 2011 Recalls a career of manufactured tabloid outrage, from a fake letters-page furore over Myer's window mannequins to a night spent hiding in a wardrobe from a sword-wielding husband, prompted by reflecting on the collapse of the News of the World.
  • Once, footy dominated our lives. Now, things are much worse — 22 Aug 2011 Surveys the exponential growth of Australian Football League saturation since Keith Dunstan and Douglas Wilkie founded the Anti-Football League in 1967, from a single Saturday afternoon competition to year-round coverage, gambling and coaching sackings.
  • Obituary: Walter Joseph Hauser — 11 Feb 2012 An obituary for Walter Hauser, one of the touring Vienna Mozart Boys' Choir singers stranded in Melbourne by the outbreak of war in 1939, who was taken in by Archbishop Daniel Mannix and went on to a career as an industrial chemist and Keith Dunstan's regular bridge partner.
  • Oh, for the daily thud on my lawn — 21 Jun 2012 A tribute to the ritual of reading a printed daily newspaper in bed each morning, tracing Keith Dunstan's own newspaper family history against the decades-long death toll of Melbourne mastheads and Rupert Murdoch's prediction that print itself would be gone by 2020.
