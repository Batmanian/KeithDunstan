Sports

Published in 1973, 'Sports' is part of Dunstan's quartet of books examining Australian character, alongside Wowsers, Knockers and Ratbags.

About the book

Sports looks at the Australian obsession with sport and sporting heroes, from suburban football fields to the country's biggest sporting institutions. It grew out of a 1969 La Trobe Library exhibition on Victoria's sporting past, and argues that sport, more than politics or war, is the real key to the Australian character.

Acknowledgements

In sport-adoring Australia it is a fine compliment to call someone 'a sport' and the ultimate compliment is to call him 'a good sport'. There are many good sports I have to thank. First there is Peter Howson, former Minister for the Arts, who sent the telegram giving the good news of a Special Projects Grant from the Commonwealth Literary Fund. This money gave time, a precious three months, which enabled me to finish this book.

Other sports? My old friend Phillip Garrett, the master researcher, was in the background, advising me where to dig. Mrs John Jonas provided me with hard-to-find information about her grandfather, billiards king, Thomas Upton Alcock. Forbes Carlile one sunny morning in Sydney, patiently took me around his swimming pools. Dr A. W. Willee at Melbourne University, equally patiently, revealed all his researches into the physical fitness of Australians. George Blaikie, the expert on 'Our Strange Past', and Roy Morgan willingly opened up the entire files of the Gallup Poll.

Libraries? We fossickers cannot exist without them and the librarians never receive the proper credit for their work and knowledge. Particularly I would like to thank Miss Patricia Reynolds and the staff of the La Trobe Library. They provided many of the old prints in this book and the Library Council gave permission for the publication of two Troedel & Co. posters, plus a kangaroo hunting scene by S. T. Gill. The Mitchell Library gave permission for the use of the incredible old 'In Memoriam' notice to Harry Searle, the great photograph of Trickett's triumph, the silk picture of the Burns-Johnson battle and a strange old sketch of an emu hunt.

Roy Terry, Herald and Weekly Times Ltd librarian, made available his huge resources of news clippings and I am indebted to Alf Guest of the Herald and Sun picture library for most of the photographs in this book. The others, particularly the astonishing picture of Norm Proven caked in mud, are by courtesy of the Sydney Morning Herald. Ern McQuillan took the photograph of Forbes Carlile and his swimming pupils.

Thank-you to Jack O'Hagan and Allans Music Australia for permission to reproduce his famous song 'Our Don Bradman' and to Nicholson's Pty Ltd for permission to print Jack's song 'Our Marjorie'.

Then there were the publishers who let me rob their books: John Farquharson Ltd, who let me quote from Betty Cuthbert's Golden Girl, Cheshire Publishing Pty Ltd for the lines from John McLaren's fine book Our Troubled Schools, Angus & Robertson (Publishers) Pty Ltd for the extracts from The First 100 Years by Geoffrey Blainey, James Morrissey and S. E. K. Hulme, plus the story of Bluey Truscott's wartime return to football from Bluey Truscott by Ivan Southall. Then I must thank the Hutchinson Group Pty Ltd for the use of a trio of good sporting books, The Dawn of Swimming by Ken Cox, Captain Blood by Jack Dyer and Boots and All by my old friend Lou Richards. Cassell and Co. Ltd gave permission for the extracts from Herb Elliott's The Golden Mile.

And for the most frightful job in book publishing thank-you to Mrs Elspeth Jolley for the typing and the index.
