---
title: The Purity of our Sunday
date: 1988-03-03
summary: Melbourne's Presbyterian clergy are outraged in March 1874 by a Sunday railway picnic to Mount Macedon in which 1500 railway workers enjoy brass bands, dancing and liquor, the latest in a long battle over the Sabbath that has ranged from the opening of the flagship HMVS Cerberus to kite-flying boys at Emerald Hill.
tags:
  - Melbourne
---

There has been a deplorable case of desecration of the Sabbath, and the entire Presbyterian world is up in arms about it. There is no question the Melbourne Sunday is in danger.

The battle to keep our Sunday pure really began back in 1854; until then we had bar sales of liquor on Sundays. Despite the fact that 150,000 citizens petitioned and wanted the bar sales to go on, the Sabbatarians staged a splendid onslaught to get their way. The Moderator of the Free Presbyterian Church wrote to the Argus, 28 November 1855:

We do solemnly declare, testify to you this day, Beloved Brethren, that the blessing of God can never rest on the temporal gain or profit that may be made by profaning the Sabbath. Woe unto him that striveth with himself, say ye, who is hardened against God and prospered?

There was consternation when the flagship of Her Majesty's Victorian Navy, Cerberus, was thrown open for public inspection on Sundays, and that caused a fearful row. Two years ago, a certain Mr Miller hired the Town Hall for an entertainment and waited on the Mayor to provide entertainment on a Sunday.

Last year we had complaints about boys flying kites at Emerald Hill on Sunday. 'It is high time public attention was directed to these iniquities' said a letter to the Argus.

However there has always been a battle about railway trains running on Sundays. Most respectfully, the Railways have only what they call 'church trains' which convey people to church in the morning.

The absolutely worst affair took place at Macedon. The Cerberus opening on Sundays caused a fearful row last week. It was the occasion of the Sunday Railway Picnic to Mount Macedon. Over 1500 railway workers went to the bush by train. They had sports, they had four brass bands, they had dancing and there were booths where liquor was served. Melbourne Punch reported that everybody had such a good time that some had to be left behind when the trains left that night, yet they still turned up at work the next day with the excuse 'lost in the bush, all night, Sir, and couldn't make anyone hear'.

Today at the monthly meeting of Presbytery of Melbourne the Reverend Dr Cameron called the picnic one of the greatest outrages ever carried out under the sanction of Government, and called for a deputation to the Chief Secretary.

The Reverend J.C. Symons has prepared a memorial, which will be read at the Chief Secretary at this deputation next week. We believe that the entertainment of the railway workers has done more to undermine the purity of our Sunday than anything in the past decade. We protest in the strongest terms.
