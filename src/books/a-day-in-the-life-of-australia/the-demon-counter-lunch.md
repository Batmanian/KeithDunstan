---
title: The Demon Counter Lunch
date: 1988-02-28
summary: The free counter lunch, Melbourne's magnificent tradition of roast lamb, beef, turkey, goose and hot fish that grew from bread and cheese in the 1870s into a full daily feast underwritten by the Brewers Association, officially ends in February 1916 after an alliance of temperance campaigners and hotel-keepers unite to ban it as a dangerous bait luring young men to drink.
tags:
  - Melbourne
---

The free counter lunch in Melbourne officially comes to an end tomorrow, banned. It is something one can report only with tremendous sorrow. It has been so beautiful. It all began back in the 1870s. First they would give you just bread and cheese. But then the competition developed, one against the other, and the free feed became utterly noble, with huge plates of roast lamb, roast beef, turkey, goose, and hot dried fish, salads, bread and cheese.

Here is a recent report of how lovely it used to be:

A leading Swanston Street hotel makes a great feature of the counter lunch. It is but 11 o'clock, yet biscuits and cheese, Fritz sausage, cold corned beef, salads, pickles and white puddings, and bread are spread out for the benefits of the morning supper. At midday the regular lunchers begin to arrive. If they patronise the front or the side bars they are welcomed by the appetising odour of hot pigs' cheek. In the back bars hot roasts or boiling joints are on the table. At 1 o'clock hot fried sausages and saveloys make their appearance. This kind of thing goes on until closing time.

Well, an extraordinary alliance developed between the temperance people. The Reverend A.R. Edgar told in church how evil the counter lunches were. They were a dangerous bait for the younger generation. A young man would go into a bar to get his free counter lunch; he would feel a little bit obliged to buy a beer, and then he was hooked.

As Melbourne Punch put it, the problem wasn't so much the demon drink as the demon counter lunch.

The powerful Brewers Association has been the main financier. Mr Monie Cohen has decreed this institution is no longer needed. The brewers have actually been subsidising hotel counter lunches, provided the hotel sells their beer.

Now Mr Cohen shares the ugly news that the counter lunch has experienced 'an insidious growth' and hundreds of people have been living on the food provided. These people would have a substantial meal at 11.30 p.m., another at 5 p.m. Some leading hotel-keepers were paying out £3000 a year on lunches. 'I don't know any part of the world where this sort of thing goes on.'

The Bulletin summed it best with some tragic verse:

Farewell! the riddled meats, the gnawed and nibbled feet of hogs,
Farewell! Farewell to these.
We thought he liked to see us eat, watch us daily stuff,
With crumbs and bones about our feet, until we'd had enough.

We piled with bread and ham,
Was sauced with bitter rage and hate
And poisoned with a 'damn'.
