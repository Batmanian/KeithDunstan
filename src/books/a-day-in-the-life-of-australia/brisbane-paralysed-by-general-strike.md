---
title: Brisbane Paralysed by General Strike
date: 1988-02-03
summary: A general strike paralyses Brisbane in February 1912, with 34 unions out, shops and theatres closed, mounted police clashing with crowds in the streets, and Premier Denman appealing for volunteers after trouble began when the American superintendent of the tramway company sacked workers for wearing union badges on the job.
tags:
  - Brisbane
  - Queensland
---

Brisbane is paralysed today with 34 unions taking part in a general strike. There have been processions and riots; and the Premier, Mr Denman, has appealed for volunteers to help the police.

A special mounted corps has been formed. As the Brisbane Courier puts it: 'Many hundreds of sturdy sons of the soil have left their farms to take a hand in preserving the peace in the capital city. They are coming from many parts of Southern Queensland'.

It all began when Mr Badger, chief of the tramway company, dismissed workers for wearing union badges on the job. Mr Badger is an American. Everyone calls him 'Boss Badger' and he is known as a union breaker.

Most of the shops have closed, the theatres are closed, the hotels are closed, and if you want to get to work you have to walk. The main problem now is getting food.

The scene in the West End yesterday was a great crowd of women and children gathered outside a bakery clamouring for bread.

The workers have formed a patrol corps. They tour the city and demonstrate in front of any shop or cafe that should decide to stay open.

Outside the Savoy Hotel, the crowd became threatening, and mounted police came to the rescue. The workers retaliated with wooden blocks. One constable received a blow that left him permanently incapacitated from the force; two others had their helmets smashed in.

Soon afterwards a man was trying to remove barrels he had bought in the Roma Street sheds. The crowd refused to let it pass. Foot police charged the mob and there was a fierce struggle.

The Premier, Mr Denman, has appealed to Her Majesty's Government for troops. After this the police kept order at considerable cost.

Our Labor Prime Minister, Andrew Fisher, is not yet coming up from the south but said he was watching events with the greatest concern.
