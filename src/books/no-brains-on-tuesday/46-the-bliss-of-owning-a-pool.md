How is your domestic-possessions-social-rating? Are you moving forwards, gaining the envy of your neighbours? Kindly write down points:

Mercedes, Jaguar, Ferrari, Porsche: 12
Tennis court: 15
Mansion in exclusive suburb with ducted heaters and air conditioning: 15
Private gardens set in grounds suitable for domestic staff: 13
Sauna lined with divine timber: 19
Sunken rock pool with 'articulated fountain': 8
Country place peopled with Aberdeen Angus and white fence rails surrounding paddocks, one hour by Mercedes from town: 25
Lock-up flat on the Gold Coast: 16
Lounge set in exquisite Spanish designer tiles with outdoor heating and bar service: 10
Personal jogging track: 15
Private gymnasium with ergometer, pulse meter, exercise bike and weightlifting gear: 12
Swimming pool: 3

If you have scored above 30 then your life is a private hell, and it is practically impossible to communicate with your friends. You cannot comfortably take the usual annual holiday at Cannes or Aspen.

You will notice the pitiful rating received by the swimming pool. I think swimming pool owners were definitely a 1970 or even 1975 phenomenon. We visited a friend at Mooroolbark who had it all freshly done that week — clumps of freshly-planted chrysanthemums all around the back.

A year later I visited him again. 'At last we have done it,' he said. 'What have you done?'

'I got rid of the swimming pool. It was a large fibreglass pool. I didn't even bother to sell it. I gave it away. They came and took it away in a truck. Best thing I ever did in my life. Don't you like my new garden? I feel so liberated.'

I knew exactly what he meant. My heart went out to him.

We moved into our present house in 1977. Swimming pools — I pined for one. I have a piece of blurb on the subject now.

'Your swimming pool should be more than just a water-holding construction in your garden and a very attractive one at that. Become the thoughtful landscaper. Whether it is built in or above ground the sheer bliss of being able to swim in your own backyard is pure bliss.'

He then went on: 'Your swimming pool is an investment. Real estate experts say the value added to your house is at least three times the cost.'

That was the line that got me. Actually the swimming pool was already installed. The man who sold us the house said: 'And this pool — actually it's labour saving. You will never have a lawn to mow.'

'Yes, yes, yes,' I said excitedly, 'where can I sign?'

A kindly man from the pool company came around even before a month was up and demonstrated how to keep the water healthy. He ran his stethoscope over the pump. Immediately he diagnosed that the filter needed replacing — $220; the pool light didn't work — $55; pump needed replacing — $220; more diatomaceous earth — $25.

But worse, I discovered that owning a swimming pool was like owning an acre of vegetables; it had to be worried over daily. Chlorinating, pH balancing, skimming leaves, shocking the water, the filter running — even worse, when you had friends in and the thing had to perform in public.

My friend was right. The day we got rid of it was one of the happiest of my life.
