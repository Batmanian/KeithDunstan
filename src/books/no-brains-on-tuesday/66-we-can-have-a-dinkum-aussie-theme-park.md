Aren't you fascinated with the news that the Japanese Ministry of International Trade and Industry is planning to build an Australian Theme Park or even Aussie Multi Cultural Polis?

Probably it will be in Mishima or maybe Hokkaido and the theory is that it will give Japanese a deeper understanding of Australia and its people.

I wonder if I could have a hand in its planning? For a start, I would insist on a triple-fronted cream brick veneer on the classic quarter acre block, electric light pole right outside, liquidambar in front garden, a few beds with pansies, marigolds and azaleas.

Ah, but the key to the Australian character would be the backgarden. Its piece de resistance would be the Hills Hoist. The artist Donald Friend always used to insist that our only contribution to world culture was the rotary clothes hoist.

We would have a slightly leaking galvanised iron tank beside the kitchen, an outside dunny in the yard's right hand corner and in the left hand an incredible little cubby made out of packing cases, hunks of old iron and discarded bits of four by two. On the door would be the message: ADULTS KEEP OUT UNDER PAIN OF DEATH.

The silverbeet, cabbages and tomatoes, valiantly defying the weeds, would be on the left hand side and on the right we would have the one time woodshed filled with an amazing collection; the two-stroke power mower, the old pushmower now rusted solid, three bicycles, two skateboards, cricket bats, golf clubs, tennis rackets, the old refrigerator, the old wardrobe full of tools and Dad's motorbike which Mum won't let him ride.

Junior's go-kart, which he never wanted to throw out, would be there too. It was made out of a Marchants soft drink box, a hunk of timber off the back fence, and junior steered the pram wheels with a piece of rope.

In the centre of the lawn, little brick patio built all round, we will have Dad's joy; a splendid hand-made brick barbecue, chrome grill, little pegs on which to hang the tools, and a table area for the beer and meat. Recently it was converted to gas.

Japanese, I think, would be fascinated by the Australian apartheid system where the female cooks inside and the male cooks outside.

If they moved into the kitchen I think the Japanese would be delighted by some great old Australian dishes; dim sims, pizza, flake and chips, the lamington, meat pies, Flo Bjelke-Petersen's pumpkin scones and, oh yes, hot crumpets dripping with butter then soaked in honey. What's more I would really like to show them a grand old favourite, the chocolate malted milk like they used to make at Hilliers in the city with a genuine, decent blob of ice cream mixed in.

There would have to be an Australian school classroom, but I would be really interested in giving them some Australian uniforms, particularly those antique uniforms worn by girls at polite schools in Malvern, Hawthorn and elsewhere. I reckon some of those uniforms beat the hell out of the kimono.

I would love to have a mock up of Bay 13. Indeed, now that the MCG is about to pull it down, this might be the chance to preserve it for all time, a priceless piece of our heritage. We could send the Japanese the sessions, say, at 11 a.m., a football session; Collingwood versus the Demons. We could give a loving portrait of typical football fans when only two or three points separated the teams.

However, I think I would prefer a sample of the 4.30 p.m. cricket session. We could depict the Christmas Test against the Brits, that lovely time; the shadows are lengthening on a Saturday afternoon, Dennis Lillee is bowling, you can hear the gentle noise of Fosters Lager cans rolling down the concrete steps and English blood be spilt.

That's right, we would need to illustrate the Australian character, our wonderfully honed attitude to leisure, which would be such an interesting curiosity to the Japanese. We would show our ingenuity, how we find holidays in honour of horse races and agricultural shows and if we plan to celebrate the birthday of a monarch or a day of immense national importance, always we have it on a Monday in order to enhance a weekend.

Indeed we know how to nurture holidays, we give a pay loading for not working, and four to six weeks is a reasonable thing as opposed to two weeks which reigns in most other places. Yes, and we would like to help the Japanese character. Could we have a booth manned perhaps by Norm Gallagher and a few chaps from the various transport unions on how to time and tune a good strike?

Then imagine how intrigued the Japanese would be by our financial system. I wonder if we could find some of those old TV commercials from the Pyramid Building Society or the Estate Mortgage lot? You know those ones, pledging eternal retirement bliss and interest rates of anything up to 18 per cent. I mean in a country where you would be lucky to get four per cent that sort of stuff would have them agog on their tatami mats.

We could have a collection or artifacts discarded by Messrs Skase, Bond, Elliott and Herscu; Falcon jets, 737s, yachts that would have been the envy of Aristotle Onassis, stately homes in England, the South of France and even Toorak.

But my ace item would be the gigantic supermarket. I think I would call it the Koala Privatisation Mart. It would have absolutely everything that was going for sale that week. We would have Telecom, Qantas, Aust Post, TAA, the ABC, Melbourne's Gas & Fuel Corporation, State Government Insurance, and why not Parliament House, Canberra? We don't need it. We've got two.

Indeed, I like to show off our politics, a screening of Question Time in Parliament. And we could let them see the true compassion of our M.P.s. It wouldn't be hard to give them pictures of both Bob Hawke and Wilson Tuckey in tears.

I tell you, my theme park would be so interesting, so lovable, we would have a million Japanese down here on Nippon Qantas in the first two months.
