---
title: What Hope for the Humble Drinking Man
date: 1991-01-01
summary: A defence of the moderate drinker against a new wave of government restriction, arguing that Australian consumption has already fallen sharply and that a proposed police levy on alcohol is punishing the 99.7 per cent who pass the breathalyser for the sake of the tiny minority who do not.
tags:
---

*From the Angelic ranks goes forth a groan,
How long, O Lord, how long?*

Macaulay.

Yes, O Lord, they are at it again. Is there any hope at all for the humble drinking man?

The persecution began with 6 o'clock closing. The wowsers agreed that the drink was the cause of all evil and it would be no trouble at all to defeat the Kaiser if only they closed the hotels at 6 p.m. We defeated the Kaiser but we kept hotels closed for 50 years.

How long, O Lord, how long?

It was a sin for a cafe or a restaurant to have a wine licence. Such establishments which could serve drinks with meals had a curfew. Remember how you would be half way through your Steak Diane then come 8 o'clock they whipped the glasses off the table?

How long, O Lord, how long?

The new refinement was .05. Driving down the Pt Nepean Highway. 'Pull over Driver,' and one had to blow into a little bag.

A further refinement was the dry area at the MCG. A few rowdies were hollering at the game, but we all had to share the blame. Through the Outer there was a two can limit.

How long, O Lord, how long?

We became the most thoroughly taxed drinking nation on earth. We contribute 2.3 billion dollars to the Commonwealth Government every year. But that's not all, there is a further gift of $100 million to the Victorian Government in licence fees. We do so much more than the non drinking classes. More than half of every glass is a kindly contribution to Government.

In 40 years of drinking I think I have contributed at least half a frigate or maybe the wing of an F18.

How long, O Lord, how long?

This week we are being assaulted again. Dr Carmen Lawrence, the new Premier of West Australia, has called for a national ban on advertising. Senator Janet Powell of the Democrats is bringing a bill before the Senate to do just that.

What's more our Victorian Minister for Police and Emergency Services, Mr Sandon, is threatening a levy, a new tax on alcohol, to pay for police and correctional services. He says 40 per cent of assaults and 50 per cent of rapes are alcohol related.

Back in the 19th century our grandparents had the naive belief that if only man would banish alcohol, crime would cease, the gaols would be emptied, lunacy would disappear, husbands would remain faithful to their wives, the enormous wealth spent on drink would be spent on culture, and in the new, splendid dry community, Eldorado would be at hand.

Nothing like that ever happened anywhere, but there are those who still cherish this 19th century dream. It is a strange over-simplification to blame all sin, all road carnage on alcohol. Do the 99 per cent of we who handle alcohol wisely always have to live in guilt for the one per cent who do not know how to use it?

I say 99 per cent because when it comes to those breathalyser tests we have a pretty good record. The police find that 99.7 per cent of us are below the .05 limit.

Just as a humble user I would like to say a word in its defence. From the time I was born I was told alcohol was a sin. My father, against the wishes of his father, did not think so. From the age of 14 on he started giving me the occasional glass of wine. It has been one of the great joys of my life and I don't want to be penalised at every turn by some misguided politician.

At our place every evening come six o'clock we have our glorious ritual. I have a bottle of beer, my wife has a Scotch. We discuss things, the bad things that happened during the day. We discuss the good. It would go just as well on lemonade or barley water, but I doubt it. The alcohol is a relaxing balm and, besides, it tastes better.

Then whenever friends are around we open a bottle of wine. I don't think I have ever extracted a cork without a sense of anticipation and excitement. This is because no vintage is ever the same, no grape is quite like any other, and if you are looking for the extraordinary glory of creation you get it in the magic subtle variations of a glass of riesling, chardonnay or, ah yes, cabernet sauvignon.

There is not the slightest doubt in my mind of the health benefits of a glass of wine every day, and most of my mates in the wine industry seem quite capable of living for ever.

But now the Demon Drink is under assault again, curiously at a time when consumption is going down. Now look here, the demon, if that's what you like to call it, hit its peak in 1975-76. Australians were drinking 9.8 litres a head of pure alcohol. It is down now to 8.7 litres and that is a mighty drop, better than 10 per cent.

Take beer. In 1975-76 we drank 140 litres a head. It is now 110 litres. There used to be a theory beer consumption would never fall, it would only go up, but that is a drop of 21.4 per cent and the big reason why the beer producers shifted their activities overseas. But those figures don't tell anything like the real story. Back in 1975 we had no interest in low alcohol beers and the consumption then was all full strength beer. Now, don't you see, 18 per cent of all our beer is low alcohol.

Wine consumption is down eight per cent, particularly with the large volume cask wines. Yes, there is a change in the way people handle their drink and I see it in the people around me. I am fascinated by the young, particularly the young journalists, They are a most abstemious lot, compared with the rambunctious men of my day. We used to have a group called the Morning Tea Club, and always they would gather in the Back Bar of the Oriental Hotel at 11 a.m.

There was a famous finance writer who had his first beer at Young & Jackson's on his way to the office. Carefully he would time his arrival by train at Flinders Street for 9 a.m. That was when they opened the doors.

Mr Sandon, don't slug us with yet another tax. Could we ask precisely what you are doing with all the money you are peeling off us already? We don't want to pay more for what we consider our humble joy. Nor do the producers want to charge more. You are close now to destroying the wine industry. Half the producers in Victoria are teetering on bankruptcy.

One might ask whether you are concerned about our health or the billion odd dollars that the Victorian Government has let go astray in recent years.

How long, O Lord, how long?
