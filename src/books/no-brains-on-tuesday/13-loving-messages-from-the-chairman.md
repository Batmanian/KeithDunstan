Columns Which Refer to Filthy Lucre

BHP sent us a charming letter in the mail several weeks back. If we did not wish to be bothered by their regular statements just fill in the form etc. Indeed we have had several of these from other companies.

You know why? They can't afford to send them out any more. Not only do they keep sending you obscure letters about their half year progress, their new issues, and the deep mysteries about new taxation arrangements, but their annual reports are so large they are getting like the telephone books. Very soon they will have to start splitting them into two sections.

Our balance of payments situation gets worse and worse, and some economists have given dire predictions for 1991. But please don't worry. I think everything looks marvellous. My personal survey is this. Annual reports are 31.2 per cent bigger than in 1987 and they show a definite upswing in the number of pictures used, 21.7 per cent; and the quality of the colour has never been better. As a company annual report observer, one could say that conditions rarely have been more promising.

Many of them clearly have been inspired by illustrious magazines like *Fortune*, *Vogue* or even *Architectural Digest*, and they drop into the letter box with a heavy clunk.

Now take the National Australia Bank annual report, that was the latest to arrive, an absolutely gorgeous production. Back in the sixties and early seventies annual reports were dull affairs, about as bright as taxation returns. Now the object is to make everybody associated with the company look warm and lovable, like dear friends, particularly the directors. Some of the group pictures have all that warmth and nonchalance they used to show in pictures of Edwardian cricket teams.

The National Australia Bank directors get a full page in superb colour. They are wandering easily down a very elegant marble staircase and I believe it is the head office in Melbourne. First down the stairs is the chairman, Sir Rupert Clarke, he is washing his hands in a Shylock motion and beaming at the wonder of the year's profit. Right behind him is the vice-chairman, Sir Peter Finley, and he looks desperately worried. On the landing is the managing director, N.R. 'Nobby' Clark and he is giving a benevolent parson's smile in the direction of the staff.

I'm concerned though about Mr W.R.M. Irvine. He is pointing down across the Italian marble baluster. What is he pointing at? Could it be a suspect bank robber?

Then all through the report there are colour shots of staff members on location around the globe. They are so artistic National Geographic could not have done better. There's Doug Robertson, National Bank man, deep in frightful traffic in Bangkok. There's Yi Chun Chang amongst bicycles in the great square at Beijing. Hard looking solemn. I think he has just left Mao's tomb. But absolutely the best is National man Michael Xu in Shanghai amongst all his customers writhing round doing tai chi.

Absolutely the most beautiful annual report we received came from the Lend Lease Corporation. On the cover there is an almost surrealist picture of Mr G.J. Dusseldorp. It is all a luscious shade of sapphire blue and there way down in the right hand bottom corner is Mr D, his hands in his pockets, half smiling looking towards the horizon and, obviously, a wonderful future. The paper is parchment quality and the picture I liked was site carpenter, Les Johnson, holding, way up over his head, a stunning ballerina, taken at the National Ballet Centre in Melbourne. You don't get sex like that in every annual report.

The Elders report we enjoyed. That had everything, a picture of the old sailing ship *Alma Doepel*, Australian Rules footballers playing at 'The Fosters Oval' in Surrey, the Foster's Grand Prix in Adelaide and mouth-watering shots of beer produced around the globe. In the make-your-directors-look-friendly stakes I thought they did well. They are in a sort of bush setting, all looking wonderfully relaxed with trees behind them and you suspect they are about to have a very good lunch.

I thought Western Mining disappointing, nothing much to look at beyond pictures of mining, and Mt Isa Mines was no better.

BHP was tasteful. They managed to spread the pictures of their directors across two pages, although Mr Brian Loton was lost in the centrefold. I hope there was nothing symbolic in that. News International was hugely entertaining. We received a report on all the activities at Wapping, including some of the best headlines from the *Sun* and *News of the World*. The one I liked best was Maggie Thatcher's message on the Falklands STICK IT UP YOUR JUNTA and there was a splendid juicy picture of a *Sun* and *News of the World* Bingo girl.

Our National Bank annual report also carried a slip telling us we didn't have to receive it if we didn't want to. But we've voted to keep on the list. Annual reports are improving at a rate — why would you want to spend money on things like *Ita's* new magazine?
