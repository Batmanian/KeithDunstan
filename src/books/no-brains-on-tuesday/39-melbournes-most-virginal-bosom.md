The Princes Bridge Hotel, alias Young & Jackson's, wants to put up an illuminated sign on the Melbourne City Council. It is causing trouble with the Melbourne City Council. At 4.30 pm yesterday I managed to obtain this exclusive interview.

D: Chloe, I wanted to —

Chloe: 'Oui, dare you call me Chloe. If you call me anything, call me Mademoiselle Chloe or Madame Chloe. I deserve names of distinction. I am not an ingenue. I am Chloe, painted by Monsieur Jules Lefebvre.'

D: I beg you to accept my pardon, Madame. I have always loved you and am your greatest admirer. You have the most virginal bosom, the most exquisite skin, the most enchanting haughty look in your eye.

Chloe: 'Mmm, merci. Of course. I am not merely Melbourne's most beautiful tourist attraction. I am notorious throughout ze world. What do people think of when they think of Melbourne? Chloe!'

D: Well, yes. It has been said that Phar Lap went to the Museum, you know. There's Captain Cook's Cottage.

Chloe: 'Phht! You compare me with CAPTAIN COOK'S COTTAGE?! What pictures are there in books. You compare me with ze glorious youth, the delicacy of ze body. I am number one by a 100 kilometres.'

D: All right. Of course I understand that the management is planning to put up an electric sign outside, three and a half metres long, brightly illuminating your... ah... wonderful outline.

Chloe: 'Well, I think there are objections from ze City Council. It is somewhat distracting having a naked female opposite St Paul's Cathedral when people are going in to pray.'

D: Oh, surely a little distraction would be good for the soul.

Chloe: 'Ow dare you! Me distract? What do they wear now, an 'orrible lycra suit on a bicycle in peak hour — zat is what would make people run off ze road. I would be much better. I would give taste, chic, je ne sais quoi.'

D: It is not the first time you have been involved in controversy, is it?

Chloe: 'Non. I 'ad been brought 'ere from Paris in 1883. I was on show at ze National Gallery for ze International Exhibition. And when it was opened, ooh la la, there was a chorus of stupid protest. Nevair to speak of 'er, said one newspaper, without a public figure tingling with shame. But zen I was defended by Monsieur Longstaff and Monsieur McCubbin and they said my divine figure, rather than a corrupting influence, was intensely moral and inspired all ze young men to matrimony.'

D: Well, how did you get here, to Young & Jackson's?

Chloe: 'Sir Thomas Fitzgerald, ze famous surgeon at ze Melbourne Hospital, 'e was one of ze greatest. When 'e died in 1908 'e entry price was 300 guineas. Mr Young, 'e was a very nice, decent man and art collector, not like 'otel people many times. When 'e put me in ze saloon bar, I must say Young & Jackson's became Melbourne's number one drinking 'otel.'

D: You don't think being on the corner of Swanston and Flinders had something to do with it.

Chloe: 'You know nozing. I tell you — there was a soldier, an American, accused of being a spy. 'e could not remember much about Australia. But 'e said: ze only thing I remember about Melbourne was an old building like a railway station, and inside ze old building was a picture of a nude woman. Zats all anyone ever remembers about Melbourne. Me!'

D: That's not all that happened. Didn't someone throw a glass of beer at you?

Chloe: 'Zat was an American serviceman in 1943. Very poor shot. It 'it me up 'ere above ze shoulder. Very put out, I was.'

D: What's it been like, I mean, standing here under glass all these years?

Chloe: 'I tell you. Before we 'ave six o'clock closing I use to show only between nine and six. Then zey bring back late closing. Now — seven days a week I am on show. Very tiring for a 125-year-old.'

D: But you've gone a bit up market, haven't you. You're not in the saloon bar any more. Before you were only with the blokes and diggers downstairs.

Chloe: 'Blokes and diggers?'

D: I mean it was a men only bar.

Chloe: 'Yes, I preferred ze men. Now I 'as all zees women, eating lunch, avocado salads and gins and tonics. Pfui.'

D: I agree, ruined the place. Just so that I could talk to you I bought a Swan Extra, charged $2.30. In the old days you know what they charged, three-pence.

Chloe: 'Are you complaining? You say zats too much, a million francs to talk to me. Allez vous en! Get out!'

So I fled. I warn the Melbourne City Council. They had better let her have her sign. She is a very touchy lady.
