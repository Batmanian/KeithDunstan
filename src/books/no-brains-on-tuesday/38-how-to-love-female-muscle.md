Just looking furtively this way and that, and just not trying to say it too loudly —

I think I am turned on by Martina Navratilova.

Oh I know what you're thinking. When Martina was at Wimbledon this year there was a companion of hers in the grandstand. *Time* magazine described her as 'a great and good friend' or 'constant companion.'

That is not the point. Martina Navratilova symbolises the new breed, the athletic woman. Gone is the anorexic nervy clothes horse of the nineteen seventies.

I saw one at the stage of the Melbourne Regent Hotel just last week and she was modelling swim suits. Can you imagine? Her hip bones were sticking out like pieces of furniture.

I saw the new breed on the walls of a German magazine. They had a whole page photograph of an American female, stripped to the waist, showing a muscle flex. She looked fantastic, breasts round and hard as cheese wheels, biceps bigger and stronger than mine. Wonderful.

I can see the new breed when I am out jogging. Good Lord! It is amazing how big females have become. They have big shoulders, they wear tiny shorts, often in two colours, with hair going long at the back. They stride, and there is a pretty swing of hands dancing from side to side.

All right. You see these girls on the tan going round Melbourne's Botanical Gardens and it is a curious thing: the new female is captivated by this athleticism. There are more females than males.

The way they run is beyond belief. No waddle, they stride. One morning early I was jogging round the Yarra and I tried to match it with one of them, a splendid brunette Boadicea of a woman. Alas, she left me at the speed of a space shuttle. In three years of jogging I have never managed to overtake any of them.

Now Martina Navratilova symbolises all this modern athletic wonder. Admittedly there are some things about her that distress me. I am not sure about her hairstyle. Nor do I approve of her indifferent shorts. If only she wore a little wrap-round skirt.

But sometimes when I need inspiration I go to the photographic library and take down the Navratilova file. And here you find really something.

It shows Martina stretching for a forehand drive. I can see that mighty left arm reaching almost to the camera lens, and her mighty left arm is powerful beyond measure.

Her biceps brachii is a noble mound and when it is truly flexed there is a lovely vein that comes up like a pleasant decoration.

As for her legs, I stand back in adoration. Look at those upper thigh muscles, the splendid adductor magnus, the noble rectus femoris — and look, as you get down around the calves, how could any photographer resist her gastrocnemius or her tibialis anterior.

Hana Mandlikova said some very unkind things about Martina during Wimbledon. One quote comes to mind: 'I don't respect Martina for her achievement. When you see how she looks, my divine figure should be asking the doctors how she got that way.' I think people should be scolded for such remarks.

The truth is that Hana has not worked hard enough at her fitness. She should do more pumping and she might also have a biceps brachii to match Martina's.

There was a time when my ideal female existed between the 16th and 18th centuries. I admired Botticelli nudes — those glorious creatures, the delicacy of those wrists and ankles, the immaculate whiteness.

But the times they are a-changing.
