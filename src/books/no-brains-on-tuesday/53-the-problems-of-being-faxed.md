Aren't you fascinated with the news that Andrew Peacock has a fax machine in the back of his car?

He also has cellular telephones, so he can take a call on the run almost anywhere in Australia.

So the minute Bob Hawke makes a statement Andrew can think up a reply, put it on the fax machine in the car boot and send out the instant press statement, which presumably chats into faxes in newspaper offices, radio stations and such all over.

I feel terribly underprivileged and out of touch that I don't possess a fax. At least three times a week I get a call from some Government department or organisation and the conversation goes like this:

'O.K. We'll put it on the fax to you this afternoon.'

'I don't have a fax.'

(Long shocked silence.) 'YOU DON'T HAVE A FAX!!!'

'No, I haven't got round to one of those.'

'Oh dear. Um. That's our message. Could we fax it up there.'

'Oh, we don't fax our office there.' That's difficult. You can drive down to the Post Office and hand over your local message, which you didn't want to say anything over the phone. What will we do?

There are firms all over the city now, that are so excited, all over the Post Office and hire local post offices which don't use the mail anymore. Give us another five years, the mailman will be totally obsolete and everything will pump information into nothing else but your living room.

Here's a Dunstan 1991 survey to check whether you are electronically aware.

(1) Do you have your own personal fax machine? 25 points.

(2) Do you have a car telephone that is its own? Is it a special line? Ring up anywhere, go anywhere while walking the dog in the park? 20 points.

(3) How about just a plain cordless phone which allows you to take calls anything up to 100 metres from your base phone, while pruning the roses, sea, while dozing by the swimming pool? 10 points.

(4) Do you have an answer phone? 10 points. Is it one of those special phones which has a coded signal, so that privileged callers can dial your secret coded number and go direct to you, wherever you are, Portsea, Antibes or Bali, while all other calls go on to the recorded messages and you decide whether or not you want to call back? 20 points.

(5) Do you have a P.C.? If you don't know what a P.C. is, dock yourself 10 points.

(6) Of course you have a garage with a door that operates by remote control. You have your little black box sitting on your dashboard, you press the button as you turn into the street, and lo, the door opens. 10 points. If you have a remote which also opens the security front gate add another 20.

(7) Talking of security, you do have a horrid little phone system at the gate or door, don't you? And you let people in by pushing a button. 10 points. Burglar alarm system with screening add another 20 points. If you have heat sensors in every room and automatic connection to security service or police, 20 points.

(8) One can only presume that you have a video. 5 points. With remote control 10 points. Then you have a hi-fi system with C.D. You don't know what a C.D. is? All right, back 10 points. Add 10 points. It is one of the latest C.Ds. It has not only remote control, but you can take your pick of any one of half a dozen discs in the machine and pick off any track you wish from any of those discs and even arrange them in the order you want. 20 points.

If you have scored over 250 points you are in the super executive class and I would advise you to keep all your gear in action. If you have scored over 200 you are an outstanding class yuppie. Well then, 150 to 200, you are a normal 1990s victim.

If you haven't scored any points at all, or even magnificently minus 20, I suspect you a very nice civilised human being.
