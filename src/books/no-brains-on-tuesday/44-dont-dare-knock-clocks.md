Columns of Canine and Farmland Wonder

Mr Keating has had it now. I have been very tolerant of his escapades in Parliament, his arrogance over economics, but once he attacks clocks, he's through.

He had what the critics call a tirade about his devotion to clocks the other day. Leo Schofield isn't interested in clocks. Oh no, he's far more than that. He was a connoisseur of the spread much.

'It is a terrible thing to be written off as a clock collector,' said he.

A terrible thing? I don't collect Chippendale, Sheraton, or American colonial. I put it as happening that I collected clocks — little 19th century Americans with names like Ansonia, Waterbury and Seth Thomas.

Actually I am quite mad about clocks. In my collection I have at least three in every room. For a man obsessed with a heart, it is a comfort, it gives a sense of life that is enormously reassuring.

Digital clocks are horrid. They work silently and have those big round faces. There is a terrible inevitability about those digital dials — it's there waiting for the next one to go. Sometimes you can almost count down. Instead of reminding one of life spilling sweetly away, they just sit there waiting for the next digit.

The world is divided into those who love clocks and those who can't stand them. 'No more than two clocks in any room,' she says. 'Fair go.' So I do have a rationing program — carriage clocks, little steeple clocks, all sorts of clocks. It is something superb and admirable about a piece of mechanism which can go on working for hundreds of years.

Then there are the domestic digital clocks. There is one on the stove, one in the television and one in the electric heater. You need only a half-second power interruption from the SEC and they give up. You get blips; the electric heater sits there flashing on the misdemeanour. You know precisely when the SEC had its problem. The digit washout means setting them all again — every complex complication. You have to get out the manual.

It is interesting that the grandchildren are much more interested in the digital than the old fashioned ticking clock. How to set the alarm on a clock is the only trick they need to know. He sets it so that the alarm goes off in a series of hysterical beeps at 3 a.m. Terrible. I'd rather have a dial.

I love to listen to the night. Particularly I love to hear them chiming through the night: three o'clock, four o'clock, five o'clock. You know God is in heaven and all is right with the world.
