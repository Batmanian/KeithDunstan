title: The Achilles Knee

summary: A survey of the epidemic of knee injuries in Australian football, with the argument that Polly Farmer's knee was the biggest news story of 1962, that the cartilage is God's most poorly designed joint, and that there should be a Cartilage Council alongside the Heart Foundation and Anti-Cancer Council.

Every week one of our contemporaries prints a list of all the footballers that have been injured. It goes down almost a full page.

One runs an eye down it very personally. So many names is reminiscent of those huge casualty lists in the newspapers after Gallipoli and the Battle of the Somme.

I have the latest list in front of me now. Among the ailments of these injured men, 21 are knee problems. Without going into details, we have Gary Ablett, Craig Gosling, and others.

It is generally agreed that Polly Farmer's knee was the biggest news story of any kind in 1962. It was photographed repeatedly and zoom cameras came within 5mm of it on television. Teams of specialists descended upon it. Best of all, we followed it through stresses and strains until finally, at the end of the season, it was removed.

It was surprising that the Museum did not seize the opportunity to grab Farmer's cartilage and put it on display, like Phar Lap's Heart.

Apparently God did not do a very good job with the knee. Allow me to parade medical knowledge gained simply from studying sporting news for 40 years. The knee is not an efficient joint. The end of the tibia is like a flat plate; this holds the femur in place. The meniscus is tough gristle, it acts as a cushion between the joints. This cartilage is a piece of tough gristle shaped like a full moon.

Unless you are Polly Farmer, every knee has two of them — the medial and the lateral. Upon injury it becomes like a loose rug. The cartilage can be removed but the greatest danger of all is torn ligaments. Unless treated promptly they can become permanent.

Perhaps all footballers should have their cartilages removed, like tonsils, before being given a kneeworthiness certificate before each season.

Some specialists are saying cartilages are getting smaller and smaller and, on the Darwinian theory, maybe in a few years they will disappear altogether.

Almost inevitably all sportsmen eventually retire because of crook knees. We have an Anti-Cancer Council, a National Heart Foundation, societies to fight asthma. Isn't there a Cartilage Council or a Society for the Saving of Kneecaps?

No question, knees are the biggest worry in sport. The knee is as unfair to the human as the seahorse is to the horse.
