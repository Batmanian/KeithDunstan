Undoubtedly you are aware of the cover-up theory of economics.

In good times skirts tend to go up and limbs are exposed to the view. In bad times skirts go down, and they descend almost to the ankle.

Times indeed must be bad because I have seen some regrettable skirts in Collins Street this past week, girls with skirts so long and baggy they look like Mongolian refugees.

When times are bad nostalgia becomes all the go. We grab at the illusion that almost everything was better yesterday.

If you have been studying your female magazines you will have noticed that the inspiration for the new season bathing costumes has gone back to the nineteen thirties. Last summer not only did we have girls topless at Middle Park and St Kilda, but the bottomless look was, if not in, out.

Girls were displaying bare bums with what appeared to be a G-string.

Now for the coming season avant garde is to wear a costume just like we did when Claudette Colbert and Ginger Rogers were young girls, high at neck, modestly low at the hip, some of the models for 1990 even have the old fashioned short sleeves and legs.

Just to give you a rough time table of what we used to wear in the water, up til World War 1 women went into the water virtually fully clothed. From 1919 to 1932 we favoured the backless costume. The year 1932 saw the Congregational Minister, the Reverend S.H. Cox, say that girls who wore these daring, disgusting, backless costumes were not the kind to uplift the moral tone of the community, nor when married, to teach morals to their children.

The bikini first came to Surfers Paradise in 1955 and from then on everything was downhill so to speak.

So you can see what will happen, if the economy gets any worse backless costumes will go and when interest rates pass 20 per cent presumably girls will be entering the water in full length skirts.

Now gentlemen, I want you to readjust your bi-focals. When you see a beautiful young girl on the beach this summer, topless, or even bottomless, I trust you will avert your gaze, blush even, and mutter to yourself: 'How unfashionable!'
