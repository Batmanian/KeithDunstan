---
title: Are Pitches Human?
date: 1991-01-01
summary: A mock-scientific proposal for a summit chaired by Bob Hawke to design the perfect artificial cricket pitch, prompted by furious complaints that English curators are doctoring pitches against Australia's fast attack at Trent Bridge and Old Trafford.
tags:

---

Our cricket writers haven't accused the Poms of cheating in this Test series, but by heavens, they have gone close.

For example, Rod Nicholson of the *Melbourne Herald* has written that England's fear of the Australian speed attack could cost it the coming Test at Old Trafford. He says the pitch was prepared 'deliberately to blunt the Australian attack of Geoff Lawson and Craig McDermott.'

'The Trent Bridge pitch was brazenly out of character,' says he.

Now we are told that both captains are inspecting the pitch for the coming Test at Old Trafford. The forecast is that the pitch at Manchester will be slower than a one-wheeled bicycle.

I really find it most exciting when our chaps knock up some good old totals, but I get most irritated when Messrs Gooch, Botham and Gower all help themselves to centuries.

But it is wretchedly unfair when we always have this trouble with dumb, tedious, ill-behaving wickets. Should some groundsman, curator in Leeds, Manchester or wherever be able to say: 'Eeze, O'll fix these lads. O'll mi' cop a nice concoction of Clag, molasses and rolled oats.'

The truth is that pitches are fairly unpredictable. If a pitch has bad temper on a certain day almost everything depends on who is going to win the toss.

In recent times I have seen these descriptions of cricket pitches: volatile, responsive, fiery, spirited, uneven, rewarding, quixotic, meaningful and good grief, even a 'Christian' pitch. Some commentators have the suspicion they are human.

Now look here, we are approaching the 21st century. Grass. Miserable, unreliable stuff. Wears faster than paper napkins. Cricket will be the last little cell on earth still using it. Wimbledon too.

Grass is an anachronism for sport, either for running, football or baseball. They have artificial grass in the United States — infinitely more hard-wearing, efficient too.

So why do we persist with turf? The mysterious totally un-British wicket at the Melbourne Cricket Ground almost destroyed Victorian Sheffield Shield cricket in the days of Billy Ponsford. They have dug it up about 15 times and still they don't know what's wrong.

There is a suspicion that the turf at the MCG is sulking. It has never got over the shock of pyjama cricket.

Now my idea is this: we should have a cricket summit. You can't get anywhere now without a summit. Cricket's top dog Bob Hawke could be the chairman. CSIRO, ICI, all the universities, plus some old and reliable characters from Australia's past — Bradman, Lindwall, Richie Benaud and Keith Miller.

We would want them to come up with a pitch. This pitch would be good for the fast bowlers, would have just the right texture so that it would turn for cutters, always good for the off spinners and, hey, particularly good for the leg spinners.

On the other hand I don't want a pitch just for the bowlers. I want a nice pitch for everyone to understand, a pitch that will be a test of skill for everyone.

My pitches will be capable of use again and again. Draws will be a rarity.

Ah yes, the design will be an international standard accepted by England, West Indies, Pakistan, India, Australia, the lot.

We don't want these Poms cooking up their own pitch that's no good for Lawson and McDermott.
