---
title: You Too Can Talk Like a Financial Wizard
date: 1991-01-01
summary: A mock classroom seminar in financial journalism, in which students named Bondy, Elliott, Spalvins, Brierley, A'Court and Murdoch compete to find the most eloquent language for market crashes, corrections and recoveries, culminating in Murdoch attempting a take-over bid for the entire classroom.
tags:
  - Rupert Murdoch
---

You will have noticed that we are perilously near the end of the nineteen eighties. Journals everywhere are preparing their brilliant summings-up of the decade.

One deeply trusts no-one will overlook the surge in interest in money. Back in my day the news stands sported two types of magazines. Those for male interest which always had girls on the cover and those for female interest which always had girls on the cover.

In the eighties there was the astonishing flowering of weeklies and monthlies which devoted themselves entirely to money and to those who were making a lot of it.

Indeed, the biggest difference between the eighties and the seventies this was. Leading money makers became almost as important as footballers and in some cases even surpassed them as beloved personalities on front pages and TV screens.

Nor were the finance writers the dull fellows they used to be. All of us who observed the share market this past week must have been impressed by the eloquence, the excitement and sheer beauty of the language used.

It is something we must not lose. In order to keep up the pace your correspondent has been conducting a seminar for aspiring young money writers. It just so happens that we have a tape of one of the first lessons, which we have transcribed for the public benefit.

Good morning everyone. Today our lesson will be on words. We want to get away from cliches and I want to tell you about all the wonderful words we can use to describe what the market is doing.

Now A'court, you're a smart young man. Tell me, how would you describe a dull week on the exchange.

Sir, normally the important thing is not to make it too obvious that people are losing money. The market fades, it drifts, it slides. It can go off the boil. I think it is a good idea to say there was poor competition.

You are quite right. Easing, too, is a very good word. You can say that the market eased yesterday. This implies that everything is more relaxed and the truth is it's actually a darned sight worse than it was yesterday. Then if you haven't the slightest idea what the market is really doing, you can say it is nervous, it is brittle.

O.K. We will proceed. Last night for homework I announced that there had been a spectacular fall in prices on Wall Street and I asked you to come up with phrases and words that you would use to describe it. Now Bondy, you have been to Perth, you ought to be good at this. Tell me what you have.

Yes Sir, the market dives, it sinks, it crashes, it plunges, it plummets, it goes into a tailspin, investors are mugged by reality and there is slaughter of shareholders.

That's a start but I was looking for something a little more eloquent. Spalvins, how about you?

Oh Sir, I have here: Today was Black Tuesday the most fearful day on the exchange since 1892; frenzied scenes gripped the national markets, in three hours of mad excitement when two and a half billion dollars disappeared down the financial black hole, chalkies had their most desperate day.

I like that very much. Yes, Elliott.

Free fall. I've noticed that since all these balloon disasters, free fall is all the go. And the market has been free falling all the week.

Sir, Sir, Sir.

Yes, Brierley, what is it?

You've got to get blood into it. I have written, there was a huge bloodbath on the national market yesterday as investors rushed to shed their stocks.

Yes, that's good. But you can't keep it up day after day. If you have a crash on Tuesday it is vital to have a different mood on Thursday and Friday. Any ideas on that one?

A'Court: You have to explain that it wasn't such a bloodbath at all. Actually it was just a rationalisation of an overpowered market and it had to come. The market had been unrealistically overheated and it was a necessary shake-out that was good for all of us. Merely a temporary cash flow crisis.

Bondy: Yair, and we're clawing our way back.

Spalvins: And investors are returning, indulging in bargain hunting.

Brierley: Really Sir, there is a special term for it. You know how it is wrong now to say that people go to gaol. They are merely put into a correction facility. That's what we call a market disaster, a financial correction.

My goodness, you are learning fast. What's that Bond. Were you whispering something to young Elliott?

I was just trying to tell him Sir, that you are never broke or ruined or short of money. The fact is that you are highly geared.

Quite so. Quite so. And you must remember that the market is a living thing, it bounces back, it trampolines, it roller-coasters and it shows excessive volatility. And I want you to remember that you will need a new range of words for the boom of 1990.

Murdoch, you're a great boom man, what would you suggest?

Sir, that's easy. The market soars, it sky rockets, it splurges, it jets to unprecedented heights, the Dow Jones goes berserk, there is a floodtide of stocks as brokers go mad with enthusiasm and in some cases, prices go into orbit.

Yes, that's nice. That's nice. Tomorrow I think we will have a lecture on the dollar. Back in the sixties and the seventies neither the pound nor the dollar were ever mentioned. Now it gets a position on the front page and everyone loves it even more than the weather.

How do you find the dollar Bondy?

Sir, it is practically human. I have noticed lately that it is weak, ailing, suffering, it's nervous, close to death, and needs a transfusion of liquidity.

A good draught of Fourex eh? (Hah! Hah! Hah!)

Hey, what's that? Murdoch what are you doing? What's that you are writing down. Give it to me here. Thank-you. I see, you are making an offer, a take-over bid for the entire class room. Well, it's not on. The class is dismissed. I'll see you at this time tomorrow morning and don't forget to do your homework.
