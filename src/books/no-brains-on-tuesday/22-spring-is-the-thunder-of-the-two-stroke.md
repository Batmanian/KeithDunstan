Columns on the Greatest Sport — Cricket

John Keats back around 1820 exclaimed: 'Where are the songs of Spring? Ay, where are they?'

I'll tell you where they are. They're with all the latest power mowers going. There is no chance of hearing a cuckoo's song. It is lost to the damned two-stroke motors.

We don't get bird songs around us so much any more, we get the chain saws.

This has been very nearly the wettest Spring on record. At our place the grass is growing so rapidly you have to be careful when you walk or it will actually push you.

You will notice on the television that the fancy mowers are like the BMWs and Toyotas. And the mowers have the same dreams-of-glory names as the motor cars, names that will give you a feeling of prestige and importance when you get out to do the mowing.

You will notice the Mustang, the Ambassador, the Whirlwind, the Monarch, the Beaver; there is a V.I.P. mower but the most prestigious of all is called the Executive. You get solid state electronic ignition, finger-tip height adjustment and rust-free mowing comfort.

But I get anxious when I see the 1990 ride-ons being test driven on the screen. Clearly my old ride-on is not up to the standard.

I mean, they don't call them ride-ons any more. They are garden tractors and they come with dazzling two-tone paintwork, wrap-around headlights, presumably so that you can go mowing after dinner in the dark; push-button starting, single-touch pedal which controls forward and reverse, disc brakes, hydrostatic transaxle, and five-speed cruise control.

It doesn't stop there. The true gardener now requires a power-operated brush cutter or whipper snipper. You will also need a power-operated hedge cutter, another two-stroke motor, and one could recommend a power-operated spray for working over the roses and the vegetables.

The pure beauty of the wood chipper is that you can become your own home mulcher and turn the entire garden into mulch.

There are devices called Rotahoes and Rototillers. 'Save yourself money, time and effort all the year round.' Your cultivator with its lusty appetite for work will effortlessly work your plants, flower beds and weeds, raking the soil to a fine tilth, digging deep and breaking up everything in sight.

What else do you need? You must appreciate the power-rake. There is the electric powered blower, which blows at 160 mph. They tend to be noisy altogether. In more areas of California they are so noisy they have been banned altogether.

By the time you have all this equipment your machinery shed will be full, you will need to employ a full-time mechanic to keep them in working order, and one day you will decide to put up a HAZCHEM sign outside the gate.

And don't think you won't get your exercise. The two-stroke motor is the most contrary, ever-stalling device. The string-pull starting device — you will develop a right arm so powerful you will be able to star in Rocky 14.
