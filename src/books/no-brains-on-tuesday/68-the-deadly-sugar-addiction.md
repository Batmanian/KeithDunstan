---
title: The Deadly Sugar Addiction
date: 1991-01-01
summary: A confession of two decades of sugar and chocolate addiction, from a devotion to fresh liquorice and black jelly beans through the seductive TV advertising of chocolate bars to a subscription to the American magazine Chocolate News, with its profiles of famous chocoholics and reports on chocolate as an Aztec aphrodisiac.
tags:
  - Dick Smith
---

You appreciate that sugar has had a very hard time lately.

So much so, that the industry has organised its own backlash. There is a television commercial which comes on almost nightly. You see a succulent liquid drop of sugar in the middle of your screen and a soprano voice coos: 'Sugar, it's a natural part of life.'

Last week there was a passionate defence of sugar by Rudi Michelson, assistant secretary of the Confectionery Manufacturers of Australia. He said the US Food and Drug Administration had found it did not interfere with body vitamins; did not play a unique role in obesity; was not an independent risk factor in heart disease and was not addictive.

All this was superbly comforting, except the part about being addictive. We have personal evidence to the contrary. At our house we have been sugarholics and chocoholics for more than 20 years.

In my case it started quite simply. I developed a love for fresh liquorice. There is a shop in the city which features huge coils of gorgeous liquorice which gives off the most divine smell. Irresistible. This developed into a devotion for black jelly beans.

One would keep liquorice or jelly beans in the glove box of the car. Soon one was hooked beyond redemption.

Now for the sugar junkie; the difficult time comes immediately after a good meal. Suddenly the system seems to pant for sugar. They are very clever. All the Confectionery manufacturers hit the prime TV air space between seven and 7.30 p.m. You see gorgeous girls deep throating all sorts of Confectionery bars. Or they have lovely Edwardian country scenes depicting a boy and girl in dappled sunlight walking hand and hand into bucolic splendour and the message is that their love is made possible by a chocolate bar. Then they have a female university student, saucy delightful looking creature, slim waist, and we are told the only thing that is getting her through her studies is a Snicker bar.

After a couple of these messages, I'm gone. I am down to the Seven-Eleven for some Crunchie Bars, a Violet Crumble or I am particularly addicted to a thing called a Bounty, sugary coconut covered in chocolate.

Well, I am not alone in this. Addiction exists everywhere. You can't go into a restaurant now where they don't provide chocolates and sweets with coffee. I belong to a particularly monastic club. The latest lurk is this. Coffee is served in the smoking room. Here they provide chocolate coated scorched almonds, sugared ginger, stuff like that. It is fascinating the way company directors, senior physicians, members of the house of representatives and judges pounce upon them.

Yes, some of the judges who pass sentence daily in court are deeply addicted.

The problem is mounting. The rage all around our district is for shops that make hand made chocolates. Wonderful, they are. We have four of them within walking distance. We have friends who have given up home weaving, wood carving and beachside painting. Now they go in for chocolate making. They have their own special moulds and they sit up late at night devising new wondrous fillings.

So when they come to dinner, no longer do they bring around a bottle of Brown Bros Cabernet Shiraz, you get a bag of home made chocolates, beautifully got up in a white box tied with a pink ribbon. We addicts haven't a chance.

In the United States they take it very seriously indeed. They have a publication called Chocolate News to which I became a subscriber. It comes on chocolate paper and every issue is drenched with the smell of chocolate.

I have learned a great deal from Chocolate News. Every issue they have a profile on some famous chocoholic, like Vincent Price or Woody Allen.

Chocolate News also pointed out that not only does chocolate contain caffeine but it has a mood elevating chemical called phenylethan. This is a pick-me-up. It gives a sense of euphoria. It is one of the reasons they gave soldiers chocolate bars before going into battle.

So when things are really bad and you are suffering from unrequited love or the Bankcard bill has just arrived, you go on a binge with a whopper bar of Cadburys or a kilo of caramel creams.

Chocolate also has a history for being an aphrodisiac. According to Chocolate News the Aztec ruler Montezuma used to fortify himself with 50 cups of chocolate before entering his harem. This explains the sensuous nature of some of the television commercials.

Oh yes, I have seen Monopoly sets made out of chocolate for $600 each. I have seen chocolate chess sets. You can eat your opponent upon victory. There is a place in California where you can buy chocolate business cards, $12.50 for a set of 12. There is a very convenient way of disposing of cards upon receiving them.

Chocolate News reports that you can go on a chocolate lovers tour of Europe; probably beats the hell out of the cathedral tour or the vineyard tour.

But meanwhile, what hope is there for us addicts. Do you think there should be sugar free areas in aeroplanes and restaurants? Do you think there should be a sugar-chocolate ban in the public service?

Oh, we have tried to stop, really we have. You know, like only half a Mars bar each tonight. We have been told too that an apple or an orange immediately after dinner is beneficial. Then immediately after eating the apple, go jogging or a long walk with the dog until the sugar desire disappears.

But the temptation is overpowering. I'm going to write to Dick Smith and ask him if he will start a campaign to get all Confectionery ads off television.
