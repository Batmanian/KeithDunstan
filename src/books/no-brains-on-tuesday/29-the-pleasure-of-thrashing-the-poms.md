It is nice thrashing the Poms. There is a huge deep satisfaction in it, don't you think?

We slaughter everybody, but it is not quite the same with the Indians, the Pakistanis, the Sri Lankans and so on. The Poms are the greatest. We absolutely love to kick them to death.

All through this Test series Alan Border made it clear he was a one-man judge of the whole contest. That included State matches, games up country, plus anything they did in one-day pyjamas. I think even if we played them at ping pong, hop scotch or Monopoly we would want to slaughter them in that too.

You could see Mr Border's intense disappointment when two of the Tests were drawn. We could have swept through both of them. Nor was Bobby Simpson satisfied.

I think I have the same savage atavistic streak as most Australians. I am only half listening when Merv and all the Reids, Alderman and Matthews are carving through the batting. I turn to the cricket notes with only one-tenth of enthusiasm when Dean Jones has gone for a duck.

It is only on those regrettable occasions, when I blush at the thought, that I derive a certain amount of satisfaction from the knowledge that I am not alone. There can be few Australians without some prejudice.

I have always believed this takes place for the reason that children ultimately need to beat their parents. We relish the same thing. It has been going on for a very long time and it was even more important in the old colonial days.

Whenever teams won in England they came home to triumph akin to those offered by Julius Caesar. I think the greatest homecoming was in December 1882 after the famous Ashes Test Match when Spofforth took 14 wickets for 90 runs.

All Melbourne was decorated with Welcome Home banners. Then they had one of the most spectacular processions Melbourne has ever seen. They gathered at the Burke and Wills monument in Collins Street. Then they marched to the MCG, where they had a special display of electric light plus a huge show of fireworks. Every cricketer received a gold medal the size of an old five-shilling piece.

Nor did England take kindly to being thrashed by a colony. The Poms didn't like it then and they don't like it now.

Ted Dexter, who has had a big hand in the selection of MCC touring teams, once wrote in *Wisden*: 'I have on occasions taken a quite unreasonable dislike to Australians. Sorry for telling it publicly, I derive a certain amount of satisfaction from the knowledge that I am not alone amongst English cricketers, highly responsible though they be; there can be few Australians without some prejudice.'

Mr Dexter could even be understating the matter here. I would like to suggest that we move forward a hundred years and just show perhaps a modicum of sophistication and kindness towards our visitors.

Could we perhaps delete 'thrash', 'pulverise', 'murder', 'slaughter', 'decimate', 'destroy' and a few other unsavoury words from our vocabulary?

Yes, I for one feel very guilty over the unsporting way in which we have treated Mr Gooch and his men. Our euphoria had the air of the cannibal just after he had roasted and pickled the visiting missionary.

This doesn't mean to say I would have let Mr Gooch win any of his matches. Certainly not. Perhaps after yet another 100 years.
