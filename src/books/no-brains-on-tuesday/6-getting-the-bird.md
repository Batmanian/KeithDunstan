We need psychological help. We are in the midst of a deep emotional crisis over birds.

At our house we have always been bird lovers. We try to identify every bird that comes on the property. We have the beautiful Gould League bird charts pinned up all round the walls of the spare room. So when a New Holland Honey Eater does his incredible acrobatic stuff hanging upside down to get his feed we have his type exactly identified.

We provide splendid washing facilities for all birds, ornate Grecian bird baths, constantly supplied with fresh water so that everything from rosellas through to magpies can go through their daily ablutions.

We hang ceramic bowls of seed from the branches of the wattle in the hope of inviting every type of thrush, tit, fantail or wren that might come our way.

But the worst has happened. Suddenly birds have become, if not the enemy, creatures which are very faintly popular, like apples and chooks. Now everybody is switching to vineyards.

There is nothing that a parrot, a starling, or silver eye, adore more than a succulent fat bunch of grapes. They like shiraz, they love rhine riesling, they are very partial to pinot noir as a dessert and will fly through an artillery barrage to get to a choice acre of chardonnay. It's unbelievable. A squadron of starlings can go through 200 grapes in half a minute.

I tell you, when you have slaved all the year, pruned, weeded, irrigated, fertilised, trained your vines patiently into exquisite canopies, sprayed against invading weeds, sprayed against caterpillars, sprayed against downy mildew, prayed for sunshine, prayed for rain, your bird devotion dies in a hurry, when the attack squadrons move in and take the lot.

Take John up the road. He was very kindly at first. He and his wife built a gorgeous scarecrow. It was most realistic, known to everybody as Bob Hawke. Bob might have deterred one or two birds back last January, but now they treat him with indifference, even contempt. I have suggested to John they might do better if they replace him with Paul Keating, or even better, Wilson Tuckey.

Finally he became fed up with the ineffective Mr Hawke. He started warfare, every day at dawn and every night at dusk. He has a shotgun and you can buy special cartridges called 'Frife.' First they go off 'Phut', then there is a three second fuse delay, and they go off with an almighty 'Bang' and a cloud of smoke.

Well, last week 'Frife' was failing utterly. At first the birds scooted for their lives. It was a bit like the Toorak folk carting their possums across the Yarra into Hawthorn. John blasted his crows and parrots. They took off to Fred's place up the road. Fred blasted again so they settled on Leon's vineyard. Leon gave them a go and off they went to Reg's place.

Then they returned back to John's vineyard. By last Friday the birds were a complete wake-up. The scare delay before they returned became shorter and shorter, until finally they were like triumphant Spitfire pilots doing victory rolls. Those Frife shells cost a dollar a time.

Of course, if you have a 1000 acres under vine it doesn't matter so much. You have so many grapes you can spare a few for the birds, it's the small growers who suffer. Reg has tried putting up fake hawks on strings attached to tall poles. They sail, swoop and swing across the vineyard. That wheeze works for some of the dumber birds.

A variation of the attack is rocket warfare. He puts rockets in champagne bottles, lights the fuse and lets them go right across the top of the vineyard, so sometimes it is like the last night at Moomba. That too is expensive.

He confesses absolutely the best thing is to get his wife to stand out in the middle of the vineyard and bang saucepan lids. Wives, regrettably, will only do that for a certain length of time.

You can buy a spray which makes the grapes taste disagreeable to the birds. Sadly it also makes the grapes disagreeable to humans, so we have abandoned that. Almost everybody has gone for scare guns. They are gas operated. You set them up in the vineyard and they go off automatically, say, every 10 minutes or so. Our district sometimes sounds like the dawn artillery barrage at Ypres or on the Somme.

*The trouble with that system is the neighbors get upset.* You have to go round and compensate every one of them with a case of wine.

Oh they're crafty (the birds not the neighbors). The blackbirds and those pretty parrots actually hide in the trees until you have walked past. Joe has covered his entire vineyard in a sort of permanent cage. It has cost him a fortune. The trouble is if any birds do get inside they are there for ever.

Most growers are now going for nets. You spread plastic nets over every row of vines. It doesn't deter the little silver eyes. They nick in underneath and you don't even know they are there until the grapes are all gone.

Our neighbor has gone for desperate measures. Not only has he spread nets over all the grapes, his devoted wife, family and friends, have sewn all the nets together underneath, a labour of grape devotion which has taken three weeks.

This week-end, as one bird lover to another, I played a dirty trick. I muffled the telephone and called up:

'It is Fred Avion here, honorary secretary of the Gould League of Bird Lovers. We are making a survey in the district of the birds you have seen and admired this past month. Could I have your comments?'

There was a long pause at the end of the phone, a dreadful silence. Then he said:

'I'd kill all the bastards.'
