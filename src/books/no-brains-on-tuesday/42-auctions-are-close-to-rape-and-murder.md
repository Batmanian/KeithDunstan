Extraordinary things happen in Melbourne as soon as the football season ends and the auction season begins.

Back lawns get mowed for the first time in seven months.

The auction season commences and four out of five houses in the suburb suddenly sprout those auction boards. Coaches get fired. And the Marital Stress Factor climbs towards the sort of territory previously occupied only by finding your spouse in bed with another.

The real estate men told us the house would be on show for six weeks. There would be two public viewing times a week, and other buyers would come through by appointment.

We had lived in our street for eight years and maybe we had met five per cent of the residents. Our auction time we met them all. Some went through our house three times.

'Your kitchen's not up to show condition,' they said. 'For how much is it?'

It depends on what you use your kitchen for. But keeping the place in show condition for six weeks — there is no greater hell.

What are bedroom chairs for? For hanging your outspread clothes over. What are tables for? Instruments for covering assorted shoes, newspapers, library books. Beds are for covering anglets and underpants while you read. What will people think?

'You realise that when they go through the house, they look in the rear cupboards,' said my wife. 'I have always believed in keeping everything properly. You aren't honestly going to leave your anglets and underpants in there?'

Then in the dim early light one morning we read the beautifully typed card outside the house. It made our home sound like the sort of noble residence that Governor Sir Brian Murray may have wished he had lived in.

'This most beautiful Victorian residence, superbly renovated throughout to perfectly blend with all the charm and character of its era.'

It mentioned that parking was provided. It was too. You were not permitted to park in the street.

This board was written by someone who has become a student of the language. I too have become a student and I am not allowed to express myself as a board writer. Here is a glossary of terms:

*Excellent home for young married or investment.*
Translation: Too dear to live in, in a suburb even the map makers don't know about.

*Retention dreams.*
Translation: A nightmare, literally. Don't lean against the weatherboards, you will fall through.

*Ideal purchase for imaginative buyer.*
Translation: You have to use your imagination to overcome the view of the tip.

*Delightful bush setting.*
Translation: There's a colony of possums in the roof.

*Owned until last week by a 90-year-old widow.*
Translation: Nothing has been done to the place since 1936.

*Character and charm.*
Translation: It comes with the dingy old patina that no amount of renovation can quite shift.

I can remember the precise layout of every house I ever lived in. I wander through them in my dreams.

Oh yes. Midge the Corgi was buried over here. Sam the Siamese was interred there. Every morning a little fat willy wagtail would dance by the pool and say hello and the silver eyes would bathe in the bird bath.

I hope the newcomers are kind to Willy.
