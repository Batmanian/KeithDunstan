The tubes are alive with the sound of consumption tax.

John Elliott wants a consumption tax. John Howard almost wants a consumption tax. Andrew Peacock would like a consumption tax when anybody allows him to say it loud enough. John Stone used to want a consumption tax, but now it is great fun not to want it.

A consumption tax really is a percentage tax on every damn thing you buy. Now in my naive innocence I thought that was something we had already. Everything I can think of is taxed and all those things I love passionately, and are not good for me, are taxed abominably.

Taxation in years gone by has been used as a moral weapon to put us on the road to purity. The Government, for example, has always put an outrageous tax on cigarettes, then at the same time heavily subsidised the growing of tobacco.

It has put murderous taxes on beer, then encouraged the production of barley, hops and sugar. It has told us what a wicked thing is gambling, then done everything possible to facilitate the vice as a revenue raiser, by building lovely TABs, running its own lotteries and creating casinos as temples of joy.

Seeing that we have always worked on this principle, let's get on with it. Let us improve our society with just, wise, and truly crafty consumption taxes.

Are we agreed that the vilest thing in our society today, worse than heroin, alcohol, and uranium, is the automobile? Let us put a violent tax on the internal combustion engine. All cars and engines which can produce speeds, say, higher than 80 kmh, receive taxes which go up on an ever mounting curve. All cars and engines will receive a pollution, earth-destroying tax. Each vehicle will be assessed on the vile emissions it puts into the atmosphere and be taxed accordingly.

Indeed a lot of my taxes will be directed at the earth and the future of mankind. We will tax spray cans out of sight. We will tax without mercy those paint spray cans and marking pens, so useful for making graffiti messages on walls. We will put a tax on plastic, on plastic bottles, and particularly the madness of all those plastic bags in supermarkets. There will be a consumption tax on all those horrid hard plastic containers, which seal everything from wood screws and torch bulbs through to mini portions of butter and marmalade. You know the type, you need a tungsten tipped drill to get them open.

Under the present system take-overs are actually encouraged by providing kindly tax advantages. Under NICK, which is short for New Inspired Consumption Kill, we handle take-overs in two ways. First there will be the 25 per cent tax on borrowings, then we will have the 20 per cent tax on the bid. So that when, for example, a Maxwell, a Packer, a Murdoch, or whoever, bids 800 million for the *Age*, $160 million goes to the Government in NICK tax.

NICK will be brutal on noise and brutal on outdoor advertising. We will create a new era of peace and beauty. NICK will attack all devices that amplify musical instruments. The guitar, for example, is particularly atrocious when it is not boosted to 200 decibels. We will tax non-silent motor bikes, noisy chain saws, mowers and over-boosted hi-fis.

As for outdoor advertising, the bigger the sign, naturally the greater the charge. I mean some of the signs which urge us to smoke super milds or gaze upon our most loved newscasters and commentators, could be worth $20,000 each a year for the mutilation they do to the landscape. Minor signs such as you see on the backs of taxis might be a very good deal to the Treasury for only $1000.

Taxation already is severe on the beer and the wine drinker. Yet the biggest rip off in our society today is the diet industry. We have 16 million people in this country. It would be safe to say that at any one time 12 million of them are attempting a diet with a success rate of maybe 0.15 per cent. Diet books now even outsell books on sex, and diet shops under the guise of health food shops are proliferating across the nation. NICK could get into them.

There might even be money in joggers. They too are growing in numbers, particularly the lunch time joggers who have given up business lunches. We could tax their running shoes, their shorts and, by heavens, their track suits. The women at our super market, 40 per cent of them do their shopping in track suits and joggers. Consumption tax there could be very interesting.

There are so many ways NICK could improve our society. Have you noticed under the new let-em-rip system with advertising on television, the better the program the more the commercials? Ye Gods, sometimes you get six commercials in a row. I reckon that when you get more than three in a row, when you get more than a dozen to the hour, then the NICK tax ought to hit hard.

There could be a NICK tax for repeats. The tax for a repeat should rise according to the number of times it has been repeated. For example, the ABC is giving us 'Upstairs Downstairs' yet again. Is this the fourth or the fifth time we have seen it? We could have $200 per station per episode for the first repeat, $1000 for the fifth repeat. Putting on 'Casablanca' again would cost them almost a million. Would you like, too, a body tax? How about $1000 for every creature who perishes on TV, $800 for every king hit? Sex, copulation? Maybe we will let them get away with that. After all, it is supposed to be an act of love.

This is of course just the first approach. The NICK office will be open to all sorts of suggestions. Give us a month or two and I am sure we could get this economy back on its feet.
