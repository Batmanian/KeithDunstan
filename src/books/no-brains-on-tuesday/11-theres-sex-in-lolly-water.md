title: There's Sex in Lolly Water
date: 1991-01-01
summary: A survey of increasingly sexualised soft drink advertising, from Coca-Cola's frenzy and Tina Turner's writhing for Pepsi to spa water ads that promise everything, contrasted against the strict codes preventing beer advertisers from suggesting any pleasurable effect at all, with three proposed scenarios showing how soft drink ads could instead honestly reflect Australian life.

Maybe like me you are becoming disturbed by the soft drink ads.

I was always brought up to believe liquor was dangerous and utterly evil.

My marvellous old headmaster used to say: 'There are three temptations you will encounter in this life — wine, women and motor cars. Any one of these is dangerous, but any two combined is lethal.'

There were all sorts of splendid alternatives, lemonade, raspberry vinegar, creamy soda, ginger beer. When I was young barley water was all the go. Stick with these things — and I often wish I had — the perfect life would unfold.

Well, I think something has gone wrong. The soft drink ads that I see on television do not fill me with — how shall I put it? — with wholesome pure thoughts. They are so redolent with sex they bring me to the edge of the sofa. On occasions I have had to push the dimmer switch on the remote control.

The Coca-Cola ads always show young people in a state of frenzy. Boys and gorgeous young females leaping up and down, dancing, throwing themselves into each others arms. All having such a frenetic good time, you feel it would be dangerous to give young people Coca-Cola in mixed company.

Diet Coke has gone for Australian celebrities, Grant Kenny the Iron Man, John Alexander and Paul McNamee tennis players and of course, Elle Macpherson, the most curvy and enticing of all Australian models. I mean you would leave home and even drink Diet Coke for her.

Pepsi seduces you with the beautiful Kate Cebrano, Robert Palmer sings 'Simply Irresistible' and then they have Tina Turner doing her super sexy writhing, groaning, singing act. Give Tina Turner a Diet Coke and not only does she stay slim but she goes berserk and smoulders.

I can remember, too, one of those Schweppes ads. A very suave young man invites a *soignee* comely young lady to his apartment. You think he is going to show her his etchings but he lights up an entire wall, a library of seductive soft drink and she can choose anything. You tremble excitedly at what is certain to happen next.

I can remember ads for spa water. Yes, just plain old spa water. The voice over keeps whispering 'sexy' and then says: 'Have you ever wanted your spa water to be sexy?' There's a shot of a girl slowly running her tongue about her mouth. Oh boy. The message is the girl who drinks this brand of spa water will do anything.

Some commercials are designed to depict all kinds of macho masculine violence. Have you seen some of those Solo ads. There is one which asks whether you are a Solo man. You see a fellow going through some sort of military daring-do as if he were being trained for under cover work with the Contras in Nicaragua.

He swings on an overhead wire, drops his can of Solo, then with a magnificent leap down into a lake he grabs his can of Solo with all the frenzy of a man needing a fix. It worries me so much I would be almost frightened to touch it.

On the other hand, beer and cigarette ads are associated with decent, honest endeavour. The cigarette ads show chaps out on horses — rugged looking chaps in $100 shirts and wide hats. They have just spent all day rounding up 2000 cattle. And as they look across to the blue-tinged mountains they light a cigarette.

The advertising industry on the other hand has to endure a voluntary code of good behaviour.

The beer people have a problem. They must not have people in their ads that are too young, look under 25 and never, never, never, must there be a suggestion that sex could be the reward for good drink. They can't suggest either financial or social success acquired by drink, nor can alcohol appear at sporting functions or with automobiles and boats.

Although at times I am convinced a glass of this or that has done me a wonderful lot of good. No indeed, the ads must not suggest that alcohol has any wholesome therapeutic qualities.

They can't bring in celebrities. Paul Hogan could promote Foster's in Britain but not here. So what is left? The beer ads tie in with the nobility of work or the spiritual quality of hard-fought endeavour. The theme is that the foaming glass of beer is your reward, you have 'earned it'. Everything is pure decent and manly.

Soft drink ads can get away with almost anything. However I would humbly suggest they have an air of unreality. They ought to identify much more with the life you and I lead.

- You are sitting there in the evening. The air is warm, the smell of jasmine comes from the garden. 'Moonlight becomes you' is lilting from the radio.

'Darling...'

'Not tonight dear. I think I've picked up a terrible wog from the office air conditioning.'

Camera zooms in on miserable, frustrated partner. 'My God, I might as well have a lemon sparkling spa.'

- Or how about this. Scene: Husband in one of those gigantic super markets. His wife has given him a shopping list of 25 items including obscure things like lemon flavoring essence. The super market has few directions, it is a mind destroying maze with everything superbly hidden.

Husband in terrible frustration collides his shopping basket with another, but the understanding female opposing driver says: 'Don't worry dear. All you need is a glass of Mother O'Reilly's Sweet Barley Water.'

- Or maybe this. You wake up in the morning. It's Monday. Another day. The boss hasn't said anything nice to you in years. You have to go to the dentist in your lunch hour. Your pay packet is loaded with so much wage restraint and suffering from so many deductions you are working almost for nothing. The female clerk at the next desk is anything but a sex symbol.

'Aw to hell with it.' You have a Black Label Diet Creamy Soda, get back into bed, close your eyes — and a faint look of happiness flits around your lips.

Cut.
