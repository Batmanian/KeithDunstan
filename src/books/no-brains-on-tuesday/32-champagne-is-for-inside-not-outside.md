---
title: Champagne is for Inside Not Outside
date: 1991-01-01
summary: A proposal for a Society for the Restoration of Dignity in Sport, arguing that champagne hosing (which began with Australian cricketers) should be banned, that fieldsmen who take catches should remain unkissed, and that winning batsmen should shake hands rather than run headlong into each other's arms.
tags:
---

Do you think we could start a Society For The Restoration Of Dignity in Sport?

I am grieved by the sight of sportsmen shaking up bottles of champagne after a race, squirting their thumbs on top and spraying each other down like children with water pistols.

The French have been very trying lately. Their nuclear tests and the whole Rainbow Warrior affair. We decided someone had to do something, so we put a black ban on all French champagne. No Veuve Cliquot Brut. Dear Reader, eventually we cracked. You can only go so far.

It has been said that champagne is the one alcoholic drink a gentleman can take at breakfast. Also the only drink that a lady can drink all day and still remain a lady.

When you go down into those cellars beneath the great champagne houses you are downed by about 33 million bottles, round and oozing maturity. Champagne is made with infinite care and love. The chardonnay and pinot noir is fermented and pressed down to the neck. Then those bottles, every month or so, have to be given a turn so that the sediment collects at the neck. Finally the sediment is disgorged, a fine cork put in and sealed. Some of us think it is the most divine way to make a drink.

So what do they do with it? I believe the disgraceful practice of champagne hosing began with Australian cricketers. They, of course, are rich. Usually they have magnums or jeroboams. It is only right that Formula One drivers should spray the stuff standing beside their quite hideous racing cars.

Now the idea has spread to cricket. I was watching the Australian team celebrating and the champagne was the real thing. They were having a spray orgy with bottles at least $30 each. Our Society for the Restoration of Dignity believes that cricket players should put it inside, not outside.

Now this dreadful wasteful game is spreading like a virus. When Bill Larkin won the Doncaster and Templestowe Council election as Mayor, what did she do? She opened a bottle of champagne and sprayed it all over herself and her supporters.

Champagne spraying will become the practice at all council meetings and at the passing of examination results. Could you see Messrs Bond, Elliott, Packer and Murdoch spraying each other down with appropriate abandon?

We were told it was the first time Australia had regained the Ashes in England when champagne came into its own. There was greater national euphoria over winning those Ashes than anything else in cricket history.

From now on, whenever a bowler takes a wicket, instead of leaping in the air and exchanging indiscriminate embraces with everyone except the umpire, could we not just have a bowler and his captain exchanging respectful bows?

Could fieldsmen who take catches please remain unkissed?

When the batsman has hit the winning run, need Messrs Marsh and Boon run headlong into each other's arms like a long-parted Romeo and Juliet? No. The Society would prefer that they quietly walk up to the wicket and give each other a firm handshake.

I think the practice of cricket kissing has had its effect on the public. Their hysterical behaviour — how do you explain the national response to a very nice, blonde, clean-cut young man who, unquestionably, would be very good to his mother? You would suspect from the headlines he was guilty of terrible crimes, like raping 80-year-old nuns, or writing filthy graffiti.

Come to think of it, the older I get the less I think it matters who wins what game of cricket. The most extraordinary player, no matter what side he is on, bowling or batting with such grace and skill — you just hope he stays there for the rest of the afternoon.

For that sort of a performance, English or Australian, I will pour a glass of champagne and certainly not pour it over my head.
