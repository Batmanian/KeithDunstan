title: Hail to the Umbrageous Coast

Don't you feel a trace of sadness that the era of sun worship is over.

Now when you see a superb sun-bronzed Australian, no longer do you think, 'Ah, here is a member of the climate-blessed race, where else on earth could you get such abundant health?'

Your sun-bronzed Australian could be an old fashioned Digger, a life saver at Bondi, or one of these triathlon characters roaring along the beach at Coolangatta.

Now instead of looking with envy at the beauty of that chestnut skin, you think he or she is one of the four out of five that is doomed to get skin cancer. If I look carefully I can see cancer spots, melanomas and all.

Remember how after World War 2 we all went hatless? It wasn't just convenience. The theory was if only we could get enough sun it would bleach our hair, make us all a more lovely shade of blonde.

Remember too the start of the Christmas holidays, getting down to Anglesea, Lorne or Flinders with the shame of white legs under those shorts, or most shameful of all, a white back and a white chest? The first four days were devoted almost entirely to the careful cultivation of the mandatory brown skin.

Most of our old pleasures have now turned into sins. The innocence, loveliest of them seemed to be sunbathing. The indulgence of lying face down on the sand, feeling the sun's rays on your back, this always seemed even better than getting into bed with an electric blanket. Total and marvellous indolence. Now it is a sin.

For health we go back to the English beauty, utterly white skin with pink cheeks and any display of flesh totally vulgar. See a topless girl on the beach, no longer a surge of passion, just a feeling of sympathy. What will the sun do to her delightful nipples?

But wait, we can make use of this new fear of the sun. I have a plan which can restore Victoria's tourist fortunes.

We have been warned that with the wrecking of the ozone layer eventually ultra violet rays could become so dangerous we might find it wise to sleep during the day and only go out at night.

You will have noticed that on the ABC TV news we now get a daily UV count, a graph which tells us of the menace of the sun's rays during the previous 24 hours. For example, the hours between 11 a.m. and 4 p.m. arch up on the graph a terrible puce colour to show when the danger is extreme.

Until now anything to do with sunshine has been tourist bait. Queensland has exploited it with every means in its power, the Sunshine State, the Sunshine Coast, and every second motel has a title like 'Sunburst,' 'Sunray,' 'Casa del Sol.'

I seem to remember even Alan Bond out west got his start with Sun City at Yanchep.

Sun was the cult. It has been the fashion for those rich people in Toorak, Hawthorn, Camberwell and Brighton to desert our cloud blessed city. As soon as the weather has become chilly in May their Mercedes and Jaguars have headed north like migratory birds seeking warmer climes to breed.

Now, don't you see, they will merely be heading into cancer country.

Take it from me, it is just a matter of time before they will have to build verandas on all those beaches on Queensland's Gold Coast. Perhaps the return of the 19th century bathing machine, the tiny house on wheels which you push into the water.

On your behalf I have been making a study of the Australian Year Book, looking up all the charts. Which is the worst, the most dangerous capital city? Answer: Perth.

Perth gets 108 utterly clear days, 47 cloudy days and a mean average of 7.9 hours of sunshine. Darwin does better with an average of 8.4 hours of sunshine, but then it gets 105 cloudy days. Brisbane, I think, is the most dangerous rival, with only 34 cloudy days and an average of 96.7 hours of sunshine in an entire year. Oh, very dangerous Brisbane.

Now you might ask which capital of all has the safest, most blessed climate? Answer: Melbourne. I have looked at the statistics very carefully and I think Melbourne just edges out Hobart.

I am not quite sure what constitutes a clear day, but we get 48 and Hobart gets 22.1. We both get 132 cloudy days. We get 143 days of rain and Hobart gets 160. We get 18.2 fogs, Hobart gets 5.8. But here's the crunch. Hobart gets an average of 5.8 hours of sun daily, but Melbourne wins out as the most sunless capital with an average of 5.7 hours.

Admittedly even on cloudy days you can be ultra violated to ruin, but surely some cloud cover is helpful? Until now Melbourne has had a dubious record for weather. Not any more. Smart tourists will be looking for the cities, the beaches, with the least sun.

The problem is to find the right title, the sensitive catch phrase which will attract tourists to our Victorian beaches. The phrase has to allude instantly to their lovely sunless character.

The Umbrageous Coast? The Obfuscated Sands? No, such titles sound impressive but people would have to go to their dictionaries. The Shady Coast. Mmm. Not bad. But Shady can mean many things, ghosts for example. Shade can make it attractive, but it might also suggest malpractice. That could also suggest something disgusting, which would be unattractive.

No, I think I have the answer. How about Pale Skins Paradise. I pass over the suggestion free of charge to the Victorian Tourism Authority.

We could even change our number plates from the Garden State to the Paleskin State.
