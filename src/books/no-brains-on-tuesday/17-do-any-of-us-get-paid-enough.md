---
title: Do Any of us Get Paid Enough?
date: 1991-01-01
summary: A column on fair wages, arguing that a Flinders baker, Montsalvat violin and piano makers, leg spin bowlers, teachers, priests and policemen are chronically underpaid while golfers, share brokers, disc jockeys and newspaper columnists are a more interesting question altogether.
tags:
---

Frankly I have a certain sympathy with the airline pilots.

Mr Hawke claimed that flying an aeroplane was easy. He flew one when he was at Oxford. Anyone could do it.

Dear Reader, I always found flying an aeroplane difficult to the point of being unnerving. The biggest problem was returning to earth. Earth was never precisely where I wanted it. Either it came up too quickly — then my machine bounced across the aerodrome like a kangaroo.

Or it came up far too late. Then there was that awesome sinking feeling, descending like a lift in a 60-storey building, climaxing with a resounding crunch on the undercarriage.

The next problem was avoiding getting lost. In an automobile one can stop and ask the way at any service station. When I was flying the only people I could ask were the angels and they never seemed to be available. The best solution was to dive down to a height of 50 feet and read the names of railway stations. This tended to be disturbing for people standing on railway platforms. Nor did one always find out what one wanted to know. There was the time when I climbed away from the railway station with the knowledge that when you took Irish Moss Gum Jubes you could feel them doing you good.

Then came the time during the war when they removed place names on railway stations to confuse the Japanese. You have no idea how it confused mug pilots.

Now I understand pilots don't need railway stations. They have both beacons and computers which are close to black magic. If, say, they are going to Los Angeles, they key in LAX, the computer announces the distance in nautical miles, gives them the course and the aeroplane automatically takes them straight there. But what if the computer breaks down? Or if the luggage door falls off? I never had that trouble.

My ultimate problem was returning home in horrid weather and not being able to see the airport. We always described clouds in the manner of Newman's chocolate. There were soft centre clouds and hard centre clouds. Hard centres were the ones with mountains inside them.

Then sometimes one had passengers aboard. They would have this pathetic look of blue-eyed absolute trust. You did not tell them you did not know where the aerodrome was and you were asking God what to do next.

Yes, flying an aeroplane is a complex and difficult art, but how do you assess the worth of a human to the community? How rare it is for any person, male or female, to receive precisely their correct cash rewards?

For example, we have a baker at Flinders, who makes a dark rye bread in old fashioned wood-fired ovens. Because of him every week-end becomes an over-eating joy. His crusty wholemeal is so rich and splendid you don't need anything else, not even butter. I reckon he is an artist. His bread is the best for several hundred kilometres around and his skill is easily akin to that of an airline pilot. But I doubt if he earns much money.

At Montsalvat in Victoria I met a man who makes grand pianos and another who makes violins and cellos. There would not be a dozen, no, not half a dozen in the world as skilled at their craft. The music that comes from their instruments is sublime. They make enough money to live, no more.

Leg spin bowlers are a rare and wonderful breed. I would double their salaries immediately. They never get the publicity, rarely get much co-operation from their captains. Wretched fast bowlers always win the publicity, the love and the devotion. There are thousands of airline pilots, very few leg spin bowlers.

Take teachers. Teaching, I believe, is the most important task of all. Not only do they have to maintain peace in class, they have to fill in and do all those things inept parents, often indifferent parents, have failed to do. Teachers have to be mentally skilled and inspiring in the classroom, then physically brilliant when instructing on the playground. What percentage of an airline pilot are they worth? By my reckoning on an average you can equate their salaries under one third airline pilot.

How about your local priest? He is your spiritual pilot, there in times of life and death, and with a bit of luck your guide to the hereafter. So far as I know there is no Spiritual Pilots Association of Australia which can organise, threaten strike action or even announce that from now on their services will be provided only from nine to five. One sky pilot by my reckoning has the salary of five spiritual pilots.

What is a policeman worth when you call him in the midst of a bank robbery or an armed assault? Or how about your fireman when he is risking his life by a falling wall or coping with a raging chemical fire?

My wife is utterly irreplaceable, a wonderful gardener, a perfect secretary, a superb nurse, she makes the best pasta and the best pie I ever tasted. She has no award, operates only by direct negotiation, and thank heavens, has never threatened to go on strike. I hope she doesn't read this but if she exerted industrial muscle she could increase her salary ten fold.

On the other hand there are those who make salaries so large they are madly over-paid. Are golfers and tennis players worth a million dollars a year? What would you pay a top jockey? Do share brokers, news readers, disc jockeys, anchor men, and bearded TV commentators deserve all that money?

And how about newspaper columnists? Hey, wait a minute.
