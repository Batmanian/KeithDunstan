From now on it is official. The dear old Oval at Kennington, Surrey is the 'Foster's Oval.'

After the passage of time I have learned to live with the Foster's Caulfield Cup, with the Foster's Melbourne Grand Prix. But just give me a week or two to absorb the Foster's Oval. It is akin, possibly, to Foster's Westminster Abbey and maybe the Foster's House of Commons is not far behind.

You must appreciate the Oval is in the heart of Posh-land. It is within a cover drive of Big Ben. It is within a cab ride of the Houses of Parliament. The Surrey Cricket Club, until C.U.B. and Courage offered them $2.74 million, was in dire poverty.

Practically any suburban Australian football ground has better facilities. The accommodation is lamentable. The roof, on main match days, is the sort that makes you shudder in provincial France. However, every cricketer remembers the glory of playing there. The grass, the turf, is superb.

One time a parishioner asked the late Cardinal John Heenan what he would have done if he were exiled to a desert island and he paused for a moment and said, 'I just hope there is a small English lawn to mow.'

All right, if the Surrey Oval is up there His Eminence will be very happy indeed.

Unlike Australians the English do not profane their cricket grounds with football or tennis. The Oval was used only about 11 times a year. There were the home Surrey games, a Test match or two, and that was it. It was utterly wasted all winter.

There was actually a football match there last year. Foster's sponsored a game between North Melbourne and Carlton. The curator almost swooned with the horror of it all. He said he could not believe that boots would pounce across his cricket pitch.

One would never decry the deep social importance of Foster's but one hopes they appreciate what they are getting. Cricket was played on the Kennington Common from as far back as 1730 and between 1792 and 1815 Surrey played All England. All England trounced them and were so good they even gave their opponents odds.

It was in 1844 that the club found a meadow garden in Kennington. The Oval became famous as the scene of great phenomena of cricket.

Dear Reader, remember that final day, August 29, 1882. England in the final innings needed only 85 to win. Ah, but Spofforth, bowling what we say medium, or slow, was devastating. Even W.G. was out for 2. Lyttelton for 2. Boyle bowling two successive maidens, and several respectable English spectators were actually credited with devouring pieces out of their hats.

Eventually England was all out for 77 and Spofforth took a six-wicket haul. He was chaired off the ground. 'The shouting and cheering that followed was the finest performance I shall remember to my dying day.'

Then there was that fateful week at the Oval in late August 1953 when Len Hutton made 364 out of an England score of 903 for 7 declared.

When Hutton made his record score Neville Cardus wrote these deeply moving words: 'As the ground became resonant with the cheering, the thought occurred to me that it was being heard for miles. People walking down Collins Street would hear it, not only in this country but in Kandy, Calcutta, Allahabad, Penang; they would hear it in the Cocos Islands and some permanent resident in a club in Pall Mall would smile.'

It was precisely 15 years later, in the same area of '53, that we had Laker, Miller, Morris, Archer, Davidson and Harvey.

Suddenly Trueman came in for the first time and many critics didn't think much of him. The august C.B. Fry had said: 'He doesn't even like to bowl.' But Trueman devoured Australia in the first innings then Messrs Lock and Laker completed the job in the second. The joy throughout Britain was so profound it was the equivalent of the Relief of Mafeking during the South African war.

Denis Compton credits Keith Miller with starting the fun in the dressing rooms. The story goes that Lindsay Hassett completed matters by throwing a bottle at the clock. Didn't go down at all well in Pall Mall.

Presumably the clock still needs repairs and, of course, Foster's will attend to that. However, just one thing worries me. Mrs Thatcher is a disturbing influence. She is of the same attitude to Australia as she was to Argentina over the Falkland Islands.

Do you think she will counter-attack? We could finish with the Watney's WACA, the Blue Nun MCC or even the Guinness Gabba.
