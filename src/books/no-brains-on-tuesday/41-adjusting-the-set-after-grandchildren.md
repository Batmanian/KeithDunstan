title: Adjusting the Set after Grandchildren
date: 1991-01-01
summary: Eleven grandchildren depart after the Easter holidays leaving a house full of mementos, a boomerang on the roof, a baby's dummy by the telephone, digital clocks set to strange hours, an Alice in Wonderland video locked on child lock, and a four-year-old's parting observation that the author hasn't got much fur.
tags:
---

They have all gone home after the Easter holidays. Eleven grandchildren were here.

The quiet is sepulchral. I suspect there was a quiet like this in Hiroshima after the Americans dropped the bomb.

I am disconsolate. I need an adjustment period. I wander around the house picking up mementos. Always they leave so much behind.

There is a baby's dummy by the telephone. Amazing how they cannot put the dummy back into vogue. When we had our third child the doctors said: 'Give a child a dummy and its teeth will be damaged.' So we gave it the dentist.

There is a boomerang on the roof. I have no way to get up the ladder and retrieve it until it stops raining.

Tinkertoy, I think, with the possible exception of Meccano, is the best toy that has survived right through from the children's earliest years. You can still build windmills, but the trend here is entirely for space ships. They look exactly like those in Star Wars.

I sit down and Lotte, our little black poodle, jumps into my lap. A few hours ago it would have been a four-year-old granddaughter. I find it marvellous to be adored by a four-year-old female. One or two of them invaded our bed at the very first suggestion of dawn light.

Well, at that hour you try to calm them and suggest that they could have a little doze. So I say: 'Shssssh, we are still asleep.'

This granddaughter replies: 'Don't talk so much, you will wake up my dollies.'

This brings back further memories. Was it her mother? No, of course it wasn't. It was her aunt — back around 1958. There was that dreaded child, so cry at 2 a.m. Mother was already feeding another child. So into the cold, found this child in the kitchen for a glass of water.

'I don't want a drink. I'm not thirsty. Dolly wants a drink.'

There is another indication that the grandchildren have been here. The digital clocks have gone berserk. The microwave is flashing. Our bedside digital clock is behaving very strangely indeed. I know it has an alarm. It went off at 3.37 a.m.

O.K. we have an Alice in Wonderland video tape — very nearly our best keep-them-quiet line. My wife suggested I put it on. The video machine absolutely refused to work. So I cursed! It was barely a year since we bought it. The damned thing will have to go back to the shop to be fixed.

It was a seven-year-old male who stepped forward. 'Give it to me here,' he said. He took the machine, inspected all the buttons, found one down at the bottom and pressed it. Lo, the video started working and Alice came up on the screen.

'There you are,' he said. 'You had it on child lock.'

It's like trying to undo child-proof bottle tops. It takes a child to understand these things.

Computers are the beginning and end of everything. One young man has a computer game called Dark Castle, which is complicated beyond belief.

You put the software in and on comes this castle, complete with lightning flashes and hauteur. The theory is that you are a wart and you have to enter the castle to defeat the wicked black knight who lives there.

It is infested with vultures, dragons, gargoyles, fireballs, rats, whips, burning eyes, mutants and tongue torturers. One nip and you are dead. You get a shield and a bag of 60 rocks with which to protect yourself.

Last night only two minutes after I crossed the drawbridge I died from a tongue-infested rat. On my second attempt I tried a Douglas Fairbanks jump from a castle wall to a rope. I misjudged the gap and fell to my death.

But it is no trouble to the young boys; they rack up thousands of points and smash down mace-wielding torturers, no worries.

They are not as good as we are at making go-karts, fashioning bows and arrows, and building cubby houses in trees. Ah, but they are much better at art works. Our refrigerator and the entire walls of bedroom number three are now covered with paintings and drawings. Our bedroom number three would be the envy of Sid Nolan or Arthur Boyd.

Also, very interesting, their sex education at five is better than ours at 16. What's more, they wander the house with complete freedom. Take yesterday morning. A young man aged four wandered into the bathroom while I was having my shower.

He looked at me for quite some time and commented: 'You haven't got much fur have you?'

That remark will keep me disturbed for at least a fortnight.
