---
title: Would You Kiss a Merv Hughes Moustache?
date: 1991-01-01
summary: A meditation on the invasion of kissing in public life, from Brezhnev bear-hugging Kissinger and Jimmy Carter startling the Queen Mother, to Dennis Lillee starting the hugging fashion in cricket and the thought of kissing Merv Hughes's moustache or Alan Border's beard.
tags:
  - Merv Hughes
---

Yes, I understand why that Alice Springs magistrate complained about kissing, hugging Australian cricketers.

He would have been in deep sympathy with my old headmaster. Headmaster believed it was the tremendous tradition of pluck and duty never to show emotion on the sporting field.

There was the appalling example one year when a mile runner collapsed from exhaustion. A gentleman should never show it. That was being un-British. He should walk quietly from the field, head high.

After this disgrace to the school, headmaster at assembly laid down the rules. Always remain cool under fire. Emotion was the ultimate sin, a sign of weakness.

Now kissing goes on everywhere. People even kiss each other in the midst of church services to show their warm fellowship. As for kissing at railway stations at 8 a.m., virtually eating each other is now all the go.

Mr Brezhnev was a great kisser. In his heyday he not only kissed Dr Kissinger on the lips but embraced him in such a bear hug he lifted him clear off his feet.

President Carter was an outstanding kisser and used to kiss everyone in sight. The Queen Mother was startled when she received a Jimmy Carter kiss. She is quoted as saying 'He is the only man since my dear husband to have had the effrontery to kiss me on the lips.'

Kissing is terribly 'in' on talk and interview shows. When Parkinson or Michael Aspel kiss it reveals that wonderful intimacy and rapport they have with their guests.

All the old stiffness began to disappear during Mr Packer's pyjama game. Hugging and kissing appeared then. It helped TV ratings to have the cricketers in each other's arms.

Dennis Lillee did more than anyone else to promote this new fashion. When a player took a catch all his team-mates would run 20 metres to give him a good hug.

However it was Prince Charles who made it decent and put kissing under royal patronage. Prince Charles embraced his brother then kissed him on the cheek.

The Daily Express commented: 'Has Prince Charles forgotten he was a man?' He has indeed. There is now an avalanche of osculatory extravagance for all sexes and in all places.

Kissing was very different in my day. Fathers did not kiss sons, only daughters. My father-in-law used to complain it was disgusting the way young men were always kissing each other in Rugby Union. That was something males and fathers did very discreetly and certainly never out in the streets.

Time magazine quoted Murray Davis, an eminent Californian sociologist:

'Removed kissing is part of the general inflation of social rituals. We kiss people we used to hug, hug people we used to shake hands with and shake hands with people we used to nod to. Not to kiss everyone else is not relating. Everyone is expected to kiss everyone else.'

Indeed party kissing is now overwhelming. It is compulsory to kiss your hostess when you arrive. Should you or should you not kiss the female guests also?

There is a momentary second of tension. Should you or should you not? Indeed it varies with every social group.

Some hostesses are remarkably kissable. They offer a warm pair of lips awaiting you. If the kiss comes just to the right of the lips with a lingering press on the cheek you know things are pretty good.

But from then on you work further and further into areas of frigidity. I know hostesses who give me that Bass-Strait-in-the-winter look and offer me an area beside the north of the ear hole. Then there are those who dance up and give me the farthest kiss. The whole act is performed so that lips and cheek are 10 millimetres apart.

You can get away with hand kissing. That still is considered gallant. However, if you really want to be the wow of the party, kiss your hostess on both cheeks.

A goal in soccer is such a rare and extravagant event it is natural that players should get excited. But Australian Rules footballers have more style.

I prefer the cool approach of my old headmaster. I don't think I could bear to kiss a Merv Hughes moustache. I'm damned if I'm going to kiss any chap with an Alan Border beard.
