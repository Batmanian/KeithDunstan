The Victorian Football League has had a lovely time this past week handing out Brownlow Medals.

It discovered that back in the days of yore many players tied for the medal. But then they went through a countback system and awarded it to only one man. Now the VFL has had a change of heart and given retroactive Brownlows to all those who missed out. There have been Brownlow Medal parties, reunions and tears.

This naturally must be taken as a precedent. The brilliant ideas of the VFL could apply to every field of activity.

We so had a number of important occasions with very delicate consciences, so now we will have to award a number of belated honours. We had Kerry Packer, who had better things to do with his time, but it so happens that Mr Packer so will have to get his Wilkie in the mail.

The Hawke Government abolished the old system of handing out knighthoods. Australians adore receiving awards. Here are some suggestions — people who should never have missed out:

Dame Susan Renouf for services to marriage.

Sir Norm Gallagher for staging the great battle of the MCG and helping to return the Cain Government to power.

Sir John Cain for services to women. He got women into the inner sanctum of the members betting ring at Flemington and into the most sacred seats of the Long Room at the MCG.

Dame Florence Isobel Bjelke-Petersen for services to cooking and returning scones to their rightful place in the culinary diet.

Mr Derryn Hinch for services to the legal profession and litigation.

Sir Clive Robertson for compassion on television and ability to extract tears from a Prime Minister.

Sir Jack Herbert for telling so much about fellow Queenslanders and keeping up newspaper circulations week after week.

Sir Warwick Fairfax for services to banks and banking.

Sir Robert Hughes for writing 'The Fatal Shore', that first appearing in many Australians the full weight of our convict history.

Sir Germaine Greer for granting her beauty, her discourse and making its qualities known to all across the Commonwealth.

Sir Patrick Cash for services to the language and discourse, and for not letting American players have it all their own way.

Sir Paul Keating for services to antiques and horology as a means of generating publicity.

Sir Russell Hinze in gratitude for defying all dieticians and adding kilos.

Sir Mark 'Jacko' Hudson for services to advertising and television.

Sir Andrew Peacock for services to Telecom and the promotion of the car telephone.

Lord Bill Hayden for his wonderful example in travel and tourism.

Sir Donald Dunstan for breadth of human spirit. After resurrecting Australia from the dark ages, he tried valiantly to do something for Victoria as Tourism director.

As Freddie Trueman once pointed out, Sir Francis Drake was the only bowler ever to get a knighthood. So we could have one for Keith Stackpole. His citation would run: the first opening batsman who thought there was no art to immediately making runs; he just thought about digging in.

Here we have just a few recommendations for knighthoods. I am sure you can think of many more. But what a party we had — almost every dazzling investiture. It would be so brilliant it might even return the Hawke Government.

Then we could quietly say 'Goodbye' to Bob by shifting him to the House of Lords.
