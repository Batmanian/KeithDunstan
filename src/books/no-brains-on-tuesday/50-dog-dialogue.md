Aren't you amazed at organisations which keep conducting research into matters we know already.

At the week-end we learned that the Petcare Information and Advisory Service had called in market research to discover that 80 per cent of Australian dog owners admit to talking to their dogs.

There is only one thing wrong with this figure, it is absurdly modest. I would suggest 99.9 per cent of owners talk to their dogs and presume utterly that they are human. What else is a dog for if you can't talk to it. Your dog is your perfect listener.

All our dogs have been human. Judge, Claude, Basil, Midge, Bessie and Lotte, all human. During the war years my father named dogs after his brother. He was always getting promoted so we had Bombardier, then Sergeant and Major. Dad would have loved a Brigadier but he never got far. But those military dogs were human too.

We have conversations with our Lotte which can go on for 20 minutes. We ask her questions about her day, whether she has noted any good smells.

I won't say we always get answers, but it is not difficult to define Lotte outside, a pathetic, beautiful plea for when she wants to come in.

She can be most eloquent about food. She always believes, seeing she is a human dog, she should be allowed to eat human food, and she says so.

She has a special throaty noise when we have chicken. The throaty noise takes on the tone of a Joan Sutherland aria when we have avocados. She coos also for apples and loves bran muffins.

Her disgust is extreme when we give her that dry carbohydrate dog food. We point out to her that it is recommended by three out of four vets and on television, but that separates the meat, then you see dogs bolting it down as if it were caviar, but that does not make any difference.

We have long conversations too about her sleeping habits. We point out to her firmly that dogs sleep in their own bed and not on our human bed. She has an aria for this too. It is more Barber of Seville.

We are as stern about this as you can be with a poodle, but our resistance is not strong enough to fight the battle when she sneaks up on the bed at 3 a.m.

There is another thing that dogs are aware of: they never let you out of their sight, and they are perfectly literate in English and know precisely what you are saying, and they eavesdrop all the time.
