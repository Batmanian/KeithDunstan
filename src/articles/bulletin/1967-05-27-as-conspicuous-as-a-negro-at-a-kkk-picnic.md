THE WORD FROM London is that male model Bill's measurements are, except for his extraordinary height, similar to David's — 26-33-4, and his hair grown out. He looks best described as emaciated like an Indian fakir. Bill's model received circus-style publicity distributed free by the fans. The model was conscious at all times. Your correspondent found him — he was drinking beer. And there were 70 of the same fans with him. There were at least 7000 fans waiting, and 15 per cent of them female.

Little Stevie Wonder was born, according to the PR team, on May 13, 1948. At 8 stone 7 lb he is the biggest turn-out ever to hit Sydney. The reception here, he said, has been marvellous. In Europe they are quieter, more adult. They listen. They mutter gaps at the end. 'Our biggest problem,' said Little Stevie, 'of all the hotel rooms we come to is we can't even get away from fans. They find 'em full through the fanlight.'

News of the Easybeats was enormous. Flags, balloons, WE LOVE STEVIE messages. There were girls passionate about Little Stevie and there were 7000 fans waiting. Only 20 per cent of them were female.

At Brisbane out in wonder of stopped. The sports fan delivered. Differently was impossible to find. This was a hearing of five great guys. Haven't you got loyalty of you — fans put the lot there. Let's face it, one of them, he shows the show.

Just before the show Little Stevie shook with nervousness. He was near. He took the microphone: 'We are magnified 1000 mega times.' And then he started and went and placed his right hand on someone and extended his hands and went into high-speed electric output of the eight speakers. Nobody ran down the aisle. The music was enormous, impressive. Little Stevie then only did he shake hands with people, and with his fingers extended — I Love You Stevie — shining somewhere out of place.

The patterns also fell on the dancers, on the band, on everything, so all that hit the eye was the shrieking assault of the music.

Excitement built and built. Some tugged at their own hair or put fingers in their own eyes, screaming, and not being able to do anything. Toward the finish there was a general rush from all sides. Police, security men, red-coated chuckers-out, long-armed heroes were everywhere on duty. And as there — women to be fainting, and were straight for the exit, sat on their mother's shoulder, still sobbing.

Just to get the record straight, it seemed wise to send for the St John Ambulance Officer. Bourke Street on a Friday night said they had treated 12 girls for fainting and two had to be taken to St Vincent's. 'I couldn't see anything out of control,' he said. 'This is business. This happens with girls aged 14 to 17. It starts usually when they pull their own hair, hit their heads with their hands, and the next thing they're sitting on the floor. Just a build-up of emotion.'

And your correspondent? As conspicuous as a negro at a KKK picnic.
