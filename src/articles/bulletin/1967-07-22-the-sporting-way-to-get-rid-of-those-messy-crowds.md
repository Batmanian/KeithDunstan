BATMAN, AS EVERYONE knows, is a mighty force for conquering evil. For over a year now your hero has been working on a plan that will eventually be of marvellous assistance to the Football League, the Lawn Tennis Association of Australia, the VRC, the AJC, plus assorted authorities controlling Soccer, swimming, athletics, boxing — you name it.

Talking as one of Australia's leading non-spectators, it seems to me an interesting fact that there is hardly a paying public that is gathering pace along with the growth of population. Actually most of the public are going the other way. After tediously adding up one figure after another, the Batman survey finds that VFL attendance for the first nine rounds in 1967 was 1,346,098; for 1966, 1,402,796; for 1965, 1,379,915; and 1964 — the best year for four — was 1,463,895. We are down 117,795 on 1964, and this is the worst drop in 40 years, since 1904.

Or take racing. A good meeting used to gather 25,000 or 30,000 through the gates. At the six Melbourne meetings in July we have had 162,697 — barely 25,000 average for each. Are you bored? Hang on, there's a message coming eventually.

At the moment all sport is terrified of TV. For example, when the Melbourne Cup is run, the great event is banned from our screens, yet it is shown live in other States. As a typical Melburnian viewer, I feel this is an injustice. I have the same problem with the Davis Cup. When it is played in Melbourne we get the match live; good. But when it is in Sydney, Brisbane, or Adelaide, we get it live from there as well. Frequently one has had the comfort of watching the Davis Cup at a seaside house, martini in hand, blue ocean out the window, on live TV. But they do have, as TV stations are maintained, a whole terrible deal of cheap entertainment.

The answer is television and the doing away with live spectators. If they don't really want to go, so why have them there?

An ideal location for one would be in Melbourne — a football-cricket ground, racetrack, tennis courts, cinder track, swimming pools, the whole works, plus a whole TV studio. Sentimentalists might want crowd reaction dubbed in, but this could alternatively provide great centralisation of industry for the area. There would be coin-in-the-slot systems for all sports, like cable TV for each match, saving the clubs the expense of maintaining grandstands, car parking spaces, and the paying gate.

The various sporting authorities would be happy to co-operate. For some time now the TAB is now providing so much money to the racing clubs they would be only too happy to fall in with the idea. For some time now spectators have been an economic nuisance.

Flemington, provided with lakes and a nice display of shrubs, could make an entirely charming park. One could do the same with Sandown, various stadiums in Melbourne, Sydney, etc. Cities need these open spaces.

There's just one difficulty. First, those who have bought in tooth and nail.
