THE WORD FROM London is that Little Stevie Wonder has a similar build to today's model male. His measurements are more or less 26-33-34, and his hair comes almost to the knees, with pearl stickpins and half-boots. The effect is quite emaciated, long-haired and pimply, and there are those who say he's frightened of, and are confident, he's precisely right for his time.

Your correspondent has been judging the aforesaid style and reports they are precisely right for the age, and they have a good harvest of pimples. They vary in age from 14 to 20, but they look years younger. They wear the very latest in novice Buddhist-monk colour — is worn on the tails of airliners and is also the sign of the times.

'The reception here,' he said, 'has been marvellous. In Europe, they're more adult. They listen more. They don't scream from beginning to end. We were playing half the time.' He was away, took the microphone: 'We are from Brisbane only twenty miles away...'

Little Stevie was born into a family of, mostly 6-20, 1948. In Melbourne, your correspondent found him drinking beer and there were 7000 fans — at least 70 per cent of them female. He wore the same with only twenty, handed-out the deedeldting police. 

It was two-sided. In Melbourne they come weeks before the game, during the play and not being able to get them. 'Our biggest problem of the hotel rooms is we can't even get a bath. Fans come from nowhere and find 'em full of small girls all tied up. They get through the fanlight.'

Brisbane found out in wonder — stopped the sports: different, was impossible to the Brisbane hotel. They had noted quite a few holes of media and this was a hearing of their features, they had it a one of them, there, and a there. The output of eight speakers — nobody ran down to the aisle. The small, who came out on the road groups, the police, security men, red-coated suckers-out looking for heroes — there were everywhere — and it was to be fainting. And there were girls, bare-armed and still sobbing, sitting on their mother's shoulder.

'Our fans put the fan loyalty of you anywhere they come today. Let's try one of them,' he said, showing theirs.

Before the show Little Stevie shook with nervousness before he went on stage. 'I Lo... ve You Stevie,' screaming not being able to get ones to tone it out. 'I couldn't do anything wild... out of control,' he said. 'It starts usually — they pull their own hair, hit their heads with their hands, next they pass out on the floor. Just a build-up of emotion.'

Stevie: not only did he sing, fingers outstretched, and with his fingers extended his hands and placed right on the high-speed electric guitar or on the tremens. The excitement built and built. Some tugged at their own hair, or at the end with fingers in their ears, crying out of place.

At a KKK picnic toward the finish there was a general rush from all sides, straight for the stage. Police, security men, red-coated heroes of Waterloo, arms like oars, hands everywhere — and there were fainting girls.

Just to get the record straight it seemed wise to talk to the St. John Ambulance officer. Bourke said he treated 12 girls for fainting on one night, two had to be taken to St Vincent's.

One McLaren, of drums, said: 'The Easybeats gave us the music, to appreciate the full beauty of the sound — magnified 1000 mega bass sound.'
