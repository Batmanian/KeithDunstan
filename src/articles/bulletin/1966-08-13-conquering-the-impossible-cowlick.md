THE sad truth is that the noble word 'bar' has been shockingly profaned. In the great days of Melbourne we averaged at least 20 bars to the block. We had little hostesses, they were, with hourglass here and free counter lunches. We still have 20 bars to the block, but primarily they're noted for hostesses. The pubs couldn't stand the high rents. The latest addition to the toiletry trade is a 'male toiletry barman.' Just as an aside, do the young ladies who work in male toiletry bars call themselves male toiletry barmaids?

The variety of male cosmetics is remarkable. There is a male bubble bath from Italy which smells divinely of crushed lemons. The blurb reads: 'After a bath or shower you will feel cool, relaxed, alert and clean, smooth, fresh, and scented. Together with a youthful sensation of well-being it will also give you joy of living.'

But wait. The climax of my investigations was at the Aramis Bar at Georges, and there I can best describe it as the Maginot Line for Entre Leather in Australia. The whole layout is the most exclusive of American male barbering. Mrs. Vaughan demonstrated the Aramis Male Skin Conditioner and applied to my hand the Aramis After Shave Splash. Next there was the Aramis marvellous rolling-on male deodorant. There's quite a lot to being a beautiful man these days.

'It's very popular,' said Mrs. Vaughan. 'It really appeals to the Aramis male purchaser.'

Next she brought out the Aramis start-out. This was a skin-coloured product designed for covering up spots on the skin or for covering up after a late night. Your correspondent tried some of that, too.

Finally, your correspondent took out a box of Aramis eye pads. 'The idea,' said Mrs. Vaughan, 'is that if you are disturbed, you sit in your easy chair with pads on your own, and watch TV. It's most effective.'

After all these preparations I would call the Aramis male purchaser lasting, alert, smelling like the very finest restaurants in central Melbourne. Only one thing disturbed your correspondent: I read, in the Aramis marketing pamphlet, that 'Aramis is an exotic root obtained from a plant known as an aphrodisiac.' It's no good to feel like that at 10 a.m.
