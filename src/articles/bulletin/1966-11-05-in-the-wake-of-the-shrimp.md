The year 1965 will always be remembered as the year when a female outranked horses at Flemington. I shall never forget one red-faced, furious owner. 'I went into the ring to see my horse coming back. I couldn't even get near it. You know what was happening? They had the Shrimp in there, judging blasted female get-ups. I reckon the VRC has gone out of its mind.'

But it has to be admitted that females are much better for attracting world-wide publicity than horses. Last year for the Fashions in the Field contest, the VRC had splendid backing. Wool, on behalf of natural fibres, brought out the lovely French model Christine Borge. Dupont, in the opposite corner, brought out Jean Shrimpton on behalf of unnatural fibres.

You know what happened. The Shrimp arrived beautifully groomed, walked across the tarmac. The publicity was tremendous and she remained on front pages for three days. Wool was ahead and Christine said: 'I'd like to see how the Shrimp does next year.' What the Shrimp did next year was arrive with a miniskirt and no hat, no gloves, no stockings. Full of confidence, Dupont opened us to the challenge.

This year Wool, not beaten, came back brilliantly by bringing out Baroness Fiona Thyssen. Not only was she a name of world renown, but she was also practically a local, having been born in New Zealand. Admittedly the Baroness left New Zealand when she was 10 months old, but here in Melbourne we like to keep up a tradition of welcoming back our local talent at Melbourne Cup time.

After the announcement that the new challenger was coming, Melbourne waited eagerly to see what Wool would do. How could they outshine Baroness Thyssen? Rumours were afoot that they would fight back with Princess Ira Fuerstenberg, or maybe even Princess Anne. Alas, this was not to be, and finally they pulled out of the race. However, Dupont snatched its triumph by also bringing over Mrs Peter Young, Tania Verstak, no less.

The Baroness duly arrived and at the press conference at Essendon airport somebody asked her: 'What is your favourite material?' 'Wool,' said she innocently. Perhaps not able to believe it, someone asked again. 'WOOL?' 'Yes,' she said, 'white wool.'

She said later: 'That went down very badly with Dupont.'

But this was the first time I had been confronted by a whole crowd of reporters. I was petrified. There was radio, TV cameras, everything. My mind boggles at the thought of people going through this all the time. I have only been at a press conference once, when I was married. My husband answered the questions that time — in three languages.'

Actually the Baroness speaks four languages herself. English, French, German, and Italian 'not quite so well'. 'In Europe you have to have the choice. You really have to do this.'

The John Batman Inn in Queens Road was delighted to have her, and, as it does for all celebrities, it put out a welcome sign: THE JOHN BATMAN WELCOMES FIONA BARONESS THYSSEN. The Baroness didn't like this. 'Either that comes down or I go. It is in the worst possible taste.'

At 34, to the jaded eyes of a newsman, she is quite, quite lovely — very lean, long slim legs, exquisite hands, and her bone structure is so pretty, but beautiful in the manner that only grows more beautiful. One remembered her particularly from a film when she was depicted as living the life of the millionaire's wife — motor-cars, speedboats, personal jet aircraft, a glorious collection of art treasures — so it was interesting to piece together her life as it is now, two years after the separation.

She lives at St Moritz, one servant, just a few duties. Until she can choose where she wishes to live she feels she is entirely in the right, driving on the right-hand side of the road now and then. 'I was hit by a stupid Englishman. As he dragged me unconscious from the car he said he was trying to teach me a lesson.'

She loves St Moritz and she skis all the winter. 'My daughter, Francesca, 8, skis like a bomb. She'll be right away from me in a year or two. My boy, Lorne, 3, he doesn't ski yet, but he skates beautifully. He learnt when he was two.'

So life now is very quiet. The Baron had three brothers in Germany. Two of the others have taken on the interest. The sad thing, said the Baroness, 'was that he wasn't interested in horses racing. I went to the races in Europe. Now that's finished and his sister has taken over the interest.'

Money? 'I live simply and have enough for everything I want. I like to travel, and if I want to do that, well, I have to do things like this. In the past year or so I have done a job for American Vogue in Arizona, another in Ceylon, and I did a job for Cecil Beaton in Paris.'

The Baron, she said, didn't like it and was most annoyed when she took up modelling again as Baroness Thyssen. 'But I have done tasteful things. There was one picture — I won't show you but it's the loveliest picture ever taken of me. Not lovely me, lovely picture.'

She has arrived here with marvellous clothes. They have come from the Paris houses of Givenchy, Courreges, and Ungaro. The Ungaro clothes are a little bit kooky, she said. 'Not the sort of things you would normally wear, but clothes to wear when being stared at.'

The Baroness is still a brave 34-year-old. She wears her skirts well above the knee, which she assures everyone is still the Paris fashion. When asked whether the mini skirt was finished: 'Definitely no. It is getting shorter and shorter, even to 12 inches above the knee.'
