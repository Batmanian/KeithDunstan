THE THING about male vanity is that it has to be disguised as something else. The moment a man starts to worry about his hairline or the colour at his temples, the elaborate fiction of not caring must be maintained. This is the genius of the new executive men's hairdressing salon that has opened on Collins Street.

It has to be discreet. There's no sign that blares out its purpose. Inside, the decor was that of an all-male club. Actually it's not a club at all — it's a place for actors and services. The walls are covered in pewter and there are Venetian scenes on the wall. There is good beer on the tables. One was met by Mr. Bruce Young, who said the atmosphere here was entirely private.

For men only, and everything so discreet. Well, they had to be. When your correspondent looked around he could see executive hair assignments clearly underway. Young men with frizzy hair and State assignments couldn't quite believe what was happening to them. They had booked in under vague pretexts and here they were, settled into cedar-lined booths, with six weeks of careful colour change quietly underway.

To get Leslie to Wesley — or more to the point, to get grey-haired executives looking somewhat less grey — the process goes in small steps. 'You will notice,' said Mr. Young, 'that the colour is getting more youthful. It's better to be getting more youthful than to be getting more grey.'

Prices were these: razor cut, $2; wave, $2.50; permanent wave, such as straightening, $2 to $6; and a straightening, $6. For the hairpiece assignment, a man could come in and have anything from a touch on the temples to a complete hairpiece fitted. The hairpiece was on a swivel base and attached by castors, so that in the barber's chair one could be turned in all directions.

'Do you ever mention,' your correspondent said, 'or point out that someone needs a hairpiece?' 'Oh no,' said Mr. Young. 'Goodness no.' He was thinking of frightful things. 'We wait until they are ready, until they themselves bring up the subject.' 'Monsieur,' he said, 'we are always available. Come in, have a free consultation. Take away the information, and think about it.'

There were six booths, each a long period away from the 'colour' of their hair, and nobody would notice. 'It's better,' said Mr. Young, 'doing it that way.' He was speaking of change. 'It brings on youth.'

Then there were the cosmetic services for men. First, a massage. This starts with a cleansing cream — astringent — applied across the cheeks and forehead. She then places a muscle-toning process on. Next, to continue toning, there was an electrical gadget — the same sort of gadget the Eastern market has known since the 1880s. 'Electro-vibrating.' It was very soothing.

Then Miss Valda — a pretty little soul — came with a face mask. 'It dries,' she said, 'and gets rid of wrinkles.' After about five minutes the face was tightened, and finally, freshly scrubbed with fingernails, one stepped into Collins Street, never so young.
