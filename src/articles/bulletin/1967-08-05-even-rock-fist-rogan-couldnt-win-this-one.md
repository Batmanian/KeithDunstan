The idea of Greater Melbourne — an amalgamation of the city's many suburban councils into one coherent authority — has been a recurring civic dream since at least 1880. It keeps returning, and it keeps failing.

This time around, the push for amalgamation has again come to grief. The local councils, fiercely protective of their boundaries and their autonomy, have seen off another attempt. Not even a Rock-Fist Rogan — the mythical tough fighter invoked whenever Melburnians need a symbol of stubborn, bludgeoning persistence — could have punched through the wall of entrenched opposition.

The inner suburbs remain islands unto themselves: Fitzroy, Richmond, St. Kilda, Port Melbourne, Collingwood, Abbotsford, Prahran. Each with its own mayor, its own rates, its own sense of proud independence. The Greater Melbourne ideal, which would rationalise all of this into a single powerful city government on the model of London's Greater London Council, remains as elusive as ever.
