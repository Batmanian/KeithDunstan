It is considered very nice to build out in the outer suburbs. We build out in Wantirna, Scoresby, Mulgrave, Glen Waverley, Doncaster, and just anywhere flat where you find about 80 percent of the outer Melbourne subdivisions. It's the popular pastime, almost as big as football.

Now there is something different. 'Appletree Hill,' or better known, perhaps, as Boydtown. Inspecting it by semi-circular tour along the ridge drive from Glen Waverley, the estate sits up on a ridge — a ridge that looks across to Mount Dandenong. Here you see a sign: 'Appletree Hill — The Way to Live.' What brochures! Designed by the Melbourne architect partnership of Romberg and Boyd. Most of it, though, seems to be Robin Boyd.

A distinguished practitioner in his own right, Mr. Boyd is one of Australia's great architectural theorists and has spent much of his adult lifetime campaigning against the featurism and the 'austerica' (the austere, American-style niceness) which pervade the Australian residential scene. He even wrote about it in his book *The Australian Ugliness*, which he proposes architectural solutions. It is planned that Appletree Hill will be the living expression of these solutions — 'a brilliant and satisfying practical demonstration of what hitherto in Australia has been seductive theory.'

He decided Boyd could do with the lot. Two exterior colours, say blue and white, and there will be no front fences. They are trying to get away from a conventional Australian qualification and not trying to be something extraordinary. As the brochure says: 'A careful discipline will be maintained. No garish colours will be permitted. The result is perfect. Nothing assaults the eye. All is harmony. No pop-art, no violent terracotta roofs. All the roofs that get their red from the sky.'

Two exterior colours: charcoal grey and white, and there will be a certain class distinction in Boyd. Four bedrooms, two bathrooms, double garage and in the centre a large courtyard decorated with white stones on bushes and such. At the back the house looks into this glassed-in area, at maybe on a 100-degree Melbourne summer evening you wheel up to the TV, and all is serenity.

It is not exactly cheap housing. The prices of the three- and four-bedroomed brick veneer houses with land, plus a number of extras, range from $16,500 to $21,000. There are now five houses available for inspection. They are built, as we expect them all to be built, around a little cul-de-sac with grass and trees in the centre. Every house is angled on its block so that it is not offensive to its neighbour, and every house gets a share of that morning sun.

The kitchens look like the best hotels, always with beautiful tones like the pale golden-browns, or good beige matched with natural wood cupboards — none of the traditional antiseptic white.

Indeed, just occasionally, the heart leaps for an outbreak of sand-blasted glass doors decorated with sailboats, a Genoa salami hung up the wall, or maybe a china gnome in the garden. All this is magnificent and beauty done well. Yet before taking up membership in Boydtown you would have to decide whether you could fit into the Boyd mould. The front gardens are all landscaped and the back gardens often pretty small. There is a place for everything, correctly filed in its position. The toolshed is a cupboard with a clip and position for every piece including motor mower and garden bags. If you don't put it back where it belongs you've had it.

One wonders how you managed if you wanted to, say, build a boat, breed a pig, or put in a treehouse for the kids. Yet, if you get a chance, drive out to Appletree Hill. The big hope is that this will start a new era out in the outer suburbs. As the brochure says: 'The children will have a playground available for the kiddies.' And Mr. Boyd would have been furious.
