One of the great tragedies of our beloved city is that we have never had a symbol, a sort of civic thumbprint which would signify 'This is Melbourne.' Sydney has its Harbour Bridge. Paris has its Eiffel Light statue, but in Melbourne we have nothing. Occasionally we use an idyllic picture taken from Alexandra Gardens depicting a clump of spires and insurance palaces reflected in the waters of the Yarra. But help one, it's a bit like putting on a boomerang and plastic rings as the important seat of government.

Already some people are taking pictures of the proposed bridge superimposed over the Sydney Harbour Bridge. Speaking utterly prejudicially, our bridge seems to have a distinct edge. Let us have some vulgar comparisons. Our main span will be 1072 feet. Sydney's is 1600 feet. Our height above water will be 176 feet. Sydney's is 172 feet. The length of the Sydney bridge deck is 3000 feet, ours will be approximately 2000 feet. The Sydney bridge has eight lanes, each 9ft. On wider courses ours will have eight lanes, 12 ft each.

On cost, the Sydney bridge cost £9,577,507. Now considering the difference in prices between 1932 and 1966, you might suggest that this figure was rather more than our projected cost of more than $32 million. Yarra and Kings Bridges direct to Denong. There's no hope of the bridge being open before late 1971 and I'm sure that by that time they can push up the price to $40 million at the very least.

Actually, we have been talking about this bridge now for years. We talked for more than 50 years on whether we should have any bridge at all. The idea is to make a new crossing of the Lower Yarra near Williamstown Road. This will open up the area between Melbourne and the West — Altona, Laverton, Footscray, et cetera — and Laverton. Already the ferry, which this spot carries 1500 vehicles a day, has delays of up to 15 minutes for each vehicle. Our fine new bridge will mean 30,000 cars a day within seven years.

A former Lord Mayor, Sir Bernard Evans, has told us that the bridge will bring all the area from Point Cook to Altona within 15 minutes of the city. Before you get too romantic about it, you should be advised that this area of Port Phillip is a bit short of scenic splendour.

Yes, Mr. R. F. Vian, the authority's chief design officer, will make a pretty fair thumbprint, one that will be visible from quite a few points over Melbourne.

Of course, what has held up progress in Victoria on new roads and bridges is the terror of the toll. For this we might have had something better than the goat track that we call the Tullamarine freeway. Back in the days of yore in Wales, in the early 19th century, there were riots over the toll gates. The leader was dressed as a woman, calling herself 'Rebecca,' taken from Genesis: 'Let thy seed possess the gates of those which hate them.' Rebecca, in female garb, with her lieutenants in female gowns, used to make long marches in the night, then with the blowing of horns and firing of guns descend on a toll house and burn it to the ground. Certainly things were done in a more colourful manner in the last century, although the Sydney Harbour Bridge in fairly recent times has the episode of the mock opening by Colonel de Groot to boast about.

Just like the Harbour Bridge, the Lower Yarra Bridge will have most probably 20 toll booths. The suggested tariff at the moment is 20 cents for cars and 30 cents for trucks.

Whether this will cause any further Rebecca Riots remains to be seen. Certainly it is much easier these days to find female impersonators than it was in 1843.

To get on to the Geelong Road, for example, the driver has to pass through 59 intersections and 11 traffic lights. With the bridge starting from Spencer Street and Dudley Street there will be none. Eventually when St Kilda Junction is redesigned one will be able to drive in a splendid sweep from Geelong via the Lower Yarra Bridge road all the way from Geelong Road. Test work for the foundations is under way and actual construction begins in 1967.
