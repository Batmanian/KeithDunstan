Imagine 36 kilometres through some of the most beautiful country in Australia, safe for children, pedestrians, horses and bicycles - and completely free of motor vehicles.

The Lilydale-Warburton Rail Trail was completed this week and will be opened by the Minister for Sport, Tom Reynolds, on Sunday 8 November.

The rail trail movement is gathering momentum in Australia. Victoria has 700 kilometres of former railway line that can be converted into trails. So far, 120 kilometres has been transformed.

On 16 October, the Great Southern Trail, comprising eight kilometres of track from Leongatha to Koonwarra, was opened. Work is continuing and, eventually, the trail will wind its way 93 kilometres over several spectacular trestle bridges to Yarrum.

The 30-kilometre East Gippsland Trail, from Bairnsdale to Bruthen, is expected to reach Orbost by 2000. The O'Keefe Trail, from Bendigo to Axedale, now has 13 kilometres open with another six due for completion early next year. Other trails have been opened at Myrtleford, on the Bellarine Peninsula, Moe, Ballarat and Mirboo North.

The Government is spending $1.75 million on rail trails over three years with additional funds from local shires and communities.

The jewel in the crown is the Warburton-Lilydale Trail. Riders can start at either end, but Warburton offers a better chance of a downhill run. The hills are gentle with the gradient never more than one in 20. Riders who start from Lilydale enjoy splendid views of the mountains, particularly the dark mass of Mount Donna Buang.

The Warburton-Lilydale line opened in 1901 to serve local fruit growers and market gardeners. It was closed by Henry Bolte in 1964 and all the rails immediately removed. As a result the space became a dump for everything from old cars and car engines to broken refrigerators and bottles. Blackberry and thistle grew freely.

The State Government provided $207,229 for the trail, the Shire of Lilydale $35,000 and $36,000 came from other community contributions. The track surface is impacted gravel over old railway ballast. The trail has 13 bridges, 11 of which required extensive restoration work. The bridge over Woori Yallock Creek is 175 metres long, great fun to cross on a bicycle. Another, over Forest Road, is a lofty affair, appearing to climb into the sky.

The ride is an adventure in nature. Cyclists pass strawberry farms, vineyards, market gardens and orchards, but mostly the trail is through bush with the branches forming a serene canopy.

The trail is the result of a remarkable community effort. The Friends of the Warburton Trail helped cleared blackberries, restored old sidings and conducted a planting program along the length of the track.

The local fire brigade also helped and farmers were remarkably co-operative. Many lost grazing land and right-of-way that had been virtually theirs for more than 30 years. Some helped to build bridges, erect fences and even provide seats along the way.

David Heale, senior project officer for Rail Trails in Victoria, believes the trails have tremendous tourist potential and will quickly pay for themselves.
