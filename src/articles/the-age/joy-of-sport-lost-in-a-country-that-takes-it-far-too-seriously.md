MAYBE you didn't see the headline, which covered the whole front page of our tabloid contemporary. If not, glance to the right.

A disgrace! Surely not. They weren't convicted of cheating, lying, betting, or stealing the takings. But we are treating the agony of the Ashes as a national calamity.

So one wonders, has there been a bigger Christmas disaster? Cyclone Tracy, the 2004 tsunami, the Bogle murders, or the odd bushfire?

The unfortunate Ricky Ponting used to be everybody's hero. Now we see pictures of Ricky shoulders down, miserable, desperation etched all over his face. Will we put him in the stocks, banish him to Christmas Island? The letter writers want him sacked at once.

We have no time for losers. Australians are only there to win, win, win. Oh dear, we do take sport seriously.

Henry Lawson wrote: "In a land where sport is sacred, where the labourer is God. You must pander to the people, make a hero of a clod."

D. H. Lawrence thought the same. He wrote that we played sport as if our lives depended on it. Nothing else really mattered.

Now I have always adored cricket, particularly Test cricket, because it is so slow, so delightfully boring. Now everything is being speeded up, telephones, automobiles, 20-over cricket. We are spending billions on broadband so that things will go faster.

Not Test cricket. It goes for five days and you can doze off in the sun, quietly waiting for the next thing to happen, and it might not happen for three or four hours. So it has never occurred to me that it matters who wins. It is the beauty and culture of the game that is important.

After all, it is only sport.

We have been told that Ponting's men did not triumph in the second Test because they were too politically correct, not enough sledging, not enough fiery abuse of their opponents. That was Percy Cerutty's theory. The human body thrives best on "heat and cold, love and hate". Remember how nice it was when Pat Rafter occasionally gave a clap to a good shot by his opponent.

Now here is a theory regarding sport. Sport lovers are so single-minded that if their national team is not winning, their interest in the game drops to zero. A prime example is the Davis Cup. Our team is struggling just to get into the act. The successes of winning teams such as the Spaniards, the French and Russians only make tiny print in the back of the sporting section. Who won the Davis Cup this year? Don't know, really. If we had won, the news would have covered the first four pages and every player would have received his Order of Australia from the Governor-General.

We spend millions of dollars in the hunt for Olympic gold medals. Say we win eight, 10 or 20, it won't make the news except here in Australia. There is one headline I have always treasured. It appeared in London's Daily Telegraph concerning a skier: "Injured Divina is 18th despite wrist in plaster". Who won? It doesn't matter. She wasn't British.

When you play sport with a determination and manners that border on murder, it is war and your opponents are the enemy. The only people who enjoy triumphant bliss are those in the winning country.

Now here is a good idea. Let's set up several touring sides in various sports. Let them all be charming men and women. We could send them to places where our international relations are rather tricky, Africa, Indonesia, Malaysia, China, and, of course, make sure that when we play cricket against Pakistan, India and Sri Lanka we are not overaggressive.

Just think, when we are defeated by India, we will give joy to about a billion people.

I will suggest this at once to Julia and Kevin.

But there's no problem with the Poms. We are providing Christmas bliss for them already.
