FRANKLY, I couldn't live without my morning fix of a daily newspaper. In the old days we had a newsagent who delivered the paper tightly rolled in plastic. He could throw it so that the paper landed right on our door mat.

But he could do better than that. Our neighbour left his bedroom window open and our newsagent could throw the paper up through his firstfloor bedroom window and land it right on his bed. One morning he forgot to open his window and... crash!

Our paper is delivered every day at 6am. I sneak out in my pyjamas and take it back to bed. Bliss! I spread it all out over the blankets, none of this awful tabloid stuff. It is good to have five or six stories all on the page. Then I pick out bits, first the weather up the top right-hand corner. Down to the bottom to read the Odd Spot and a careful check to see if Tandberg has a mini cartoon. I make sure not to read the front page headlines. They're always too tough, too awful for a chap at 6am. Maybe after a cup of tea.

Then I nose into sport. I like to see if Sam has won a tennis match. I like to see what Ricky Ponting and Michael Clarke are up to. Are they still trying to retire Ricky? Actually, it is hard to find him because he is hidden behind 15 pages of football. Next I look at the cartoon and read the letters. The letters are always marvellous, particularly the short pithy ones on the righthand side, the more offensive the better. Then it is the obituaries and I find out if any of my mates are still alive.

Finally one turns to the front page, but occasionally one forgets. I walk down the street and a friend says: "How about that? Did you see that the Cardinal has run off with a nun?"

"Noooo, I didn't."

"What's up with you? It was on the front page."

The point is, the daily newspaper is vital. I like the feel of it, I like to smell the ink, I like all its compartments. Our household is not that old-fashioned. We bought an iPad the minute they came out and we also bought a Kindle. They are superb for downloading books or playing Scrabble, but they haven't the quality of a newspaper.

My problem is this. It is in my DNA. My father worked for the Herald & Weekly Times Ltd. The Herald rolled off the presses every day at noon. He used to wander down from his office and watch the Goss presses move into action with their thundering roar. He claimed the roar was the most thrilling noise he ever knew, better than a Beethoven symphony.

Newspapers died. The Argus died, The Star died, Newsday died, The Herald died, and more recently the wonderful Bulletin. If ink is in your DNA, then the death of a newspaper is akin to the death of a child. We used to live in an East Malvern flat. The lady upstairs had Alzheimer's disease. Every afternoon she used to wait out in the street at 4 o'clock. She would be there for half an hour.

"What are you waiting for?"

"My Herald hasn't come."

"You won't get a Herald, it stopped publishing 10 years ago."

Yet I understand her plight. I feel the same.

But I have faith. I believe the daily newspaper in your hand is as essential as breathing. There are plenty of others like me. Even if Rupert is claiming newspapers will be gone by 2020. I don't. I reckon The Age will survive all this and still be here.
