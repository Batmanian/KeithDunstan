DE COURCY LUCK WAS APPRECIATED From KEITH DUNSTAN T ONDON, May 31 . — l-- Jim de Courcy lifted his green cap and showed his curly hair to the Oxford crowd and they roared, Gil Langley ran over and jut his arm around his shoulders. De Courcy's lucky break lad come at last, with a smashing, delightful century. If ever there were doubts ibout De Courcy, he dis missed those doubts yester 3ay. He is a batsman of slass. There have been two un lucky, unhappy men on this tour — De Courcy and Colin McDonald. De Courcy got his break, but Colin's has yet to come. Yesterday he went for a duck to a ball that swung a little further than he ex pected. McDonald still cannot fathom English wickets. He has tried everything, from brash, vigorous openings to blow away his lack of con fidence, to slow, carelul play. But always he has been un lucky. Every tour has its unlucky man. Last time it was South Australian Ron Hamence, and now its McDonald. ? However, he is not com plaining. He is not that kind. After his duck yester day, when he must' have been feeling low, he laughed and said, 'Know any good jobs for an opening bats man?' Report, Page 9; latest scores, Stop Press
