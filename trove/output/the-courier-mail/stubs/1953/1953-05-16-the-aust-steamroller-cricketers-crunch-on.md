THE AUST STEAMROLLER CRICKETERS CRUNCH ON From KEITH DUNSTAN CAMBRIDGE, May 15— A local Cambridge news paper wrote: 'These Australians; they're soft-spoken, they're gentle; why are they so nerce on the field?' And they are fierce. This Australian steamroller, which crunched its way over one county after another, did not let up for an instant for this boyish Cambridge side. For Lindwall and Archer there was the same in timidating eight-man death trap behind the wicket. There was no frivolity; there were no half -measures. Once they even had a little fielding practice between batsmen. Now: 'Some work' All this power ,with even the weather playing dirty tricks, was too ' much for Cambridge, and they col lapsed twice in a day. When play finished soon after 5 pm yesterday, a Cambridge physics professor remarked: 'Now I think we all will be able to do some work.' So now there's no cricket to-day— and to-morrow we face the MCC. Archer hurt— Pag* 8
