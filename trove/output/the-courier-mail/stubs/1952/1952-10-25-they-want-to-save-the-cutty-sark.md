Yet even though the brilliant brasswork had turned to green, she still had the atmosphere, the breeding, the lines of one of the most beautiful things ever made by man. The Cutty, the ultimate achievement in sailing ships, was built in 1869, of the best teak money ' could buy, and her designer went bankrupt in the making of her. He designed her for the tea trade, between London and Singapore; he made her lean and low, to be the fastest ship in the world. She was that all right, but it was on the wool run to Aus tralia she made her name. I wandered along the maindeck and opened a door of the deck house. The home of the appren tices, and what a miserable little hole! Two bunks in a cupboard, nothing more, and the bottom bunk always a foot deep in water, they say. when a blow, was on. He kept a pair of prize col lies on board which he used to exhibit in shows at Sydney and Melbourne, and on calm days he would mount his trlnod and take photographs of everything In sight. He knew the exact amount of canvas he could put on without uprooting the masts. He created the record of 370 miles in a day.
