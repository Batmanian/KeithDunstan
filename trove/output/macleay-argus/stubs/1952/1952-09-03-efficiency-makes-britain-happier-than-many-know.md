EFFICIENCY MAKES BRITAIN HAPPIER THAN MANY KNOW There is no need to feel sorry for the Bri tish Ib many ways they are better off than Am ericans, said Keith Dunstan on his arrived in Lon don from New York. When we decided to leave New York for London our American friends shook their heads sadly. "Do you think you ought to go?" they said. "What about the poor baby —the awful food? How will you live — no car, no washing machine?" . "They were very kind, but they couldn't understand why anyone should voluntarily leave comfortable America and tney maae our trip souiia as; rugged as the Kon Tiki expedition. They warned us about so many things we really began to believe England must be a land of starving, wretched people. We expected to find . the shops empty and dull, noth ing to buy. But everywhere, even in the country, the shops are full of good things, wash ing machines, television sets, pressure cookers, electric mix ers, ' fresh fruit, imported cheeses and Continental wines. Before we left New York we stocked up on toys for the baby, but the English toys are better and cheaper. We bought plastic raincoats; kit- chen gadgets, slick potato peelers and can openers, but the London shops have all these things. rpHE CARS were a shock. London is jammed with dazzling Austin Sheerlines, Mark VII Jaguars, smooth black Bentleys, dashing little Triumphs and long delicious sports cars, so low they bare ly rise .to your knee-cap. I have asked many people: "How - can an over-taxed people afford cars like these?" A common, answer: "Oh, they belong to the Embassies and tourists." That is nonsense. The cars I have seen have been filled with English families and it is impossible to mistake an English family for any other. They, have had so much austerity preached to them and they: have" read so much about rthe good things to be had in other countries that they fail to appreciate the qualities of their own country. "POR EXAMPLE, the London transport system is in comparably the best, the most efficient I have seen. , „ Trains and buses run to the minute, so that it is the sim plest matter to get to the most remote part of this sprawling city. The buses swarm to the bus stops, and it is even possible to sit down at the peak hours. The- bus conductors won't let more, than five people stand. What a change from New York, where the crush at 550 is suffocating. It is impos sible to lean, and one is sus pended above the floor like an olive in a well-packed bottle. But most things are done efficiently here. All the trades men deliver. No questions about it—meat, milk, grocer- iesj !beer, fish and ice-cream. The housewife doesn't have to move from her door.
