Clattering along at 30 miles an hour, I admired the paddy fields, rivers, rubber plantations and the heavy-timbered jungle where the trees met high overhead; forming a dark, leafy tunnel for the train. We were travelling at full speed when the natives screamed for the train to stop. ' They were wise, for they started their yelling a mile before their ; stopping place.' The train takes half a mile to even slow down. The driver ,'piit on his' brakes' arid slowly the train lost speed until it was doing about eight: miles an hour. They shouted again and the train slowed down to a crawl. The driver- didn't stop completely In case he could not start again. Men, women and children jumped from the mov ing train, then ' running along side. dragged their luggage from the carriage. I saw one woman throw her naked baby out of the window for her husband to catch below. He caught it. It took a quarter of a mile to do this, 'while all the time the driver, terrified the jeep was going to stail, was shouting: "'Have you finished?" ... "Have you finished?" It took five hours to reach Beaufort, where we were to change trains for Tenom the next morning. Tenom is a beau tiful village, high up In a valley. 90 miles Inland. This time we had one carriage, two jeeps and a crew of coolies whom; I pre sumed, were for additional power. They .were ... Before we had gone far, rain started. While it was dry; the Jeeps had been straining to climb the hills. Now, In the rain, all eight wheels Just spun on the greasy rails. The coolies hopped out to sprinkle sand on the rails, and again we pushed, with water streaming off our bodies. The coolies couldn't sit inside, four having to sit on the bonnet of each jeep. I didn't envy them their job. sitting precariously on those bonnets, shivering with cold, and the train threatening to leave the rails at any moment. Charging downhill at 30 miles an hour, we swung round a sharp turn to be confronted by a land slide, barely 50 yards ahead. The driver applied full brakes until the wheels stopped spinning, and shoved the jeep hard into re verse. It made little difference, the train kept going ahead with the wheels spinning in the op posite direction. We crashed into the landslide and climbed a pile of rocks. Natives, fish, bags and men were thrown everywhere, but nobody was hurt. It took an hour to get the train moving again. We continued the mad journey to crash into two trees, splash through a flood with water running through the floor boards, and to finally arrive at Tenom with only two. casualties. An English major at' Tenom was quite unimpressed by our story. He just said, "You should have been nere when we had Aus tralian drivers.. They used to push their Jeeps along at 50 miles an hour." For once I was glad that I was not with Australians.
