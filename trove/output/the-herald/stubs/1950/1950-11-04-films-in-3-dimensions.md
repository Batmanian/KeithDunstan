Films in 3 dimensions Keith Dunstan's Hollywood New york.— al films which will make you feel that you are almost in the arms of Elizabeth Taylor or perhaps the cockpit of a 600 m.p.n. dive-bomber have now been perfected in America. The process is called Cin erama and according to the makers the screen comes to life and the motion picture seems a living actual event as if you were there on the spot beside the camera man. Maybe this is going too far for some people. Not everybody wants to be In the arms of Elizabeth Tay lor or the cockpit of a 600 mp.h. bomber, but there Is no doubt Cinerama will bring changes in the motion picture industry as big as sound did 20-odd years ago. The Cinerama people say that if the film were about surfing you would feel you were aboard that kicking, bouncing surfboard with the spray hitting you in the face. Or say it was about a naval action, maybe you would really experience the sensations of standing beside the 16-inch guns of the U.S.S. Missouri as they hurled shells into North Korea. For Cinerama, , theatres will need a curved screen with three projectors work ing simultaneously and the sound will come from six microphones operating into one amplifier. Studios when they take the films will need three-eyed cameras and six sound tracks to pick up the sound. The idea of all the sound equipment is that you will hear exactly the same noise as if you were on the scene, Whether inside an aeroplane or, say, half a mile away. The curved screen gives the audience a range of vision 180 degrees more than normal — with an amazing depth. . Naturally the costs of switch ing over to this new process will be enormous. For an in dividual theatre they would vary from £5000 to £20,000.
