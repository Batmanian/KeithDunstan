U.S. LOSING FAITH IN ITS NYLONS By KEITH DUNSTAN NEW YORK. — For months now word has been whispered around the U.S. that nylon stockings aren't what they used to be. Some women have even ut tered the heresy that they would rather go back to old-fashioned silk. One woman complained: . "I'm fed up. I. spend a fortune every week keeping my legs covered. Why these wretched nylons don't stand up to anything. "If I even cross my legs or bend over, it's suicide. You can't tell me they are as strong as they used to be. It's these manufac turers, that's what it is. They tell you nylon thread Is strong enough to pull a battleship, but they make it weaker and weaker all the time. Those are strong words which call for investigation. Good House keeping magazine in New York decided to do just this, v Tlicy interviewed 582 women and found that the average wear from a pair of nylons, washed out after every use, was only 21 days. Good Housekeeping discovered that the manufacturers had not been playing tricks, but that women themselves had been causing the trouble. When nylons first appeared they were tough, but during the past few years women had been de manding a sheerer and sheerer stocking until they now were more wispy than a spider's web. NYLONS for sheer elegance — but U.S. women say they are becoming too sheer. " 1
