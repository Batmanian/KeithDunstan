WATCH THESE NEW STARS SHINE HOLLYWOOD - from KEITH DUNSTAN NEW YORK. — Hollywood is digging deep, scrap ing to the bottom of every talent bin, trying to find the stars who will replace the near- elderly ladies and gentlemen who "packed 'em in" before the war. Clark Gable, Barbara Stanwyck, Spencer Tracy, Marlene Dietrich, Gary Cooper, Bette Davis and Claudette Colbert are fine actors and actresses and people still love to see them. But they are moving into specialised character parts and after all they can't go on for ever. It is foolish to expect women in their mid-forties to play the parts of loving young debu tantes. Joan Crawford and Marlene Dietrich are still good to look at, but their beauty has acquired a cold hard qual ity like polished Jade. The job of replacing these people is not easy. A pretty face, a smooth leg, or features . like a Greek statue are not enough. They must have a certain amount of acting abil ity and a somewhat unusual fascinating type of person ality which fits in with the fads of the day. Studios are now picking up most of their talent from the stage, television and radio. Of course one still hears the odd publicity story about the fam ous director who found the rare exotic beauty hiding her talents behind a milk bar. However, these stones are rarer now. (It always aston ished me how these directors ever got into milk bars In the first place.) ? 1 THE most obvious of the young stars rushing In to fill the places of the great are Elizabeth Taylor, Jane Russell, Kirk Douglas, Montgomery Clift, Jeanne Craine and Richard Todd, the good look ing young English ex-com- , mando and paratrooper. ' Macdonald Carey with his : fashionable crew cut is an other. His fists are tough, too, , and he can lay the villains on their backs with all the aplomb of a Bogart or Cagney. Shelley Winters Is a young lady who has taken over the role of Deanna Durbln as first lady at Universal. In recent pictures she has played the role of a sleazy bar-room blonde who would give the boys the back of her hand at the drop of a hat. She has an impudent ex pression, and has already made it felt that she wants to run the works round at Universal. Jane Greer, described as the type of girl who would shat ter a glass eye at 50 paces, is the most promising on the RKO lot, but she has held up her career somewhat during the last two .years by having > two babies. Robert Ryan, wno Is 6ft. 2in., and starring with Claudette Colbert in "Secret Fury," is a godsend. He looks meaner, nastier, and more horrible than James Mason ever did. Already, the women love him. MGM's Howard Keel looks like another Gary Cooper— a great, striding colossus of 6ft. 4in. But instead of drawling he sings. He played the lead In the- London production of "Okla homa." His first film was with Betty Hutton in "Annie Get Your Gun," and now he Is making a film with Esther Williams in Hawaii. Warner Brothers are hank ing. on Ruth Roman to take the place left empty by the departure of Bette Davis. They say she is such a good actress she can cry out of one eye — the -one nearest the camera. She worked her way up from the slums of Boston, and she carries an expression sultry enough to make Errol Flynn's hair stand on end. ' JANE GREER . . . "she would shatter a glass eye at 50 paces."
