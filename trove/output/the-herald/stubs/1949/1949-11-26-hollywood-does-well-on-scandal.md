HOLLYWOOD DOES WELL ON SCANDAL from KEITH DUNSTAN of The Herald's New York Staff NEW YORK. — Nothing seems to hurt irrepressible Hollywood. For months now press agents have been trying to cover up for the shocking scandals that keep turning irom me city, uven mougn it may sound like tipping the scales of justice the wrong way, bad publicity is good publicity also. Headlines rarely hurt the box office. Bob Mitchum, for example, now has more drawing power than before he hit the head lines. Cynics are saying that publicity men should stop describing Hollywood as a town filled with normal people and admit that it's the best side-show on earth. Here are a few shockers: A Hollywood store is now sell ing "Bogart Panda" bears. Hair stylists say that pink hair Is the thing for milady. A Paris film critic has chal lenged film producer Walter Wagner to a duel for ques tioning his ability after he had said what he thought about "Joan of Arc." Poor Betty Hutton had to go from Paramount to M.G.M. to star in "Annie Get Your Gun," and now she's homesick. It is the first time she has been away from the Paramount studio for 11 years. She misses her wardrobe girl, her make up man, cameramen, and everybody. One night she rang up the chief make-up man at Para mount and asked wouldn't he come out to M.G.M. — 10 miles from Paramount — every morn ing and make her up himself, please — at 6 a.m.! Her old colleague miserably replied that he was a departmental head and couldn't do such things. Betty hung up in tears. Even cowboys have to move with the times. The latest westerns, instead of showing rugged prospectors panning for gold, feature cowsboys hunting for uran ium. Roy Rogers and Gene Autry appear in such scenes in their latest films. Ellen Drew has changed the color of her hair for the third time this year. For her lead with Vincent Price in "The Baron of Arizona" she has it coal black. Barbara Stanwyck, who has appeared opposite almost every Betty Hutton Dan Dailey important male star during her distinguished film career, will be teamed with Clark Gable for the first, time in M.G.M.'s "To Please a Lady." It will be a comedy, and she will play the part of a newspaper columnist. She uses her column to heckle Gable, a dashing racing car driver. This means, of course, that they fall in love. Dan Dailey, like Montgom ery Clift, has catapulted to the top, and he is the pin-up boy of every bobby-soxer in Amer ica. However, he is giving 20th Century Fox more trouble than they dare to admit. He has no ambition. He does not care about being famous, and he has no interest in money. He is warm, agreeable, casual and relaxed at all times. The studio executives find that un usual also. Dailey made his name in "Mother Wore Tights." when he was cast opposite Betty Grable. As for Montgomery Clift. this young star, who makes more money than most people in Hollywood, arrived at the premiere of his film "The Heiress" in a hired dinner suit. Few people believed this at first, but now it has been con firmed. Furthermore, until a few weeks ago, he possessed only one suit, and tne studio had to speak frankly to him to make him buy another. The bobby-soxers wouldn't care if he went round in sack-cloth and moccasins. Joan Crawford is still in the drama. Her new film, "The Victim." has a terrific ending David Brian beats her to death
