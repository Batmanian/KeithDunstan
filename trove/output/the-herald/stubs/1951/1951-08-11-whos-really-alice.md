Who's really Alice? TWO charming little Alices in Wonderland are here in New York tearing at each other's throats. Walt Disney thought he had an open field with a three million dollar, gorgeous, animated cartoon. Then in came an English "Alice" by Leo Bunin just as splashy and just as ingenious. Walt Disney was furious.. He had spent five years, in making this film and he refused to have his market destroyed. So he launched an attack through the law courts. But Leo Bunin was just as adamant. He too had spent five years in making his film. He beat Disney into New York by two days and he hired three theatres for his Alice instead of Disney's one. They both bought up expensive newspaper soace and ran advertisements such as these: "Don't be deceived, ours Is the true Alice." . . . "There is one Disney - there is only one Alice." Bunin then offered admission to all children for one quarter (25 ' cents), which, to a New Yorker, is the equivalent of offering theatre admission for six pence. - The two Alices are com pletely different. Mr Bunin decided that as Alice was real he would present her as a real live girl, and, as the animals were imagin ary he would present them as puppets. Carol Marsh played the part of Alice and the music was by the Royal Philharmonic. Dis ney, of course, used animated cartoons. So far Disney's "Alice" has had the better reviews and is making the most money. The two Alice's will now presumbly proceed to fight it out in city after city all round the world.
