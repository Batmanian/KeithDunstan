[?] from a Neapolitan cafe to the Metropolitan Opera House. In all the fuss and commo tion it is hard to gauge how good Mr Lanza is. He has suddenly become one of Ame rica's most popular singers without any great build-up or publicity. R. C. A. Victor has now sold more than a million copies of his record "Be My Love." Although his Caruso film will not be showing until next week, Victor has already sold 100,000 sets of arias Trom it. So far he has associated himself chiefly with Holly wood. yet he claims his am bition is to sing at the Metro politan Opera House. He could have done this years ago, but he says he is not ready. He believes it takes 10 years to train a really great voice. He started singing seriously at 20 and he is now £9. That means he has one year to go. Caruso actually took 15. and he did not make his big debut until he was 33. Mario intends to sing at La Scala in October. Then he will set his sights on the Met. He has had so much wild - praise such as "the greatest singer this century," it might be a good idea to give you this sober piece of criticism from the New York Times: "It is a bit unfair to Mr Lanza to compare him with Caruso, but this seems to be intended. The young man has a beautiful, rich voice. It Is of fairly thick texture, plenty of power arid adequate range, although the singer has to work hard for some of his notes, and allows his voice to go back in his throat on others. "At the present time he sings on the surface, as it were, consequently without much resonance or expres sion." MARIO LANZA
