TIGHTENING THE BELT [?] NEW YORK, — Hollywood is in bad shape. Swimming pools are being drained, two Beverly Hills drug stores admit a sharp increase in the sale of. sleeping pills, and tables at Romanoff's are empty. The Un-American Activities Committee is mauling such stars as Judy Holllday and Jose Ferrer as alleged Com munist sympathisers. Larry Parks, Sterling Hayden, Rich ard Collins and Marc Law rence. who admitted their Communist past, are now all out -of work with no prospects In sight. Warner Brothers have gone through their staff and sacked everybody that could be spared. Half their New York publicity staff received notice last week. Yet 20th Century Fox did an unheard of thing. The direc tors cut the salaries of 132 executives. 25 to 65 per cent.— enough to make an annual saving of 2,500,000 dollars, This Is the first time In Hollywood's history salaries have been cut from the top down. It's customary to start with the run-of-the-mill studio hacks. Now all the major companies admit if they are to survive the alarming in crease In darkened theatres all executives must accept salary cuts.. So' belts are being nulled In everywhere. One 1250 dollar a week producer complained: "I can't live on what I get now. How do they expect me to take a salary cut?" Fox's president Spyros Skouras said the average cost of a feature film in 1947 was 2.400,000 dollars, for 1951 it was 1,430,000, and for 1952 re leases it wouid be 1,230,000. That's despite the tremendous inflation we have had during the past four years. RKO's star producer, Jerry Wald. said: "The motion pic ture industry Is scaring itself to death, ana unless we get rid of the prevailing fear of in security, the fear of what l.o do. we are all lost and we will end up trying to catch up with the men who used to be in the vaudeville business.
