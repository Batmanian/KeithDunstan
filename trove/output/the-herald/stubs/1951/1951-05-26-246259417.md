Also, for weird climes where there is no air or sound, he must have oxygen gear and a walkie-talkie so that he can talk to his mates. But the space and science boom is not just kid stuff. Hollywood is busy turning out heavy, morbid, blood-curdling material for the adults. George Pal started the fashion last year with "Destination Moon," and now everybody is getting into the swim. Michael Rennie plays the part of a gentleman from an other planet in "The Day the Earth Stood Still." He lands his space ship in Washington to tell earth folk they are rushing headlong towards de- struction (as if they didn't know already!). Then there are a heap of titles such as "W hen Worlds Collide," "Planet X" and "To the Centre of the- Earth." Perhaps the most spectacu lar of these films is "The Thing," the story of an odd visitor from another planet. Howard Hawks, the producer, chalked on pavements and re leased "catchy" publicity about it for months, always keeping a secret the exact form of "The Tiling." Time Magazine has let out the secret. Time said: "The Tiling is a ferocious vegetable, eight feet tall, delivered on a flying saucer from another world. It bleeds green, howls like an aggravated banshee, multiplies by dropping seeds into the earth, it thinks like Einstein, looks like Franken stein's monster and thrives on a diet of human blood." You can't wait? Never mind, "The Thing" will be in Mel bourne in a month or so.
