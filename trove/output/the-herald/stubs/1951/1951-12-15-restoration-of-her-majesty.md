At cocktail parties the guests' often' rose to their feet when Mary Pickford en tered the room. Mary was Queen in Hollywood but in the rest of the U.S. they called her "America's Sweetheart" or "Our Mary." Tp those people over 50 her return will bring back memories of the silent era. Mary is now 58. Her hair is still blonde and still naturally wavy. For her age she Is an incredibly good looking woman. But now, after 19 years' absence from the screen, she didn't have to come back. She is worth more than four mil lion dollars, and she has never ceased to be one of the most prominent execu tives in Hollywood. MARY PICKFORD TODAY. Mary Pickford was born in Toronto with th,e plain name of Mary Smith. She started acting on the stage at 4, and she made her first film at 14. Like Shir ley Temple she was the perfect child actress, but, In addition, she had a shrewd business brain. Within three years she was making 10.000 dollars a week, plus 50 per cent, of the profits. In another year she had her own com pany. In 1919 she fell in love with Douglas Fairbanks Sr., one of the most fabulous Americans of his day. They both got divorces and mar- ried in 1920. Their six- week honeymoon outdid any of the films they had made. They were entertained by Royal Families and Prime Ministers all over Europe. A corps of publicity men preceded them and they attracted crowds wherever they went. It was claimed to be the most publicised honeymoon in history. In London Mr Fairbanks sat on a balcony of the Ritz while thousands cheered in the street below. In Venice they went for a moonlight trip in a gon dola, trailed by a bargeload of cameramen, and Fair banks scaled the side of an American warship in the harbor. Their romance was de- cribed as something of "perfect beauty." It was reported that Douglas never left Mary for more than 24 hours. They de fied all social etiquette, ignored all seating ar rangements, to be side by side at formal dinner parties. No other woman's lip ever touched the lips of Douglas Fairbanks.
