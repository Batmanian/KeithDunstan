WICKET COVER RULE I SPOILED TEST | From KEITH DUNSTAN TRENT BRIDGE, Tues. — The frus- tration, anticlimax and disappointment of one of cricket's most thrilling Test matches being ruined by rain has caused some people to ask: Why not wait for sunshine and play the game to a finish? But the official view is I this: | All touring sides work to a tight schedule of cricket six days a week. ; County matches may be unimportant . to Aus tralia, but they arc im portant to England and they can't be cancelled. Counties wait excitedly for years for their big Aus tralia match. If rain in a Test caused their matches to be can celled they would lose the big money from high- ' priced seats. Their fin ances, already in a poor state, would be ruined. The answer seems to be to drop this English rule of just covering the ends, and to cover the whole of the pitch. But every time sugges tions to cover the wickets come up, English cricket dicliards complain bitterly. They say the Australians cannot match the skill of Englishmen on wet wickets and they believe to cover wickets completely would kill one age-old excitement of the game when a bowler at last has his chance for the kill.
