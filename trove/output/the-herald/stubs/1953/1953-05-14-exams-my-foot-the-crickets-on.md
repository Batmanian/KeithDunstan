EXAMS, MY FOOT! THE CRICKET'S ON From Keith Dunstan CAMBRIDGE, Wed. — Final exams were only a fort night off, but nobody cared. Miller was out, Harvey was out, and Cambridge were on top. From the lecture halls the students dashed down to the old ground at Fenner's. True, they brought with them a few copies of the Iliad and the Odyssey. They were solid protec tion agaihst the damp grass. At Fenner's — named after its founder. F. P. Fen- ner, it is the custom to squat on the grass under the shady chestnuts. Fenners is really one great, soft, green lawn. (The story goes that an American once ashed a groundsman why Cam bridge lawns were so lush and perfect. The groundsman re plied: "Well, you just cuts 'em, waters 'em and rolls 'em, and waters 'em and rolls 'em and cuts 'em — for hundreds of years.") The students rushed to Fenner's In the hope of seeing Australia all out cheaply. But they hadn't reckon- ed on the power of our bowlers Davidson, Archer, even Jack Hill. Alan Davidson was the boy to see. He got 71. He made 50 in 35 minutes. He was so excited that he couldn't stand still. As the bowler came in he would beat his bat up and down In the relish of the moment. Then he would stride down the pitch, catch the ball on the half volley and send it to the boundary.
