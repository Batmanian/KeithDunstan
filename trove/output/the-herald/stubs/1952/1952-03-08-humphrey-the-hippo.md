Humphrey the hippo | KEITH DUNSTAN'S HOLLYWOOD ! NEW YORK. — Even at the Stork Club, dressed in a white tie, Humphrey Bogart looks like a battle-beaten marine, but, believe me when vou see him -in the African iunale with a week's growth of beard, sucking at a bottle of gin, all in color, he's a nightmare. HE'S pretty, weird, too, when y o u see him carrying on like a hippopotamus and giving a classic imitation of a monkey. There's no doubt about it. He is in his ele ment in his new picture, "The African Queen." A British and an Ameri can company put up the capital to make it in the Belgian Congo. A first- rate idea, for in England they think it's an English picture and in the U.S. they think it's American, so, naturally enough, it is doing extraordinary busi ness on both sides of the Atlantic- The critic of the London Daily Express confessed to tears, and the man on the New York Morning Tele graph said: "It was a rousing, rip-roaring whale of a movie." Bogie and Katherine Hepburn (Katie as he calls her) went out to Africa last -year, their lives care fully insured for two mil lion dollars each. Lauren Bacall, Bogie's young wife, went along for the trip. So with an English crew they travelled 6000 miles through Africa,- and had a pretty uncomfortable time. Everybody became sick. They had to put up with malaria, bugs, leeches, filthy \yater, mildewed clothes and the terrible heat. Once they were so short of water they had to run the generator on Coca-Cola But It was all worth it. Their story was a beauty. C. S. Forester, of "Horn- blower" fame, wrote "The African Queen." It is a tale about, a boozy, bawdy, clown of a Canadian (Bo gart) and a prim, straight- raced sister of a missionary (Hepburn). Unexpectedly, they get caught up in the First World War, and together they sail a wheezy steam launch hundreds of miles down an uncharted river through dangerous rapids, past dangerous forts, dan gerous crocodiles, elephants and lions. Undaunted by this, they sail to the mouth of the river, turn their boat into a great torpedo, and blow up a German gunboat. Then they fall in love. Katie, the missionary's sis ter, loses all her inhibitions — Bogie never had any — and' there are some very funny, witty scenes. The producers thought the film so good . they rushed it into one Los Afigelesrrthfeatre' before De-: cember 31 so they could put it up for all Academy Awards — best actor, best actress1, best photography, best direction — best every thing you can think of. Bogie enjoyed himself. He likes to think of him self as one of the few re maining "characters" In the movie business. He said recently: "A character has to be slightly nuts. I suppose I am. All these newcomers in the film business are afraid to ;be characters, afraid they'll ' lose their jobs." BOGART
