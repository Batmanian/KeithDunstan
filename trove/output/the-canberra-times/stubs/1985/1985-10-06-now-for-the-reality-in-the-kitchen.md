Now for the reality in the kitchen By TONY BILSON* I SUSPECT that Keith Dunstan's fascination with the cauliflower stems from a wish to return to the familial security of the Sunday lunch. Sunday was, after all, the one day where the Australian family would sit together and have a formal meal. In pre-television days the winter afternoons would be spent, post roast, falling asleep in front of the fire listening to the radio play on the ABC. The Sunday afternoon "nap was certainly helped by a healthy portion of the ubiquitous cauliflower in cheese sauce sitting comfortably in the stomach. Cauliflower is usually, served with piquant sauces, the contrast being complementary, adding interest, and sometimes elegance to an otherwise ordinary vegetable. Let's forget for the moment the cheese sauce, for which recipes abound, and look at some of the more elegant ways of serving cauliflower. The following recipes require that you separate the flowers, discarding the leaves and large stalks, and then simmer the cauliflower for 10 to 15 minutes in salted water until it is tender but not sodden. It is important that the cooked cauliflower is well drained, otherwise the water will dilute the sauces and diminish the flavour and presentation of the dishes. All recipes are for 4 people. CAULIFLOWER SOUP Puree 3 cups of cauliflower and add to 3 cups of chicken stock and 1 cup of thick cream, season and garnish with sippets fried in butter and thin strips of ham. CAULIFLOWER HOLLANDAISE Simply portion the hot cauliflower on to the plates and spoon a hollandaise sauce over the flowerets, coating them generously. This dish may be served as an entree. A variation that is wonderful with game is to add a little orange or cumquat juice to the hollandaise and garnish the dish with a sprinkle of blanched orange rind. A preparation that accompanies braised dishes well is a PUREE OF CAULIFLOWER: Puree the cauliflower in a food mill with a quarter of its bulk of boiled potato. In a pot over the flame add thick cream until the puree reaches a rich consistency. Season with salt and ground pepper and beat in some butter with a whisk just before serving. □ *Tony Bilson is one of Sydney's leading restaurateurs.
