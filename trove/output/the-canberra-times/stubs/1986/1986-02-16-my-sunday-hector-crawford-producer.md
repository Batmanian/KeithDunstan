MY SUNDAY HECTOR CRAWFORD producer By KEITH DUNSTAN WHICHEVER way you look at it the Crawfords are an extraordinary doubles combination . . . Glenda Raymond, soprano. Hector Crawford, conductor, producer, for 30 years saviour of Australian-made TV drama. ■ Hector, born in 1913, still has that wonderful mane of white hair. You would think he would have excellent reasons to retire to the Crawford houseboat at Lake Eildon but they only have time to get there at Christmas and Easter. Hector had an operation for throat cancer five years ago! Did this slow him * up?,It left him with a No. 4 grade sand-3 paper.voice and still he kept going as! vigorously as ever. More recently he had a lens implant in one eye, part of a battle' against cataracts. That has been successful but his other eye may require' simi|ar treatment. Does that slow him up? A little. If you hit a tennis ball straight at him, he finds it difficult to pick up the focus. Hector used to have the most demonic serve north of Toorak Road. He says it is not quite as ferocious as it used to be but never underestimate the determination? of Hector William Crawford on. the 5 court or anywhere else. "Retire? That's a laugh," said Glenda. "He works as hard as he ever did." ^There is one concession. He has retired as managing director of Crawford Productions but he is still chairman, very much chairman and he goes off to work at 11 am. He still reads all the scripts, looks at all the rushes daily, views every completed episode. He employs 275 regularly on the1 payroll but there is an army of casual writers, make-up artists, actors, cameramen and, like Portsea in the summer, the team expands to over 600. But there is one aspect of the Crawfords that is not well known. Hector-is the grand seigneur, .master, of perhaps Melbourne's most remarkable tennis club. It is so distinguished it should : really be titled the Royal Hectors;It is one of these all-male ; clubs. Sexist? < Heavens no, Glenda doesn't see: its that way at all. It is just male tennis.sThere have been occasions when women^have been called in an emergency. - The Crawfords have nearly, always lived in the same lovely Melbourne suburban house.which has a tennis court and swimming pool.- Hector- says the club began with John Mcllwraith-Smith,: chairman and managing director of Mcllwraith Industries,"; about • 40 years ago. Hector was introduced by David Worrall, manager of 3DB. Mcllwraith Smith retired and went off to Merimbula ? He said: "For a time we played at John Teague's place, at Bob Montgomery's, then about 30 years ago it came here and we have been playing here every Sunday ever since. Always there have been 10 or 12, mostly around pennant class and it has been really beaut." He rolled off" some names on the club list, past and present, Sir Zelman Cowen, Professor Norval Morris, Sir Alan Westerman, Sir David Derham, Phillip Law, portrait artists Sir William Dargie and Paul Fitzgerald. Bill Dargie, he reported, was a very good" player. "Some of the old hands," he said, ''who don't play any more come in for drinks around 12.30 pm and usually everyone is still around at 2.30. They're all from different occupations; We get some very good discussions . . . tends to .be a bit right wing . . . everything from capital punishment through to the state of the leadership in the Liberal Party . ah ... what leadership?" When the others have gone, maybe the Crawfords have a swim. Then on the Sunday evening almost certainly they will be checking on coming Crawford productions. Crawfords in the past year invested over $16 million in TV mini series and feature films. One recent Sunday night, for example, they relaxed by looking at three hours of future episodes; of Prime Time;' This is the new series on. TV behind-the-scenes which has just begun on the Nine Network. Hector Crawford is executive producer. Hector said: "I watch, take notes, offer comments to the directors on acting of the artists, ideas for direction. Glenda has some good advice on this. She is terrific on casting." But tennis is what really takes place at the Crawfords' on Sunday. "We couldn't possibly make -any other arrangements," said Glenda. □
