COME TO C.W.A. COUNTRY Mr GORDON DE'LISLE is the ■ Australian photographer who won a place in Keith Dunstan's book 'Knockers' with a description of the Melbourne Arts Centre. Sickened by all the "marshmallow self-congratulation", he said in an article headed 'A Nice Little Art Gallery' that it was nothing better than "an elegant ceilinged hayshed", resembling "an obscure, grey tele phone exchange, floating in already scungy moats floored with lolly papers on St Kilda Road . . Now, Mr De'Lisle has a nice little - art gallery of his own, up on the Sunshine Coast. A newssheet an- nouncing the fact goes: "Lovers of fine art will find our gallery most felicitously situated at Montville, a salubrious, drowsing hamlet atop the Blackall Range scenic drive ... "We are situated on The Village Green, wherein nestle halls for the nice ladies and gentlemen of the CWA and RSL . . . "For the comfort of the weary traveller, there are a classic red brick 'comfort station' .... and set tings for the picnicker, the better to enjoy a bird and bottle or Chiko Roll and Passiona . . . "There are miles of panoramic views over emerald crops to the coast and the whole sapphire Pacific .... Our gallery occupies the south end of our home, 'Manjalda'. The origin of the name is lost in the veils of history, but is said to mean 'where man sits down by the gal lery' ". And to add weight, Mr De'Lisle notes comments from some visitors to date: "Though quintessentially and archetypally your above - the - norm conservative-traditionalist gallery, the nod is also eruditely given to aesthetic eclecticism in that the moderns are treated with a degree of empiric pragmatism" enthused the "art critic of the Maroochydore Bugle (Incorporating the Cod-hole Clarion)". "Grouse", said "Senior Public Ser vant, Canberra".
