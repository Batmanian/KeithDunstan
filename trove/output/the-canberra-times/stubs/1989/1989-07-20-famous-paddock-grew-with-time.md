Famous paddock grew with time THE PADDOCK THAT GREW. The Story of the Melbourne Cricket Club. By Keith Dunstan. 404pp. RRP: $39.95. Reviewer: GEOFF THOMSON. Sport in Victoria has centred around the Melbourne Cricket Ground since the days of "the paddock" in the early 19th Century where spectators ventured to view cricket matches right through to the present day where it attracts huge crowds to witness the traditional VFL finals as well as the popular one-day cricket Inter nationals. Intertwined with the development of the famous MCG is the equally famous Melbourne Cricket Club which was formed on November 15, 1938. The two identities have been a major part of the sporting scene in Victoria since that time with both showing excep tional growth in the 150 years of their existence. You have not witnessed the drama of a huge sporting event if you have not been a member of the crowd at a VFL grand final and the atmosphere is just as charged when there is a big turnout at a cricket match. In its early years the cricket club strug gled to overcome financial losses as the sport battled to establish itself but once the spectators started to flock along to view the likes of the legendary W.G. Grace the coffers started to fill and the club was well on its way to financial security. Once the financial situation improved the developments of the ground were able to be undertaken and the gradual change in the structure of the old pad dock took shape. The Melbourne Cricket Club was the dominant force in Australia until 1912 and endured many battles with the con trolling NSW-Victoria bodies before the issues came to a head in that year. At one stage the MCC resigned from the VCA but rejoined at a later stage after a private conference with the VCA. As cricket grew more popular with the Melbumians, crowds started to increase at an enormous rate as the game's lead ing players strutted their stuff on the hallowed turf. Such was the popularity of the sport with the cavalier West Indians partici pating, a ground record crowd of 90,800 turned out to witness a day's play during theTest match in the 1961 series. Stories abound about the battle for women to be given rights to become full members at the MCG among many other arguments that arose surrounding the MCG, among them the right to install lights for night cricket and football.
