Canada Is Tourist Conscious By KEITH DUN STAN MONTREAL.— Canada, by providing slick American - type comforts, and first-class accommoda tion, is . becoming ' the greatest tourist nation on earth. ' Every vear 24 million Ameri cans with hundreds of dollars of holiday pay to spend, swarm across the border in their Cadil lacs, Buicks, and Lincolns. Last year they spent $280 million to make the tourist trade, second only to newsprint, Canada's ?greatest dollar earner. If our Government ever Wants . to turn Australia into a country for tourists, it should take a close look at Canada. These i, people have done the job pro : periy. They have exploited everything from the Rockies to the Mounties. It wasn't so long ago that the Red-coated Mounties were a ' forgotten sight in Canada. They shed their famous red coats and ' went to work in drab khaki uniforms. But the publicity men decided it would be a smart idea if they sprinkled a few particularly handsome Mounties around the public buildings at Ottawa and Banff. Now, if you go to Ottawa, you win see these magnificent crea ' tares astride their horses with . about thirty tourists in the background taking pictures. ''' ' * One of the first things Canada did to attract tourists was to build a chain of really superb ' hotels from coast to coast. At Toronto, for example, Cana * dian Pacific Railways built the . 1,200-room Royal York Hotel. - It is the largest in the Empire. ' with garage space for 1500 cars, a private subway to the railway station, and 14 dining rooms. There is a. radio for each room, and the banquet . hall seats 3,200 people. Then there are so many -? others, such as the world famous Chateau Frontenac at Quebec, which has its own to boggan slide and skating rink, the Chateau lake Louise in the Rockies, where, at 5,680 feet, American tourists sip Martinis by the emerald green lake and swim in a glassed-in pool, which boasts, 'glacier water heated to 78 degrees.' On the West Coast they have smartly recognised that the American likes to do every thing on wheels, so they have built drive-in movie theatres and drive-in cafes where the motorist can eat without get ting out of his car. Waiters clip tables from window to window in the front and back seats. Another American innova tion is the motel. These are cropping up all over the west. A motel consists of a dozen or so. fully furnished cabins, large enough to take a family and equipped with a garage. They rent oy the night at a very cheap, rate. The tourists can either take meals from the motel restaurant or cook their own in small kitchenettes. The Canadians spend vast sums of money every year in advertising their country in the US. They tell about their great Canadian Pacific Railway which goes over the Rockies, the province of Ontario with its million lakes and the old world charm of the French Canadian province of Quebec. They have cut out almost all border formalities. Once an American comes to the border, all he has to do is announce that he is from Brooklyn, the Bronx, or Ohio and the immi gration officials with a smile wave him through. He doesn't even need a passport. Then as he drives through Montreal or Toronto, he sees signs every where: 'Welcome to our Ameri can friends.' In fact, Canada is so keen to attract tourists, this year the Travel Bureau appealed to the various cities to make their golf Courses a little easier. 'Tough courses don't attract anybody,' they said. Of course, this aroused a storm of protest, but nevertheless by next year more Americans will be breaking 90 on Canadian courses. Canadians are naturally a charming people, but the gov ernment has gone even further. It has told every Canadian that officially or unofficially he is in the tourist business, and to look after all visitors. In this way, Canadians are selling their country to the world. It's a pity we can't do something like it in Australia. ? Beauty spots are not the only things -that Americans find at-, tractive about Canada, how-' ever, for it is not ridiculous to suggest that- one day Canada may be the wealthiest part of North America. The United States in the past 10 years has passed from a 'have' to a 'have not' nation. The US in a fantastically short period built herself into the most powerful nation on earth. It cost her a great deal to do this and she poured out her natural wealth in a reckless way. Before World War n, the US was rich hi oil, copper, zinc and lead, and shipped these commo dities all over the world. Now the US has not enough of these things even for herself and, since the Korean war, she has started to look around almost desperately for some way to build up her stockpile of these materials. ? Steel is one of the biggest wor ries. For over half a century American steel mills have mined then- iron ore from huge depo sits near Lak* Superior. Chief of these is the fabulous Mesabi range. In 1948 some 82 million tons of ore came from this region alone. But iron ore doesn't reproduce itself, and the experts are saying that 'Mesabi has fought its last war.' There may be 10 to 20 years' supply left, but to a far sighted nation this is no time at all. America also hasn't enough aluminium, timber, uranium, radium, asbestos or nickel. Canada has all these things and she has them in vast quantities. At Burnt Creek, a womanless, shopless, sub-arctic frontier town near the Labrador border, geologists have discovered enough iron ore to produce 10 million tons a year until the end of the century. The geolo gists are still finding more, and they describe it as one of the greatest mining discoveries in history. It wasn't so long ago that Al berta was a province famous only for its wheat, and as re cently as 1948 Redwater was known merely 'as a nice place to pick blueberries.' But Redwater is now one of Canada's most im portant oil fields, and Alberta's known oil reserves are 1,000 million barrels. ? Apart from oil and iron. Canada is one of the world's largest exporters of gold, alu minium, nickel, copper, lead, sine and asbestos. She has millions of acres of untouched timber, which is producing 80 per cent, of the world's news print. She has the best uranium und radium mines on the con tinent, about one-third of the world's supply of fresh water, and four-fifths of the country's industry is run by electricity. * Of course, Canada has her problems. One of them is popu lation. Like Australia, it is too low with just over 13 million. Another is that one-third of the people speak French as their first language. Most of them are in the province of Quebec and this means that every sign, every public announcement, has to be made in two languages. The French and the English get along well together; they have to. but the French insist on keeping their own heritage, language and traditions. Most of them prefer to bring their children up to be lawyers or hi the Church rather than take on engineering or other sciences. But these are minor problems, rhe French Canadians aren't one scrap interested in France. They just want to be French Canadians. On the whole Canadians are intensely na tionalistic and more British than most people realise; par ticularly Americans. They have a fear of being absorbed by the US, yet no two countries could be on more friendly relations. There is much talk of the border between»US and Canada being the longest undefended frontier in the world. This is a real fact and 'the US and Canadian defence plans are now being completely integrated. Canadians like Americans and Americans like Canadians, but as one Canadian put it: 'Hell, just because you like the girl next door that doesn't mean you have to marry her.'
