Memorial For Lost Airmen From KEITH DUNSTAN LONDON. — As the Comet airliners of 1953 rise from London Airport the passen gers can see way below a square tower on a hill. The lower, with its memorial, is a reminder of those airmen of 1939-1945 who fought and died over JSorth-West Europe. They-died, some in bomb ers over Berlin or Bremen, some over the North Sea, some died fighting the Luftwaffe over London, but they never came back, and that is their qualification for remembrance in this memorial. The Queen will unveil the memorial on October 17, and invitations are in the mail to all next-of-kin to attend the ceremony. More than 1,400 Australians as well as relatives in New Zealand, Canada and South Africa should be receiving their in vitations. The memorial is at Cooper's Hill, not far txpm Windsor, and it cost £140,000. Last week I went there to see the work. It is on a site that is still known simply as 'The Look Out,' and from it you can see some of the most beauti ful country in England. Down below, the Thames winds through the green plains of Runnymede, where [ing John is said to have Shrown himself on the ground and in his rage smashed at/ twigs as the Barons forced him to sign Magna Carta. This was the oundation of English liberty which Common wealth airmen fought to de fend seven centuries later. From Cooper's Hill it is possible to see so much of historic England. On a clear lay you see 11 counties and look deep into the north. To the left is Windsor Castle and the playing fields of Eton and to the right is Lon lon Airport. Cloisters The memorial is a tower of Portland stone with two gently curving wings at its sides, and these have a look put at either end. Behind is a courtyard with cloisters. In the cloisters there are 52 chapels, which have long narrow windows deeply set in the stone walls. These windows throw light on the names of the lost airmen which are written on stone reveals, made to look like the pages of an open book. The small chapels are a good idea, for it has given the Imperial War Graves Commission the opportunity to keep squadron members together. On the coffered ceilings are the squadron in signia. This memorial is Only for the men ' of the European theatre. The men of no known grave, who were lost over the Mediterranean, will have theirs at Malta, for the men who operated in the Middle East and Eastern Mediterranean there will be another at El Alamein and for the Far- East a fourth at Singapore. RAAF airmen of no known grave who died in the Paci fic are commemorated on separate memorials at Port Moresby, Lae, Labuan, Ra baul and other famous battlefields. The memorial at Cooper's Hill is something to see and many Australians will be wandering up there this Oc tober.
