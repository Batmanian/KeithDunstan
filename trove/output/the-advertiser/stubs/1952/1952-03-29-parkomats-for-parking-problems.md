Park-o-iats For Parkin Problems From KEITH DUNSTAN X B W Y O R K.— Parking space for motor cars has taken on super prioritv in the United States. The American is a person who believes that in cluded hi the fundamental freedoms are, freedom to use your automobile, and free dom to never have to walk more than 100 yards from it. On these lines he hasn't had such freedom lately. Motor car production has moved along at ' an unbelievable pace since 1945. There are now more than 40,000,000 cars in the US, and 1.300.000 in New York alone. There just isnt space for them all and in the past 12 months New York, San Francisco and Baltimore have had some of tbe worst traffic jams in their history. City planners have come to the conclusion that buildings need garage space as badly as they heed passages, stairs and elevators. Already some of the biggest cities have made it against the law to build any office, house, de partment store, or block of flats which doesn't provide off-the-street parking space. In Louisville. Kentucky, they won't even let you build a church which doesn't provide space for cars. These laws provide for the future but they don't help the thousands of city build ings which now have no parking space at all. How ever, Washington believes it has the solution for this in he Park-o-Mat, a weird me chanical device opened last December. The Park-o-Mat Is a skinny 18 storey building capable of handling 73 cars —four on each floor. It works like a great pin ball machine. You simply leave your car at the door, the at tendant pushes any one of 73 buttons on his panel and then things go into action. A lift door opens, a large dolley slides out. grabs your car by the front and rear bumpers, pulls it back into the Oft and away it goes at 250 feet a minute, to the spot selected. If say it were position A on floor 17, the lift would stop there and the dolley would place your car in position A. The traffic people in Washington say there was never a parking device as good as this. They are cheap to build. This one cost only $300,000. The Park-o-mat is built on a simple girder framework and as no-one ever has to go above the ground floor it needs no ven tilating system. It has no costly ramps or aisles and it is all run by one man. He has only to push buttons and he could be a cripple. In Washington they say Park-o-mats will be all over America soon like drive-in theatres. But if Washington does anything smart New York has to do It better, so a firm here is building an $8m., 32 storey skyscraper with park ing space for 400 cars. An the space opening on to windows will be for offices but a column up the centre will be an outsize Parko mat It will have three double car double deck elevators with automatic dolleys which win park 20 cars to a floor. The attendant will have a board with 600 buttons, and when a position becomes vacant a fight will go on. They claim the high-speed elevators will deliver any car to its owner inside two minutes. This system, which could become standard for modern buildings, will make life much more comfortable in New York. It means that people who work in or visit that building win never be without then* ear. When they are about to leave the build ing they will call the atten dant on the phone and by the time they have taken . tbe lift to the ground floor their car will be there also. Every large city here is building. multi-storey park ing areas. Some go four or five storeys below the ground and they can be used as com bination garages and air raid shelters. Flat Roofs The latest department stores in New York's suburbs have flat roofs, where you park your car on the roof, then take the escalator lownstairs to do your shop ping. - Cities are also clearing the streets by eliminating what they call 'the parking hog.'* and they do this with the parking meter. There's hardly a city in America large or small that doesn't use parking meters, simple coin operated gadgets which time a car's stay at the kerbside. For example, a motorist might pay five cents for 15 minutes. If he wasn't back by the time 15 minutes was up he would get a ticket. Not only do the parking meters get rid of the parkini hogs, but they turn in mil lions and millions of dollars. New York has it all planned: They are going to put parking meters all through the busy sections of Manhattan With the stacks of gold they make from the meters they will build a chain of 1.000 car park-o-mats.
