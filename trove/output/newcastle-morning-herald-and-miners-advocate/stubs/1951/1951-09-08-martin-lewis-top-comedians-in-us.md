Martin, Lewis Top Comedians In U.S. From KEITH DUNSTAN, in Hollywood. Teenagers in this country are wildly demonstrative, enthusiastic to the point of hysteria, warm in their passions and utterly fickle. Once it was Harry James who set them afire with his trum pet, once Frank Sinatra made them swoon and collapse in the aisle. The latest and biggest craze is the young comedy team-Dean Martin and Jerry Lewis. There is no stopping the.teenagers. Martin and Lewis have just fin ished one of the maddest marathons in the history of show business. They have broken the records set years ago by Hope, Abbott and Costello, Sinatra and Jack Benny. They are simply the nation's top comedians-nobody else can come near their attendance figures. The mr A marathon started in Chicago. They were given six ses sions six days a week and seven on Sunday. The teenagers flatly refused to leave the theatre, and some were staying for five and six sessions. The theatre management wanted to take away the popcorn and candy machines so that the teenagers would leave the theatre from sheer hunger. But they were too smart. They brought packets of sandwiches. Martin and Lewis found the only solution was to give a free perform ance outside the theatre after each show. And they did this all over America. They performed from fire escapes, through windows, from the tops of cars, anywhere just to get their audience out of the theatre. BEFORE I came Dto the United States t never really believed that American teen agersawent so crazy for Harry James or swooned for Frank Sinatra. But when Martin and Lewis went to New York, they started to line up outside the Paramount Theatre, in Times Square, at 6 a.m. By midday 43rd Street, outside the stage entrance, was jammed with thousands of teenagers scream ing: "We want Deatt and Jerry. We want Dean and Jerry." The famous pair went to the window, threw out a few thousand photographs, dozens of bow ties, handkerchiefs, straw hats, slippers, shirts, socks, towels, anything to excite their fans. There were mad sslueals, as in a menagerie. Boys and girls fought and pushed for their souvenirs, jumping up and down as in a war dance. One little girl, proud and successful, went off home with half a straw hat, her memento of the great Jerry Lewis. The Paramount broke every record and in two weeks took 289,000 dollars (£Al30,000). Martin and Lewis have been work ing at a furious pace, making films, personal appearances and giving shows on television. This year they will gross 1,500,000 dollars (£A669,000), but most of it will go in taxes. When asked why they took things at such a pace, Lewis replied: "The Government needs tanks." It would be nice to tell you ex actly why Dean Martin and Jerry Lewis are so marvellous, but that epparently is something known only by the American teen-ager. The re views of their films have been con sistently bad, yet, "At War With The Army," which came out in January, has made 3 million dol lars (£A1,338,000) and "That's My Boy," just released, is the top tnoney-making film in America, beating even Disney's "Alice In Wonderland." Jerry Lewis is the madcap comedian. He has a crew cut, a voice which makes a noise like an oboe, and he looks like an orang outang. In 1950 you would have called their humour "slapstick"; but for 1951 its "zany." They bolted into stardom at New York's Copocabana. Here they went crazy, ripping off their clothes, dashing in and out between the tables in their underclothing. They watered customers' cigars, spilled their food, chopped off ties at the knot, and embraced and battered each other. They rode about New York in glass-topped taxis, poked their heads through the roof. and played gin rummy on the sliding, top. One time, on a train travelling from New York to Chicago, they went through the sleeping cars at 3 a.m.. bawling: "We'll be in La Salle Station, Chicago, in 20 min utes."
