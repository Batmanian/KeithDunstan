First, Universal found a so called talking mule named Fran cis, produced a picture about him, and gave him the full-scale star build-up. The film Francis vwent on to make millions of dol lar'S. Now he is valuable property, more valuable than your glamour boys or starlets. He is busy making a whole series of "Francis" films. And, of course, he is allowed to do cnly the lightest of work. He is watched over constantly by mule doctors. IHe has at least three stand-ins. Last week, for the first time, I was invited to a Press conference to meet a cat. The cat, Rhubarb, an ill-tempared, overgnown alley cat, is insured for something like 100,000 dollars. He is making a personal appearance tour of 20 American cities. A cat food company held a lun cheon and Press conference for Rhubarb at the Hotel Granada, in Brooklyn. Invitations went out for this "At Home" to meet Rhubarb, "the world's first cat movie star." HE reception room at the hotel was an extraordinary sight. The cat food company had invited what they des cribted as "New York's most dis tinguished cats," which included the Rhubarb Cat Fan Club and cats from the New York Cat Orphan age. In fact, so many cats were there they installed a cat-check room, where people could leave their cats. There were Siamese, Persian, Burmese, alley cats, all kinds of cats everywhere. A number of smart, elderly women were leading collared Siamese cats around the room on leashes, like dogs. The cats were frightened, wvonder ing what it was all about. One poor thing in a pink ribbon clung rotnd her mistress's neck and re fused to be put on the ground. A group of women in a corner were dressing utp a dejected-looking cat in all sorts of clothes. They said he was a model. The centre of attraction on the table of honour in an elegant trans parent cage, looking elegantly bored, was the guest of honour, Rhubarb. His trainer, Al Yank, explained that the personal appearance tour was hard on Rhubarb. "You can't treat cats that way," he said. "He was 221b when we left Hollywood. Now he's 141b. He eats well, though. WVe give him top rump steak, boiled chicken and thin cream. But it's too many people, too much fuss all the time." After the guests had finished their cocktails and roast beef, and the cats their own food, the klieg lights went on, newsreel cameramen, Press and magazine photographers ran off thousands of feet of film on Rhubarb. The star was made. ND don't think they picked any old cat for this job. It all started early this year when Paramount bought the rights to H. Alien Smith's best seller, "Rhubarb," the story of a cat that inherited a baseball team and 30 million dollars. The cat had to be mean and mangy. Yet it had to handle as many tricks as Lassie, put up with television broadcasts, lights, films and the fuss of oress-agentry. Paramount searched for six months. interviewed 3000 cats and answered 15,000 queries from most parts of the world. The search received satisfactory publicity and Hollywood, with.its flair for un derstatement, called it "the great est casting search since 'Scarlet' in 'Gone With the Wind'." Finally, the choice came down to Barrelhouse Dan, a dreadful cat from Brooklyn, and Orangey, a sour.. empered cat from California. They chose Orangey, re-named him Rhubarb, and a three-way national radio hook-up, with the pressmen as witnesses, watched the ssilning of the contract. Rhubarb, with the help of five stand-ins, made the film with co stars Ray Milland and Jan Ster ling. There-wasn't much harmony. Ray Milland was mauled by Rhu barb on a number of occasions.
