Anniversary For Bing Boom From KEITH DUNSTAN, in Hollywood Bing, "The Groaner," has just passed his 20th anniversary as a big-time crooner, and sentimental America has moved into another delirious Crosby croonina craze, In normal times, it was bad enough. Every time you turned on the radio, Bing's lackadaisi cal, velvety tones were sure to come over the air. But now, disc-j6ckeys are giv ing non-stop sessions through the day. Newspapers and magazines are running anniversary "profiles." Bming was never so popular. They are citing him as one of the greatest money earners of all' time, the man whose voice has been heard by more people than any other, and as the perfect example of the "Good Joe,' typical Ameri can. Bing is taking this in his usual nonchalant easy-going way. He wanders in for his weekly radio pro gramme with his hat on the back of his head, his yellow sports shirt flopping over mustard green trou sers and, of course, never, never a tic. When the time comes for him to sing, he takes the corroded, carbon caked pipe from his mouth, moves the wad of gum against one cheek and comes out with his effortless, romantic rumbles. Americans dearly love, but hardly know, him. Perhaps they love him so much because Americans are an emotional, tense, desperately hard working people. Most of them have ar. inability to relax. They envy this man, who relaxes so easily and so completely. They think he is lazy and easy-going, and naturally they try' to be like him. B UT Bing is no sluggard. He is a shrewd hard working man, who has an incred ible, insatiable urge to make money. He has a finger in countless busi ness enterprises. He receives 21 cents (2*d) royalty on every Crosby record. There have been more than 70 million. He receives 150, 000 dollars (£A67,000) a picture and 7500 dollars (£A3110) for each half-hour radio show. He owns real estate, including the Crosby building on Sunset Boulevard; he has a 10,000-acre ranch; he breeds and sells race horses; he has big interests in oil and frozen vegetables; he is chief stock holder in the Del Mar Turf Club, which has a 500,000-dollar manufacturing plant; he has inter ests in various publishing firms, and at odd times he has owned shares in baseball clubs and prizefighters. Apart from these activities he manages to keep his golf handicap under 10. There's an easy-going man for youl Bing explains it this way: "In 1939 I made 1,400,000 dollars--I think. Of that total I had less than 30,000 dollars left after liv ing the year. Taxes take a bite. And I like to live high. Houses here and there and other things. Retire? Why, I'd be broke after two or three years. I have got to keep work ing to pay the bills." His four sons are doing well con sidering their age. They each have a bank account of 200,000 dollars. Gary, 17, the eldest, has a fine singing voice, so they say. As for Bing, he is singing as well as ever. His latest film, "Mr. Music," is his biggest success in years, so it seems you will be hear ing from the Crosbys for a long, long time. AUSTRALIA is becoming almost as popular as the Sahara Desert, darkest Africa and the exotic East as subject matter for films. Edgar J. and Harry Lee Dan ziger, an independent team, are plan ning to make a million-dollar tech nicolour film in May on colonial Australia, titled "The Queen's Mark." It is a story of the convict days and the original title was "The Broad Arrow." But instead of making it in Aus tralia, much to everybody's amaze ment they plan to shoot the film in Belgian West Africa. They believe it will be cheaper that way; and they will be able to fly the technicolour film to Eng land for processing. If they were in Australia, they would have to fly it much further to the U.S. They are hoping to tie in with some British firm and they are try. Ing to get Sir Ralph Richardson, Orson Welles and Robert Morley for the cast. rJIHE newspapers are publishing almost daily pictures of Eliza beth Taylor with a handsome young film director Stanley Donen. Col umnist Sheilah Graham said enthu siastically: "Make no mistake about this. They are madly in love. The date of their marriage depends on how soon Stanley can get a divorce from his wife, Jean."
