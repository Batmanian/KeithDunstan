Hollywood Falls For Horatio From KEITH DUNSTAN, in Hollywood Lord Horatio Hornblower, tall hand some, utterly fearless, is a widely admired Englishman in Hollywood. Perhaps Americans admire him so much because he was born on the remarkably significant date, July 4, 1776. Perhaps they admire him for his energy. Whatever it is, they are insatiable in their demands for more information on every one of his dauntless doings, and these have been many. Hornblower is the creation of the English novelist C. S. Forester. I-Ie made Horatio such an indomitable character that he ran away out of his hands till Horatio was control ling Forester and not Forester Horatio. The old seadog became a public myth, virtually alive, as did D'Artagnan, Sherlock Holmes, Bulldog Drummond and Tarzan before him. Forester, a naval historian of international repute, is fed to the back teeth with Horn blower. He would like to sink Horn blower beneath the waves, but he knows what happened to Sir Arthur Conan Doyle when he tried to kill off Sherlock Holmes. Forester would much rather spend his time writing more serious novels. He does, too, stuch as the recently published "Randall and the River of Time," but no, thse public won't have it. The serious novels don't bring the same sales, and the public wants more of Horn blower. So Forester sits in his house in California writing Horn blower stories for the Saturday Evening Post, movie scripts. television scripts - and the Commodore lives on. It is not easy to say why this is so. He doesn't act that well, and there are others more handsome. Nevertheless, it is undoubt edly in his features. In one of his fan letters, a woman said: "You can sort of trust Gregory. There's a sugges' tion of nobility in his rugged face." He doesn't get in. volved in Hollywood scandals and he looks equally solid in the role of a priest or a cow boy. It is more easy to see why people fall for Hornblower. Hornblower is impeccable of honour, noble of character, shrewd and daring. Yet he is human. Under his austere surface there are all kinds of doubts and worries. He is irascible when nervous, occastonally garrulous, a little bit vain on occasions, a little Isammy in his grand moments. But he knows deep down his own shortcomings, and you can't help liking him for that. Mr. Peck makes a good Hornblower. In the film he does the usual things. He boards and captures a Spanish ship, rescues the Duke of Wellington'f lovely sister, whips the French fleet single. handed, escapes from the French under the nose of Ni poleon, and returns to Eog. land to become an Admiral.
