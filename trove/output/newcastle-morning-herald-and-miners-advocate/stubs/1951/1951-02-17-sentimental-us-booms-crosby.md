Sentimental U.S. Booms Crosby From KEITH DUNSTAN, in Hollywood. Bing, "The Groaner," has just passed his 20th anniversary as a big-time crooner, and sentimental America has moved into another delirious, Crosby crooning craze. In normal times it was bad enough. Every time you turned on the radio, Bing's lackadaisical, velvety tones were sure to come over the air; but now disc-jockeys are giving non-stop sessions through the day. Newspapers and magazines are running anniversary profiles and Bing was never so popular. They are citing him as one of the greatest money-earners of all time, the man whose voice has been heard by more humans than any other; and as the perfect example Oa the "Good Joe" typical Ameri can. Bing is taking this in his usual nonchalant easygoing way. He wan ders in for his weekly radio pro pramme with his hat on the back of his head, his yellow sports shirt flopping over mustard green trous ers and of course, never, never a tie. When the time comes for him to sing, he takes the corroded, car bon caked pip from his mouth, moves the wad of gum against one cheek and comes out with his effort less romantic rumbles. MERICANS dearly love but hardly know him. Perhaps they love him so much be cause Americans are an emotional, tense, desperately hard-. sorking people. Most of them have an inability to relax. They are envious of this man who relaxes so easily and so completely. They think he is lazy and easygoing, and naturally they try to be like him. But Bing is no sluggard. Actu ally, he is a shrewd hard-working man, who has an incredible, insati able urge to make money. He has a finger in countless business enter prises. He receives 2V2 cents royalty on every Crosby record. There have been more than 71 million. He receives 150,000 dol ars a picture and 7500 dollars for each half of a radio show. He owns, real estate, including the Crosby building on Sunset Boulevard; he has a 10,000-acre ranch; he breeds and sells race horses; he has big interests in oil and frozen vegetables; he is chief stock holder in the Del Mar Turf Club, whichl has a 500,000-dollar manufacturing plant; he has inter ests in various publishing firms; and at odd times he has owned shares in baseball clubs and prize fighters. Apart from these activi ties, he manages to keep his golf handicap under 10. There's an easy going man for you. Bing explains' it this way: "In 1939 I made 1,400,000 dollars-I think. Of that total I had less than 30,000 dollars left after living the year. Taxes take a bite. And I like to live high. Houses here and there and other things. Retire? Why, I'd be broke after two or three years. I gotta keep working to pay the bills." His four sons are doing well con sidering their age. They each have a bank account of £90,000. Gary, 17, the eldest, has a fine singing voice, so they say. As for Bing, he is singing as well as ever. His latest film, "Mr. Music," is his biggest success in years. So it seems you will be hearing from the Crosbys for a long, long time. AUSTRALIA is becoming almost as popular as the Sahara Desert, darkest Africa and the exotic East as subject matter for films. Edgar J. and Harry Lee Danziger, an independent team, are planning to make a million-dollar Techni color film in May on Colonial Australia, titled "The Queen's Mark." It is a story of the con vict days. The original title was "The Broad Arrow." But instead of making it in Australia, much to everybody's amazement, they plan to shoot the film in Belgian West Africa. They believe it will be cheaper to do this and they will be able to fly the technicolor film to England for'processing. If they were in Aus tralia, they would have to fly it much further to the United States. They are hoping to tie in with some British firm, and trying to get Sir Ralph Richardson, Orson Welles and Robert Morley for the cast. JEAN SIMMONS is in for some hot competition. M.G.M. is battling to keep Elizabeth Taylor busy so that she won't brood too much about her marriage break-up. "Lis," as the tabloids call her, has been told she will woo Stewart Granger in her next film "Scara mouche." Furthermore, she will go to London in April to play in "Ivanhoe," again opposite Mr. Granger. .REGORY PECK and Jane Wy man are the world's favourite screen stars, according to a Foreign Press Association poll conducted in 50 countries in the past four months. Runners-up were Ingrid Bergman and Alan Ladd. The only non-Hollywood star who made any sort of a showing was Maria Felix, a beautiful Mexican actress, popu lar in Spanish speaking countries. LIERE are some odd items: Holly wood has just finished making one of its great sex and sand films titled "The Golden Horde." Cam eraman Russ Netty decided the dust raised by Genghis Khan's troops was too dreary for colour film, so he sprayed it with golden liquid. Sign in a Hollywood coffee shop: "Morning special-large glass of tomato juice, two cups of black coffee, two aspirins and our sym pathy--" ONE DOLLAR.
