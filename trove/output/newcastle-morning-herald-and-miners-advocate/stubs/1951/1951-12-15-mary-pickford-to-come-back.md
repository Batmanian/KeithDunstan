Mary Pickford To Come Back From KEITH DUNSTAN in Hollywood There's a feeling of joy and excite ment here---call it sentimentality if ypu like-over the news that Mary Pickford will return to the screen next year. Mary Pickford and her husband, Douglas Fairbanks, Sen., were the royal couple of Hollywood. This is not movie jargon; they were lit erally King and Queen of Hollywood - the world's two most successful film stars. They lived in a great man sion called "Pickfair." It was their Buckingham Palace and all the Kings, Queens, Dukes and Duchesses that went to Hollywood automatically paid homage to Mary and Doug be fore they went anywhere else. Over the years they received such people as the King of Siam, Prince William of Sweden, Einstein, the Lind bergs, former President Cool idge, Marquisa Marconi, Sir Arthur Conan Doyle and Dukes, Duchesses, Barons, Counts, Lords and Ladies by the dozen. N invitation .to "Pickfair" was the prize social plum. At Grauman's Chinese Theatre Sid Grauman never started a show till the "royal couple" were in their seats. At cocktail parties the guests often rose to their feet when Mary. Pickford entered the room. Mary was Queen in Holly wood but in the rest of the U.S. they called her "Ameri ca's Sweethgeart" or "Our Mary." To those people over 50 her return will bring back memories of the silent era. In those days pictures may have been brassy and absurd but they were new and very much alive. Even more than that they were made by char acters who aroused the imagi nation, and Mary was one of them. The New York "Herald Tribune" in a leading article said: "To the members of the older movie-going generation, Mary Pickford's tresses are still lustrous in memory and her heart remains as big as America. "For the sake of the gen eration that has grown up since her retirement we hope she puts over a. resounding success." Mary now is 58. Her hair is still blonde and still nat urally wavy. For her age she is an incredibly good looking woman. But now, after 19 years' absence from the screen, she didn't have to come back. She is worth more than four million dollars, and she has never ceased to be one of the most prominent executives in Hollywood. T WAS the as tute young pro ducer Stanley Kramer who talked her into making a film entitled "The Librarian." Work on it has already started. All the details are secret, but this much is known: she plays the part of a librarian who has a great influence on the lives of young people. Mary Pickford was borr in Toronto with the plain name of Mary Smith. She started acting on the stage at 4, and she made her first film a. 14. Like Shirley Temple, she was the perfect child actress, but, in addition, she had a shrewd business brain. Within three years she was making £4500 a week, plus 50 per cent. of the profits. In another year she had her own company. In 1919 she fell in love with Douglas Fairbanks, Sr., one of the most fabulous Ameri cans of his day. They both got divorces and married in 1920. Their six-week honey moon outdid any of the films they had made. They were 'entertained by Royal Families and Prime Ministers all over Europe. A corps of publicity men pre ceded them and they attracted c:owds wherever they went. It was claimed to be the most publicised honeymoon in his tory. In London Mr. Fairbanks sat on a balcony of the Ritz while thousands cheered in the street below; In Venice they went for a moonlight trip in a gondola, trailed by a barge load of cameramen, and Fair banks scaled the side of an American warship in the har bour. T HEIR' romance was described as something of "perfect beauty." It was re ported that Douglas never left Mary for more than 24 hours. They defied all social etiquette, ignored all seating arrange ments, to be side by side at formal dinner parties. No other woman's lip ever touched the lips of Douglas Fairbanks. During Fairbanks's love scenes on film it was al ways Mary Pickford, made up to look like the leading lady who, at the last second, did the kissing. But Mr. Fairbanks did leave her for more than 24 hours. He started' making trips to Alaska, China, the Amazon, until finally, after 14 years, the marriage broke up. Fairbanks married Lady Ashley and Miss Pickford married an orchestra leader named Buddy Rogers. Mary Pickford's biggest suc cesses were in the years of the First World War, and the '20's. Some of her famous pictures were: "Tess of the Storm Country," "Hearts Adrift," "Such a Little Queen." "Mistress Nell," "Little Annie Rooney," and "Little Lord Fauntleroy." ER long blonde curls were the fashion for little girls the world over, and she received 10,000 letters a week. In 1929 she cut off those long blonde curls. According to one biography, "It made the nation gasp." For some reason or other the disappearance of Miss Pickford's curls meant the end of an era. Her curls had been a symbol, and there was a scream of protest to Holly wood. Newspapers carried on an angry debate for weeks. Mary Pickford went on to more sober pictures, and the film industry was never as lively again. Hollywood is still searching for extraordinary personalities, great drawcards like Douglas Fairbanks and Mary Pickford, but the times that produced them aren't here any more.
