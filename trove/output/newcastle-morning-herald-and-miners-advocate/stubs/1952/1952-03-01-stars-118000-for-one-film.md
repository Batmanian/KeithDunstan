Star's £118,000 For One Film From KEITH' DUNSTAN in Hollywood Errol Flynn Tasmanian-born film star, has won Hollywood's most fantastic contract. For Warner Brothers he will now make just one pic ture a year, and for that they will pay him £118,000. Furthermore. this one pic ture must not take up more than 14 weeks of his time. The rest of the year he can make as many pictures as he wishes for the other studios, Flynn is now 42, but appar ently his charm, handsome fea ttres and superb muscles are indestructible, for the con tract.runs for 14 years. HE is making a film foir Universal International titled "Against All Flags." Bags of action, pirates, Maureen O'Hara, everything! He has a 50-50 contract, the least he can earn is £150,000. But he is still short of money. Things were so bad six months ago he almost had to sell his yacht. That costs him £450 a month. His first wife, Lili Damita, costs him £450 a week. E, supports three children and his parents. He has a 3000-acre plantation in Jam aica, an O80-acre island. And he has to pay for the upkeep of a large house in Beverly Hills. Flynn's reputation is based on his ability as a great lover. He is indeed handsome, but so are other men. Obviously, he has something else, something which another mere male finds it impossible to explain. No doubt Casanova, Romeo, Don Juan and Valentino had it, too. According to the fan statis tics, girls are most likely to fall under Flyrin's spell be tween the ages of 14 and 18. Men often dislike him at all ages. Many have a remark able desire to pick fights with him in nightclubs and bars. His wife, Pat Wymore, said: "I used to read about people wanting to punch Errol, but it's all true and worse. They want to fight me, too." A Canadian millionaire, Duncan McMartin, punched him in Jamaica a few months ago. Then he punched him again in a New York night club. Errol is asking the Jamaica court for £90,000 damages-£45,000 a punch. ERROL has been in arid out of court cases for the past 10 years. In 1942, when he was sued by two teen-agers, police had to control the crowds at the court. Warner's publicity agents had a bad time answering phone calls from screen celebrities who wanted to re serve courtroom space. Masses of girls brought cut lunches so they wouldn't lose their seats. Errol took it in his stride, smiled at the witnesses and nonchalantly signed auto graphs. All this did him no harm at all. He became a bigger and a more tantalising star than ever. Yet he has other accon plishments. He plays a game of tennis well above the aver age, and he has played exhibi tion matches with Bill Tildeh and Vincent Richards. In 1945 he wrote a novel called "Showdown." When it appeared on the book lists at first people refused to believe it wasn't ghosted. They said it was impossible to visualise Errol crouched over a type writer straining his fine blue eyes. But Errol was serious. He said: "This book has been more important to me than anything else. I get more of a kick out of writing than I do out of making motion pic tures. I worked and worked over it. I actually rewrote It three times. It took a year." He even mentioned that he might retire and devote his life to literary work. Last year while making a film he hurt his spine. Three weeks ago, he slipped on the deck of a pirate ship and brok an. ankle.
