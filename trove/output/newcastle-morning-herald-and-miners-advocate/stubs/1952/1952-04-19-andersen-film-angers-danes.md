Andersen Film Angers Danes From KEITH DUNSTAN in Hollywood. The Danes are angry. A feud is on between Hollywood and Denmark. To the Danes, Han Chris tian Andersen is a figure al most as great as Shakespeare is to the British or Dante to the Italians. The idea that Hollywood is making a technicolor movie on his life is mon strous. Any such film from Holly wood, the Danes say, could only be a disaster. "Hans Christian Andersen" will certainly be the biggest and most exciting production for 1952. Sam Goldwyn is spending four million dollars of his own money to make it a success, an extraordinary sum at a time when most studios are economising. It is the most ambitious film he has made On one set alone he spent 200,000 dol lars. He - rebuilt the main square of Copenhagen, com plete with city gates shops hotels, opera house, bridge, canal and bronze statues on a 30,000 square ft. sound stage. D ANNY Kaye, Farley Granger and Renee Jean maire, a French ballerina, have the leading roles. Goldwyn originally signed Moira Shear er for the top ballerina part, but she had to drop out as she is expecting a baby this year. A Danish official in New York said: "Everybody in Copenhagen feels the same way. We don't want Hans Christian Andersen to be known to the world as the hero of a song and dance movie. "We wouldn't mind a free interpretation of his life, but apparently this thing is ridicu lous, and the people in Holly wood won't listen to any sug gestions. "It's too bad that we Danes haven't a great film industry of our own. Then we would make our own film on Hans Christian Andersen, and do it properly. But this Danhy Kaye as Andersen! It's just an awful joke." O the Danes are protesting to the State Depart ment. They rejected Sam Goldwyn's offer of the world premiere in Copenhagen.
