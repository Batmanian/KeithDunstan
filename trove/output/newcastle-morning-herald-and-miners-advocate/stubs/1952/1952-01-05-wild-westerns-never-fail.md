The Wild West, of course~ died in 1895, but now it is still the nation's greatest source of entertainment. In recent years, it has become a big in dustry and Hollywood must take the blame. The Western film gives a myth of icourage. fearlessness and a direct challenge between good and evil; a sort of 'Mitty" dream which requires no effort of the brain what ever. IN SHORT, it provides the perfect relaxation away from fears of war and the monotony of work in offices and factories. The cowboy life is a romantic ideal which gives a lift to the imagina tion. Wild Bill Hickock would have had an education had he been in America this year. He would have noticed that 20 per cent. of all the records sold were cowboy songs, he could have sat in New York and watched cowboy shows on television for 17 hours a week, or heard them on the radio for eight hours a. week. Of every five films that came out of Hollywood, one was a Western. He could hard ly have missed them, because 14,000 out of 18,000 American theatres took them as a steady diet. On a holiday tour he could have visited any one of 500 dude ranches. Here he could have taken moonlight rides, pone roping and cow-punch ing, played a guitar, led the cow-girls in a square dance and perhaps taken a banana split and chocolate malted for supper. He would have been startled to see that American universit ies were 'fielding rodeo teams as a. competitive sport, and that the annual rodeo was one of the biggest attractions at Madison Squ'are Garden. Mr. Hickock killed 45 men -not counting Indians, natur ally-and spent most of his time° playing poker. What would his comment have been on Hopalong Cassidy, Gene Autry and Roy Rogers. three of the most successful enter tainers of our generation? Men who earn up to a million dol lars a year, but couldn't shoot a bottle off a bar at ten paces. HE Western boom started in earnest after the Second World War. In the past five years, there have been nearly 200 new Western novels. The pocket book people in four years circulated 25 mil lion copies of these on the news-stands. Comic strips such as Hopalong Cassidy and the Lone Ranger spread out into 300 newspapers. Three years ago, there were only 25 companies making toy pistols and holsters. Now there are 250. Even politicians found it profitable to dress as cowboys. Senator Glen Taylor, from Idaho, sang a cowboy song on the Capitol steps before he took his oath of office. Western musicals couldn't fail. First "Annie Get Your Gun" and then "Oklahoma!" More than 41 million people saw "Oklahoma!" in New York alone-it ran for five years. The West had its biggest vic tory among children. In the past year, toy soldiers, machine-guns, tanks and bazookas have been a failure. Korea was of no interest to them. They insisted on cow boy suits, double-action Colts, spurs and Hopalong Cassidy. You are in for many, many. more Westerns from Holly. wood. There will be blazing million-dollar affairs starring such cowboys and cowgirls as Clark Gable, Robert Taylor, Ann Sheridan, Alan Ladd, Gary Cooper, Denise Darcel.
