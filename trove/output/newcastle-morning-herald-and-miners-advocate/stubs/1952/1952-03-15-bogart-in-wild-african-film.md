African Film From KEITH DUNSTAN, in Hollywood Even at the Stork Club, dressed in a white tie, Humphrey Bogart looks like a battle-beaten marine. But when you see him in the African jungle with a week's growth of beard, sucking at a bottle of gin, all in colour, he's a night mare. He is pretty weird, too, when you see him carrying on like a hippopotamus and giving a classic imitation of a monkey. There's no doubt about it. He is in his element in his new picture "The African Queen." A British and an Ameri cani company put up the cap ital to make it in the Belgian Congo. A first-rate idea, for in England they think it's an English picture and in the United States they think it's American, so, naturally enough, it is doing extraordin ary business on both sides of the Atlantic. The critic of the London "Daily Express" confessed to tears, and the man on the New York "Morning Tele graph" said: "It was a rous ing rip-roaring whale cr a movie." Bogie and Katherine H-ep. burn (Katie as he calls her) went out to Africa last year, their lives carefully insured for two million dollars each. Lauren Bacall, Bogie's young wife, went along for the trip. So with an English crew, they travelled 6000 miles through Africa, and had a pretty uncomfortable time. Everybody became sick. Thev had to put up with mal aria, bugs, leeches, filthy wat er, mildewed clothes and the terrible heat. NCE they were so short of water they. had to run the generator on coca cola. But it was all worth it. Their story was a beauty. C. S: Forester, of "Hornblower" tame wrote "The African Queen." It is a tale about a boozy, bawdy, clown of a Canadian (Bogart) and a prim, straight-laced sister of a mis cionary (Hepburn). Unexpectedly, they get caught up in the First World War, and toge her they sail a wheezy steam launch hundreds of miles down an uncharted river through dangerous rapids, past .dangerous forts, dan gerous crocodiles, elephants and lions Undaunted by this, they sail to the mouth of the river, turn their boat into a great tor pedo, and blow up a German gunboat. Then they fall in love. Katie the missionary's sister, loses all her inhibitions-Bogie never had any-and there are some very funny, witty scenes. The producers thought the film so good they rushed it into one Los Angeles theatre before December 31 so they could put it up for all Acad emy Awards-best actor, best actress, best photography, best direction-best everything you can think of. OGIE enjoyed himself. He likes to think of himself as one of the few re maining "characters" in the movie business. He said recently: "A char acter has to be slightly nuts. I suppose I am. All these new comers in the film business are afraid to be characters, afraid they'll lose their jobs." Bogie lives up to the part pretty well He has been ban ned from the El Morocco and the 21 Club in New York. The story goes he was even banned from a night club at Stanleyville in the Congo. It was the P,ogarts' wedding anniversary. He announced to the party that "Baby" Bacall didn't think any party was a party unless there were cham pagne and caviare. So they took their own champagne and caviare to a restaurant three miles out of town. According to the col umnist, Leonard Lyons, the manager of the restaurant was offended. He said' "I run a decent place. We serve champagne. What'll the customers think?" He told them to leave. He ddin't want their custom. Miss Hepburn then had her say: "The first time in my life I go out with Bogart and I'm thrown out of a joint. Where does it happen? Not the Stork Club, not the 21 or the El Morocco, but in the middle of Africa."
