Australia New Film Theme From KEITH DUNSTAN in Hollywood Australia is Hollywood's newly dis covered continent. For 30 years Holly wood has pulled drama from Africa and romance from the Pacific. Now it's our turn. Script writers are checking facts in the public libraries, photo graphers are looking at travel films for back grounds. There have never been so many opportunities for ac tors with English and Aus tralian accents. These films are on the books. "Kangaroo," which 20th Century Fox made in Austra lia. "Botany Bay," with James Mason and Alan Ladd, now nearing completion. Greer Garson is busy fill-' ing the bill as Marjorie Law rence. Paramount is working on a screen play from a novel by Henry Handel Richardson. But the film getting ,ill the publicity, and the one likely to make the most money, is "One Piece Bathing Suit," the life of Annette Kellerman, with inevitably Esther Wil- liams. YOU must re- member An- nette Keller- man. There was a time when she was more exciting to the world thant Sonia Henie, and as controversial as Mrs. Pank- hurst. For more than 30 years they called her the aquatic marvel, the beautiful mermaid, the Australian girl with the figure of Venus. Annette was born in Syd- ney, an awkward little girl with legs so bent she had to wear braces to her thighs. But she had a love for swim- ming and the sport most rel- ished by boys. She swam in a boy's bathing suit, and soon she was able to throw away the braces. She swam such times as 1 min. 38 sec. for the 100yds. and became champion of the world for distances up to five miles. But Annette was more than a swimmer; she was a bathing beauty. She laughed at the Victor- ian swim suits with bulky skirts and flounces. She de- signed one of her own-a one- piece affair. This was extra- ordinary, and on Annette the suit looked mighty exciting. IT was in 1908 that she tried to swim the Eng- lish Channel, something no other woman had attempted before. She was three-quarters of the way across after 10 hours when she became vio- lently ill. So they had to pull her out. Yet the newspapers called it a great feat, and she gave a swimming exhibition before King Edward VII and Queen Alexandra. Next year she caused a moral crisis in America. Now her one-piece suit had no sleeves, and the trousers fin- ished at a daring level, two inches above the knee. Unashamedly she swam at all the fashionable beaches. She was denounced as a wan- ton, a wicked woman who aroused "unpleasant sensibili- ties." Clergymen from pulpits foretold a dark future for America if this sort of thing went on. She was even arrested in Boston. She said. "I can't swim wearing more stuff than you hang on a clothes line." EVENTUA LLY Annette won her battle. The one-piece suit became stand- ard wear on the beach, and she a national hero. She went on the stage, star- red in vaudeville and pictures. She toured the world swim- ming and diving into a great portable glass. She is still a darling of the Press here, and it looks for her comments on the latest Bikini suits. All this makes the best story yet for Esther Williams and fulfils Metro Goldwyn Mayer's prime object of get- ting her into a bathing suit as quickly and as often as pos- sible. M.G.M. has to do this. If Esther makes a film and fails to show herself in the water, there is a national outcry. This happened when she played a dramatic role with William Powell in "The Hood- lum Saint." The bobby soxers made such a fuss when they saw Esther kissing "that old man" that the producers had to cut the sequence from the film. IT WAS the only Esther Williams film that ever made less than 500, 000 dollars. Things are right this time. She wears a record number of bathing suits-28-and stays in the water a record length of time. But you won't be worried by Esther in a 1910 bathing costume. M.G.M. has decided to let the bathing suits remain out of character. Annette Kellerman, now 65, and still a trim athletic figure, had a hand in this. She said in Hollywood: "The. whole point of my story would be lost if we tried to keep the bathing suits in period. I was always known as a daring pace setter in styles, and so I was. "But to keep the audience interested we decided that Esther would have to go a lot further than I ever dared."
