'Boy Millionaire' Great Showman From KEITH DUNSTAN, in Hollywood Howard Hughes, "the boy million aire,'" has lost none of his verve or his gift for showmanship; but for him Holly wood would appear almost normal. And Mr. Hughes is a man who hates to appear normal. He likes people to think of him as the perfect ex ample of American courage, vigour and snterorise. Hi. capacity for working out new stunts and keeping his name in the paper is remark able. This month he went all out for publicity for his new film "The Las Vegas Story." He invited 66 columnists to a pre view at Las Vegas, Nevada. He sent for them in chartered Constellations. When they arrived, he presented each one with a buckskin bag contain ing 50 silver dollars. Just so they could have a crack at the local gambling tables, he said. He then booked them into the best hotels and presented each one with a ballpoint pen. Just so they could sign for anything and everything they nedeed, he said. R. HUGHES really put on a show. There was a fireworks display, a torchlight parade, film cuties landed from helicopters and local citizens put on cowboys suits. As for the film, it wasn't too good. It's only asset was Jane Russell; and Jane Rus sell's only film asset is her figure. She arrived during the celebrations with a great black bruise on her cheek, which started a good deal of specula tion. "I ran into a door," she :aid. Now that Sid Grauman is dcad, Howard Hughes is the ast of the showmen. He ;eems to be the only man who can spend with an amused dis regard for money. Just before Christmas, lughes threw Hollywood Boulevard into a state of con "usion with the premiere to 'Two Tickets to Broadway." He had a parade, hundreds of dancing girls, vaudeville acts and brass bands all in a jost ing crowd outside the theatre. It was almost as good as the show for "The Outlaw" in 1946, when Hughes floated a 167ft. blimp, shaped to the rather obvious curves, of Miss Russell, across Los Angeles. Hughes is a man who star ted from the top and made good. He inherited his father's tool business in his teens. When he was 20 he was mak ing two million dollars a year. HE made a big name for him self in Holly wood by discovering Jean Harlow, but as an aviator he became even more notorious. -He broke too many records to list here, but his biggest achievement was a flight round the world in two days 19 hours, with a four man crew, in 1936. In New Yorl, they judge your popularity by the amount of paper they throw at you in a ticker tape. parade. The cleaners weigh it up the next day. When Hughes returned from the 1936 round-the world flight, they threw 1800 tons at him. Only one man has done better. They threw 2850 tons at General Mac Arthur last year. His bineest worry ridht now is his flying-boat. He says it is the bigpest aeroplane in the world. It probably is. It weighs 200 tons, has eight en gines and a wingspan of 300 feet. He started work on it in 1942, with the aid of 13 mil lion dollars from the Govern ment. Years went by and the plane never flew, till in 1946, and after Hughes had spent 20 million of his own money -it rose 10 feet off the water. HE Govern ment is ndw demanding that Hughes show some action. He has till June 1 to complete his flying tests. Meanwhile, Hughes is spending money on it at a rate of £300,000 a month. Why he is persevering so hard is something of a mystery. It would be no trouble to him to give the Government back its 13 million dollars. But Mr. Hughes is a man of sur prises, a man not easily licked.
