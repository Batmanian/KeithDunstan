Hughes Has Two Controversial Films By KEITH DUNSTAN, in New York. Howard Hughes, Hollywood millionaire, is the man behind the promotion in America of the two most controversial films for 1950--"Stromboli" and "The Outlaw." It was Mr. Hughes who built the massive £7 million eight-en gined flying-boat during World War II. The one that never rose more than 10 feet above the water and later was wrecked. He signed the contract for the distribution of "Stromboli" with Roberto Rossellini, and released the film in a flood of torrid pub licity. Yet, at the same time, he had another film which is making almost as big a splash as "Strom boli." Back in 1941 he made "The Outlaw," a western, and gave Jane Russell the biggest Hughes ballyhoo since Jean Har low. The film was described as a "scorcher." The censors said they would let it pass if he cut out the scenes showing Miss Russell in a low cut blouse and lolling in a haystack with Jack Beutel. Hughes stood by the blouse and shelved the picture. In 1946 "The Outlaw" was re leased for international consump tion, but not till this year did American censors relent. They passed the blouse but banned post ers which announced "Primitive Love." They couldn't have no ticed a remarkable 167-ft. blimp, outlined with Miss Russell's curves, that Hughes floated across Los Angeles. The picture is unexciting, but the fact that it was banned for so many years has made it a big hit in New York. As for the "wicked" Miss Russell-she is now a sedate married woman with a short hair cut. She has taken up religion and attends regular Bible study meetings. Film of Rommel Following on Mr. Churchill's praise of Field Marshal Rommel, Darryl Zanuck plans to make a film version of the adventures of the German General. This will be the first Hollywood film to look at the war from the German angle and it will be made on location in Africa. The title will be "The Desert Fox" and Zanuck is seeking the rights of Brigadier Desmond Young's book "Rommel." Young, a British Army officer, was captured by Rommel during the war in the desert It is difficult' to associate Mickey Rooney with any other character but Andy Hardy. He announced lately that he is celebrating his 25th year in motion pictures and, after a few more roles, he plans to produce and direct only. Everybody seems to be getting old fast. Sydney Greenstreet has had his 70th birthday. Talking of age, think quickly of any leading half dozen Hollywood male actors and it becomes ap parent that all the best men are over 40. It wasn't long ago that Hollywood wouldn't look at a leading man if he was over.25. If a leading lady got married that meant instant dismissal. "Average Girl" Here are a few short items: Judy Garland is again sick and her "Show Boat" picture has been indefinitely postponed. One sees some strange things on Broadway. This was a sign out side a newsreel theatre: "Mercy Killing-25 cents up to 5 p.m." As you know. Sid Grauman likes to have the foot and hand prints of all the famous stars in concrete at the Hollywood Chinese Theatre. John Wayne made a permanent record of his fist in concrete. He "figured" he had usedtl his fists more than his hands lately. Cornetia Burchard, a German who plays opposite Montgomery Clift in "The Big Lift," has de; manded that the studio use her real name in the picture. The studio is grudgingly obliging. Her real name is Cornell Borchers. Betty Grable's mother and her best friend were entertaining a group of neighbours and naturally the obvious question arose: "What kind of a girl is Betty Grable really?" iHer mother's friend replied quickly: "Betty Grable is just an average American girl who makes 300,000 dollars a year."
