Annie's Gun Was Loaded From KEITH DUNSTAN, In Hollywood. BETTY HUTTON and "Annie Get Your Gun" exploded on Broadway this week like a gigan tic firecracker. Poor Betty was too sick to attend the premiere, but she wrapped herself in a blanket, drove her car to town, and gloated over the crowds outside the theatre. The theatre in Times Square was something to see. Erected outside was a special neon sign outlined to the curves of Miss Hutton. The gaudy affair was 30ft. high, and from her smoking guns bolts of light shot across the front of the building to a target on the other side. People watched it for hours. Already, it is obvious that this film is a tremendous success. Those songs, • "Doin' What Comes Natur'lly" and "They Say It's Wonderful," are on everybody's lips again. • " MIjETRO - Goldwyn - Mayer put £A1,339,000 into this techni colour production This is Once that Hollywood has r.ot turned a stage show completely upside down. There has been no fiddling around with the story. The dialogue is much the same. There are ex travagant Hollywood touches in the transmutation naturally, but these are justified for 'he sake of colour and entertainment. It is Betty Hutton's crowning achievement. Overnight she has shot from a mediocre, rather rowdy Hollywood star to the very top. She tears into :he part of Annie with all the zip of bulldozer-power let loose in a baby Austin. The story goes that Betty Hut ton got her first break in show business by going crazy. She was working as a singer in a Phila delphia night club. One night she heard she was going to be fired, so she gulped down three brandy and benedictines and literally laid 'em flat in the aisles. She jumped somersaults, tore up sheet music, made flying tackles, pulled people's hair, and hopped into musicians' laps. She made her trade mark by being crazy, and she's been crazy ever since. It has paid off. She is booked for a list of pictures as long as your arm. 'THERE used to be a time at Saturday afternoon matinees, between gulps from ice-cream dixies, bags of peanuts and boiled sweets, when the small boys used to love Buck Rogers serials. The science films are coming back again, but this time on a grander scale, and for adult taste, too. In the local theatres, there is a film titled "Rocketship-Expedition Moon." Another coming is titled "Destination Moon." Just so they won't be left out of things, 20th Century Fox is producing "Fare well to the Master," a film all about Martians who pay a visit to the earth. Anne Baxter will play the part of an earth girl. Then "Abbott and Costello Move to the Moon" is another. It seems we are in for an entire flood of inter-planetary films. To cope with this new interest in the universe, New York stores are being filled with space suits. They are complete, of course, with such necessary items for moon exploration as "walkie-talkies" for communication. There is no air or sound on the moon. The suits also have oxygen tubes. They have air-pressures guages to supplement the loss of gravity so they can stand solidly on the surface. Instead of cowboy boots, the fashion for the young manl is mag netic boots. These keep the astral explorer from floating off into non gravitational space. This Hollywood surge into outer space is really a very serious thing. Surely America is in enough political strife without discovering other unhappy worlds. Before President Truman knows where he is, he will have to start Marshall Aid to Mars.
