Hollywood Finishes With Child Stars By Keith Dunstan, in New York. Hollywood in its earnest desire 'to turn out new realistic, thoughtful pic tures has laid aside the child star - a fabulous money-spinner of the '30's. The last of the clan was Mar garet O'Brien. She is an adolescent 13 and there is no one to take her place. There are still child actors, many of whom have a great deal of talent, but there are no stars and the studios do not seem to want any. Last year no child star was listed smong the first 25 box office at. tractions. Apart from Miss O'Brien, there has not been a child star since the '30's. Then we had. Shirley Temple, the greatest of them all. T.here was also Jackie Coogan. He/banked 4 million dol lars before he wds of age. Other famous children were Freddie Bar tholomew, Bonita Granville, :Ieanna Durbin, Jane Withers, and Mickey Rooney. Their films were often sticky sweet with sentiment, but the women folk, particularly, flocked to see them. The war killed off the child star film, and now, perhaps, we are a little hard-boiled for that sort of thing. Anyway, the studios say, what with education problems and labour laws, child stars are so longer worth the trouble. One day a group of Metro Gold wyn Mayer photographers walked up to Elizabeth Taylor, and their leader said shyly, "Miss Taylor, we thought we would like you to know -hat you are the most beautiful wossan we have .ver photographed." That was a remarkable compli ment, because those men have photographed a great number of beautiful women. Ready-made Elizabeth Taylor has thick black hair, bushy eyebrows, large blue eyes, so blue that they are almost violet, and that slightly dangerous expression which to Hollywood means a ready-made goldmine. One entranced studio man said when she first came on the set: "Boy, that girl hasn't got a bad angle. Her complexion's like a bowl of cream with a rose floating in it." Elizabeth recently left high school and this week turned 18. She has now moved into the 1500 dollars a week salary range. According to the men who know her, she is a natural dramatic actress, and pro mises to become one of the great est stars of our time. Her parents are American, she was born in England, and came here at the age of seven. She is a fun-loving young lady, and she has set tongues wagging by becoming engaged for the third time. She likes men, convertible automobiles and reading film magazines. She caused a stir by getting the giggles during her high school graduation ceremony. Asked why, she said: "You see, they wouldn't let any photographers in the hall." She reached the top of her career at 16, and will probably stay at the top for a long time. Demolished Universail is tearing down Holly wood's oldest movie set-a two block western town packed with saloons, stables, rustlers' dens and hotels. It began in 1921 with one building known as ' the "Bar of the 1000 Ghosts." The bar, long and ancient, was made of liquor stained mahogany. During the years it was graced by such stars as Tom Mix, Buck Jones, Marlene Dietrich and Gary Cooper. Six months ago a fire destroyed the old saloon and even 100 cowboy extras could not put it out. Now they find the rest of the town is riddled not so much by bullets but by termites. Universal made 987 westerns at tihs old town. But this does not mean the end of the cowboy era. It seems they are going to build a new western town so we will have to put up with westerns for another 30 years. The Churchill name is magic in Holly wood. There was a rumour that Charles Chaplin's marriage with young Cona was all but finished.
