f Glass * and Bottle BY FRANK DOHERTY Bundarra's prizes I V ' Melbourne’s-Keith Dunstan, writing of the wines from Bundarra near Glenrowan in the Warby Ranges, once likened them to “liquid steak and eggs.” ' V ’ An English visitor with a professional palate for Euro pean wines drank a glass of Bundarra red in the cellar and remarked: "This is not just wine but wine, a meal and-a good cigar”. • For generations since. Richard Bailey began the winemaking business in the 1860s the Bundarra wines (with a label to remind football enthusiasts of an Essendon guernsey) have been among the biggest-bodied reds in the country'. According to the present winemaker,.Harry Tinson, wine judges have usually found them "too big" with "too much of everything" and, in recent years, he has also pro duced a lighter-style red for those who may not be happy with a steak and eggs diet. So it is good news for Victorians that this com paratively small winemaking enterprise recently col lected no less than seven gold medals at the Royal Brisbane Show plus two silvers. Two of the golds went to wines definitely in the old, traditional Bundarra style — a 1978 Shiraz in,the claret class and a Shiraz of the same year in the Burgundy class. These are the wines which, in their way, have made Glenrowan as famous in wine circles as Ned Kelly — who was captured by police after a gun battle centred on the old Glenrowan hotel — ever did in other fields. ACHIEVEMENT Of the other five gold medals, three were awarded for what wine Shows define as sweet whites which are actually fortjfied.wines, not the Spatlese style of table wine, and two went to Muscats which also picked up the two silver medals. I think this Is a great Achievement for Bundarra wines and for Harry Tinson and his wife, Catherine, both graduates In science from Sydney University. They were appointed in 1972 by Davis Consolidated industries, the makers of. that ‘well-known powdered gelatine, after it had bought Bundarra from Alan Bailey, great-grandson of Richard Bailey who began it all in the 1860s. As all other winemakers of the northeast regions of this State, the Baileys had known many hardships, the [worst of which was the Phylloxera plague late last century. Harry Tinson (and Davis, for that matter) has shown excellent judgment in retaining the original style of Bun darra reds and has, in his quiet way, done much to enhance the reputation which they have long enjoyed among discriminating wine-iovers.
