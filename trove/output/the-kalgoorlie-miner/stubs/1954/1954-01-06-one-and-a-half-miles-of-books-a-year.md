ONE AND A HALF MILES OF BOOKS A YEAR London.—The British Museum, that black sombre enormous pile on Great Russell 'street, has run out of space. The world's greatest library, with 6,000,000 books, is groaning under the weight of paper, but still the books arrive bv the thousands, says Keith Dunstan from London. Under the British copyright act the museum must store for the public everything that is printed here. This means all the books, the magazines, the newspapers, the maps and the music. So the sentimental love stories, the Westerns, the children's books come in by the truck-load and they find shelter under the same roof as the original manuscripts of Ben Jonson, Newton, Pope and Milton. Every year the museum needs another one and a half miles of bookshelves. It is an incredible situation, like the sorcerer's apprentice who started his magic and could not stop. Some years ago the museum shifted the newspapers to another building, and now they are undertaking the unbelievable task of microfilming the lot; millions and back through the years. "When that's done youll have millions of pictures of newspapers and the satisfaction of dumping several centuries of newspapers," I said. "Certainly not," a museum official replied. "We'll have the microfilm but by law we'll have to keep the newspapers as well." There are building plans, extremely vague, for the Museum to put up a structure to house books, two or three hundred miles of them, but nothing is going ahead just yet as the building site is still providing some good rents. The indelicate truth is this. The old place is Short of money. It holds treasure worth countless millions, hefty ornaments of solid gold, jewellery of forgotten queens, priceless relics of ancient civilisations going back 4000 years. Yet there is barely cash for tea at 11 and the museum can't afford to pay a full staff of warders. So different wings of the building are open only on alternate days. The museum actually was short of money at the very beginning. In 1753 a trust headed Dy the Archbishop of Canterbury ran a State lottery to raise £30,000 to make the first purchases. Like most lotteriesit was a huge success and they more than doubled their £30,- 000
