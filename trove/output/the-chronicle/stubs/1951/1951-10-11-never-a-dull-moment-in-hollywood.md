NEVER A DULL MOMENT IN HOLLYWOOD NEW YORK. JUST whenever we start to get used to the idea that Hollywood is full of simDle hardwork ing people with barely enough money to pay their income taxes a series of startling in cidents hit the front pages. Hollywood has had its bad times before, but during the past six weeks there hasn't been a dull moment. Frank Sinatra had his jaunt in Mexico with Ava Gardner, Tom Neil knocked Franchot Tone senseless in a fight over Barbara Payton, who married the loser, there were strange circumstances regarding the death of Robert Walker, and David Brian was defendant in a pa ternity suit. John Agar went to gaol for six months for drunken driv ing. Cesar Romero was fined 250 dollars along with a 90-day suspended gaol sentence for being drunk in charge of a car. Lawrence Tierney had the same trouble. There were far too many divorces and marriage mix ups to mention here. But Lana Turner left Bob Topping, Ida Lupino left Col lier Young, and there was even talk that all was not go ing well between Roberto and Ingrid. Errol Flynn made big headlines with the news that the Government was about to seize his yacht because he had failed to pay back taxes. 'Poor Errol, he's down to his last yacht,' one paper commented. On top of this the Un American Activities Com mittee has reopened its en quiry into Hollywood Reds, with the customary publicity. All this has come at an awkward time. The film in dustry for October has planned a great million dol lar advertising campaign to build up the good name of Hollywood and to lead people away from their television sets to the theatres. Over 100 film stars will go on the road making personal appearances in towns from Maine to Florida. They will stress the theme to country people that film stars are homely, quiet-living folk like themselves. It is easy to be cynical on a subject like this, but it is difficult to recall a time when bad publicity hurt a Holly wood star. Errol Flynn, Ro bert Mitchum, Lana Turner, Ingrid Bergman, and Rita Hayworth have all received bad publicity and even public condemnation from politi cians, but they are still among the top box office stars. — Keith Dunstan.
