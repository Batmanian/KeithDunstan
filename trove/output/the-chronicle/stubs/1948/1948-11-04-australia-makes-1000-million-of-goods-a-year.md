Australia Makes . £1,000 Million Of Goods A Year by Keith Dunstan THE vision of Australia as the land of grain, of tall, sun-tanned squatters, shearers and jackaroos, has been swept oway by the Second World War. In proportion to population we are now one of the most highly industrialised nations in the world. Last year the value of the goods produced by our factories was 17 p.c. higher than all those of our primary industries. One in every four of our employes now works in a factory, and from being Britain's largest supplier of food, we have dropped to fifth place. In the 160 years of its history Australia has established a fac tory output of £1,000 million a year. No other nation in the world can equal this rapid growth We now make every thing from 12.000-ton ships to instruments that measure to a millionth of an inch. Even so. this growth of indus try is only just starting. New projects or planned expansion since September, 1945, will in volve an eventual capital ex penditure of £150 million. The study of secondary in dustries and the responsibility of advising large concerns is done by the Division of Indus trial Development, which has its headquarters in Melbourne. At the end of the war the division had to find factory space for hundreds of mush rooming industries. It was able to help a little by turning over 33 huge munition factories, scattered all over Australia. For example, there was the £4,500,000 factory at St. Marys (NSW). This has 200 buildings and houses 84 private firms which produce such things as cooking appliances, chocolates, aluminium, Venetian blinds, artificial jewellery, tiles and cosmetics. St. Marys is a satel lite town with its newly-built houses, and has a community &i\ of its own. * * * * BUT this is only, one muni tion factory. Since 1939 the number of factories in Australia has increased by 29 p.c. The number of factory workers has increased by 50 p.c, and the value of production has doubled. Oversea firms have been re sponsible for much of this. In the period between the end of the war and December. 1947, new capital investments with which overseas firms were asso ciated totalled £40 million. Apart from bringing money to Australia, these firms bring new dollar-saving industries. They bring their trained technicians and technical 'know how' which Austra lians could only learn by years of costly trial and error. In quick time they go into production and, using a large percentage of Australian mana gerial staff and capital, they produce goods already accepted as among the world's best. Before oversea firms decide to come here they ask the most searching questions. They want to know where they can get fac tory space, all about customs regulations, docking facilities, conditions of employment, tem peratures at various towns at all months of the year, and analyses of soil and water. They even want to know intimate things such as details of local picture theatres, hotels and the price of beer. One firm wanted to know the facilities for Roman Catholic worship in an obscure country town. All such information is provided by the Industrial De velopment Division. Among new firms now pro ducing in Australia is the big gest rayon textile producers in the world, Burlington Mills Corporation of North Carolina. They have gone into production at Rutherford, NSW, and will produce 10 per cent, of all rayon piece goods in Australia. Bitumen and Oil Refineries Ltd., now at Sydney, which is associated with the California Texas Oil Company, is another, new company and will produce 100,000 tons of bitumen a year. Australia's requirements are 140*000 tons, which before the war was all imported. Imported bitumen costs £23 a ton, but the local stuff sells for £15, and there is also a by product of 20,000 tons of motor spirit a year. Another firm set up recently is the Sheffield Corporation of Ohio— the largest manufac turers in the world of high pre cision measuring instruments In Australia before the war there was not the need for in struments' that measured to millionths of an inch. Indus try has gone ahead at such a pace that these instruments are now needed in quantity. A similar case is that of B. O Morris Ltd., of Birmingham, the biggest makers in the world of rotary files. Before the war Germany was the only country that had the secret for produc ing machines that make rotary files. In 1939 this company got one of those machines out of Ger many. But for this move England would have had poor production figures for aircraft engines during the war. These are a few examples. You will find many others simnly by looking at advertise ments or by buying your every day goods. Many more are coming. ?
