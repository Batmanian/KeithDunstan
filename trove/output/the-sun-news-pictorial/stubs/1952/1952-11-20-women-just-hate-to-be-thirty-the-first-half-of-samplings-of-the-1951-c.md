Women just hate to be thirty! The first half of samplings of the 1951 census of Britain came out last July. Now we have the second half . . . From KEITH DUNSTAN London. — The British census has shown how baffling, how hopeless it is to deal with women. Many women, particu larly the 30-year-olds, tried to make believe they were girls of 20 by slashing off the years right and left. They forgot that in vincible officialdom has only to look up their birth dates from the last census to discover their lady-like folly. The bashfulness of the thing was dreadful. The women wouldn’t believe the census was secret, so 320,000 refused to tell when they were married or how many children they had. . HEN, most baffling of all, the cen isnnnnvenled there al'e 130,000 more wives in England than husbands Does this make England a nation of bigamists or thousands of women mink it was just nicer to say they were married, and t° risk a £io fine loi the niceness of it. . there's another odd thing. The census showed there are 2,000,000 heads of households in England who are not "gainfully occupied.” They are not pensioners or retired — they simply don’t work. Some of them are women, widows perhaps, but most are happy men who live comfortably on their investments. The population goes up and up, until now it is 48,841,131. A hundred years ago Britain’s popu lation was 20,183.277, and one of the prophets of the time announced: "Our population has reached saturation point." The prophets of 1952 are still talking about saturation, but Britons continue to multiply. Living density for England at 750 to the square mile is second only to Holland which is 788.1. France is 194, United States 49 and Australia 2. Even so, the English are no longer a fertile race. As many as 21 per cent, of the married women under 50 have no children and 30 per cent, have only one. IT might have been an idea to ask some of the over 50 year-olds, for according to the Daily Express, 40 mothers in the last 18 months were 50 or over when their babies were born. The first half of the census made the indis creet observation that only 45 per cent, of houses in Britain have a fixed bath. Now the Government has taken the subject a little fur At this time of the year 59 per cent, of the popu lation have one bath a week and eight per cent, have less than that. Only seven per cent, have as many as five baths a The survey admits that proof on these things is difficult, and probably many people admit to more baths than they really have.
