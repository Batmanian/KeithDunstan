Nobody wants from Keith Dunstan LONDON. — Britain’s “Queen Mary of the air” — the Saun ders Roe Princess — is all dressed up with nowhere to go. In the air she is a suc cess, her performance is magnificent, but the 130 ton Princess, the largest flying boat in the world, ife unwanted, unloved and unbought. Saunders Roe origin ally designed the Prin cess for the Atlantic run and she was to fly direct from London to New York. She was to have everything the Queen Mary had, bar the swimming pool — luxury sleeping berths, cocktail bars and smoking rooms. British Overseas Air ways Corporation aban doned the Princess for two reasons. They wanted to go all out for the Comets, and they felt the cost of running fly ing boat bases, just for the Princess alone, would be too heavy. This Is a real tragedy for Saunders Roe. Sir Arthur Goudge, the same man who gave us the Empire flying boat, also designed the Prin cess. She is a beautiful piece of engineering. There are 10 Proteus turbo-prop engines which deliver a total of 35,000 horsepower. Eight of them are mounted in pairs and they drive contra-rotating propel Thls is an almost un believable concentration of power — enough to drive the huge bulk through the air at 380 m.p.h. for 3500 miles. Enough to take 200 migrants to Australia in three great leaps or 100 passengers in luxury comfort. When you see it on the water at Cowes. Isle of Wight, it looks exactly what it is, a great ship. One Princess has been built and she will go the R.A.P. as a troop carrier. Two more are nearing completion, per haps never to fly. They will go into mothballs, to wait in storage until someone thinks of the Princess boats, worth more than £2 million each, as something more than an interesting ex periment. But the flying boat de signers feel time is on their side. As aeroplanes get bigger, landing wheels become heavier, aerodromes more ex pensive. There is enough concrete at London air port to make a broad, double track highway from Melbourne to Al The hull of a large fly ing boat doesn’t add much more to the weight and with jets designers believe that it will be cqjne, aerodynamically, a perfect instrument. No longer will the wings have to be extra high to make the propeller blades clear the water. Saunders Roe hopes to sell to Tasman Empire Airways a four-jet high speed flying boat called the Duchess, capable. Saunders Roe thinks, of licking its weight in Comets.
